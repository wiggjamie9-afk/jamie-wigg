# RHYTHMIX Brand Positioning Prompts

Purple Cow / Seth Godin framework prompts adapted for RHYTHMIX. Use these for the strategic layer — sharpening what RHYTHMIX *is* — before tactical sales-copy work in `sales-copy.md`.

**Standing context (assume in every prompt):**
- Brand: RHYTHMIX, AI music platform.
- Audience: independent musicians / producers / content creators trying to build a sustainable music business without a label.
- Existing visual identity: red→purple brand gradient, orbital hero motif, wordmark with 3px tracking. Don't reinvent it; sharpen it.
- Voice: confident, outcome-led, creator-first.
- Smallest viable audience: creators who want a *business*, not a hobby tool — the ones already paying for distribution + samples + mastering separately and tired of stitching them together.

---

## 1. Signature symbol

> Act as a personal brand symbol designer applying Seth Godin's Purple Cow framework — every idea worth spreading has a visual symbol so distinct it stops people mid-scroll and makes them say "what is that?"
>
> **Task:** Design a signature symbol for RHYTHMIX that makes it instantly recognisable and impossible to confuse with Suno, Udio, Splice, or DistroKid.
>
> **Steps:** (1) confirm the core idea (the unified create→monetise loop) and audience before starting; (2) identify the single most remarkable concept the brand represents; (3) propose 3 symbol options — visual, object, or metaphor — that capture the idea instantly; (4) test each: can it be drawn, described, or demonstrated in under 10 seconds? (5) select the strongest and show how to use it across landing page, social, and decks.
>
> **Rules:** must be remarkable (worth remarking on), ownable (not already used by competitors), survives without colour, describable in one sentence to a stranger, redrawable from memory a week later.
>
> **Output:** 3 symbol options → remarkability test → strongest symbol → how to use it everywhere.

> *Note:* RHYTHMIX already has the orbital + gradient wordmark. Use this prompt to pressure-test it, not to replace it from scratch.

## 2. Signature slogan

> Act as a brand slogan engineer applying Godin's permission marketing framework — every idea worth spreading has a phrase so specific people repeat it without being asked and share it without being paid.
>
> **Task:** Create a signature slogan for RHYTHMIX that becomes the handle people use to remember, repeat, and recommend it.
>
> **Steps:** (1) confirm core idea, audience, and what RHYTHMIX wants to be known for; (2) extract the single most remarkable truth about what we do; (3) generate 5 slogan options — short, specific, impossible to misattribute; (4) test each against Godin's smallest-viable-audience: would *they* repeat it without prompting? (5) select the strongest and show how to embed it in hero, footer, bio, and pitch decks.
>
> **Rules:** under 6 words, specific enough to own, survives without context, no buzzwords ("innovative", "disruptive", "game-changing" kill it). Test: would someone repeat it in a meeting and attribute it directly to RHYTHMIX?
>
> **Output:** 5 slogan options → remarkability test → strongest slogan → how to embed it everywhere.

> *Current candidate to beat:* "Turn any idea into music."

## 3. Signature surprise (counterintuitive truth)

> Act as a counterintuitive idea specialist applying Godin's Purple Cow principle — the only ideas that spread are the ones that surprise people into sharing them before they even think about it.
>
> **Task:** Identify the single most counterintuitive truth about the music industry that RHYTHMIX is built on, and turn it into the Purple Cow that makes the brand impossible to scroll past.
>
> **Steps:** (1) confirm the field (independent music monetisation), core belief, and audience; (2) identify the most widely accepted belief in the field that we know to be wrong or incomplete (e.g. "you need a label", "AI music is a gimmick", "streaming is the only path"); (3) articulate the counterintuitive truth that challenges it; (4) test the surprise — does it make creators stop and say "wait, really?"; (5) build the reveal sequence — how to deliver the surprise for maximum spread in landing copy, posts, and demos.
>
> **Rules:** must be genuinely remarkable (not just contrarian), defensible (provable when challenged), connected to the core idea (not a random hot take), tension-built before reveal (never lead with the answer). Test: does it make someone screenshot it and send it to a friend?
>
> **Output:** widely accepted belief → counterintuitive truth → remarkability test → reveal sequence.

## 4. Salient idea

> Act as a core-idea sharpener applying Godin's "be specific to someone" framework — brands that get famous are never for everyone, they're unforgettable to someone specific.
>
> **Task:** Identify and sharpen the single salient idea that defines RHYTHMIX — specific enough to own, remarkable enough to spread, clear enough that the smallest viable audience immediately gets it.
>
> **Steps:** (1) confirm everything RHYTHMIX does, teaches, and sells before starting; (2) map every idea, concept, and message currently communicated across the landing page; (3) apply Godin's smallest-viable-audience test — which idea resonates most deeply with the specific creators we want to serve; (4) sharpen it until it can't be confused with Suno's, DistroKid's, or Splice's positioning; (5) show how every feature, testimonial, and section on the landing page points back to this one idea.
>
> **Rules:** one salient idea only (two means none), must be for someone specific not everyone, every piece of content must connect back, if a competitor could use it it's not salient enough. Test: does the smallest viable audience immediately say "this is exactly for me"?
>
> **Output:** idea map → smallest-viable-audience test → sharpened salient idea → how every section points back to it.

## 5. Signature story

> Act as a brand story architect applying Godin's "stories are how we make meaning" framework — every brand that gets famous has a story that makes people feel something before they decide anything.
>
> **Task:** Build the signature RHYTHMIX brand story — the narrative that makes the smallest viable audience feel immediately understood and makes everyone else want to become part of it.
>
> **Steps:** (1) confirm background, core belief, and the defining moment that made RHYTHMIX exist; (2) identify the turning point — the specific moment the founder/team saw the gap; (3) apply Godin's tension-and-resolution arc: before, tension, turning point, after; (4) extract the universal truth that makes the founding story resonate with creators who weren't there; (5) write the signature story in under 3 minutes spoken — specific enough to be ours, resonant enough to spread.
>
> **Rules:** tension before resolution (Godin: tension is what makes people lean in), universal truth extracted explicitly (never left implied), under 3 minutes spoken (never a biography), the "before" must make the audience *feel* the struggle (not just describe it). Test: does this story make a listener say "that's exactly how I feel"?
>
> **Output:** turning point → tension/resolution arc → universal truth → 3-minute signature story script.
