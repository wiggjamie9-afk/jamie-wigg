FAQ

Straight answers.

Will it work with my Suno tracks?
Yes. Suno is the primary track source. We integrate Suno Voices, Custom Models, and direct API. You can import via share link on Free, via API on Pro and Studio.
Can I upload my own track?
On Pro and Studio, yes. MP3, WAV, FLAC up to 12 minutes. Free tier is Suno-only because Kling's tempo detector is trained against Suno-style stems first; uploads from other sources are reliable but Kling renders them slower.
What about voice cloning?
Studio tier only. We use Suno v5.5 Voices — clone one vocal sample, then carry that voice across every track on an album as a persistent virtual artist. Free and Pro do not include this.
Who's the merchant of record for the merch?
Fourthwall. They handle tax, fulfilment, and the legal side in every region they operate in. LIVE provides the storefront wiring and the release-themed templates; Fourthwall does the rest.
How long does a render take?
A 15-second vertical cut, around 90 seconds end-to-end. A full 4-minute landscape cut, ten to twenty minutes depending on the Kling queue. Studio gets priority placement.
Can I use the videos commercially?
Pro and Studio include a commercial-use licence — you can monetise on YouTube, run them as ads, sell merch off them. Free tier is for personal use and watermarked previews only.
What about copyright?
If the track is yours, the cut is yours. Suno-generated audio carries Suno's licence — read Suno's terms; we follow them. We don't claim any rights to the audio or the cut output.
Why is this not headphones-required?
Wrong product. You're thinking of FREQUENCY · RESONATE. LIVE is a video generator. The audio is the input, not the output.
What's the actual difference between $19 Pro and $49 Studio?
Pro ($19/mo) is the default tier for releasing artists — 10 videos a month, no watermark, full Fourthwall merch integration, commercial use. If you're shipping monthly on Suno, Pro is the right pick. Studio ($49/mo) adds two things for serious release operators: persistent voice cloning across a catalogue so a 12-track album sounds like one artist (Suno v5.5 Voices), and a priority Kling render queue that jumps the line during peak. Otherwise Pro is genuinely enough.
How is LIVE different from CapCut, Canva, or Sora?
CapCut needs you to cut to the beat manually — LIVE does it via Kling 2.6, which reads your audio waveform directly. Canva resizes a single video; LIVE renders three native formats in one pass with format-specific framing and cut spacing. Sora is great at Hollywood cinematography but doesn't know your song — LIVE treats the waveform as the script. We're a creator tool for music video specifically, not a general video model replacement.
Which genres work best?
Output is strongest on genres with clear beat structure — electronic, hip-hop, house, trap — because the beat-sync cutter has more transients to lock to. Ambient, classical, and folk render beautifully too but lean harder on the cinematic camera moves than on hard cuts. Spoken word, podcast, comedy, and field recordings are not a fit — there's no musical structure for the cutter.
Can I monetise the videos on TikTok, Reels, or YouTube?
Yes — Pro and Studio include a commercial-use licence on the rendered video side, and you retain rights to your audio. Monetise via TikTok Creator Rewards, Reels Play, YouTube Shorts, ads, sync placements, merch. Practical note: TikTok and Reels run their own AI-content detection — the beat-lock and intentional framing make LIVE output read as composed rather than raw AI, which seems to help, but we can't promise an algorithmic outcome.
Do you take any royalty share?
No. Zero. Whatever you earn from streams, syncs, ad revenue, sync placements, or merch on a video rendered with LIVE — you keep all of it. We don't register a publishing claim, insert a co-write, or take a backend on placements. The subscription is the entire economic relationship. Fourthwall handles its own standard production margin on merch separately — that's between you and them.
What about copyright on AI-generated video?
You retain copyright on your music unconditionally. On the rendered video itself, copyright in purely AI-generated frames is legally unsettled (the US Copyright Office position as of 2025 is that the AI-generated frames alone aren't copyrightable, but your creative direction and the arrangement can be). Practically: you own and control the rendered MP4, can post, monetise, sell and license it for sync. Treat it like any AI-assisted creative work in 2026.
