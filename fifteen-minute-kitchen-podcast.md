## Podcast Positioning

**15-Minute Kitchen** is the daily cooking companion for people who are hungry, short on time, and done with takeout guilt. Every episode walks you through ONE genuinely fast, genuinely tasty meal — start to plate in about fifteen minutes — narrated clearly enough to cook by ear while you chop. No 40-minute videos. No scrolling past someone's life story to reach the recipe. Just: here's dinner, let's go.

**Why it works:**
- Cooking is a DAILY need (everyone eats, every day — built-in habit loop)
- The audience is ENORMOUS and underserved in audio (most food content is video/blog)
- "Fast + easy" is EVERGREEN and recession-proof (people cook more when budgets tighten)
- Premium sponsors LINE UP for food audiences (meal kits, grocery delivery, kitchenware, pantry brands)
- Format is repeatable and cheap to produce — one recipe = one tight episode

**The audio problem (and how we solve it):**
Audio cooking is unusual for a reason — you can't *show* the listener the pan. So the show is engineered around that constraint instead of fighting it:

1. **Ingredient roll-call up front.** Every episode opens with a quick, spoken "here's what you're grabbing" list so listeners can pause and pull it all out before any heat starts. No mid-recipe scramble.
2. **Steps in plain, sequential order — never more than one action at a time.** "Now the garlic. Smash it, peel it, roughly chop. Into the oil." The host narrates *as if standing next to you*, leaving natural beats for the listener to catch up.
3. **Sensory cues replace visuals.** Instead of "until golden," we say "you'll smell it turn nutty and toasty — that's your signal." Sound, smell, and timing carry the information a camera normally would.
4. **Built-in pause prompts.** The host literally says "pause me here if you need a second" at the chop-heavy or simmer moments, so the audio matches kitchen reality.
5. **Forgiving recipes only.** Every pilot is chosen to be hard to ruin — flexible quantities, swappable ingredients, no precision baking. By-ear cooking demands by-ear-friendly food.
6. **Full written recipe in the show notes, every single episode.** This is non-negotiable. The audio is the *coach*; the show notes are the *reference card* — full ingredient list with quantities, numbered steps, swaps, and timing. Listeners cook along by ear and glance at notes only if they lose the thread. Show notes also serve SEO and give sponsors a clickable home.

### **EPISODE 1: "The 15-Minute Weeknight Pasta That Tastes Like You Tried"**
**Air Date:** Today (Day 1)

**Script (~11 min):**

```
[INTRO MUSIC: Warm, upbeat 5-second sting — light acoustic + a sizzle]

HOST: "Welcome to 15-Minute Kitchen — I'm your host, and if you're hungry, tired, and twenty minutes from giving up and ordering in… don't. Put the phone down. Tonight we're making a garlic-and-tomato pasta that tastes like it took an hour, and it's going to take you about fifteen minutes.

Here's your shopping-bag check before we start. Grab these: a pound of pasta — spaghetti, penne, whatever's in the cupboard. Four cloves of garlic. A can of crushed tomatoes, the regular fourteen-ounce size. Olive oil. Salt. A pinch of chili flakes if you like a little warmth, totally optional. And if you've got fresh basil or a hunk of parmesan, set those aside for the finish — also optional, also lovely. Got it all out? Great. Let's cook.

[THE RECIPE — WALKED THROUGH]

Step one: water on. Fill your biggest pot, set it over high heat, and the second it's boiling, salt it hard — like, a big pinch, until it tastes like the sea. That salty water is most of your flavor, so don't be shy. Lid on, let it come up. Pause me here if you're still filling the pot.

Step two — and you can do this while the water heats — the garlic. Take your four cloves, give each one a good smash with the flat of your knife. The skin slips right off. Now slice them thin. Don't worry about perfect.

Step three: a wide pan over medium heat. Pour in a good glug of olive oil — about three tablespoons, enough to coat the bottom. Slide the garlic in. Now listen — you want a gentle, lazy sizzle, not a roar. If it's spitting, turn it down. Cook it about ninety seconds, just until you smell it go soft and sweet and a little nutty. The moment it smells amazing, it's ready. Do NOT let it brown — burnt garlic is bitter, and we want sweet.

Step four: your tomatoes. Pour the whole can in — stand back, it'll spit for a second. Add your salt, a good half-teaspoon, and the chili flakes if you're using them. Stir it together. Now turn it down to a low bubble and just let it sit there and get happy for the next eight, ten minutes while your pasta cooks. Pause me here and go drop your pasta in that boiling water.

Step five: cook your pasta one minute LESS than the box says. Set a timer. While it cooks, here's the move that makes this taste restaurant-good: right before you drain, scoop out a mug of that starchy pasta water. Don't skip it — it's liquid gold.

Step six: drain the pasta, then tip it straight into the tomato pan. Add a splash of that pasta water — maybe a quarter cup. Now toss it over the heat for one full minute. You'll see the sauce go from watery to glossy and clingy, coating every strand. That's the starch doing its magic. Add more pasta water a splash at a time if it looks dry.

[THE TIP]

Quick tip — this is the one thing most people get wrong: finish your pasta IN the sauce, never sauce-on-top in the bowl. That last minute of tossing together is what turns 'noodles with sauce' into actual pasta. Free, takes sixty seconds, changes everything.

[CLOSING]

Kill the heat. Tear in some basil, grate over parmesan if you've got it, one last drizzle of olive oil. Into the bowl.

That's dinner. Fifteen minutes, six ingredients, and it tastes like you tried — because the technique did the work, not the time.

The full recipe with exact amounts and a few swaps is in today's show notes, so save it and make it your Tuesday default. Tomorrow: the stir-fry formula that means you'll never need a recipe again. I'm glad you cooked with me. See you then.

[OUTRO MUSIC: 5 seconds]
```

**Show notes (full written recipe):** 15-Minute Garlic Tomato Pasta — exact quantities (1 lb pasta, 4 garlic cloves, 1×14oz crushed tomatoes, 3 tbsp olive oil, ½ tsp salt + pasta-water salt, chili flakes, optional basil/parmesan), numbered steps, reserve-pasta-water reminder, and swaps (jarred tomatoes, gluten-free pasta, vegan parm).

### **EPISODE 2: "The Stir-Fry Formula — Never Need a Recipe Again"**
**Air Date:** Tomorrow (Day 2)

**Script (~11 min):**

```
[INTRO MUSIC: 5-second sting]

HOST: "Welcome back to 15-Minute Kitchen. Today is the most useful episode you'll hear all week, because I'm not giving you a recipe — I'm giving you a FORMULA. Learn this once and you can stir-fry literally anything in your fridge, forever. Ready?

Grab whatever you've got: a protein — chicken, shrimp, tofu, beef, even just eggs. A couple handfuls of veg — anything, frozen is great. Garlic, ginger if you have it. Soy sauce. A neutral oil. And rice or noodles to serve, if you want. No exact amounts today — that's the whole point. Let's learn the formula.

[THE FORMULA — WALKED THROUGH]

The stir-fry formula is five moves, always the same order. Memorize the order, swap the ingredients forever.

Move one: PREP everything first. This is the secret. A stir-fry cooks in three minutes, so there's no time to chop mid-cook. Cut your protein into bite-size pieces. Chop your veg. Mince a couple cloves of garlic and a thumb of ginger. Line it all up by the stove. Pause me here and get everything chopped — I'll wait. Done? Good. From here it's fast.

Move two: HOT pan, then oil. Get a wide pan or wok screaming hot over high heat — give it a full minute. Then add a tablespoon of oil and swirl. You want it shimmering. Hot pan is what gives you that smoky, restaurant flavor instead of sad, steamed veg.

Move three: PROTEIN first, and don't touch it. Add your protein in one layer. Let it sit thirty seconds to catch some color, THEN stir. Cook it about two, three minutes until it's nearly done, then scoop it out onto a plate. Yes, out — we come back for it.

Move four: AROMATICS then veg. Same hot pan, tiny bit more oil, throw in your garlic and ginger — ten seconds, just till fragrant, don't burn it. Then your veg. Harder veg like carrots and broccoli go first, soft stuff like spinach goes last. Toss it around two, three minutes. You want it bright and crisp-tender, never floppy.

Move five: REUNITE and SAUCE. Protein back in. Now the sauce — and here's your no-recipe sauce: a couple good splashes of soy sauce, and if you've got it, a teaspoon of honey or sugar and a splash of vinegar or lime. Toss everything together thirty seconds till it's glossy and coated. Listen for that loud sizzle — that's the sauce cooking onto the food, not pooling underneath.

[THE TIP]

Here's the tip that fixes 90% of home stir-fries: don't crowd the pan. Pile too much in and it steams instead of fries — you get gray, watery veg. If you're cooking for a crowd, do it in two batches. Thirty extra seconds, completely different dinner.

[CLOSING]

Spoon it over rice or noodles, or eat it straight from the pan — I won't tell.

That's it. Protein out, veg and aromatics, protein back in, sauce. Five moves, any ingredients, forever. You just stopped needing recipes.

The formula's written out in the show notes as a fill-in-the-blank cheat sheet — print it, stick it on the fridge. Tomorrow: sheet-pan dinners, where the oven does all the work and you do almost none. See you then.

[OUTRO MUSIC: 5 seconds]
```

**Show notes (full written recipe):** The 5-Move Stir-Fry Formula — printable fill-in-the-blank cheat sheet (PROTEIN / VEG / AROMATICS / SAUCE blanks), a default 3-ingredient sauce ratio (3 tbsp soy : 1 tsp honey : 1 tsp vinegar), and three worked examples (chicken-broccoli, tofu-veg, shrimp-snap pea).

### **EPISODE 3: "Sheet-Pan Dinners — Let the Oven Do the Work"**
**Air Date:** Day 3

**Script (~11 min):**

```
[INTRO MUSIC: 5-second sting]

HOST: "Welcome to 15-Minute Kitchen. Today's episode is for the nights you have zero energy, because the whole idea is this: you do five minutes of throwing things on a tray, the oven does the other twenty-five, and there's basically nothing to wash up. This is sausage, potatoes, and peppers — but the method works for almost anything.

Here's your check: a pack of sausages, any kind. A couple of small potatoes, or a handful of baby ones. Two bell peppers, any color. An onion. Olive oil, salt, pepper, and any dried herb you like — Italian mix, paprika, whatever's in the rack. One baking tray. That's the whole list. Let's build it.

[THE RECIPE — WALKED THROUGH]

First, turn your oven on — hot, about 425 Fahrenheit, 220 Celsius — and let it preheat while you chop. A hot oven is what gives you crispy edges instead of soggy steam.

Now the chopping, and the rule is simple: everything in roughly the same size so it cooks evenly. Cut your potatoes into small chunks, about an inch — small, because potatoes take the longest. Slice your peppers into thick strips. Cut the onion into fat wedges. Leave the sausages whole. Pause me here and get everything chopped onto your tray.

Now season right on the tray — fewer dishes. Drizzle everything with olive oil, a good two tablespoons, so it's lightly coated, not swimming. Big pinch of salt, some pepper, and a teaspoon of your herb or paprika. Now get your hands in there and toss it all so everything's glossy. Spread it out in ONE layer — this matters — give everything room to touch the hot tray.

Into the oven. Here's the only bit of timing: because potatoes are slow, give them a head start. If you've got five minutes, roast just the potatoes first, then add everything else — but honestly, cut the potatoes small enough and you can do it all together for twenty-five to thirty minutes. Halfway through, around the fifteen-minute mark, pull the tray and give everything a flip with a spatula so it browns on both sides. Pause me — go set a timer for fifteen minutes.

You'll know it's done by your nose and your eyes: the sausages are browned and the potatoes have crispy golden edges and slide easily off the tray. If a potato chunk still resists the spatula, give it five more minutes — they're the boss of the timing.

[THE TIP]

The tip that makes or breaks every sheet-pan dinner: do NOT crowd the tray. If it's all piled up, it steams and goes pale and limp. Everything needs space to touch the metal and crisp. Too much food? Use two trays. A crowded tray is the number one reason sheet-pan dinners disappoint.

[CLOSING]

Pull it out, and if you want it special, a squeeze of lemon or a spoon of mustard on the side. Eat it straight off the tray or tip it into bowls.

Five minutes of effort, one tray to wash, a proper hot dinner. That's the deal.

The full recipe — quantities, the temperature, and a swap list so you can do chicken-and-veg or salmon-and-asparagus on the exact same method — is in the show notes. Tomorrow: five-ingredient meals, for when even the shopping needs to be easy. See you then.

[OUTRO MUSIC: 5 seconds]
```

**Show notes (full written recipe):** Sausage, Pepper & Potato Sheet-Pan Dinner — quantities (1 pack sausages, 2 small potatoes, 2 bell peppers, 1 onion, 2 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp herb/paprika), oven temp 425°F/220°C, 25-30 min with a flip at 15, plus a swap table (chicken thighs + root veg; salmon + asparagus at lower temp/shorter time).

### **EPISODE 4: "5-Ingredient Meals — When Even Shopping Should Be Easy"**
**Air Date:** Day 4

**Script (~10 min):**

```
[INTRO MUSIC: 5-second sting]

HOST: "Welcome to 15-Minute Kitchen. Today's challenge: a real, satisfying dinner from just FIVE ingredients — not counting salt, pepper, and oil, which we all agree are free. Today it's creamy garlic chicken, and you can read this whole shopping list in one breath.

Here it is. One: chicken breasts or thighs, a couple of them. Two: garlic, a few cloves. Three: a cup of cream — or a splash of milk in a pinch. Four: a handful of spinach. Five: parmesan, or any hard cheese you can grate. Plus your free trio — salt, pepper, oil. That's five things. Let's cook.

[THE RECIPE — WALKED THROUGH]

Step one: if your chicken breasts are thick, lay your hand flat on top and slice each one through the middle into two thinner pieces. Thinner means faster and no raw middle. Season both sides with salt and pepper.

Step two: pan over medium-high, a splash of oil, let it get hot. Lay the chicken in — and leave it alone. Don't poke it. Let it cook four, five minutes until the underside is golden, then flip and do the other side, another four minutes. You're listening for a steady sizzle and looking for deep golden color. When it's cooked through, lift it out onto a plate. Pause me here — chicken takes the longest, take your time.

Step three: same pan, all those golden bits stuck to the bottom — that's flavor, leave it. Turn the heat down to medium. Toss in your sliced garlic, stir it ten, fifteen seconds till it smells sweet — don't brown it.

Step four: pour in your cream. As it warms, scrape the bottom of the pan with your spoon — all those stuck bits lift up and melt into the sauce. Let it bubble gently a minute or two until it thickens just enough to coat the back of your spoon.

Step five: handful of spinach in, stir till it wilts down — about thirty seconds, it shrinks to almost nothing. Then grate in your parmesan and stir till it's silky. Taste it. Needs salt? Add it. Now slide the chicken back in, spoon that sauce all over it, and let it warm back through for one minute.

[THE TIP]

Tip of the day: that golden crust the chicken leaves stuck to the pan — chefs call it the fond, and it is pure flavor. Never wash it away. The moment liquid hits the pan and you scrape, it dissolves into the sauce. That one move is why restaurant sauces taste deep and yours sometimes taste flat.

[CLOSING]

Plate it up — gorgeous over rice, pasta, or just with bread to mop the sauce.

Five ingredients. One pan. A sauce that tastes like way more than the sum of its parts.

Full recipe and quantities are in the show notes, along with four more five-ingredient dinners to rotate through the week. Tomorrow — quick, healthy breakfasts, because mornings are the hardest fifteen minutes of all. See you then.

[OUTRO MUSIC: 5 seconds]
```

**Show notes (full written recipe):** 5-Ingredient Creamy Garlic Chicken — quantities (2 chicken breasts/4 thighs, 4 garlic cloves, 1 cup cream, big handful spinach, ⅓ cup grated parmesan + salt/pepper/oil), numbered steps, the "fond" note, and a bonus list of 4 more 5-ingredient dinners (pesto gnocchi, black-bean quesadillas, lemon-butter salmon, tomato-feta baked eggs).

Five minutes at night buys you a calm, healthy morning. The hardest fifteen minutes of the day are the ones you can win the night before.

Three breakfasts: no-cook oats, a two-minute scramble, and a drinkable smoothie. Fast, filling, and good for you — no more grabbing a pastry and regretting it by ten a.m.

## Show Format (Every Episode)

**Structure (10-12 minutes):**
- Intro (30 sec) — Warm welcome + the appetizing hook ("don't order in, make this")
- Ingredient roll-call (1 min) — Spoken shopping-bag check so listeners pull everything out first
- The recipe, walked through (6-7 min) — One action at a time, sensory cues, built-in pause prompts
- The tip (1 min) — One genuine technique that punches above its effort
- Closing (1 min) — Plate it up + "full written recipe in the show notes" + tomorrow's teaser
- Outro (30 sec) — Music + sign-off

**Tone:**
- Warm and encouraging (like a friend cooking next to you)
- Energetic but never frantic
- Appetizing — lean on smell, sound, and texture
- Practical and forgiving (swaps welcomed, no shaming, no precision required)
- Confidence-building (you CAN cook this)

**Audio-cooking rules (apply every episode):**
- Ingredients announced before any heat
- Never more than one instruction per beat
- "Until golden" becomes a smell/sound/look cue
- Explicit "pause me here" at chop-heavy and simmer moments
- Forgiving recipes only — nothing that punishes a by-ear cook
- Full written recipe in show notes, no exceptions
