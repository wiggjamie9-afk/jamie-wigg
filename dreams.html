The Four Layers

Intention. Sound. Breath. Landscape. Recall.

DREAMS is a single nightly ritual built in four stacked layers. Each layer is honest about what it is — not a sleep aid, not a treatment, not a guarantee. A consistent ritual the brain can learn to fall in behind.

I
Spoken · 30 seconds

Intention

You speak — or type — what you're carrying into the night. The on-device LLM holds it as the seed for the soundscape and the landscape that follow. Nothing is sent anywhere.

II
Audio · 8–25 min

Soundscape

The RHYTHMIX engine generates a solfeggio-tuned bed under your intention. 174 Hz Foundation. 528 Hz Mi. 963 Hz Crown. Crossfaded to your chosen length. No two nights the same.

III
Breath · 4 cycles

Breath

A whisper-soft voice walks you through 4-7-8 breath, then steps back. The breathwork voiceover matches the soundscape tempo — generated, not pre-recorded.

IV
Landscape · slow pan

Landscape

A Marble-rendered 3D dream landscape — generated from your intention — pans slowly on your phone. You watch it drift, or you don't, or you close your eyes and let it keep moving without you.

V
Morning · voice / text

Recall

The next morning, you speak whatever fragment you woke with. An on-device agent helps you find the thread — never imposes an interpretation. Yours to keep or to delete.

The Five-Minute Pre-Sleep

Second by second.

The whole ritual is short. Below is the canonical five-minute version — the one that fits between brushing your teeth and closing your eyes. The sleep-onset duration is yours; the ritual is the same.

0:00START

Tap the moon.

The screen dims to bedside-amber. The phone goes Do Not Disturb on its own. You don't have to do anything else.

0:15INTENTION

Speak one thing.

One sentence. "I'm worried about tomorrow." "I'm grateful for the call with my brother." "I want to dream of the lake." Whatever's true. The on-device LLM listens and never sends anywhere.

0:45SOUNDSCAPE

The bed begins.

A solfeggio-tuned drone fades in, seeded by what you said. Brown noise sits under it. You can put the phone down — the audio knows to keep going while the screen sleeps.

1:30BREATH

Four breaths. Then quiet.

A voice — generated, neutral, low — walks you through four cycles of 4-7-8. After the fourth, it steps back. You're not coached for the rest of the ritual. You're trusted.

2:30LANDSCAPE

The world appears.

The Marble-rendered scene begins to pan. A lake at dusk, a mountain pass, a quiet kitchen — drawn from your intention. Slow camera. No interaction. Watch it or close your eyes.

5:00LET GO

The ritual is over.

The visual fades. The soundscape continues at the length you chose — 8, 12, 18, or 25 minutes — then crossfades to silence on its own. You've already let go by then.

The Dream Landscape

A world. Rendered on your phone.

World Labs released Marble in late 2025 — a Gaussian-splat 3D world generator that exports straight to iOS via the open-source Spark renderer. DREAMS uses it to draw the scene your intention asked for, then pans through it slowly while you let go. No game engine. No headset. Just your phone, the way you already use it.

Illustrative still · DREAMS renders a 3D-navigable world from your intention each night

Marble 1.1. Released April 2026 by Fei-Fei Li's World Labs. Exports `.spz`/`.ply` Gaussian splats from a single image, text prompt, or 360° panorama. The first generative-world model with a real iOS path. Spark, the open-source renderer that ships them to your phone, is free.

Why use it for bedtime. A still image asks you to look. A 3D scene lets you drift through it. The camera moves at walking pace, the audio drifts under it, the breath voice steps back. It's the difference between a wallpaper and a window.

Morning Recall

Catch the thread. Don't pull it apart.

Most dream apps want to interpret you. DREAMS doesn't. The morning agent listens to whatever fragment you woke with — a phrase, a colour, a feeling, the name of someone you haven't thought about in years — and helps you write it down. No "your subconscious is telling you" line. No archetype lookup. The thread is yours; the agent helps you hold it long enough to see it.

VOICE · 30 seconds

Speak it.

Hold the phone, eyes closed. The morning prompt is the same every day — "What was there?" — because the question is what carries you across the gap. The on-device transcription captures whatever you say, stripped of identifiers.

TEXT · open-ended

Write the rest.

When the voice is on the page, the keyboard takes over. The on-device agent asks one open question per entry — never an interpretation. "What stayed?" "What colour?" "Who was there?" Then it gets out of the way.

ARCHIVE · local

Your archive.

Every entry lives in a CoreData store inside the app. Not in iCloud, not on any server. Search across your entries by date, by word, by colour. Export to a single text file any time. Delete the whole archive in one tap.

THREAD · across nights

The thread.

After a week of entries, the agent quietly surfaces recurring words, images, or feelings — never interprets them. "The kitchen appeared three times this week." Yours to do with what you want. That's the entire feature.

The Science

Designed to. Never cures.

DREAMS is a wellness ritual built on top of named, measurable mechanisms. The literature describes what slow breath, low-frequency tones, and consistent pre-sleep routines do to the autonomic nervous system. It does not describe what DREAMS will do for your insomnia, your anxiety, or your sleep architecture. Read the papers; then read the disclaimer.

The Lineage

Bedtime ritual is older than the bedroom.

The contemplative bedtime is not a wellness trend. Vedic Yoga Nidra — the "yogic sleep" practice — predates the chair, let alone the mattress. Tibetan sleep yoga walked the practitioner across the threshold of consciousness on purpose. Carmelite night prayer in the cloisters of 16th-century Spain was a wordless, breath-paced descent into wordlessness. Sufi dhikr at 0.1 Hz coherence pre-dated the heart-rate-variability literature by a thousand years.

DREAMS does not invent the form. It re-houses it in a phone, with a generative score and a 3D landscape, and a recall layer that holds the morning thread without commenting on it. The ritual is the same shape it has always been. The instrument is new.

Privacy

Sleep data is the most intimate data.

DREAMS sees what you carry into the night. That can't go anywhere but your phone. The intention transcript runs through Apple Foundation Models on-device. The dream journal lives in a local CoreData store inside the app sandbox. The Marble scene is generated locally from cached splats — no cloud render. The soundscape is the only thing that touches the network, and only as a stateless generative request with no identifier attached.

No analytics. No engagement tracking. No "your dreams this week" newsletter you didn't ask for. Delete the archive in one tap. Delete the whole app and nothing of you remains.

How does the on-device dream-recall agent work? Is my data private?

Short answer: nothing leaves the device. Ever.

Longer answer: the recall agent runs on Apple Foundation Models — Apple's on-device LLM stack shipped with iOS 18+. When you capture a dream by voice or text in the morning, the agent helps you find the thread — recurring symbols, emotional tone, fragments worth pulling on — without imposing an interpretation. The soundscape generation, the breath voiceover, the Marble landscape rendering (via the open-source Spark iOS renderer), and the recall agent all run locally after the first load. No audio uploaded. Will DREAMS help me sleep? Is it for insomnia?

Honest answer: DREAMS is a wellness ritual, not a treatment. It is designed to support a consistent pre-sleep practice — slow breath, low-frequency tones, a drifting landscape — that the literature associates with vagal tone, reduced sleep-onset latency, and improved subjective relaxation. It is not a sleep aid and it does not diagnose, treat, or cure insomnia, sleep apnoea, or any clinical sleep disorder.

If you have a clinical sleep problem, please see a clinician. If you want a ritual the brain can learn to fall in behind, DREAMS is built for that — and the 14-day refund is there if it doesn't move the needle for you.

Do I have to do the whole five-minute ritual every night?

No. The five-minute walkthrough is the canonical version — intention, soundscape, breath, landscape, then let go — but the ritual is yours. Skip the intention on a quiet night. Run breath-only when you're already in bed. Let the soundscape play for 8, 12, 18, or 25 minutes; the length is a slider, not a prescription.

The morning recall is optional too. Most nights people don't wake with a fragment worth catching — that's normal. The thread the agent surfaces across a week of entries is the feature; missing a day doesn't break it.
