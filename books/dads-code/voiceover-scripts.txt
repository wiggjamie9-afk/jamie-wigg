DAD'S CODE — VOICEOVER SCRIPTS (record-ready)
Read each one aloud in your own voice. ~25-40 seconds each.
Slow down on the last line. Leave a breath at the end.

------------------------------------------------------------
1. TUESDAY NEVER CAME
The last time I spoke to my father, I said, "I love you, Dad."
He said, "I'll chat to you on Tuesday."
Tuesday never came. He passed that weekend.
Twenty-five years of silence, and we'd only just found our way back.
If there's someone you've been meaning to call... don't wait for Tuesday.

------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE BOY WHO SPOKE IN NUMBERS
I'm dyslexic. As a kid, words were locked gates.
I'd freeze when asked to read aloud, and the other kids laughed.
But numbers? By nine I knew my twelve times tables.
Numbers never mocked me. I wasn't broken.
My mind just spoke a language no one around me understood yet.
If that's your kid - they're not broken either.

------------------------------------------------------------
3. NAMING HER INDYANNA
The most tender memory I have of my mum is a blanket and a VHS.
Christmas Eve. She'd cooked all night.
She pulled a blanket over us, put on Indiana Jones, and talked softly till I fell asleep.
When my daughter was born, I named her Indyanna - so that love would never leave.
We carry the people we lost in the names we give.

------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE AIR CUDDLE
My son and I invented a way to say I love you without words.
Every night, Jackson whispers "I love you, Dad,"
and we press our ears together. We call it the air cuddle.
It's the exact feeling I never had as a kid, now alive between us.
Fatherhood isn't about being perfect. It's about being there.

------------------------------------------------------------
5. CHOOSING TO STAY   (pin support resources)
There were days staying alive felt harder than giving up.
I've lived with depression for years. Some days, dying felt easier than enduring.
I never acted on it - because my life is precious. To me. To my kids. To my wife.
I've lost too many friends to that darkness, and I refused to add my name.
If you're in it right now: stay. You matter more than the lie in your head.

------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE SCRATCHED CUP
When Dad died, my brothers gave me something worthless.
A dented old cup - older than me, drunk from by everyone in the family for forty years.
To anyone else, rubbish. To me, sacred.
It sits beside his photo and reminds me every day:
nights get tough, but how you carry yourself matters most.
Be honest. Be proud. Be who you are.

------------------------------------------------------------
7. COACH DAD
I was never the star player. I became the coach anyway.
My son was on a two-year waitlist, struggling with reading like I did.
I couldn't fix the system. But I could put a whistle around my neck.
On that field he wasn't a boy waiting for a diagnosis - he was fast, capable, part of something.
Sometimes you can't fix it. You can show up.

------------------------------------------------------------
8. THEY'RE ONLY JEALOUS BECAUSE YOU SHINE
My daughter got mocked for doing well - the same thing that happened to me.
I knelt down and told her what I needed to hear at her age:
"They're only jealous because you shine."
She stood taller.
Don't dim your light to make small people comfortable.

------------------------------------------------------------
9. FOUR UNDER FOUR
I went from no kids to four children under four.
Twins barely one, and we found out a fourth was coming.
A month later I was made redundant.
I smiled on the outside and panicked on the inside.
But structure is my language, so I organised the chaos.
Love outpaced capacity - and somehow, we made it.

------------------------------------------------------------
10. BOTH HANDS
I hurt my foot as a kid, so I taught myself to kick with the other one.
Left-handed in a right-handed job? I taught myself to write with both.
It was messy. It was hard. I didn't give up.
At forty-eight I can kick and write with either hand.
Mistakes aren't failures - they're the only curriculum that matters.

------------------------------------------------------------
11. A NAME AT 47
I spent forty-seven years wondering what was wrong with me.
When my son was diagnosed with ADHD, I finally asked about myself.
Decades of feeling different, restless, "too much" - it had a name.
With the right help, the noise eased.
Peace isn't the absence of struggle. It's the presence of clarity.
It's never too late to finally understand yourself.

------------------------------------------------------------
12. BE LIKE McDONALD'S
The best life advice I have sounds ridiculous: be like McDonald's.
Think of yourself as a business - would you buy from you?
The people who last are consistent, honest, and dependable.
Always there. Always the same when it counts.
If you believe in your own product - yourself - you can be anything.
Be real. Be honest. Be who you are.
