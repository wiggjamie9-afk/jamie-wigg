## Podcast Positioning

**Side Hustle School** is the daily, no-BS coaching session for people with a full-time job and a quiet itch to make more money. Twelve minutes. One specific, do-it-this-weekend tactic per episode. No "follow your passion." No "build your personal brand." Just the exact steps, the exact tools, and the exact numbers — explained like a friend who actually did it is sitting across the table from you.

**Why it works:**
- Side hustles are EVERGREEN — cost of living rises every year and wages don't keep up.
- The audience is HUGE — most employed adults under 45 have considered a side income.
- Listeners are MOTIVATED — they're not browsing, they're searching for a way out.
- Sponsors PAY WELL — the exact tools we recommend (Shopify, Etsy, Canva, Fiverr, payment apps, AI tools) want this audience badly.

**Why now:**
- Remote and hybrid work freed up commute time and home hours.
- AI tools collapsed the cost of starting from thousands of dollars to nearly zero.
- Marketplaces (Etsy, Gumroad, Upwork, eBay, Facebook Marketplace) are mature and frictionless.
- "Quiet quitting" and layoff anxiety pushed millions to want a Plan B that they own.

**The promise of every episode:** You will end the 12 minutes with one action you can take this weekend, with $0 to $50, that could earn real money.

### **EPISODE 1: "Starting With $0 — The First $100 Before You Spend a Dime"**
**Air Date:** Today (Day 1)

**Script (12 min):**

```
[INTRO MUSIC: Upbeat, warm 4-second sting]

HOST: You're listening to Side Hustle School. I'm your host, and this is the show that gives you one real way to make extra money — every single day, in about twelve minutes.

No fluff. No "manifest your abundance." Just the stuff that actually works.

Today we're answering the question I get more than any other: "I want a side hustle, but I have no money to start. What do I do?"

Here's the good news. The biggest myth in the side hustle world is that you need money to make money. You don't. You need to sell something before you build anything. So today, you're going to make your first hundred dollars without spending a cent. Let's go.

[THE CORE IDEA — 2 min]

Here's the mistake almost everyone makes. They decide they want a side hustle, so they buy a course, a logo, a website, business cards, maybe a ring light. Three hundred dollars gone. Zero dollars earned. Then they get discouraged and quit.

We're going to do the exact opposite. We're going to get a customer first. Money comes in before any money goes out. That's the whole game today.

The fastest way to earn with zero dollars is to sell a service, not a product. A product costs money to make. A service is just your time and your hands. You already own both.

[THE TACTIC — STEP BY STEP — 5 min]

Step one. Make a list of things you already know how to do that other people find annoying or hard. Don't overthink "skills." I mean basic stuff. Can you assemble flat-pack furniture? Drive? Write a clear email? Edit a phone video? Set up a printer? Walk a dog? Clean a stove? Put a child's car seat in correctly? Every one of those is something somebody near you will pay for tonight.

Step two. Pick ONE. Just one. The one you could do half-asleep.

Step three. Go where the buyers already are. You don't need a website. You need to post in three places this weekend: your local Facebook group or Marketplace, the Nextdoor app for your neighborhood, and a text message to ten people you actually know. The text matters most. Here's the exact message: "Hey — I'm picking up a few odd jobs on weekends. If you ever need help with [the thing], I'm five dollars cheaper than anyone and I'll do it well. No worries if not." That message has gotten people their first client within an hour. Friends want to help you, and they have problems they've been ignoring.

Step four. Price it stupidly simple. Pick a flat number. Forty dollars to assemble the furniture. Twenty-five dollars to clean out and organize a closet. Thirty dollars to set up a new phone for someone's parent. Flat price, no quoting, no negotiation. Simple gets booked. Complicated gets ignored.

Step five. Over-deliver on the first job on purpose. Show up early. Bring your own basic tools. Take a quick before-and-after photo. Then ask one sentence: "Do you know anyone else who needs this?" That one question is your free marketing department.

[REAL EXAMPLE — 2 min]

Let me give you a real, specific version of this. Take "tech help for older adults." Almost every person over sixty near you is quietly struggling with their phone, their TV streaming, or their email, and they're embarrassed to ask family for the tenth time. You post in a local group: "Patient tech help for seniors — phones, tablets, TVs, fifty dollars a visit, I come to you." You will get calls. One visit becomes a standing weekly appointment, because they always have a new question. People doing this exact thing book three to five visits a weekend at fifty dollars each. That's a hundred and fifty to two hundred and fifty dollars a weekend, started with zero dollars and a phone.

The pattern is always the same: a service, a boring problem, the place buyers already hang out, a flat price, and one referral ask.

[CLOSING + CTA — 1 min]

So here's your assignment before the next episode. One: write down five things you already know how to do. Two: pick one. Three: send that exact text message to ten people tonight. That's it. Don't build a brand. Don't make a logo. Get one yes.

If you land your first client, tell me about it — we feature listener wins every weekend. Tomorrow we level up: how to turn a skill into a digital product you make once and sell forever. That's where this gets really fun.

I'm rooting for you. This is Side Hustle School. Go get your first yes.

[OUTRO MUSIC: 4 seconds]
```

**Key takeaways:**
- Start with a service, not a product (zero startup cost)
- Sell before you build — get the customer first
- Three channels: local Facebook/Marketplace, Nextdoor, ten personal texts
- Flat pricing + over-deliver + referral ask
- Realistic first-weekend target: $100-250

### **EPISODE 2: "Selling Digital Products — Make It Once, Sell It Forever"**
**Air Date:** Tomorrow (Day 2)

**Script (12 min):**

```
[INTRO MUSIC: Upbeat, warm 4-second sting]

HOST: You're listening to Side Hustle School. Yesterday we got you your first client with zero dollars. Today we build the dream most people have when they think "side hustle": money that comes in while you sleep.

That's what a digital product is. You make it one time. You sell it a thousand times. No shipping. No inventory. No customer standing in your kitchen. Let's talk about how a complete beginner actually does this — this week.

[THE CORE IDEA — 2 min]

A digital product is just a file someone pays to download. A template. A guide. A checklist. A spreadsheet. A set of icons. A meal plan. A Notion layout. A printable wall art. That's it. It feels like it should be hard. It isn't. The hard part is picking the right one, and that's where most beginners freeze. So I'm going to make that decision easy for you.

The rule: don't sell information that's free on YouTube. Sell something that saves people time or removes a blank page. People don't pay you to teach them — they pay you so they don't have to start from scratch. A template wins because the buyer opens it and the work is half done.

[THE TACTIC — STEP BY STEP — 5 min]

Step one. Pick your buyer, not your topic. "Etsy sellers" is a buyer. "Real estate agents" is a buyer. "New dog owners" is a buyer. "Couples planning a wedding on a budget" is a buyer. Choose a group of people who already spend money and already feel stressed about something.

Step two. Find the blank page they hate. Real estate agents hate writing listing descriptions. New freelancers hate writing client contracts and invoices. Wedding couples hate the budget spreadsheet. Small business owners hate their social media calendar. The product is whatever fills that blank page for them.

Step three. Build it with free tools. For a design product — printables, social templates, planners — use Canva. It's free, and it lets you make a downloadable PDF or a Canva template link in an afternoon. For a spreadsheet product, use Google Sheets and export it. For a Notion template, build it in Notion and share a duplicate link. You do not need design skills. You need a clean, finished thing that works.

Step four. Use AI to do the heavy lifting on the content. Ask a free AI tool: "Give me 30 Instagram caption templates for a coffee shop." Then you arrange them nicely in Canva. You're not inventing every word — you're curating and packaging. Packaging is the value.

Step five. List it where buyers already shop. Two platforms for beginners: Etsy and Gumroad. Etsy has built-in traffic — people are already there searching "wedding budget template" right now, today. That's the magic. You don't have to find buyers; Etsy sends them to you. Gumroad is better if you'll drive your own traffic later. Start on Etsy. Price your first product between five and fifteen dollars. Low enough to be an impulse buy, high enough to feel real.

Step six. Make the listing photos do the selling. On Etsy, the thumbnail is everything. Show the template in use — a mockup of the planner on a desk, the spreadsheet on a laptop screen. Canva has free mockup frames for this. A good photo of a simple product beats a bad photo of a great product every time.

[REAL EXAMPLE — 2 min]

Here's a concrete one that works right now. Search Etsy for "Google Sheets budget tracker." You'll see sellers with thousands of sales at eight dollars each. The product is one spreadsheet with some colored tabs and automatic totals. Someone built it once, maybe a weekend of work. At eight dollars and a thousand sales, that's eight thousand dollars from a single file — and it keeps selling. Another one: "small business social media content calendar," a Canva template, sells for twelve dollars, hundreds of sales. The maker updates it once a year. That's the entire model. One file. Real demand. Repeat sales.

Will your first one explode? Probably not. Most digital products earn slowly at first — a sale here, a sale there. But you can make five or ten of them, and the winners carry the rest. That's why this is a numbers game you can win on the weekends.

[CLOSING + CTA — 1 min]

Your assignment: pick one buyer group, find the blank page they hate, and build one simple template in Canva or Google Sheets this week. List it on Etsy for under fifteen dollars with a clean mockup photo. Done is better than perfect.

A reminder — these are projections, not promises. Some products earn fifty dollars, some earn five thousand. The point is that you find out by shipping, not by planning.

Tomorrow: the fastest path to real money for most people — freelancing a skill you already use at your day job. This one can replace a paycheck. This is Side Hustle School. Go build your first file.

[OUTRO MUSIC: 4 seconds]
```

**Key takeaways:**
- Digital product = a file people pay to download
- Pick the buyer first, then the blank page they hate
- Build with free tools: Canva, Google Sheets, Notion; use AI to draft content
- Sell on Etsy (built-in traffic) at $5-15
- It's a numbers game — make several, let winners carry the rest

### **EPISODE 3: "Freelancing Your Existing Skills — The Fastest Path to Real Money"**
**Air Date:** Day 3

**Script (12 min):**

```
[INTRO MUSIC: Upbeat, warm 4-second sting]

HOST: You're listening to Side Hustle School. Today is the episode for the person who says "I don't have any special skills." Yes, you do. You use them at work every single day. And other people will pay you directly for them, on the side, tonight.

This is freelancing. And for most employed people, it's the single fastest way to earn meaningful extra money — because you're not learning anything new. You already know how to do the thing. Let's turn it into cash.

[THE CORE IDEA — 2 min]

Here's the reframe. Your job is a bundle of skills your employer rents from you forty hours a week. Every one of those skills can be rented to someone else too. Do you make spreadsheets? Companies pay for that. Do you answer customer emails? That's "customer support," and small businesses are desperate for it. Do you schedule, organize, proofread, post to social media, edit photos, enter data, write product descriptions? All of it is freelance work that's bought and sold every day.

The thing holding you back isn't skill. It's that you've never seen your daily work as a product. Today we fix that.

[THE TACTIC — STEP BY STEP — 5 min]

Step one. Write your "skill inventory." List every task you do at work in a normal week. Be boring and specific. Not "marketing" — "I schedule social media posts and write the captions." Not "admin" — "I organize calendars and clean up messy spreadsheets." Specific tasks are what people search for and buy.

Step two. Turn the strongest one into a clear offer. The formula is: "I help [who] do [specific task] so they can [benefit]." For example: "I help busy real estate agents turn their messy notes into polished listing descriptions." That's a real, sellable offer. Vague gets ignored; specific gets hired.

Step three. Pick one platform and make one profile. For beginners, the two best are Upwork and Fiverr. Fiverr is better if you want buyers to come to you — you list a fixed-price "gig" and people order it like a product. Upwork is better if you're willing to send proposals and want bigger clients. Start with Fiverr. Create one gig for your one offer. Don't make five gigs. Make one good one.

Step four. Price to get reviews first, not to get rich first. Your first three clients are not for profit — they're for proof. Price low, do excellent work, and get a five-star review with a screenshot you can show. On Fiverr, start a gig at the platform minimum, then raise your price every time you get a new review. Reviews are the currency. Once you have ten, you can charge two to three times more.

Step five. Win with speed and communication, not talent. Here's a secret about freelance platforms: most freelancers reply slowly and write badly. If you reply within an hour, write in clear English, and hit your deadlines, you will out-earn people who are technically better than you. Reliability is rarer than skill.

Step six. Set boundaries so it stays a side hustle, not a second job that ruins you. Decide your hours up front — say, weeknights after eight and Saturday mornings. Tell clients your turnaround time and stick to it. The point is freedom, not burnout.

[REAL EXAMPLE — 2 min]

Concrete example. Say your day job involves cleaning up data in Excel. On Fiverr, search "Excel data entry" or "clean up spreadsheet." You'll see gigs starting at ten dollars and going up to two hundred. A beginner posts a gig: "I'll clean, format, and organize your messy Excel or Google Sheet — twenty dollars." They do three jobs cheap, collect three five-star reviews, then raise the price to fifty. Within a couple months, a clean spreadsheet job that takes them forty-five minutes earns fifty to eighty dollars, and repeat clients come back monthly. That's a few hundred dollars a month from a skill they were already using for free at work.

Another: someone who writes clear emails at their corporate job offers "I'll write your customer service email templates" — small online stores buy this constantly because the owner hates writing them.

[CLOSING + CTA — 1 min]

Your assignment: write your skill inventory tonight — every task from a normal work week. Circle the one you're best at. Turn it into one clear offer using the formula: "I help [who] do [task] so they can [benefit]." Then create one Fiverr gig before the weekend. Price it low, collect reviews, raise it.

These numbers are projections, not guarantees — your speed depends on your niche and how fast you reply. But this is the path that most often turns into real, replaceable income.

Tomorrow: the side hustle you can literally start with stuff in your house right now — flipping and reselling. This is Side Hustle School. Go list your gig.

[OUTRO MUSIC: 4 seconds]
```

**Key takeaways:**
- Your day-job tasks are sellable skills — make a specific "skill inventory"
- Offer formula: "I help [who] do [task] so they can [benefit]"
- Start with one Fiverr gig; price low to collect reviews, then raise
- Win on speed, clear communication, and reliability — not raw talent
- Set hours and turnaround boundaries to keep it a side hustle

### **EPISODE 4: "Flipping and Reselling — Turn Stuff You Find Into Cash"**
**Air Date:** Day 4

**Script (12 min):**

```
[INTRO MUSIC: Upbeat, warm 4-second sting]

HOST: You're listening to Side Hustle School. Today's hustle is the most hands-on one we've covered, and honestly the most fun. We're going to buy things cheap and sell them for more. It's called flipping. People have quietly built five-figure-a-month businesses doing it, and you can start this weekend with what's already in your house.

No skills required. No content to make. Just an eye for value and a phone camera. Let's get into it.

[THE CORE IDEA — 2 min]

Flipping is simple: you find an item priced below what it's worth, and you sell it to someone who'll pay full value. The profit is the gap. The whole skill is learning to spot that gap — and I'm going to show you exactly where it lives.

But here's the part nobody tells beginners: don't start by buying. Start by selling. You have things in your home right now worth more than you think — old electronics, brand-name clothes, kitchen gadgets, kids' toys, tools, video games. Selling your own stuff first teaches you the entire process — photos, listings, shipping, dealing with buyers — with zero risk. You learn on items you got for free.

[THE TACTIC — STEP BY STEP — 5 min]

Step one. Do a "first fifty dollars" sweep of your home. Walk through with a box. Anything you haven't touched in a year and could sell for at least ten dollars goes in. Electronics and brand-name items first — they sell fastest.

Step two. Pick your platform by item type. Electronics, collectibles, and brand-name goods: eBay, because buyers everywhere search it. Furniture and bulky stuff: Facebook Marketplace, because it's local and you avoid shipping. Clothing and accessories: Facebook Marketplace or a clothing app. Match the item to where its buyers look.

Step three. Photos and titles do ninety percent of the selling. Take photos in daylight, against a plain background, from several angles, including any flaws — honesty prevents returns. For the title, write what people actually type into search. Not "cool old camera." Instead: "Canon AE-1 35mm Film Camera with 50mm Lens — Tested, Working." Brand, model, condition, keywords. That title is the difference between zero views and a sale.

Step four. Price using sold listings, not guesses. This is the single most important flipping skill. On eBay, search your item, then filter by "Sold Items." That shows you what people actually paid — not what hopeful sellers are asking. Price just under the average of recent solds to sell fast. Reality beats wishful thinking every time.

Step five. Once you've sold your own stuff, start sourcing cheap. Where the gaps live: thrift stores, garage sales, estate sales, Facebook Marketplace "free" and "must go today" listings, and clearance racks. The mindset: you're hunting for items priced by someone who doesn't know their value. A ten-dollar thrift-store jacket that's a sought-after brand can resell for sixty.

Step six. Pick one category and get good at it. Don't flip everything. Pick a lane — vintage clothing, or specific electronics, or kitchen appliances, or board games — and learn its values cold. A focused flipper who knows exactly what a thing is worth makes faster, safer decisions than someone guessing across ten categories.

[REAL EXAMPLE — 2 min]

Concrete example. Sneakers and brand-name clothing. A flipper goes to thrift stores on Saturday mornings with one rule: only buy known brands in good condition for under fifteen dollars. They find a Patagonia fleece for twelve dollars. On eBay sold listings, that exact fleece sells for fifty-five to seventy. They list it at fifty-nine, sell it in a week, net around forty after fees and shipping. Do that with eight items from one thrift run and that's a few hundred dollars profit from one Saturday and a forty-dollar starting spend.

Another lane: people flip used kitchen appliances off Facebook Marketplace — buy a barely-used stand mixer locally for forty, clean it, relist it with better photos for ninety. No shipping, cash on pickup, profit in an afternoon.

A realistic note: not everything sells fast, and fees eat ten to fifteen percent. Track what you paid and what you netted, or you'll fool yourself. The winners fund the slow movers.

[CLOSING + CTA — 1 min]

Your assignment: do the home sweep this weekend. Box up ten items, photograph them in daylight, write search-friendly titles, price them off sold listings, and list five on eBay or Marketplace. Learn the whole loop on your own stuff. Next weekend, do one thrift run with the under-fifteen-dollars, known-brands-only rule.

Remember, these numbers are projections — your area, your eye, and your patience all matter. But the loop is real and it's repeatable.

Tomorrow, the big one — the episode about the side hustle that grew into a full-time income, and exactly how to know when to make the leap. This is Side Hustle School. Go fill a box.

[OUTRO MUSIC: 4 seconds]
```

**Key takeaways:**
- Flipping = buy below value, sell at value; the profit is the gap
- Start by selling your own stuff first — learn the loop at zero risk
- eBay for shippable/brand items, Facebook Marketplace for local/bulky
- Price off "Sold listings," not asking prices
- Pick one category, learn its values cold; track costs after fees

### **EPISODE 5: "The Side Hustle That Became Full-Time — How to Know When to Leap"**
**Air Date:** Day 5

**Script (12 min):**

```
[INTRO MUSIC: Upbeat, warm 4-second sting]

HOST: You're listening to Side Hustle School. This week you learned how to start with zero dollars, build a digital product, freelance your skills, and flip for profit. Today's the episode about the dream behind all of it: the moment a side hustle becomes the main thing. When the thing you do on weekends quietly out-earns your job — and you walk away from the paycheck on your own terms.

This is the most exciting and the most dangerous episode, because this is where people make emotional decisions that hurt them. So today, I'm going to give you the calm, numbers-based way to make the leap without blowing up your life.

[THE CORE IDEA — 2 min]

Here's the truth most "quit your job" gurus won't tell you: the goal is not to quit fast. The goal is to make quitting boring. By the time you leave your job, the decision should already be obvious, because the numbers made it for you. No leap of faith. A short, safe step over a line you drew in advance.

The mistake people make is quitting on a good month — one big sale, one viral post — and assuming it'll last. Then the next month is quiet, and they panic. We're going to use steady proof, not a lucky spike.

[THE TACTIC — THE LEAP CHECKLIST — 5 min]

There are five signals. Don't leap until you hit all five.

Signal one: your side income has matched your take-home pay for three months in a row. Not one great month. Three steady months. Consistency proves it's a business, not a fluke. Use your after-tax number from the job as the target, because side hustle income gets taxed differently — set aside roughly a quarter to a third of it for taxes from day one so the comparison is honest.

Signal two: you're turning away work. If you're regularly saying "I'm full, can't take it on," that's the market telling you there's room to grow once you free up your weekdays. Demand should exceed your current capacity.

Signal three: you have a cash cushion of three to six months of expenses, separate from the business. This is your runway. Income dips happen, clients leave, platforms change rules. The cushion is what keeps a slow month from becoming a crisis.

Signal four: your income isn't dependent on a single client or a single platform. If one client is eighty percent of your money, you don't have a business, you have a fragile job with extra steps. Before you leap, spread out — several clients, or several products, or two platforms. Diversify your income before you depend on it.

Signal five: you've stress-tested the boring stuff. Health insurance if you're in a country where the job provides it. Self-employment taxes. Retirement savings you were getting from an employer match. Map these out on one page before you quit, not after. The dream dies on unglamorous details, so handle them in advance.

[REAL EXAMPLE — 2 min]

Here's how this plays out in real life. Picture someone who started freelancing spreadsheet work on the side — episode three's exact path. Month one, they made two hundred dollars. By month six, eight hundred. By month ten, they had four steady monthly clients and a few thousand dollars coming in. They didn't quit. They kept going. For three straight months their side income matched their take-home pay, they were turning away two clients a month, they'd saved five months of expenses, and no single client was more than a third of their income. Only then did they give notice. Their first full-time month wasn't a scary cliff — it was just Tuesday with more hours. That's the goal. Make it boring.

And here's the honest part: not every side hustle should become full-time, and that's completely fine. An extra five hundred or a thousand dollars a month, every month, changes your life — it pays off debt, builds a cushion, takes the fear out of a layoff. You don't have to quit anything to win. Full-time is one option, not the scoreboard.

[CLOSING + CTA — 1 min]

Your assignment: write down your five-signal checklist and your real take-home pay number. Tape it somewhere you'll see it. That number is your line. You don't leap until all five signals are green — and when they are, the decision makes itself.

This wraps our first week. These have all been projections and real patterns, not promises — your results depend on your effort, your niche, and a bit of luck. But every single tactic this week is something you can start with this weekend.

So pick one. Just one. Episode one's first client, episode two's first file, episode three's first gig, episode four's first box. Start there. I'll be right here tomorrow with the next one. This is Side Hustle School. Go make it boring.

[OUTRO MUSIC: 4 seconds]
```

**Key takeaways:**
- Don't leap fast — make quitting "boring" with steady proof
- The five-signal checklist: 3 months of matched take-home income; turning away work; 3-6 month cash cushion; income not dependent on one client/platform; boring stuff (insurance, taxes, retirement) stress-tested
- Set aside 25-33% for taxes from day one
- Full-time is one option, not the scoreboard — steady extra income wins too

**Lower Tier ($500-4k/mo):**
- Podcast hosting and scheduling tools

- Shipping supplies / label services (relevant to flippers)
- VPN and password managers (work-from-home crowd)
