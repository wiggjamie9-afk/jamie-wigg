## The threshold

For most of human history, the moment before sleep was treated as a practice. The Upanishadic teacher closes with the long held *AUM* — the nasal hum that the *Mandukya* says belongs to the four states of consciousness, including the one we are about to enter. The Christian monastic day closes with the office of compline: a single short service, dim light, the same psalms each night, a small examination of conscience and a request for rest "without fear or restless dream". The Sufi *khalwa* tradition uses the night for breath, name-recital, and the gentle suspension of the self into sleep. The Hatha Yoga Pradīpikā lists Bhramari Pranayama — humming breath — among the practices that precede the night's rest. *Bhramari* literally means "of the bee".

Across traditions that share no common ancestor, the threshold of sleep is the most attended moment of the day. The reason is not mystical. It is structural: what you take into the night shapes what the night does to you.

FREQUENCY DREAMS is a way to do that, on an iPhone, in 2026.

## The four layers

The product is a single ritual, not a feature list. Four layers, composed for tonight's intention, performed in sequence.

**One — Sound.** You speak an intention. A feeling, a question, a fragment of last night's dream. The RHYTHMIX FREQUENCY engine — already in your hands — generates a custom solfeggio-tuned soundscape conditioned on that intention. Roughly 25–40 minutes of bespoke audio. It plays from your phone. It loops. It is the room you fall asleep in.

**Two — Breath.** A breathwork voiceover, synthesised in your chosen voice, paces the first eight minutes of the ritual. The cadence is **4-7-8** going into sleep — four counts in, hold seven, exhale eight — the pattern Andrew Weil published in 1981 and the one most widely associated with parasympathetic settling. On waking, the cadence shifts to **5.5 in / 5.5 out** — roughly 0.1 Hz, the resonant frequency of the baroreflex, the same cadence FREQUENCY's main orb uses for cardiac coherence breathing.

**Three — Landscape.** This is the layer that did not exist a year ago. World Labs' Marble (1.1, April 2026) renders Gaussian-splat 3D worlds from a text prompt. The open-source Spark renderer (Apache 2.0) displays them on iPhone. DREAMS feeds your intention to Marble, downloads the resulting splat, and lets the camera drift through it on a slow pan. The pan does not cut. The landscape does not perform. You watch it until you don't.

**Four — Recall.** In the morning, the app meets you with a small page. Voice or text capture. An on-device agent built on Apple Foundation Models helps you find the thread — not the interpretation, just the thread. A line, a few sentences, a thumbnail of last night's landscape. The page joins a growing scroll you can revisit.

## What this is not

Not a medical device. Not a treatment for insomnia. Not a cure for anxiety. Not a sleep tracker. Not a dream-interpretation tool. Not a chatbot. Not a subscription. Not an ad-supported library. Not a place where your voice goes to the cloud. The Marble splat downloads once and renders offline; the breath voice is synthesised once and cached; the recall agent runs on the phone.

DREAMS is a wellness practice. The lineage is documented in the in-app lineage card; the disclaimer is everywhere it should be.
