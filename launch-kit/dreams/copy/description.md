## 5. Long description (~1,100 words)

### FREQUENCY DREAMS — a nightly ritual app for the threshold of sleep

FREQUENCY DREAMS is a quiet bedtime ritual that learns the way you go to sleep. It is not a soundscape library. It is not a meditation app. It is not a dream-interpretation journal. It is, instead, a single coherent practice composed for tonight — sound, breath, landscape, and a morning page — built around an intention you speak before you switch the light off.

It sits under FREQUENCY in Jamie Wigg's RHYTHMIX family, inherits the navy-and-gold visual lineage, and goes deeper into the dark. The point is to arrive at sleep gently, with the same attention contemplative traditions have brought to the threshold of night for two and a half millennia — and to wake with the smallest of pages waiting for you, in case the dream is worth keeping.

### Who it's for

People who have tried Calm, tried Headspace, tried Insight Timer — and bounced off the bottomless catalogues. People who have used Endel or Brain.fm and missed the narrative — the *ritual* — that made bedtime feel like a practice rather than a Spotify queue. People with a journal habit who'd like the journal to meet them with a quiet thread to pull on, rather than an open page. People who like a single price and a single piece of software they own forever, not a renewing subscription that fines them for taking a week off.

### The four-layer ritual

This is the whole product. Every night, four interlocking layers, composed for tonight's intention.

**One — Sound.** A solfeggio-tuned soundscape generated by the existing RHYTHMIX FREQUENCY engine, conditioned on the intention you speak. The engine is invisible. You hear a room of frequencies that fits the night. Roughly 25–40 minutes of bespoke audio per session, with the option to fall asleep before it ends.

**Two — Breath.** A guided breathwork voiceover synthesised in your chosen voice, tempo-matched to the soundscape. The default going-into-sleep cadence is 4-7-8 — inhale four counts, hold seven, exhale eight — widely associated with parasympathetic settling. For mornings, the cadence shifts to 5.5 / 5.5 cardiac coherence breathing, which sits at the resonant frequency of the baroreflex and is the same cadence FREQUENCY's main orb already uses.

**Three — Landscape.** This is the new piece. World Labs' Marble renders Gaussian-splat 3D worlds from text. The open-source Spark renderer displays them on iPhone. DREAMS feeds your intention into Marble, downloads the splat, and lets you drift through it — a slow camera pan over the dream landscape you described, until your eyes close. The camera does not cut. The landscape does not perform. You watch it until you don't.

**Four — Recall.** In the morning, when you wake, the app has a quiet page ready. Voice or text capture, on-device only, helped by an Apple Foundation Models agent that finds the thread without imposing interpretation. No Jungian guesses. No mood-graphs. A single line, a few sentences, a small thumbnail of last night's landscape — that's the page.

### Why nobody else has built this

The four layers exist separately. Calm and Headspace have soundscape libraries, but no generative layer, no dream landscape, no recall agent. Endel and Brain.fm have generative soundscapes, but no narrative, no landscape, no journal. Dreamy and Dream Weaver have dream-interpretation journals, but no audio, no breath, no landscape. Nobody puts all four under a single ritual.

The bottleneck has been the landscape layer. Marble 1.1 shipped in April 2026; Spark made Gaussian-splat scenes viewable on iPhone the same quarter. Without those, DREAMS would have been a slideshow. With them, it's the first time a generated landscape can drift *with* you instead of *at* you.

### Lineage

Bedtime as practice is older than electricity. Across contemplative traditions — the Upanishadic AUM at the threshold of sleep, the Christian compline office, the Sufi *khalwa* — the moment before night is treated as the day's last and most attentive practice. The Hatha Yoga Pradīpikā lists Bhramari among nightly disciplines. The contemporary scaffolding is Andrew Weil's 4-7-8 (1981), Paul Lehrer's cardiac-coherence work at ≈0.1 Hz (1995), and a small library of subsequent papers on slow nasal exhalation and parasympathetic settling.

DREAMS leans on the observation rather than any single causal claim. This is a wellness practice. It does not diagnose or treat insomnia, anxiety, or any other condition. If you have a sleep disorder, see your clinician.

### What's not in the app

There is no AI persona. There is no chatbot. There is no leaderboard, streak shame, or push notification at 9 PM. There is no recording leaving the device — Marble runs offline once the splat is downloaded; the breath voice is synthesised once and cached; the recall agent runs on Apple Foundation Models, on-device. There is no second tier. There is no subscription. There is no ad.

### What's in the app

Tonight's intention. Tonight's soundscape. Tonight's breath voice. Tonight's landscape. Tomorrow morning's page. A growing scroll of past pages and the thumbnails of their landscapes, which you can revisit when you want to. A small lineage card with the disclaimer and the sources behind the practice.

### Pricing

**AU$30, one payment, lifetime access.** No subscription. No renewal. No usage tier. Available at `rhythmixapp.com.au/dreams`. The web app loads on iPhone Safari and modern desktop browsers. There is no app-store download.

### Voice

The same voice as FREQUENCY: substantive, opinionated, lineage-aware. Wellness language. Direct sentences. No marketing-speak. The medical disclaimer is everywhere it needs to be.

### Closing

Two and a half millennia of bedtime practice. Forty-five years of breath science. One Marble landscape. One page in the morning. One quiet ritual, kept on your phone.
