## 1. What each platform actually does

**Suno** is the most recognisable name in text-to-music. You type a prompt — "lo-fi hip-hop beat, female vocals, melancholy" — and Suno v5 produces a complete song with vocals and instrumentation in under a minute. It's available on web and iOS, with free, Pro and Premier tiers. The output is a WAV or MP3 file. What happens next is up to you: you download it, master it elsewhere if you care to, and upload it yourself to distributors like DistroKid or TuneCore.

**RHYTHMIX** is a four-pillar AI music platform built around the same generation step, but it doesn't stop there. After the track exists, RHYTHMIX runs it through an AI mastering chain, ships the finished master to 40+ streaming platforms (Spotify, Apple Music, Tidal, YouTube Music, Amazon Music and others), and gives the artist a royalty-split engine, a merch pipeline, fan-investment tools, and an Artist DNA model they keep regardless of subscription state. It's sold as a one-time AU$149 lifetime purchase.

Same starting point. Very different finish line.

## 2. The pillar coverage gap — generation only vs the full stack

This is the single most important difference, and it's what most "RHYTHMIX vs Suno" reviews miss.

Suno owns one pillar of the music workflow: **Generate**. It does it well — vocal coherence and song structure are best-in-class for short-form text-to-music — but the moment you have a finished WAV, Suno is done with you. There is no mastering inside Suno. There is no distribution inside Suno. There is no merch storefront, no royalty-split system, no fan-investment layer, and no path to monetise outside of streaming, which you have to set up yourself.

RHYTHMIX covers four pillars:

- **Generate** — text-to-song with vocals and instrumentation, competitive with Suno-class output.

If you only ever want to make tracks for fun and post them to TikTok, Suno's single pillar is fine. If you want to **release** music — meaning real distribution, real royalty plumbing, real merch, real audience monetisation — Suno is the first step of a five-step journey you'll have to stitch together from four other subscriptions: a distributor, a mastering tool, a merch platform, and a royalty-split service. For comparison-shopping context: of every major AI music tool on the market in 2026, only LANDR covers more than one of the four pillars (Master + Distribute), and LANDR doesn't generate. RHYTHMIX is the only platform that ships all four.

## 4. The honest case for choosing Suno

Suno is the right choice in three specific situations, and we'd rather tell you the truth than pretend it isn't.

**You only want to make songs, not release them.** If your output lives entirely inside TikTok, Reels, or your own headphones, distribution and royalty plumbing don't matter. Suno's generator is excellent and you'd be paying for pillars you'll never touch.

**You want the deepest community and the most prompt examples.** Suno's user base is enormous, and the volume of public prompt recipes, edit-section techniques, and remix tutorials genuinely accelerates a beginner. RHYTHMIX is newer; the community is smaller and more producer-shaped than hobbyist-shaped.

**You're testing the category for under US$30 before committing.** Suno's free tier (~50 credits/day) is more generous than RHYTHMIX's free tier (10 credits/day), and the monthly Pro plan at ~US$10 is a low-risk way to find out whether AI music is for you at all. If you cancel after a month, you're out US$10. RHYTHMIX is AU$149, refunded if you ask within the window, but it's still a bigger up-front decision.

Short version: if you want a generator, buy a generator. Suno is the best one.
