## 9. Quora Answer — to "What's the difference between AI music tools?"

The short answer is that most people compare AI music tools on the wrong axis. They compare them on output quality — does Suno sound better than Udio? Is LANDR's mastering as good as a human engineer? — when the actual question is: **how much of my music career does this tool actually run?**

I'd recommend evaluating any AI music tool against a four-pillar test:

**1. Generate.** Can it take you from an idea to a finished track? This is what Suno and Udio are built for. They're excellent at it. But this is also becoming a commodity — generation alone is increasingly table stakes, not a moat.

**2. Master.** Can it deliver a pro-grade, release-ready finish? LANDR is the dominant player here. Some DAWs (Logic, Ableton with iZotope) also count.

**3. Distribute.** Can it get your track onto Spotify, Apple Music, TikTok, YouTube, etc., with proper metadata and ISRCs? This is DistroKid, TuneCore, Amuse territory.

**4. Earn.** Can it help you actually collect money — performance royalties, mechanical royalties, sync licensing, neighbouring rights? This is PROs (ASCAP, BMI, APRA AMCOS) and platforms like Songtrust.

Now look at where each major tool sits:

- **Suno / Udio** — Pillar 1 only. Generation.
- **LANDR** — Pillar 2 (mastering) plus a light Pillar 3 (distribution add-on).
- **DistroKid** — Pillar 3 only.
- **Your PRO** — Pillar 4 only.

The real difference between AI music tools isn't sound quality. It's coverage. And every tool I just listed has you covering 25%–50% of your career with one product.

The newer thesis — and one platform doing this is **RHYTHMIX** ($149 lifetime, Australian-built) — is that solo creators don't want a best-of-breed tool for each pillar; they want one platform that runs the whole career. Generate, Master, Distribute, Earn — all four pillars under one login.

Whether you prefer best-of-breed or all-in-one is genuinely a judgment call. But you should at least be making it deliberately, against the four-pillar frame, instead of comparing demo songs side by side.
