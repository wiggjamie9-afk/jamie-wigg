# HUM launches lifetime humming practice grounded in vagus-nerve research

### A 90-second daily Bhramari practice for the browser — AU$30 once, no subscription.

HUM, an independent Perth-built wellness practice, opens to founding members this week. The web-based tool guides a single ninety-second humming session per day, paced to 0.1 Hz coherence breathing and supported by on-device pitch listening. Unlike Calm or Headspace, which charge annual subscriptions of roughly AU$90–95, HUM is sold once at AU$30 with lifetime access. No app store, no login, no recurring fee.

The practice draws on Bhramari Pranayama — the "bee breath" described in the Hatha Yoga Pradīpikā and practised continuously for an estimated five thousand years. Modern interest in humming sits in a hundred-year arc of nervous-system science. In 1921, Otto Loewi was awarded the Nobel Prize for demonstrating that the vagus nerve modulates the heart through acetylcholine. In 2002, researchers at Sweden's Karolinska Institute (Weitzberg and Lundberg) measured a fifteen-fold rise in nasal nitric oxide during humming, with the paranasal sinuses behaving as a resonance chamber.

HUM is built around four ninety-second programs — The Quiet (daytime settle), The Clearing (sinus and nasal-NO production), The Descent (pre-sleep wind-down), and The Tone (vocal warm-up). A glowing violet orb paces the breath at the 0.1 Hz coherence rhythm used in heart-rate-variability training. Three reference frequencies (130 Hz, 174 Hz, 220 Hz) are available on demand. A pitch detector listens through the microphone and runs locally in the browser; no audio is uploaded and no account is required.

The lifetime model is deliberate. "I'm tired of the wellness-app trap," said founder Jamie Wigg. "You buy in, you get hooked, the bill arrives every month, and the cost of cancelling is losing your streak. HUM flips that. One price, one purchase. The cheapest, most-evidenced nervous-system intervention available to a person with no equipment shouldn't cost ninety dollars a year."

HUM is a practice instrument, not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat or cure any condition, and is intended to support nervous-system regulation, breath training and vocal practice. The company recommends consultation with a clinician before adopting any new breath practice for those with heart or respiratory conditions, during pregnancy, or while on medication affecting heart rate.

A founding-member tier is open at launch. New programs, frequencies and lineage notes will be added monthly across the first twelve months and unlocked automatically for existing members.
