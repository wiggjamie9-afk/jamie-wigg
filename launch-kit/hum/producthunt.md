## FAQ — prepped replies for the comment thread

### Q1 — "Why lifetime instead of subscription? How do you keep the lights on?"

```
Honest answer: the subscription wellness model is broken. You buy in, the bill arrives forever, and the cost of cancelling is losing your streak. I'd rather charge once at a fair price than rent your nervous system back to you monthly.

The maths works because HUM runs in the browser (no app-store cut, no server-side audio processing — everything happens on-device). Hosting a static PWA for a humming practice costs roughly nothing per user. Twelve months of new content is already written and scheduled. After that, the price goes up for new buyers; founding members stay at AU$30 forever.
```

### Q2 — "How is this different from Calm / Headspace / Wim Hof?"

```
Calm and Headspace are meditation libraries — guided audio, no biofeedback, no humming. Wim Hof is breathwork without the humming or the pitch-listening. None of them combine all four things HUM does:

· On-device pitch listening (the orb pulls to your throat in real time, no audio uploaded)
· 0.1 Hz coherence-breathing pacer (the same rhythm HeartMath uses for clinical HRV training)
· Three research-linked frequencies — 130 / 174 / 220 Hz
· Lineage notes from the Hatha Yoga Pradīpikā through Karolinska 2002

It's the first humming-specific practice tool that ships all of that at sub-$50, browser-native.
```

### Q3 — "Does humming actually do anything, or is this woo?"

```
Fair question, and the literature is genuinely strong here:

· Weitzberg & Lundberg, Karolinska Institute, 2002 — measured a 15-fold increase in nasal nitric oxide during humming vs quiet breathing (Am J Respir Crit Care Med).
· Otto Loewi won the 1921 Nobel for proving vagus-nerve modulation of heart rate via acetylcholine — the mechanism humming engages through slow nasal exhalation.
· Coherence breathing at ~0.1 Hz (six breaths/min) is the rhythm HeartMath uses for clinical HRV biofeedback.

HUM doesn't claim to treat anything — it's a practice instrument. But the underlying physiology is some of the best-documented in nervous-system science. The "woo" is that nobody packaged it before.
```
