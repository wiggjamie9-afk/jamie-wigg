# RHYTHMIX — Competitive Positioning Map (2026-05-20)

> Refreshes the framing in `rhythmix-launch-60s/narration.txt` ("Suno makes the song. Udio makes the song. LANDR finishes the song. Then what?") into a full competitive map for the launch package.
>
> Anchor positioning: **RHYTHMIX is a four-pillar platform — Generate · Master · Distribute · Earn — sold for AU$149 lifetime.** Every named competitor below covers 1–2 pillars at most; none cover all four. That is the moat.

---

## 1. Suno

**Category:** Text-to-song generation (vocals + instrumental).

**Current state (May 2026):** Suno v5 is the consumer-default text-to-music engine. Paid tiers gate commercial rights, stem export, and credit count. Web + iOS. No native distribution; users export and DIY-upload.

**Strengths**
- Best-in-class vocal coherence and song structure for short-form text-to-music.
- Massive community, strong brand recognition with the "I'm not a musician" segment.
- Fast iteration: edit-section / remix / cover features now native.

**Weaknesses**
- Stops at the WAV file. No mastering, no distribution, no merch, no royalty plumbing.
- Subscription forever — Pro is ~US$10/mo, Premier ~US$30/mo. Five-year TCO on Premier is ~US$1,800.
- Commercial rights tied to active subscription on some plans — cancel and you risk your back-catalogue's licensing posture.
- No artist identity layer — every song is a one-off; no "your sound" model you own.

**What RHYTHMIX does differently**
- Generate is *one* of four pillars, not the whole product. After the track exists, RHYTHMIX masters it, ships it to 40+ stores, and gives the artist royalty-split, merch, and an Artist DNA model they keep forever.
- One AU$149 payment vs Suno's ~AU$1,800 five-year TCO at Premier.
- Music ownership is unconditional and decoupled from subscription state.

## 2. Udio

**Category:** Text-to-song generation, leaning higher-fidelity / "producer-tier" output.

**Current state (May 2026):** Positioned as the audiophile alternative to Suno. Strong on genre fidelity (jazz, orchestral, electronic), extend / inpaint workflow, stem download on paid tiers. Sued by RIAA majors in 2024; settled / licensed status still partially unresolved in some territories — adds friction for commercial release.

**Strengths**
- Higher perceived audio quality and instrument realism than Suno on long-form / instrumental work.
- Section regeneration ("inpainting") is mature.
- Producer-friendly stem outputs feed cleanly into DAWs.

**Weaknesses**
- Same single-pillar problem: it's a generator, not a career platform.
- Standard ~US$10/mo, Pro ~US$30/mo. No lifetime option.
- Legal cloud around training data — labels and sync libraries still treat Udio-only tracks as higher-risk for placement.
- No mastering, no distribution, no merch, no royalty rails.

**What RHYTHMIX does differently**
- RHYTHMIX users get clean commercial rights from generation through distribution under one platform — no "is this safe to release?" question because the rights flow is platform-native.
- Section Edit (RHYTHMIX feature #05) gives the inpainting flow Udio users like, *plus* the pipeline behind it.
- After Udio's stem export, the user is back in DIY land. After RHYTHMIX's stem export, they're one click from Spotify.

## 3. LANDR

**Category:** AI mastering — plus a distribution arm (LANDR Distribution), a sample marketplace, and lightweight collab tools.

**Current state (May 2026):** The original AI-mastering brand. Has been broadening for years (samples store, distribution, plugins, collab). Bundle tiers run ~US$13–25/mo.

**Strengths**
- Mastering quality is mature and trusted in indie circles.
- Already offers distribution (LANDR Distribution) — only competitor on this list that touches more than one of RHYTHMIX's four pillars.
- Samples library is sizeable; plugin ecosystem.

**Weaknesses**
- Doesn't generate music — you bring your own track. Off-platform for the entire Generate pillar.
- Distribution is gated behind separate tiers; "all-in" plan creeps to ~US$300/yr.
- No merch, no NFT/royalty-token economy, no fan-investment, no VR/spatial concert layer — the Earn pillar is thin (sync licensing only).
- Heavy upsell architecture across product lines makes the buying experience feel like a bundle, not a platform.

**What RHYTHMIX does differently**
- RHYTHMIX collapses LANDR's three-or-four-product stack (Master + Distribute + Samples + Plugins) into one purchase with Generate stapled on the front and Earn (royalty splits, merch, fan investment, VR concerts) on the back.
- LANDR is the *closest* competitor on coverage and still misses two of four pillars. Everyone else misses three.
- Price: LANDR's annual all-in roughly equals RHYTHMIX's one-time lifetime payment.

## 4. Stable Audio (Stability AI)

**Category:** Text-to-audio / instrumental generation (Stable Audio 2.5 in 2026), positioned at producers and content creators.

**Current state (May 2026):** Strong instrumental + sound-design output, weaker on vocals than Suno/Udio. API-first posture. Subscription + commercial-use tiers.

**Strengths**
- High-quality instrumentals, sound effects, looped stems.
- API access — embeddable in other DAWs / pipelines.
- Open-weights heritage gives credibility with technical producers.

**Weaknesses**
- Vocals lag the category — limits use for song-led release artists.
- No distribution, no mastering pipeline beyond generation, no merch, no fan economy.
- Subscription-only, with commercial rights tier-gated.

**What RHYTHMIX does differently**
- RHYTHMIX users don't need to pick a model by strength — generation is one of four pillars and the platform routes work to the right engine.
- For the same kind of producer who'd use Stable Audio for stems, RHYTHMIX adds Stem Split (feature #01), AI Master (#02), and a release pipeline so the stems actually ship.

## 5. AIVA

**Category:** AI composition for film/game/score work — long-form instrumental, MIDI-friendly, classically-trained model lineage.

**Current state (May 2026):** Mature niche tool. ~€11–33/mo. Owns the "AI for composers / sync placement" lane more than any other player.

**Strengths**
- Score-quality instrumental and orchestral output.
- MIDI / DAW-friendly export — fits inside existing producer workflows.
- Defensible niche in sync, library music, and game audio.

**Weaknesses**
- Single-pillar (Generate, narrow). No vocals, no mastering, no distribution, no merch, no Earn layer beyond their own sync queue.
- Narrow demographic — composers, not the bedroom-producer / first-time-artist segment RHYTHMIX targets.
- Subscription, no lifetime path.

**What RHYTHMIX does differently**
- RHYTHMIX explicitly serves bedroom producers, first-time artists, and solo creators (per narration). AIVA serves working composers. The audience overlap is real but RHYTHMIX wraps the *career* around the track, not just the score.
- MIDI export + DAW integration is in RHYTHMIX Pro (#07-era feature set) — table-stakes, not the product.

## 6. Soundful / Beatoven / Boomy (lightweight royalty-free tier)

**Category:** Lightweight AI music for content creators and businesses — background music, royalty-free, often subscription-bundled with content tools.

**Current state (May 2026):** Crowded mid-tier. Soundful and Beatoven target B2B / content creators; Boomy targets hobbyists with a "publish to Spotify and earn" flow.

**Strengths (collectively)**
- Cheap entry points (free tiers, ~US$5–10/mo).
- Boomy specifically touches Distribution (release to streaming, royalty share) — partial pillar overlap.
- Simple UX.

**Weaknesses**
- Output is recognisably generic — the "Suno problem" without Suno's quality ceiling.
- Boomy's royalty share takes 20–80% of streaming income depending on tier (the opposite of RHYTHMIX's "no royalty cuts" promise on landing pages).
- No mastering depth, no merch, no fan economy, no VR/spatial layer.
- Quality and ownership posture make them a poor fit for artists who want a real career, not a hobby.

**What RHYTHMIX does differently**
- RHYTHMIX takes zero royalty cut on distributed tracks (per `text 3.txt` pricing copy). Boomy takes a slice forever.
- Higher-quality engines (Suno-class output) wrapped in a real platform — not a SoundCloud-style hobbyist toy.
