## Episode 1: Season Premiere — Welcome to Dad's Code

**Description:** Welcome to jamie's Podcast, where fatherhood meets real life. In this premiere, I introduce "Dad's Code"—my personal memoir about becoming a father, navigating ADHD, managing anxiety, and learning that presence matters infinitely more than perfection. Over the next 26 episodes, you'll hear stories from my journey: the joy and chaos of raising kids, the challenges of managing ADHD as a parent, the vulnerability of admitting struggle, and the resilience that comes from showing up, even when you're not sure how. This podcast is for fathers, for parents, for anyone navigating the beautiful mess of family. Welcome.

---

**[INTRO — Warm, conversational tone]**

Hey, I'm Jamie. Welcome to jamie's Podcast. If you're here, you're probably a parent, thinking about becoming one, or you've just stumbled in by accident—either way, I'm glad you're here.

This podcast is about fatherhood. But it's not about being perfect. It's not about having all the answers or knowing what you're doing. It's about the messy, chaotic, beautiful reality of raising kids while figuring yourself out.

**[BODY]**

A few years ago, I sat down to write something. Not for anyone else—just for me. I called it "Dad's Code." It wasn't about rules I'd mastered or wisdom I'd earned. It was about everything I was learning the hard way. Fatherhood, ADHD, anxiety, resilience, the pressure to be perfect, and the radical discovery that presence matters infinitely more than performance.

I became a father expecting one thing. What I got was completely different. I expected to know what I was doing. I didn't. I expected parenting to be hard but manageable. It's been harder and messier than I could have imagined. And somewhere in that mess, I found something real.

I have twins. That alone teaches you that control is an illusion. You can't prepare for everything. You can't prevent every problem. You can't be perfect. You can't even be close to perfect. But you can show up. You can be present. You can fail, admit it, and try again.

**[THE JOURNEY]**

Over the next 26 episodes, I'm going to share that journey with you. I'll talk about the moment I became a dad and how it changed everything. I'll talk about navigating ADHD as a parent, managing anxiety, and learning that struggle isn't failure—it's just part of the story. I'll share what I've learned about discipline, joy, resilience, vulnerability, and why sometimes the best parenting looks like dancing in the chaos instead of controlling it.

I'm going to tell you about the hard moments. The times I yelled when I should have listened. The times I was so focused on being the "perfect dad" that I missed my kids entirely. The times I wanted to run away. And the times that broke me open and made me real.

**[THE REAL THING]**

Here's what I know: parenting breaks you and rebuilds you. It shows you who you really are. It reveals your patterns, your fears, your capacity for love you didn't know you had. If you're willing to look honestly at it, fatherhood is the best teacher you'll ever have.

This podcast is for fathers who are figuring it out. For parents who are exhausted. For anyone who feels like they're failing because they're not perfect. You're not failing. You're parenting. And that's the whole thing.

**[CLOSING]**

So over the next 26 episodes, I'm going to take you with me. We're going to talk about real stuff. I'm going to be honest. Some episodes will make you laugh. Some might make you uncomfortable. That's the point. Parenting is uncomfortable. Growth is uncomfortable. And the kids watching us navigate it are learning more from how we handle that discomfort than from anything we say.

Thanks for being here. Let's do this.

## Episode 2: Becoming Dad — The Moment Everything Changed

**Description:** The first time I held my daughter, my world shifted permanently. In this episode, I share the moment I became a dad—the excitement, the fear, the overwhelming responsibility, and the love that changed everything. Becoming a father isn't something you prepare for; it's something you experience and learn from. I explore what that first moment taught me about what matters, what doesn't, and why the pressure to be the "perfect dad" was actually the biggest obstacle to becoming a good one.

---

**[OPENING]**

I remember the exact moment I became a dad. Not the moment my daughter was born—though that happened. Not the moment they put her in my arms—though that mattered. I remember the moment everything shifted.

It was a few days after she came home. She was sleeping. My partner was sleeping. The house was quiet. And I was sitting there at 3 AM, completely terrified, thinking: This is real. This is actually real. I'm responsible for another human being.

**[THE BEFORE]**

Before she was born, I had an idea of what fatherhood would be like. I was going to be present. Involved. I was going to do it better than my own father did. I was going to be the dad I needed as a kid. All the things you tell yourself when you're preparing for something you can't actually prepare for.

I read the books. I took the classes. I had the intention. And then she was here, and none of the books mattered because she was screaming at 2 AM and I didn't know if that was normal screaming or emergency screaming. I didn't know if she was eating enough. I didn't know anything.

**[THE REALITY]**

What nobody tells you about becoming a parent is how little you know. How unprepared you actually are, no matter how prepared you think you are. How every decision feels like it could be the one that breaks her. How you're simultaneously bored out of your mind and terrified something is wrong.

The first few weeks are a blur of feeding, changing, trying to sleep while simultaneously listening for her to stop breathing. Your brain doesn't work normally. Your body doesn't sleep normally. You're functioning on fumes and adrenaline and a love so fierce it actually scares you.

**[WHAT I LEARNED]**

But in that terror, I learned something that changed how I approached everything after: the pressure to be the perfect dad was actually getting in the way of being present. I was so focused on doing everything right that I wasn't actually with her. I was in my head about whether I was doing it right instead of just... being her dad.

The moment that shifted everything was when I stopped trying to be perfect and just tried to be there. Not perfectly there. Not well-organized or totally competent. Just there. Present. Available. Human.

**[THE BIGGER PICTURE]**

When you become a parent, you're not just getting a kid. You're getting a mirror. They show you exactly who you are—your patience, your triggers, your capacity for love, your capacity for frustration. They show you what you're made of.

I learned I was made of more than I thought. More resilience. More love. More willingness to be humbled. More capacity to admit I don't know what I'm doing.

**[CLOSING]**

Becoming a dad broke me open. It made me realize that being perfect was never the point. Being present was. Showing up imperfectly was infinitely more powerful than trying to show up perfectly.

If you're about to become a parent, or you just became one, know this: you don't have to know what you're doing. Nobody does. You just have to show up and love them. That's actually enough.

## Episode 3: Twins and Chaos — Doubling Down

**Description:** Finding out we were having twins completely flipped my world upside down. In this episode, I talk about the moment we discovered twins were coming, the panic that followed, and the beautiful chaos that ensued. Parenting became nappies, bottles, sleepless nights, and unforgettable memories—multiplied by two. I share what I learned about resilience, support, and the fact that perfect parenting isn't possible, but present parenting absolutely is. Twins taught me more about my own limits than anything else ever has.

---

**[OPENING]**

We were sitting in an ultrasound room when the technician said those words: "Well, you've got two." And I remember my partner looking at me, and I remember my stomach just... dropping.

We'd been expecting one. We were prepared for one. We had a crib, a plan, a general idea of how this was going to work. And then suddenly, it was going to be twice as hard, twice as chaotic, and twice as everything.

**[THE INITIAL PANIC]**

I want to be honest about this: my first reaction wasn't joy. It was panic. Pure, undiluted panic. How are we going to afford this? How are we going to sleep? How am I going to manage ADHD and anxiety while parenting two newborns? What if something goes wrong? What if I'm not enough?

That panic lasted a few weeks. I couldn't sleep even when I had the chance. I was catastrophizing in my head about all the ways this could go wrong. This is what anxiety does—it takes a situation and finds every possible disaster embedded in it.

**[THE TWINS ARRIVE]**

When they finally came—and let me tell you, having twins is like going from a regular car ride to a roller coaster with no warning—everything I thought I knew about being a parent became irrelevant.

Two babies. Two different sleep schedules. Two different personalities, as it turned out. One would sleep at night and scream during the day. The other was the opposite. Just when I got the first one settled, the other would wake up. It was chaos. Pure, beautiful, exhausting chaos.

**[WHAT CHAOS TEACHES YOU]**

Here's what having twins taught me, and this applies to parenting in general: you can't control it. You can plan, prepare, and organize as much as you want, and then chaos will show up anyway. And you have a choice: you can fight it and be miserable, or you can surrender to it and find the rhythm underneath.

I learned to surrender. Not in a passive way—I was actively doing everything I could. But I surrendered the idea that I could control the outcome. I couldn't control when they slept. I couldn't control when they cried. I couldn't control whether I was doing it right.

What I could control was whether I was present for it. Whether I was patient or frustrated. Whether I let the chaos break me or whether I found joy somewhere in the mess.

**[THE HIDDEN GIFTS]**

Chaos, it turns out, is a teacher. It shows you what you're actually made of. When you're running on no sleep, covered in things you don't want to identify, and two humans are depending on you, you learn things about yourself. You learn your actual limits. You learn what you can let go of. You learn that perfect parenting is impossible, and that's okay.

Twins taught me resilience not as an abstract concept but as a daily practice. It taught me that presence is more important than performance. That good enough actually is good enough when it's real and present.

**[CLOSING]**

I won't lie to you and say the chaos was always magical. Sometimes it was just chaos. But looking back, that chaos was the making of me as a father. It burned away everything that wasn't essential. It showed me what love looks like when it's unconditional, when it's not about doing it right—it's just about showing up.

If you're in the chaos right now—whether it's twins or just the general beautiful mess of parenting—you're not failing. You're exactly where you need to be. This is the initiation. This is where the real stuff happens.
