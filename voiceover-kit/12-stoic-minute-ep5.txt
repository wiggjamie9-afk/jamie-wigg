Welcome to Stoic Minute. I'm Harry.

Today, a sentence from Epictetus that has the bluntness of a slap: It is not what happens to you, but how you react to it that matters.

He should know. He was born into slavery, his leg crippled by a master. If anyone had the right to say his life was determined by what was done to him, it was Epictetus. And yet he spent his years teaching the exact opposite. The event, he said, is one thing. The judgement you wrap around the event is another. And the suffering almost always lives in the judgement.

Consider how quickly we fuse the two. A friend doesn't reply to a message. That's the event. And then, in less than a second, comes the story. They're annoyed with me. I said the wrong thing. I'm being ignored. The silence was neutral. The story is what hurts. And the story is yours.

Epictetus asks you to slow that fusion down. To put a small gap between the thing that happened and the meaning you assign to it. In that gap is your freedom. The traffic, the rude remark, the bad news, none of it carries a built-in verdict. You supply the verdict. And if you supply it, you can question it.

So today, when something stings, try this. Say to yourself, plainly, that is the event. Now, what am I adding? Name the raw fact, then name the story on top of it. You will often find the story is a guess wearing the costume of a fact.

Remembering that you will die is, strangely, one of the most life-giving things you can do. It returns you to the one day you're actually holding: this one.
