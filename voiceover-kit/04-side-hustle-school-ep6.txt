# PODCAST #04: SIDE HUSTLE SCHOOL — Episode 6
**Voice to use:** Adam or Brian (Upbeat male coach)
**Episode 1 voice used:** Wade

---

Welcome back to Side Hustle School. This is Adam. Yesterday we put designs on shirts without touching a single box. Today we talk about a side hustle that is almost recession-proof, needs no special degree, and can start paying within a week: becoming a virtual assistant. A V A. You become the person a busy business owner hands their boring, time-eating tasks to, and they pay you by the hour or by the month to take that weight off their shoulders.

Here's why this works so well right now. Every small business owner, every coach, every online seller, every solo entrepreneur is drowning. They are great at the thing they do, and terrible at keeping up with their inbox, their calendar, their data entry, and their follow-ups. They would happily pay someone to handle it. The problem is they don't have time to figure out how. That gap is your opening, and it's a wide one.

Let me show you the path. Step one. Decide what tasks you'll offer. The beauty of virtual assistant work is that it's a menu, and you pick your items. Common ones beginners can do right away: managing an email inbox and replying to routine messages. Scheduling appointments and managing a calendar. Data entry and organizing spreadsheets. Booking travel. Light customer support. Posting and scheduling social media content. Formatting documents. Researching and building simple lists. You do not need all of these. Pick the three you're most comfortable with and lead with those.

Step two. Decide who you serve. You can be a general virtual assistant, but you'll earn more and land clients faster if you pick a type of client. A virtual assistant who understands real estate agents, or e-commerce shops, or busy podcasters, or coaches, is far more attractive than a generic helper, because you already speak their language and know their headaches.

Step three. Find your first client. There are three roads, and I want you on all of them. Road one is freelance marketplaces like Upwork, where business owners are already posting for help. Write specific proposals that name their exact problem, not copy-paste pitches. Road two, and the fastest, is your own network. Send a clear message: hey, I'm taking on a couple of clients as a virtual assistant handling inbox and scheduling. If you or someone you know needs hours back in their week, I'd love an introduction. Road three is small local business owners and online creators you already follow. A short, specific offer to solve one concrete problem can turn into steady work.

Now the numbers, honestly. New virtual assistants commonly charge somewhere between eighteen and thirty-five dollars an hour, depending on the tasks and the client. As you build trust and take on more skilled work, that climbs. Here's the move that changes everything though: aim for retainer clients. Instead of one-off hours, offer a package, say, ten hours a month for a set monthly fee. If you land three clients each paying you four hundred dollars a month for a handful of hours a week, that's twelve hundred dollars a month of predictable income from work you can do from your couch in the evenings. Predictable beats sporadic every single time.

And the honest disclaimer, because I respect you too much to sell you a fantasy. These are projections, not promises. Your first client might come in three days or it might take a month of steady outreach. Some clients are a joy and some are not, and part of this game is learning to fire the ones who drain you. This is real work that pays real money, but it is work, and the early no's are normal. Push through them.

Your assignment this week. Write down the three tasks you'll offer and the one type of client you'll serve. Then send your virtual assistant message to five people in your network, and create one sharp profile on a freelance platform. Three tasks, one client type, five messages, one profile. Do that and you're ahead of almost everyone who only daydreams about this.

Tomorrow, we get into one of my favorite ways to earn from knowledge you already have: online tutoring and teaching, where you get paid to help someone learn the thing you find easy. This has been Side Hustle School. Go offer to take some weight off a busy person's shoulders.
