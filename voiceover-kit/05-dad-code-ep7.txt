# PODCAST #05: DAD CODE — Episode 7
**Voice to use:** Brian or Bill (Warm, casual male)
**Episode 1 voice used:** Michael

---

Hey everyone, Michael Scott here, and welcome back to Dad Code. Today we're going somewhere I've genuinely been dreading, because it's the part of fatherhood that none of our own dads had to deal with. Today we're talking about keeping our kids safe online. The internet. And I want to be honest, this is the topic that keeps me up at night more than any other.

Let me say the scary part out loud, and then I promise we'll find solid ground. The world our kids are growing up in is wired to a device that can connect them, in about two taps, to absolutely anyone and absolutely anything on the planet. Strangers, scams, content that would make your stomach drop, people who are very good at pretending to be a kid. That's the real landscape. And it's no good pretending it isn't, or hoping they'll just never find it. They will. So we have to be smarter than fear.

Here's the trap I fell into first, and I think a lot of dads fall into it. I thought online safety was a software problem. I thought if I just installed the right app, ticked the right boxes, set up the perfect filter, I'd built a fortress and I could relax. And listen, those tools matter, we'll get to them. But a filter is a wall, and kids are basically professional wall climbers. A wall with no relationship behind it is just a challenge to beat. The filter is not the strategy. The relationship is the strategy. The filter just buys you time.

So here's the single most important thing I can tell you today. The goal is not to be the dad your kid hides their screen from. The goal is to be the dad your kid runs to when something online scares them. Those are completely different houses to live in. Because here's the cold truth. Your kid will see something they shouldn't. Someone will message them who shouldn't. It is not a maybe, it is a when. And in that moment, the only thing that matters is one question. Do they come and tell you, or do they hide it because they're scared they'll get in trouble or lose the device.

So the whole game becomes making it safe to tell you. And that means, and this is so hard, that when they come to you with something bad, your first reaction cannot be to explode, or to snatch the phone, or to ban everything. Because if telling you results in punishment and panic, you have just taught them, very efficiently, never to tell you again. The first words out of your mouth have to be, I'm so glad you came and told me. You're not in trouble. Let's sort it out together. Even when inside you are absolutely losing it. Especially then.

Now, the practical part, because the relationship is the foundation but you still need the bricks. A few things.

One. Keep it in the open, especially when they're young. Devices charge overnight in your room, not theirs. Screens get used in shared spaces, not behind a closed bedroom door. You're not spying. You're just keeping the internet a public square in your house, not a private tunnel.

Two. Talk early and talk often, in small, calm doses. Not one giant terrifying lecture, the talk, that just frightens them into silence. Instead, lots of tiny ongoing chats. Did you know some people online aren't who they say they are. What would you do if a stranger asked for a photo. You drip it in, low and steady, so they grow up fluent in it instead of blindsided by it.

Three. Use the tools, but tell them you're using them, and tell them why. Hidden surveillance breeds resentment and a determination to beat it. But, hey, until you're older I'm putting some limits on this, here's what they are, here's why, that's just normal parenting. The why turns a wall into a reason. Kids fight walls. They can live with reasons.

And here's the last thing, the one we forget. The strongest filter your kid will ever have is the voice in their own head. The instinct that says, this feels wrong, I'm going to stop, I'm going to go tell Dad. You cannot install that. You can only build it, slowly, through every calm conversation where they learned that bringing you a problem makes it smaller, not bigger.

Here's what I want to leave you with. You will not be able to control everything they see. Let go of that fantasy, it'll only make you a tyrant or a wreck. What you can control is whether your kid feels safe enough to come to you when the internet does the thing the internet does. Be the soft place they land. Be unshockable. Be the dad they run to. That, in the end, is the only firewall that actually holds.

Next week we're going lighter again, thank goodness. We're talking about building things together. About why making something with your hands alongside your kid might be one of the most quietly powerful things you ever do.

This has been Dad Code. Now go and tell your kid, you can always come to me, no matter what. And mean it.
