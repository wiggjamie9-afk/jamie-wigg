Welcome to fifteen minute kitchen. Today we are doing one-pot cooking, and I want to explain why this is not just about saving on washing up — although that is a real and valid reason. One-pot cooking produces better flavour because everything cooks in the same liquid, which means the chicken flavours the broth, the broth flavours the pasta, the pasta absorbs all of it. You get a richer, more unified dish than you would if you had cooked everything separately. Today's recipe is a one-pot creamy tomato pasta with white beans, and it comes together entirely in a single pot in fifteen minutes flat.

Here is your grab list. Two hundred and fifty grams of any short pasta — rigatoni, penne, orecchiette, whatever you have. One can of crushed tomatoes, four hundred grams. One can of white beans, drained and rinsed — cannellini beans are ideal. Three cloves of garlic, minced. One teaspoon of dried oregano. Half a teaspoon of chili flakes — optional but recommended. Seven hundred and fifty millilitres of vegetable or chicken stock. Two tablespoons of cream cheese or a splash of heavy cream — the one that makes it creamy. A big handful of fresh or frozen spinach. Olive oil. Salt. Parmesan to finish if you have it.

Everything goes into one pot, and here is the exact order. Set a large pot or deep pan over medium-high heat and add a splash of olive oil. Add the garlic and chili flakes and stir for thirty seconds. Then add the crushed tomatoes, the stock, the dried oregano, and a good pinch of salt. Stir to combine and bring it to a boil.

Once it is boiling, add the dry pasta. Stir it in and make sure it is all submerged. Now here is the part that feels strange the first time you do it: you cook the pasta directly in the sauce, not in a separate pot of water. Stir it every two minutes so nothing sticks to the bottom. The pasta absorbs the liquid as it cooks and the starch that releases from the pasta thickens the sauce at the same time. Cook for nine to ten minutes, checking the pasta for doneness — you want it with just a little bite.

When the pasta is almost done, add the white beans and the spinach. Stir them through and let them warm for about a minute. Then take the pot off the heat and add the cream cheese or cream. Stir it in until the sauce turns from orange-red to a rosy, creamy colour. Taste for salt and adjust.

Serve it straight from the pot with grated parmesan over the top.

The thing to watch in one-pot pasta is the liquid level. If the sauce is looking too thick before the pasta is done, add a splash more stock or water and stir it in. If it looks too soupy at the end, just keep it on the heat for another minute and stir — the starch will pull it together. You have complete control.

This is also naturally vegetarian and can easily be made vegan by swapping cream cheese for a cashew cream or just a drizzle of olive oil at the end.

One pot, one meal, more flavour than if you had used four pans. The full recipe with the pasta shape guide and the vegan swap notes are in the show notes.
