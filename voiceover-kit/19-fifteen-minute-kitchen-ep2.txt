Welcome back to Fifteen-Minute Kitchen. Today is the most useful episode you'll hear all week, because I'm not giving you a recipe — I'm giving you a formula. Learn this once and you can stir-fry literally anything in your fridge, forever.

Grab whatever you've got: a protein — chicken, shrimp, tofu, beef, even just eggs. A couple handfuls of veg — anything, frozen is great. Garlic, ginger if you have it. Soy sauce. A neutral oil. And rice or noodles to serve, if you want. No exact amounts today — that's the whole point. Let's learn the formula.

The stir-fry formula is five moves, always the same order. Memorize the order, swap the ingredients forever.

Move One: Prep everything first. This is the secret. A stir-fry cooks in three minutes, so there's no time to chop mid-cook. Cut your protein into bite-size pieces. Chop your veg. Mince a couple cloves of garlic and a thumb of ginger. Line it all up by the stove. From here it's fast.

Move Two: Hot pan, then oil. Get a wide pan or wok screaming hot over high heat — give it a full minute. Then add a tablespoon of oil and swirl. You want it shimmering. A hot pan is what gives you that smoky, restaurant flavor instead of sad, steamed veg.

Move Three: Protein first, and don't touch it. Add your protein in one layer. Let it sit thirty seconds to catch some color, then stir. Cook it about two, three minutes until it's nearly done, then scoop it out onto a plate. Yes, out — we come back for it.

Move Four: Aromatics then veg. Same hot pan, tiny bit more oil, throw in your garlic and ginger — ten seconds, just till fragrant, don't burn it. Then your veg. Harder veg like carrots and broccoli go first, soft stuff like spinach goes last. Toss it around two, three minutes. You want it bright and crisp-tender, never floppy.

Move Five: Reunite and sauce. Protein back in. Now the sauce — and here's your no-recipe sauce: a couple good splashes of soy sauce, and if you've got it, a teaspoon of honey or sugar and a splash of vinegar or lime. Toss everything together thirty seconds till it's glossy and coated. Listen for that loud sizzle — that's the sauce cooking onto the food, not pooling underneath.

Here's the tip that fixes ninety percent of home stir-fries: don't crowd the pan. Pile too much in and it steams instead of fries — you get grey, watery veg. If you're cooking for a crowd, do it in two batches. Thirty extra seconds, completely different dinner.

Spoon it over rice or noodles, or eat it straight from the pan — I won't tell.

That's it. Protein out, veg and aromatics, protein back in, sauce. Five moves, any ingredients, forever. You just stopped needing recipes.

The formula's written out in the show notes as a fill-in-the-blank cheat sheet — print it, stick it on the fridge.
