Welcome to Wanderlust. I'm William. Today we are going to a country that gets under your skin and stays there, sun-warmed and golden and impossibly easy to love. Portugal. The westernmost edge of Europe, where the land runs out of continent and tips gently into the Atlantic. It is small enough to cross in a day and rich enough to wander for a month, and somehow it remains one of the great affordable wonders of the traveling world.

Let me set the scene. You're standing on a hilltop in Lisbon at the end of the afternoon. Below you the city spills downhill in a tumble of terracotta roofs toward a river so wide it looks like the sea. A yellow tram, ancient and rattling, grinds up the cobbled slope behind you, packed with people leaning out the windows. The walls around you are covered in tiles, blue and white and centuries old, and from somewhere below drifts the sound of a fado singer warming up, that aching, soulful music that the Portuguese call saudade, a longing for something you can't quite name. The light here is famous. Painters cross the world for it. It turns every plain wall to honey.

Portugal gives you so much for so little. Let me show you how to make the most of it.

First, do not only do Lisbon. The capital is glorious, but the country fans out beautifully. An hour north, the fairytale palaces of Sintra rise out of a misty forest. Three hours up the coast, the student city of Porto clings to a gorge above the river, its cellars stacked with barrels of port wine aging in the dark. And out east lies the Alentejo, a region of golden plains, cork oaks, and whitewashed villages where time slows to the pace of a single church bell.

Second, take the train, then the regional bus. Portugal is small and the public transport is cheap and reliable. You can base yourself in one city and reach a dozen others on day trips for the price of a sandwich back home.

Third, eat where there is no menu in English and the tablecloth is paper. The food here is simple and astonishing. Grilled sardines in summer, hot from the coals. A bubbling clay pot of pork and clams. Bread, olives, and a glass of green wine, the vinho verde, slightly sparkling and absurdly cheap. And do not leave without a custard tart, the pastel de nata, still warm, dusted with cinnamon, eaten standing at the counter the way the locals do.

Fourth, go to the Algarve in the south for the coast, but go in the shoulder season, in late spring or early autumn. The cliffs are the color of amber, the hidden coves are reachable only by little boats or scrambling paths, and the water is clear and cold and clean. In high summer it heaves with crowds. A few weeks either side, and you can have a golden beach almost to yourself.

Fifth, learn one word. Obrigado if you are a man, obrigada if you are a woman. It means thank you, and it changes everything. The Portuguese are among the warmest, most welcoming people in Europe, and a single word of their language opens doors that money never could.

And here is what Portugal teaches the traveler, quietly, over a week or two. That you do not need grandeur to be moved. That happiness is often a small thing. A tile, a tart, a song, a glass of wine watching the sun go down over the Atlantic. Portugal is not loud. It does not need to be. It simply takes your hand and slows you down until you remember how to enjoy being alive.

So your homework this week. Find a recording of fado, that old Portuguese music, and listen to one song with your eyes closed. Let it carry you to that hilltop, that light, that river. That is where your trip begins.

Travel gently, travel warmly, and remember, the most beautiful place you'll ever discover might just be the simple joy you'd forgotten you were allowed to feel. I'll be here next time. Until then, keep dreaming with your eyes open.
