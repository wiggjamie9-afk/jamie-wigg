Welcome to fifteen minute kitchen. Today I am solving lunch. Not just today's lunch — I am giving you a system that means every workday lunch for the next week takes about three minutes to assemble, tastes genuinely good, and costs you almost nothing.

The secret is the grain bowl hack, and once you get it you will wonder why you ever did lunch any other way.

Here is how it works. Once a week — Sunday works great, or whenever you have twenty idle minutes — you cook one big pot of grains. Rice, quinoa, farro, or even just pasta. Enough for five portions. You also roast one sheet pan of vegetables and cook a protein if you want one. Then each day you assemble a bowl from those base components in literally under five minutes. Different toppings, different sauces, completely different lunch every day from the same building blocks.

Let me walk you through setting it up. Your ingredient grab list for a week of bowls: two cups of rice or quinoa, a couple of sweet potatoes or a head of broccoli or whatever veg you like, a can of chickpeas or a pack of chicken thighs, olive oil, salt, and then whatever sauces you like — soy sauce, tahini, hot sauce, lemon, any of those.

Grains first. Rinse your rice or quinoa, add it to a pot with double the amount of water, a big pinch of salt, bring it to a boil, lid on, turn it to low. Rice takes about eighteen minutes. Quinoa takes twelve. Walk away. When all the water is absorbed, fluff it with a fork and leave it to cool. That pot will last you four to five days in the fridge.

Roasted veg is even easier. Cut your sweet potato into small cubes, or pull your broccoli into florets. Sheet pan, a drizzle of oil, a big pinch of salt. Into a four twenty-five degree oven for twenty to twenty-five minutes. Flip once halfway. Let them cool and put them in a container. Done.

If you want a protein, a can of rinsed chickpeas roasted on the same pan for twenty minutes gets golden and a little crispy. Or cook a batch of chicken thighs in a pan — season them, medium-high heat, four minutes a side, done.

Now the assembly — every day, this takes three minutes. Big spoon of grains into a bowl. A scoop of veg on top. Your protein. Then whatever sauce you feel like that day. Monday it is soy sauce and sesame oil and a squeeze of lime. Tuesday it is tahini thinned with a little lemon juice and water. Wednesday it is just hot sauce and olive oil. Same bowl, totally different lunch each day.

The tip that makes this work: keep your sauces stocked. Tahini, soy sauce, something acidic, something spicy. Four bottles in the door of your fridge means infinite lunch variety. The sauces are doing all the heavy lifting.
