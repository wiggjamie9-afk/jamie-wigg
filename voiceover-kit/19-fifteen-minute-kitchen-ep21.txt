Welcome to fifteen minute kitchen. Today we are talking about cooking for everyone — because most of us are feeding people with different dietary needs at the same table, and it should not mean cooking three separate dinners. I am going to show you a build-your-own approach to dinner that handles meat eaters, vegetarians, dairy-free, and gluten-free people all at once, with almost no extra effort.

The recipe is a Thai-style peanut noodle bowl, and the trick is that the base is naturally plant-based and gluten-friendly, and you just add proteins and toppings on the side so everyone customises their own bowl.

Here is your grab list. Rice noodles or any noodle that works for your crowd — rice noodles are naturally gluten-free, regular egg noodles are not, so pick based on who is eating. For the sauce: peanut butter, soy sauce or tamari if you are going gluten-free, lime juice, a little honey or maple syrup, and chili flakes. For the bowl toppings: shredded cabbage or carrot or cucumber, any raw veg you like, and then whatever proteins you want to offer — cooked chicken, firm tofu, edamame, a soft-boiled egg, or all of the above. Spring onions and sesame seeds if you have them.

Start with the sauce because it comes together in two minutes and it is the whole flavour engine of this bowl. Into a bowl or a jar: three tablespoons of peanut butter, two tablespoons of soy sauce or tamari, two tablespoons of lime juice, one tablespoon of honey or maple syrup, a pinch of chili flakes, and about two tablespoons of warm water to loosen it. Whisk or shake until smooth. Taste it — you want it salty, tangy, a little sweet, a little spicy. Adjust to what feels right to you. More lime for brightness, more honey to balance, more soy for depth. Set it aside.

Noodles next. Boil a kettle and pour the boiling water over your rice noodles in a bowl. Let them soak for about four to five minutes until tender, then drain and rinse with cold water so they stop cooking and do not stick together. If you are using regular noodles, cook them as the packet says.

While the noodles soak, prep your toppings. Shred some cabbage or grate a carrot, slice some cucumber into thin rounds, and get your proteins ready. If you are doing tofu, slice a block of firm tofu into cubes and pan-fry them in a splash of oil over medium-high heat for about three minutes per side until golden and crispy. If you have leftover cooked chicken, just shred it. If you want soft-boiled eggs, drop them into simmering water for six minutes, then peel and halve them.

Now you are building. Into each bowl: a good pile of noodles, a generous pour of the peanut sauce, your shredded veg, and then everyone chooses their own protein. Scatter over spring onions and sesame seeds if you have them. A final squeeze of lime right before eating.

Each person builds their own bowl from the components, and every single dietary need is handled without drama, without separate cooking, without anyone getting a lesser version of the meal.

The technique tip for tonight: when you cook for mixed dietary needs, think about the base separately from the proteins. A great sauce and great veg are the foundation. Proteins are modular. Once you start thinking this way, feeding mixed groups stops being a headache and becomes as easy as tonight.
