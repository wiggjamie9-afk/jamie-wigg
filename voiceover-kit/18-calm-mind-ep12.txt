Last time, I promised that today we'd turn toward something almost all of us find difficult. Not knowing. The discomfort of an open question, a future we can't quite see. So let's sit with uncertainty together, softly, and find a little steadiness inside it.

Here's something true about the anxious mind. It cannot stand an open door. When something in our life is unresolved, will I get the job, will the test come back clear, will this relationship hold, the mind treats that not-knowing as a kind of emergency. And so it does the only thing it knows how to do. It tries to think its way to certainty. It runs through every possible outcome, again and again, as though, if it just worries hard enough, it can somehow force the future to reveal itself. But the future never does. And so the mind just spins, exhausting itself, on a problem that thinking cannot solve.

I want to offer you a gentle reframe today, and it's this. The discomfort you feel isn't actually the uncertainty itself. It's the fight against the uncertainty. It's the part of you that's clenching, demanding an answer that isn't available yet. And here's the quiet, freeing truth. You have survived every uncertain moment of your entire life so far. Every single one. Every not-knowing you've ever faced has, eventually, resolved into something you then lived through. You don't have a perfect record of things working out exactly as you hoped. But you do have a perfect record of getting through. One hundred percent. That's worth remembering.

There's an image I find steadying. Think of life as a path through a forest, at night, with a lantern. The lantern only ever lights the few steps directly in front of you. It does not light the whole forest. And here's the thing, it was never meant to. You don't need to see the whole path to take the next step. You only need enough light for the ground right in front of your feet. And as you walk, the light moves with you. The next stretch becomes visible exactly when you need it, and not a moment before. You have always had enough light for the next step. You just couldn't see that, while you were straining to light up the whole forest at once.

So how do we actually live with an open question, without it eating us alive? Let me offer a few gentle things. The first is to notice when you've slipped into trying to solve the unsolvable. When you catch your mind running the same worried loop for the tenth time, you can say to it, softly, this isn't a problem I can think my way out of right now. The answer simply hasn't arrived. And there's a strange relief in that. You're allowed to put down a problem that has no available solution yet. The second is to gently return to what is actually in front of you. The dishes. The walk. The next small task. Not as a distraction, but as a homecoming. Uncertainty lives in the imagined future. Your life is happening here, in the present, where there's almost always something simple and real to attend to.

And the third, perhaps the deepest, is to make a little room for the not-knowing, instead of bracing against it. You might even try saying, I don't know how this will turn out, and I can be okay anyway. Not, everything will definitely be fine, that's just another guess about the future. Simply, I don't know, and I can hold that. There's a quiet strength in those words. It's the difference between clenching against the open door, and learning, slowly, to stand calmly in the doorway.

So here is a small practice to carry with you. I call it the next right step. When you feel yourself spinning on some big, unanswerable uncertainty, gently set the whole forest down, and ask yourself just one question. What is the next small, kind, right thing I can do, right now? Not the answer to everything. Just the next step, in the light you actually have. Then take it. And let that be enough.

Please be patient with yourself here. Tolerating uncertainty is genuinely hard, and it's a skill that grows slowly, like a muscle. Some open questions are frightening, and it's completely human to wish for solid ground. If the not-knowing ever becomes so heavy that it overwhelms you, or you find yourself unable to function, that's a moment to reach for a trusted person, or a professional who can steady you. These practices can walk beside good support. They were never meant to replace it.

So as we close today, carry just one thing with you. You do not need to see the whole path to walk it. You only need enough light for the next step, and that light, you already have. The future will arrive in its own time, one step at a time, and you will meet it the way you've met everything else. By living it, when it comes.
