Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making soup. And before you click away thinking soup takes all afternoon and a stockpot the size of a bucket, let me stop you, because this is a fifteen minute soup, blended smooth and silky, and it's one of the most comforting things you can make on a tired evening. One pot, a quick whizz, done. Let's go.

Here's the kit, lovely and simple. About four hundred grams of a quick-cooking vegetable, that's the key word, quick. Think frozen peas, a couple of zucchini, a tin of tomatoes, or a bag of frozen cauliflower. One onion, two cloves of garlic, and about four cups of broth, vegetable or chicken. A glug of olive oil or a knob of butter. And to finish, a splash of cream or a spoon of yogurt, and some bread for dunking. That's everything.

Step one, the base. Set your widest pot over medium heat and add a glug of oil or a knob of butter. Roughly chop one onion, you don't need to be neat, it's all getting blended. Tip the onion in with a pinch of salt and let it soften for three or four minutes while you mince your two cloves of garlic. Stir the garlic in for the last thirty seconds, just until it smells wonderful. Don't let it brown, that's the only villain here.

Step two, the veg goes in. Add your four hundred grams of quick veg. If it's frozen, even better, straight from the bag. Give it all a stir to coat it in that lovely oniony oil for a minute. This little step wakes up the flavor before the liquid goes anywhere near it.

Step three, the liquid. Pour in your four cups of hot broth. Using hot broth instead of cold is a sneaky time-saver, it comes back up to the boil in seconds. Bring it to a gentle bubble, pop a lid on, and let it simmer for about six minutes, until the veg is properly soft when you poke it with a spoon. Soft veg blends into silk. Firm veg blends into bits, so give it that full six.

Step four, the blend. Off the heat. Now, if you have a stick blender, this is heaven, just put it straight into the pot and blitz until smooth, tilting the pot to chase every lump. No stick blender? Carefully ladle it into a regular blender, but only fill it halfway and hold the lid down with a folded tea towel, because hot soup likes to jump. Blend until it's velvety. That moment when it goes from chunky to smooth is honestly so satisfying.

Step five, the finish. Back into the pot if you moved it. Now make it luxurious. Stir in a splash of cream or a spoon of yogurt for body. Then taste, and season properly: salt, pepper, and here's my favorite trick, a tiny squeeze of lemon. A little acid at the end lifts the whole thing and makes it taste bright instead of flat. Ladle it into bowls, swirl a little extra cream on top, and crack some black pepper over.

Here's my one tip that saves every soup: season at the end, not the start, and taste as you go. Soup needs more salt than you think to stop tasting watery, so add a pinch, taste, add another pinch, taste. The difference between a dull soup and a great one is almost always just enough salt and that squeeze of lemon.

And that's a proper bowl of soup in fifteen minutes. Smooth, warm, and the kind of thing that makes a hard day feel a little softer. Tear off a chunk of bread, dunk it in, and just sit for a minute.

I've written the veg, broth, and finish ratio in the show notes for you, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
