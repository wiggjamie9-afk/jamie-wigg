Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making fried rice, the ultimate clear-out-the-fridge dinner, the one that turns yesterday's sad leftover rice and a few random veg into something so good you'll wonder why you ever ordered it. Fifteen minutes, one hot pan, and a little bit of theatre. Let's go.

Here's the kit, and the star ingredient is rice you cooked yesterday. About three cups of cold, cooked rice. Cold rice is the whole secret, I'll tell you why in a minute, but if you only have a fresh microwave pouch, spread it on a plate and pop it in the freezer for five minutes while you prep. Then you'll want two eggs, two cloves of garlic, a couple handfuls of quick veg like frozen peas, carrots, or sweetcorn, two spring onions if you have them, and soy sauce. A neutral oil. And any leftover protein you fancy, ham, chicken, prawns, totally optional. That's it.

Step one, prep everything first, because fried rice is fast and furious. Mince your two cloves of garlic. Slice your two spring onions, keeping the white and green parts separate. Beat your two eggs in a cup. Get your cold rice, your veg, and your soy sauce all lined up by the stove. Once we start, there's no stopping to chop.

Step two, scramble the eggs and set them aside. Get a wide pan or wok really hot over high heat, add a splash of oil, then pour in your beaten eggs. Let them scramble for thirty seconds until just set, then scoop them straight out onto a plate. We come back for them at the end, just like a stir-fry. Cooking them separately keeps them fluffy instead of rubbery.

Step three, the aromatics and veg. Same hot pan, a little more oil. Toss in the white parts of your spring onion and your garlic for ten seconds, just until fragrant. Then add your veg and any leftover protein. If the veg is frozen, that's fine, just give it two minutes of tossing to heat through and lose its chill.

Step four, the rice, and here's where the magic happens. Tip in your three cups of cold rice and break up any clumps with your spoon. Now, leave it. Press it into the hot pan and let it sit for about thirty seconds at a time before tossing, so the rice catches the heat and gets a little toasty and separate. This is why we use cold rice: fresh warm rice is sticky and turns to mush, but cold rice has dried out just enough to fry up light and fluffy with those gorgeous toasty edges.

Step five, sauce and reunite. Push the rice to the side, pour two or three tablespoons of soy sauce straight onto the bare hot pan so it sizzles and caramelizes for a second, then toss it all through. Add your scrambled eggs back in, breaking them into pieces. Toss everything together for thirty seconds until it's hot, glossy, and evenly coated. Off the heat, scatter over the green parts of your spring onion. Taste, and add a splash more soy if it needs it.

Here's my one tip that makes restaurant fried rice at home: get the pan really, really hot and don't crowd it. That fierce heat is what gives you the smoky flavor and those toasty grains instead of a soft, steamed pile. If you're cooking a big batch, do it in two goes. Thirty extra seconds, completely different dinner.

And that's it. Smoky, fluffy fried rice in fifteen minutes, built from leftovers you were about to throw away. That's the whole joy of it, turning the forgotten corners of your fridge into the best thing you'll eat all week.

The cold-rice trick and the order of moves are in the show notes, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
