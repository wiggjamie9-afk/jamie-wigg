Welcome to Wanderlust. I'm William. Today, no destination at all, but a skill, a quiet superpower that will change every single trip you take for the rest of your life. I want to teach you to pack light. To carry everything you need for a week, or a month, or a year, in a single bag you never have to check, never have to wait for, never have to drag. Close your eyes for a moment and picture two travellers arriving at the same airport. The first hauls two enormous suitcases off the carousel after a forty minute wait, then wrestles them through crowds and up stairs and into a taxi, sweating, exhausted, before the trip has even begun. The second walks straight off the plane, past the carousel without slowing, one small bag on her back, and is in a café sipping a coffee while the first traveller is still standing at the belt, watching other people's luggage go round. I want to make you the second traveller. It is one of the great freedoms of travel, and almost anyone can learn it.

Let me begin with the why, because the why is the whole point. Packing light is not about deprivation. It is about freedom. When you carry only a single bag, you can change plans on a whim, hop on the local bus, walk the last mile to your guesthouse through cobbled streets where no suitcase wheel would ever survive. You never pay a checked bag fee. You never lose your luggage, because it never leaves your side. You move through the world like water, quick and light and unburdened. The heaviest thing most people carry when they travel is not their suitcase. It is the fear of not having enough. So let us deal with that fear directly.

Here is the first and greatest principle. You will wear far less than you think, and you can wash clothes anywhere on earth. Almost every traveller packs for the trip they imagine, full of different outfits for different occasions, and then wears the same three comfortable things over and over, because that is what humans actually do. So pack for those three things. The secret professionals use is the capsule wardrobe. Choose a single colour palette, so that everything you bring matches everything else. Then every top works with every bottom, and a handful of pieces becomes a dozen outfits.

Now let me give you the actual method, the rules that turn a heaving suitcase into a light, joyful bag.

Number one. Lay out everything you think you need on the bed. Then remove half of it, and put it away. I am entirely serious. Whatever you think you need, you need about half. The half you remove will not be missed.

Number two. The rule of layers, not bulk. Instead of one heavy coat, pack thin layers you can combine. A light merino top, a thin fleece, a compact rain shell. Together they handle cold, wet, and wind, and they crush down to nothing. Wool is the traveller's secret fibre, because it is warm, it dries fast, and remarkably, it resists smell for days, so you can wear it again and again between washes.

Number three. Wear your bulkiest items on the plane. Your heaviest shoes, your thickest jacket. They weigh nothing on your body, but they would eat half your bag. Put them on at the airport.

Number four. Pack a small bar of laundry soap, or just use a little shampoo, and learn to wash a few things in the sink at night. Wring them in a towel, hang them up, and they are dry by morning. This single habit is what lets you travel for a month with a week's worth of clothes.

Number five. Toiletries. Buy them when you arrive. You do not need to carry full bottles of shampoo across an ocean when every corner shop on earth sells it. Bring only the small, the essential, and the things you genuinely cannot replace.

Number six. The one bag rule, made real. Choose a bag of around forty litres, the largest most airlines will let you carry on. Not a wheeled case, but a backpack, so your hands stay free and stairs and cobblestones mean nothing to you. When it is full, that is your limit. The bag itself becomes the discipline.

And here is the deeper truth underneath all of this, the thing I most want you to hear. Packing light is a rehearsal for living light. Almost everyone who learns to travel with one small bag comes home and looks at all their stuff a little differently, and realises how much of it they never really needed at all. You go away to see the world, and you come back having quietly learned something about how little it takes to be happy.

So your homework this week. The next trip you take, even a short one, even a weekend, challenge yourself to use only a carry-on. Lay it all out, remove half, and go. The first time feels like a risk. By the end of the trip, it feels like flying.
