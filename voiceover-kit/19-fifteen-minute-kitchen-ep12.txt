Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making shakshuka, eggs poached in a rich, spicy tomato sauce, and honestly it might be the most impressive thing you can put on a table for almost no effort and almost no money. Breakfast, lunch, dinner, it doesn't care what time it is, it's always a good idea. One pan, lots of crusty bread for mopping. Fifteen minutes. Let's go.

Here's your kit. You'll want one tin of chopped tomatoes, that's about four hundred grams. Four eggs. One small onion, three cloves of garlic, and one red pepper if you have one, but it's optional. For spice: one teaspoon of cumin, one teaspoon of smoked paprika, and a pinch of chili flakes. A glug of olive oil, salt and pepper, and a handful of fresh parsley or coriander to finish. And the most important side, crusty bread or warm pita for dipping. That's it. Let's cook.

Step one, get the base going. Set a wide frying pan, ideally one with a lid, over medium heat with a glug of olive oil. While it warms, finely chop your one small onion and your red pepper, and mince your three cloves of garlic. Tip the onion and pepper into the pan and cook for about three minutes, stirring now and then, until they soften and start to smell sweet.

Step two, bloom the spices. Add your garlic and cook for thirty seconds until fragrant. Now add your one teaspoon of cumin, one teaspoon of smoked paprika, and a pinch of chili flakes, and stir them into the hot oil for another thirty seconds. This is the step people skip, but cooking spices in the oil for a moment wakes them up completely, it's the difference between deep, warm flavor and a flat, dusty taste.

Step three, build the sauce. Pour in your tin of chopped tomatoes, that's four hundred grams, along with a good pinch of salt and a crack of pepper. Stir it all together, then let it bubble away over medium heat for about four minutes, stirring now and then, until it thickens up and isn't watery anymore. You want it jammy enough that a spoon dragged through it leaves a little trail. Taste it now and adjust the salt, a properly seasoned sauce is everything here.

Step four, in go the eggs. Turn the heat down to low. Use the back of your spoon to make four little wells in the sauce, spaced out around the pan. Crack one egg straight into each well. Season the eggs with a tiny pinch of salt and pepper. Now cover the pan with a lid, or a plate or some foil if you don't have one, and let them gently poach for three to four minutes. Peek at them, you want the whites just set and the yolks still soft and runny, because that golden yolk running into the spicy sauce is the whole point.

Step five, finish and serve. The second the whites are set, take it off the heat, the eggs keep cooking from the pan's heat so don't overshoot. Scatter over a big handful of chopped parsley or coriander and a final drizzle of olive oil. Bring the whole pan to the table and let everyone dig in with torn bread, scooping up egg and sauce together.

Here's my one tip that makes or breaks it: get the sauce thick before the eggs go in. If the tomato base is still thin and watery, the eggs slide around and overcook while you wait for the sauce to catch up. Let it bubble down to a thick, jammy stew first, then nestle the eggs in. A thick base cradles each egg and poaches it perfectly.

And that's dinner. A bubbling pan of spicy red sauce with soft, golden eggs peeking out, and a pile of bread for mopping every last bit. It's rustic, it's generous, it's the kind of food that makes people lean in and go quiet for a minute. Not bad for one tin of tomatoes and a few eggs.

The spice mix and the thick-sauce rule are in the show notes, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
