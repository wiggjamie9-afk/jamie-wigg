Welcome to fifteen minute kitchen. Today's episode is about getting your whole week under control in one go. Meal prep — and before you say it, no, this is not a two-hour Sunday project with forty matching containers and color-coded labels. This is a thirty-minute Sunday session that makes every weeknight dinner take about ten minutes instead of forty-five. Let me show you how.

The idea is simple. Instead of starting from scratch every night, you do all the slow parts on Sunday: the chopping, the cooking of grains, the roasting of veg, and one batch of a sauce or marinade. Then on weeknights all you are doing is assembling and finishing, which is fast.

Here is a practical example. Sunday you are going to do four things, all of which can overlap because ovens and stoves are doing the work, not you.

First: a pot of rice or grains. Two cups of rice, four cups of water, a pinch of salt, lid on, twenty minutes on low. Walk away.

Second: a sheet pan of roasted vegetables. Whatever you have — broccoli, sweet potato, zucchini, cherry tomatoes, capsicum. Chop them into even pieces, toss with olive oil and salt, spread onto a sheet pan, into the oven at four twenty-five for twenty-five minutes. While the rice cooks.

Third: cook a protein. This could be a whole pack of chicken thighs in a pan — season them with salt and paprika, medium-high heat, four to five minutes per side until cooked through. Or boil some eggs. Or drain and roast two cans of chickpeas on a second sheet pan alongside your veg. Whatever protein works for your week.

Fourth: make a sauce. This takes four minutes and changes everything. Whisk together three tablespoons of tahini, two tablespoons of lemon juice, one clove of minced garlic, a pinch of salt, and enough water to make it pourable. That sauce goes on everything — over rice bowls, drizzled on roasted veg, on chicken, on eggs. One sauce, infinite uses.

Now everything goes into containers in the fridge, labeled if you like, and your week looks like this. Monday night: rice plus roasted veg plus chicken plus tahini sauce. Ten minutes. Tuesday: same veg tossed through pasta with olive oil and parmesan. Twelve minutes. Wednesday: chicken chopped over salad greens with the sauce as a dressing. Five minutes. Thursday: fried rice using leftover rice plus an egg plus soy sauce. Eight minutes. You cooked once and ate five times.

The tip that makes this actually happen: do not aim for perfect. You are not trying to plan every single meal with precision. You are just trying to reduce decisions. If the rice is there, dinner is halfway done. That is the whole game.

One more thing — prep ingredients, not meals. If you roast veg and cook grains and a protein, you have flexible building blocks. If you cook complete meals, you get bored of them by day three. Ingredients stay interesting. Meals get repetitive.
