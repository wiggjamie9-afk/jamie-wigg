Last time, I promised that today we'd talk about worry. The way the mind runs ahead into the future, into all the things that might go wrong. So let's meet that worried mind together, gently and without judgment.

Here's the first thing I want you to understand about worry. Worry is not stupidity, and it isn't weakness. Worry is your mind trying to protect you. It runs ahead into the future and imagines the dangers, because somewhere deep down it believes that if it can just picture every bad thing in advance, it can keep you safe from being caught off guard. Worry is a watchman who never sleeps. It means well. It's just exhausting, and it's often wrong.

Because here's what worry promises, and never quite delivers. It promises that if you think about the problem hard enough, long enough, you'll feel ready. But have you noticed? The worrying rarely makes you feel ready. It just makes you feel afraid, over and over, for things that, most of the time, never even happen. The watchman keeps sounding the alarm for storms that never arrive.

So today, I want to give you a few gentle ways to work with a worried mind. Not to silence it, because you can't really silence it, but to loosen its grip, and to meet it with something steadier.

The first is a simple question, and it's a powerful one. When a worry arrives, pause and ask, is this a problem I can solve, or a fear I'm rehearsing? This one question changes everything. Because some worries point to a real thing you can act on. If the worry is, I haven't replied to that important message, then the answer isn't more worrying. The answer is to go and reply, or to write it on your list for tomorrow. Action is the natural medicine for a solvable problem.

But so many of our worries are not solvable problems at all. They're fears we're simply rehearsing. What if something goes wrong next year. What if that person is upset with me. What if, what if, what if. These aren't tasks to complete. They're storms we're imagining. And you cannot solve a storm that hasn't come, by replaying it again and again in your mind. For these, the work is not to solve, but to gently set down.

So here's a way to do that. When you catch yourself rehearsing a fear, try naming it, kindly. You might say to yourself, oh, I'm worrying again. This is my mind trying to keep me safe. Thank you, mind. I see you. Just that small acknowledgment can create a little space between you and the worry. Suddenly the worry isn't all of reality. It's a thought, passing through, the way weather passes through a sky.

And here's another gentle reframe I love. Try changing the question your mind is asking. Worry loves to ask, what if everything goes wrong? So, very gently, offer it a different question. What if this works out? Or even simpler, what is actually true right now, in this moment? Because right now, in this moment, you are most likely safe. The catastrophe lives in the imagined future. The present moment is usually far kinder than the worried mind insists.

There's one more thing I want to offer you, for the worries that come at night, when everything feels larger in the dark. You might try setting them down on paper before bed. Write the worry out, and beside it, write one of two things. Either the small next step you'll take tomorrow, or the simple words, this is not mine to solve tonight. Then close the notebook. You're not ignoring the worry. You're telling it, gently, I've heard you, and I'll tend to you in the daylight. Right now, it's time to rest.

So let me give you a small practice to carry with you. I call it solve it or soothe it. When a worry arrives, pause and ask, can I act on this, or am I rehearsing a fear? If you can act, take one small step. If you're rehearsing, name it kindly, breathe, and gently set it down. Solve it, or soothe it. Not every worry deserves your obedience.

Be patient with yourself as you practice this. The worried mind has a long habit, and old habits speak loudly at first. You won't stop worrying overnight, and that's all right. The goal was never to never worry again. The goal is simply to stop believing every worry the moment it speaks.

So as we close today, carry just one thing with you. Your worried mind is only ever trying to protect you, in the only way it knows. You don't have to fight it. You just get to gently remind it, again and again, that you are safe right now, in this present moment, where you actually live.
