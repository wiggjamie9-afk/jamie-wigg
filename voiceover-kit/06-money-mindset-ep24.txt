Most people have a vague idea that they want to retire someday. But very few people have sat down and asked the question with any real precision: at what age do I actually want financial independence? Not retirement in the traditional sense — not the gold watch and the beach chair. But the point where work becomes optional. Where you have enough.

That question forces a different kind of thinking. Because the answer changes everything.

If your goal is to reach financial independence by forty-five, you are running a very different financial game than someone who is planning for sixty-five. The forty-five plan requires a higher savings rate, earlier investing, lower lifestyle costs, and probably some version of generating income that does not trade time for money in the conventional way. The sixty-five plan allows more room to breathe, more spending in your forties, more conventional career progression — but it also means trading more of your active, healthy years for a salary.

Neither is wrong. But you cannot optimise for both at the same time, and most people never make a conscious choice between them. They default to the sixty-five plan simply because that is the one society laid out for them — the career, the mortgage, the retirement fund — without ever questioning whether there is another version available.

The concept of a FIRE number — Financial Independence, Retire Early — has become popular for a reason. It gives people a concrete target. Most versions of the formula suggest that if you accumulate twenty-five times your annual expenses in invested assets, you can safely withdraw four percent per year indefinitely and never run out of money. The math is not perfect and the assumptions have limits, but the framework forces a useful conversation.

What do you actually spend? What does your life cost? And what are you willing to change in order to have more of your life back sooner?

Financial independence at any age is not about having infinite money. It is about having enough — and knowing, clearly and precisely, what enough means for you.

That clarity alone is worth more than most people realise.
