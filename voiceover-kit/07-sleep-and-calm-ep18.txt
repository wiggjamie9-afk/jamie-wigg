Welcome to Sleep and Calm. Find a comfortable position and let your body settle.

Tonight we are going to breathe together. Because breathing — simple, slow, deliberate breathing — is one of the most powerful tools you have for quieting the nervous system and preparing the body for deep, restful sleep.

When we are stressed, our breath tends to be shallow and fast. It stays high in the chest. This sends a signal to the brain that something is wrong — that we need to be alert, ready, awake. The nervous system responds accordingly, keeping you in a state of low-level arousal even when you desperately want to rest.

But the good news is that this works in reverse too. By consciously slowing and deepening the breath, we send a different signal. We tell the nervous system that all is well. That the danger has passed. That it is safe to soften.

Let us try three techniques, starting with the simplest.

The first is called extended exhale breathing. Breathe in slowly through your nose for a count of four. Then breathe out through your mouth for a count of six or even eight. The exhale is longer than the inhale. This activates the parasympathetic nervous system — the rest and digest response — more than any other single technique.

Try it now. Breathe in... two... three... four. And out... two... three... four... five... six.

Again. In... two... three... four. And out... two... three... four... five... six.

Let the exhale be effortless. Not forced. Just a slow, gentle release, as if you are breathing out through a straw.

The second technique is called box breathing. Four counts in, four counts hold, four counts out, four counts hold. In... two... three... four. Hold... two... three... four. Out... two... three... four. Hold... two... three... four.

This technique is used by surgeons, by athletes, by people who need to find calm in high-pressure moments. And it works beautifully as a bridge between wakefulness and sleep.

The third technique needs no counting at all. Simply breathe into your belly. Place one hand on your chest and one on your stomach. As you inhale, feel the belly rise first — like a balloon slowly filling. As you exhale, feel it fall. The chest should stay relatively still.

Most of us breathe backwards — leading with the chest. Belly breathing requires a little practice, but once you find it, it is extraordinarily calming. It is how babies breathe. It is how we breathe when we are deeply asleep.

So let your belly lead now. Let each breath be full and low and slow.

There is nothing you need to do. Nowhere you need to go. Just this breath. And the next. And the one after that.

Your body already knows how to do this. You are simply remembering.

Sleep well.
