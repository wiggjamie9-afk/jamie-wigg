Welcome to Calm Mind. Wherever you are right now, let yourself arrive here fully.

Today we are going to sit with something many of us would rather avoid. Grief.

It does not matter whether you are grieving a person, a relationship, a version of your life you thought you would have, or a part of yourself you have lost along the way. Grief is grief. And far too often, we are taught that it is something to get through quickly, to manage, to move on from. We are rarely taught that grief, when we allow it, is one of the most honest and loving things we can do.

So let us begin gently.

Take a breath in. Long and slow. And breathe out, completely.

Grief lives in the body. You may feel it in your chest, like a heaviness that will not shift. Or in your throat, something tight and waiting. Or in the behind-the-eyes pressure that arrives without warning, in the middle of ordinary moments.

These sensations are not signs that something is wrong with you. They are signs that something mattered to you deeply. That is not weakness. That is the cost of having loved.

If you are willing, bring to mind the thing you are carrying. Hold it gently, the way you might hold something precious and fragile. You do not need to fix it, explain it, or make it smaller than it is. You only need to acknowledge it.

I am here with this. This is real. And it is allowed.

Grief moves in waves, not in stages. It does not follow a schedule or respect your calendar. It rises when it rises. And the more we try to push it down, the longer it tends to stay just beneath the surface, waiting.

What grief often needs is not resolution. It needs room. A little space inside you where it does not have to pretend to be something else.

You might find that sometimes the wave comes and you can be with it. Other times you cannot, and that is also fine. There is no right way to grieve.

If you are in the middle of a loss right now, please know that seeking support from a counsellor or grief group is not a sign of falling apart. It is a sign of wisdom. Grief shared is always lighter than grief carried alone.

Take one more breath. In slowly. And out.

Whatever you are holding today, you are allowed to feel it. You are allowed to take your time. And you are allowed to still find moments of warmth and even joy, without that meaning you have forgotten or moved on. Grief and love can live in the same heart at the same time.

You are not broken. You are human.
