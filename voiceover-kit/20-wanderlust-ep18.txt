Welcome to Wanderlust. I'm William. Today I want to take you to a continent that I think is the most misunderstood destination on earth. Today we're talking about Africa. Not the Africa of wildlife documentaries, though that Africa is real and staggering. Not the Africa of crisis headlines, though those are real too. I want to talk about the Africa that most people who visit never expected to find. The cities. The culture. The food. The music. The design. The sheer energy of a continent of one point four billion people, the youngest population on the planet, building things with a ferocity and an originality that is unlike anywhere else.

Let me start with Morocco. I know. You know Morocco. But most people who visit Marrakech never get deep enough into the medina to find the neighbourhood where the coppersmiths work, hammers ringing, creating the lanterns and teapots and decorative metalwork that will end up in shops all over the world. They don't visit the tanneries of Fes, where leather has been dyed in the same open stone vats using the same natural pigments for eight hundred years. Stand on the balcony overlooking those vats — the owner of the rooftop shop will hand you a sprig of mint to hold under your nose against the smell — and look down at a scene that genuinely has not changed since the medieval era. That is a thing that exists on earth. In Fes. Right now.

Let me take you to Ethiopia. The highlands of Ethiopia are one of the great and least-visited landscapes on earth. Lalibela, a small town in the north, contains a complex of eleven medieval churches hewn directly downward into solid red volcanic rock. They are not ruins. They are not monuments. They are active, living churches where priests in colourful robes conduct daily services that have been continuous since the twelfth century. You descend into them. The ground is above your head. The walls are carved from the mountain itself. It is perhaps the most extraordinary architectural thing I have ever stood inside.

Addis Ababa, the capital, has one of the most remarkable museum collections in the world. The National Museum of Ethiopia contains the bones of Lucy, the three-point-two-million-year-old early human ancestor discovered in the Afar region in nineteen seventy-four. You can stand in front of the oldest known member of our species. That alone is worth the flight.

Ghana. The first sub-Saharan African country to achieve independence from colonial rule, in nineteen fifty-seven. Accra is a modern, energetic, coastal capital with a music scene rooted in highlife, afrobeats, and hip hop. The city's food scene centres around jollof rice, kelewele which is fried spiced plantain, grilled tilapia, and street food culture that runs from morning to midnight. The Makola Market is one of West Africa's great urban markets, a place where fabric merchants, spice sellers, electronics traders, and food vendors operate in a density of commerce that makes every European market feel calm by comparison.

Head north from Accra to Cape Coast. The castle there, a white-walled Dutch and British fort on the Atlantic shore, was one of the major holding points for enslaved people during the transatlantic slave trade. Walking its dungeons, where thousands of people waited in conditions of extraordinary cruelty before the crossing, is one of the most sobering and important experiences I have had anywhere on earth. History is not always comfortable. But this is history that everyone should understand.

Rwanda is a country that has rebuilt itself with a speed that is genuinely astonishing. Less than thirty years from one of the worst genocides in recorded history to a nation with some of the cleanest streets in Africa, the fastest-growing economy in the region, and the largest proportion of women in its national parliament of any country on earth. The mountain gorillas of Volcanoes National Park require a permit that costs over a thousand dollars for a single hour's trekking visit. It sounds like an extraordinary luxury. The people who have done it describe it, almost without exception, as one of the most profound experiences of their lives.

Africa is a continent of fifty-four countries, over three thousand ethnic groups, and over two thousand languages. There is no single story. There is no single experience. The best thing you can do before visiting any country on the continent is to read a book written by someone from that country. Fiction, memoir, history, it doesn't matter. Let a voice from inside the place be the first thing that shapes your understanding of it. You will arrive differently.

Your homework: If Africa is on your list but always gets pushed back, pick one country. Look up the entry requirements, the rough cost of a flight, and one book written by a local author. Make it real. It is closer, and more extraordinary, than you think.
