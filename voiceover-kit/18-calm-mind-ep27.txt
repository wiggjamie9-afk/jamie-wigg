Welcome to Calm Mind. Find a place where you can be still for a few minutes. Let your hands rest open in your lap, palms facing upward.

Today we are going to practise loving-kindness meditation. It has a long tradition in contemplative practice, and a growing body of research behind it. Studies show it can reduce self-criticism, increase compassion for others, and gently shift the way we relate to ourselves and the people in our lives. It is simple. It does not require belief in anything. Only a willingness to try.

The practice involves silently offering phrases of goodwill. First to yourself. Then to someone you love easily. Then to someone neutral. And eventually to someone difficult. Then to all beings.

We will start gently.

Take a breath in. And out.

Begin with yourself. Place one hand on your heart if that feels natural. And silently, or very softly, repeat after me.

May I be well.

May I be at peace.

May I be free from suffering.

May I be held with kindness.

Let those words settle in, even if they feel unfamiliar. Even if part of you resists. You do not need to believe them fully right now. Just offer them, the way you might offer a glass of water to someone who is thirsty — without needing to know if they will accept it.

Again.

May I be well.

May I be at peace.

May I be free from suffering.

May I be held with kindness.

Now bring to mind someone you love easily. A person, a child, an animal — anyone whose face brings a natural warmth. Let them be present in your mind, and offer the same phrases to them.

May you be well.

May you be at peace.

May you be free from suffering.

May you be held with kindness.

Feel the difference in that. Notice how naturally it comes. This is your own capacity for goodwill. It already exists. You are not manufacturing it — you are simply directing it.

Now think of someone neutral. Someone you see but do not know well. A neighbour, a shopkeeper, a stranger whose face you remember. Offer them the same phrases.

May you be well.

May you be at peace.

May you be free from suffering.

May you be held with kindness.

If you are ready, and only if you are ready, bring to mind someone with whom things are difficult. This does not mean someone who has deeply hurt you. Start with mild difficulty. And offer the phrases, not because they deserve it, not because it excuses anything, but because holding ongoing resentment costs you most of all.

May you be well.

May you be at peace.

May you be free from suffering.

May you be held with kindness.

And finally, let the phrases expand outward to include everyone. People in your building, your street, your city. People you will never meet. People who are struggling in ways you cannot imagine. All beings, everywhere.

May all beings be well.

May all beings be at peace.

May all beings be free from suffering.

May all beings be held with kindness.

Rest here for a moment. Notice what is present. You may feel warmth, or openness. You may feel nothing in particular. Both are fine. The practice works even when it does not feel remarkable.

Take one deep breath in. And out, completely.

Bring this quality of open warmth back with you into your day. Not as a performance of niceness. Simply as a quiet orientation toward the world. Wishing well, even when it is hard.

That is enough. That is, in fact, quite a lot.
