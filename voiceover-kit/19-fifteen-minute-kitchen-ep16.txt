Welcome to fifteen minute kitchen. Today we are starting the week right with three fast breakfast ideas that are actually going to get you out the door on time, full, and not running on just coffee and regret.

First, your ingredient check for all three options. Grab whatever speaks to you this morning: eggs, bread, a ripe banana, Greek yogurt, frozen berries, rolled oats, and some honey or peanut butter. You do not need all of these — pick your breakfast, grab those ingredients, and let us cook.

Option one is the two-minute breakfast wrap, and it is exactly what it sounds like. Crack two eggs into a bowl, beat them with a pinch of salt. Heat a non-stick pan over medium-high for one minute, add a small splash of oil, pour in your eggs. Do not touch them for thirty seconds. Then tilt the pan and let the uncooked egg run to the edges. When the surface looks mostly set but still a little glossy, lay a tortilla or a piece of bread right on top. Wait fifteen seconds, then flip the whole thing — tortilla side down, egg side up — so the egg finishes while the bread warms. Slide it onto your plate, add whatever you like inside — hot sauce, cheese, some leftover roasted veg from last night — and fold it over. Done in under two minutes, and it actually keeps you full until lunch.

Option two is the five-minute yogurt bowl, which requires zero cooking at all. Spoon a big cup of Greek yogurt into a bowl. Scatter over a handful of frozen berries — they will thaw in about three minutes while you get dressed, and they release a little juice that makes the yogurt look and taste amazing. Drizzle honey over the top, add a handful of granola or a spoon of peanut butter, and you are done. High protein, genuinely satisfying, basically no effort.

Option three is overnight oats, which you make tonight so tomorrow morning you just open the fridge and eat. Into a jar or a container with a lid: half a cup of rolled oats, half a cup of milk, a big spoonful of yogurt, a drizzle of honey, and a pinch of salt. Stir it all together, lid on, into the fridge. In the morning add a banana or berries on top. That is it. The oats absorb everything overnight and you wake up to something creamy, thick, and already done.

The big tip for mornings: the night before, decide what you are having and put the ingredients at the front of the fridge. That ten seconds of planning saves five minutes of confused staring into the fridge at seven a m when your brain is not yet online.

Breakfasts should not feel like a project. They should feel like a small win before the day even starts.
