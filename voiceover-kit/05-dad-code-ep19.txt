Sport and the Dad on the Sideline. This episode is going to step on some toes and I am fine with that because someone needs to say it.

Some of us are terrible on the sideline.

I include myself in that. Early on, when my kids started playing sport, I was one of those dads. Calling out instructions. Wincing at every mistake. Cheering so loud at the good moments that my kid would glance over with this look that said please calm down. I was coaching from forty metres away and nobody asked me to.

Here is what happened when I dialled it back. My kids started enjoying sport more. That is it. That is the whole finding. When I was just the dad who was happy to be there, who clapped regardless of the outcome and did not debrief the whole game on the drive home, they played with more freedom and they had more fun.

Sport for kids should be about movement, friendships, learning to compete, learning to lose, learning to win without being obnoxious about it. It should not be about your ego or your unfulfilled sporting ambitions or your need to have produced a prodigy.

The drive home is its own topic. I have a rule now. I let my kid speak first. If they want to talk about the game, great. If they want to talk about something completely unrelated, great. If they want silence, they can have it. What I do not do is launch into a performance review. They just played. They know what happened. They do not need me to replay it.

Now, sport does teach real things and those things are worth having real conversations about when the moment is right. Persistence. Dealing with a bad referee call. Coming back from being behind. Playing well with people you find difficult. These are life skills in sporting clothing and they are gold.

But those conversations land when they come from curiosity, not criticism. What was hard about today? What did you figure out in the second half? Is there anything you want to work on? Those questions open things up. That question of why did you miss that shot at the end closes things down fast.

My favourite coaching I have ever done was when my daughter asked me to kick the ball around the backyard with her after a tough game. No instructions. No feedback. Just two people in the backyard, passing a ball, talking about nothing in particular.

That was the best training session we ever had.
