Hey everyone, Michael Scott here, and welcome back to Dad Code. This is the last one in this run, and I've been saving it. Today we're not talking about a single problem, screens or money or discipline. Today we're zooming all the way out. Today we're talking about the long game. The kind of man you're becoming through all of this. And the quiet truth that your kid is learning how to be a person mostly by watching how you are one.

Let me start here. There's a thing nobody tells you when you become a dad. You think the job is to shape this small person. To pour your wisdom into them, to fix them, to mold them. And some of that's real. But the thing that actually shapes them, more than any speech, more than any rule, is something you have far less control over. It's who you are when you think they're not watching. Because they're always watching. Out of the corner of their eye. Absorbing the whole thing. Not your words. You.

Here's the line that reorganized my whole approach to this. Your kids will not become what you tell them to be. They will become what you are. Say it again. They don't become what you tell them. A kid raised by a flawless robot of a dad learns that mistakes are unacceptable and grows up terrified of their own. What your kid actually needs to watch is not a man who never falls down. So the long game, the real one, isn't about producing a flawless child. It's about you, slowly, deliberately, becoming the man you'd want them to grow into. If you want a kid who can handle their feelings, let them see you handle yours, out loud, like a person, not a stone. You're not just raising them.
