Welcome to Wanderlust. I'm William. Today we are sailing north, far north, to a place that feels less like a country and more like a living myth. Iceland. A small island marooned in the cold North Atlantic, balanced on the seam where two great plates of the earth are slowly pulling apart, so that the land itself is young and raw and still being born. If you have ever wanted to walk across another planet without leaving this one, set your compass here.

Let me put you in it. You are driving a single ribbon of road across a plain of black volcanic gravel, and there is not a tree in sight, not a building, not another car. To your left, a glacier the size of a city pours down between two mountains, blue-white and ancient. To your right, steam rises in lazy columns straight out of the bare ground, because here the earth is thin and the fire beneath it leaks up into the daylight. Ahead, a waterfall thunders off a cliff in a curtain of white, and a rainbow stands in its mist. And over all of it, an enormous, restless sky. Iceland does not have scenery. It has spectacle, in every direction, all the time.

Now let me make this wild place feel reachable, because it genuinely is.

First, the famous loop. A single paved road, the Ring Road, runs all the way around the island, and most of the country's wonders are strung along it like beads. You can drive the whole circle in about a week to ten days, sleeping in a different small town each night, with a new glacier or waterfall or black sand beach every hour. It is one of the great road trips of the world, and it requires no special skill, just patience and a careful eye on the weather.

Second, the weather is the boss, and you must respect it. Iceland can give you sun, gale, sleet, and calm all in one afternoon, in any season. Check the road and weather forecasts every single morning, and if the warning says do not drive, then do not drive. The land is forgiving of slow travelers and harsh with reckless ones. Build extra days into your plan so a storm can pass without ruining your trip.

Third, choose your season for what you want to see. Go in summer, around June and July, and the sun barely sets at all. You can hike at midnight in golden light, and every road and trail is open. Go in winter instead, and the days are short and icy, but the dark skies give you the prize so many travelers cross the world for. The northern lights, the aurora, green and silver, rippling overhead like a curtain in a wind you cannot feel. Both Icelands are magnificent. They are simply different.

Fourth, bathe in the warm water. This island sits on so much underground heat that hot springs bubble up everywhere, and the Icelanders have built their whole social life around them. Skip the one crowded, expensive lagoon everyone photographs, and seek out the small public geothermal pools in ordinary towns instead. For the price of a coffee you can lower yourself into steaming water while snow falls on your face, surrounded by locals chatting about their day. It is the warmest welcome the country offers.

Fifth, and this matters, tread lightly. The landscape here is fragile and slow to heal. Moss that takes decades to grow can be killed by a single footprint off the path. Stay on the marked trails, take everything you brought back out with you, and leave each place exactly as wild as you found it. Iceland is generous, but it is asking us, gently, to be worthy of it.

And here is what this strange and beautiful island gives the traveler. Perspective. It is impossible to stand beneath a glacier that has been there for ten thousand years, or watch the earth steam at your feet, and still feel that your small worries are the center of the universe. Iceland reminds you, in the most beautiful way, how old the world is and how briefly we get to walk on it. You come home humbled, and somehow lighter for it.

So your homework this week. Look up a single video of the northern lights, watched in silence, and let yourself imagine standing under that sky with your breath fogging the cold air. That ribbon of green is calling. All you have to do is answer.

Travel gently, travel wonderingly, and remember, the most beautiful place you'll ever discover might just be the perspective you find standing small beneath an ancient sky. I'll be here next time. Until then, keep dreaming with your eyes open.
