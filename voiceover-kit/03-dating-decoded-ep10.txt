Welcome back to Dating Decoded, the show where we take the messy, confusing world of modern love and look at what's actually going on underneath. I'm your host. Today we're talking about something almost everyone with a dating app on their phone has felt, even if they've never said it out loud: burnout. That heavy, flat, slightly numb feeling of swiping through faces and feeling absolutely nothing.

If you've ever opened an app, scrolled for ten minutes, and closed it feeling worse than when you started, this one's for you. Because dating-app fatigue is real, it's common, and it doesn't mean there's something wrong with you. It means you're human, doing something humans were never quite designed to do.

Let's start with why it happens, because once you understand the mechanics, it stops feeling like a personal failure. The apps are built to be used, not to be finished. There's always another profile. Always another swipe. And our brains treat that endless stream a little like a slot machine. Every match is a tiny hit of maybe. Most of the time the maybe goes nowhere, but the maybe keeps you pulling the lever. Over weeks and months, that low-grade hoping and being let down, hoping and being let down, quietly wears you down.

Then there's the sheer volume of people. Meeting one new person is exciting. Evaluating forty in an afternoon is exhausting. Your brain starts to treat human beings like products on a shelf, and that's not a flaw in you, it's just what happens when you give a person infinite options and three seconds to decide. The romance drains out of it. People become a blur of photos and bios, and you stop seeing anyone clearly, including yourself.

And here's the part that really stings. When you're burned out, you show up worse. You're more cynical, quicker to dismiss, slower to reply, less open. Which means the version of you that's swiping at eleven at night, tired and flat, is not the version of you that actually connects with people. You're fishing with a frayed net and wondering why you're not catching anything.

So let's talk about what actually helps, because the answer is not just try harder or swipe more.

First, take a real break. Not a guilty one, a deliberate one. Delete the apps for two weeks. Not forever, just two weeks. The world will not end. Your future person is not going to vanish in fourteen days. What happens instead is that you reset. You stop measuring your worth in matches. You remember that you are a whole person with a life, and that life is not a waiting room for romance.

Second, when you come back, go narrow, not wide. Burnout thrives on volume. So slow down. Pick one or two people who genuinely interest you and actually invest in those conversations, instead of keeping fifteen lukewarm chats limping along. Quality attention beats quantity every single time, both for them and for you.

Third, move things off the app faster. The longest, most draining part of online dating is the endless texting limbo, the chat that never becomes a plan. If someone seems interesting, suggest meeting within a week or so. A short, easy coffee will tell you more in twenty minutes than three weeks of messages ever could. The app is a doorway, not a destination. Walk through it.

Fourth, protect your sense of self. Your value is not a number of likes. A quiet week is not a verdict on whether you're loveable. The apps can make you feel like you're being constantly judged and graded, and if you let that feeling run your self-worth, it will hollow you out. You are not an item being assessed. You're a person looking for another person, and that's allowed to take time.

And finally, remember that the apps are a tool, not the whole game. Some of the best relationships still begin in ordinary life, through friends, through hobbies, through pure chance. If swiping has stopped feeling like hope and started feeling like a chore, that's your signal to live your actual life a little louder. The right kind of tired is the tired that comes from doing things you love, not from scrolling through strangers.

So if you're burned out today, hear this clearly. You are not broken, you are not behind, and you are not running out of time. You're just a person who got tired of a tiring thing. Rest. Reset. Come back gentler. The goal was never to win the app. The goal was always to find one good person, and that has never depended on how fast you swipe.

That's it for today's Dating Decoded. Next time, we're stepping away from the screen entirely to talk about meeting people the old-fashioned way, in real life, without an app in sight. Until then, be honest, be kind, and text them back. You'll be okay.
