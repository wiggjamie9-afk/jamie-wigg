# PODCAST #07: SLEEP & CALM — Episode 3
**Voice to use:** Sarah (soft, slow, soothing)
**Episode 1 voice used:** Sarah

---

Welcome back to Sleep and Calm. Tonight, you don't have to do anything at all. You don't have to try. You only have to listen, and let the words carry you, the way a train carries a sleeping traveler through the dark.

So settle in. Let your body grow heavy against the bed. Feel the weight of you sinking down, soft and slow. And when you're ready, let your eyes close. Tonight's story is called The Night Train.

You are stepping onto a warm sleeper train, late at night. The platform behind you is quiet and empty, lit by one soft lamp. And the train is waiting for you, glowing gently from within, golden and warm. You step up, and the door closes softly behind you, and the cold night is left outside.

Inside, the corridor is dim and golden. The light is low, the color of honey, the color of candlelight. You walk slowly down the narrow hall, your hand trailing along the smooth wooden wall, and you find your little room. Your cabin. It is small, and snug, and it is entirely yours.

There is a bunk made up just for you. Soft sheets, a thick warm blanket, a pillow that seems to have been waiting all day for your head. You climb in. You lie down. And you pull the warm blanket up, all the way up, so that only your face feels the cool air of the room.

And then, very gently, the train begins to move.

You feel it first as the softest motion. A slow, easy rocking. Side to side, side to side, like a cradle, like being rocked by someone who loves you. The train pulls away from the station, and the gentle rocking settles into a rhythm, slow and steady and sure.

And under the rocking, there is the sound of the rails. A soft, distant rhythm. A quiet clicking, far below you, steady as a heartbeat. The wheels on the rails, again and again, the same gentle sound, over and over. You don't have to listen closely. Just let it be there, underneath everything, carrying you along.

Outside your window, the countryside is dark and peaceful. You can just see it, beyond the glass, lit faintly by the moon. Wide, sleeping fields, silver and grey. Low hills, soft against the sky. Everything out there is asleep, and the train moves quietly through it, careful not to wake a thing.

And it begins to rain. Softly. So softly. You hear it first as a whisper on the roof, and then little drops gather on the window beside you, sliding down the glass, slow and shining in the golden light. Rain on the window. The warm bunk beneath you. The cool air on your face. And the gentle rocking, side to side, side to side.

You let your head sink a little deeper into the pillow. You let your shoulders soften. There is a small lamp above your bunk, glowing low and golden, and its light is so soft it barely reaches the corners of the room. The shadows are gentle here. Everything is gentle here. The warm wood, the soft blanket, the quiet hum of the train as it carries you on through the dark.

You are so warm here. You are so safe here. There is nowhere you need to be. The train knows the way. All through the night, it will carry you, while you do nothing at all but rest.

The rain falls softly on the glass. The rails click quietly below. And the train rocks you, gently, side to side.

Far away, across the dark fields, a village goes by. Tiny houses, all asleep. One small light in a window, golden and far, and then it is gone, behind you now, fading into the dark. The fields return, wide and silver and still. And the train carries you on, slow and steady, deeper into the peaceful night.

You don't have to watch the window. You can let your eyes rest. The countryside will keep drifting by whether you watch it or not, soft and silver and sleeping, and the train will keep rocking you, slow and steady, side to side. There is nothing you need to do. There is nowhere you need to be. The night is long, and gentle, and it belongs to you.

A bridge passes underneath, somewhere far below, and the sound of the rails changes for a moment, a little deeper, a little softer, and then it returns to its quiet rhythm. The same gentle clicking. The same far heartbeat. Over and over, carrying you on.

You feel your body growing heavier. Your arms are heavy. Your legs are heavy. Your shoulders sink down into the soft bunk, and the warm blanket holds you close. There is nothing to hold onto. Nothing to think about. Only the rocking. Only the rain. Only the soft, far sound of the rails.

Side to side. Side to side.

The rain on the window. The warmth of the blanket. The gentle, endless rhythm of the train.

Another village drifts by, far away in the dark, its little lights soft and golden, and then gone. The fields go on and on, silver under the moon, sleeping. And the train moves through them, slow, and quiet, and sure.

You are drifting now. Drifting like the train through the dark. Your breathing has grown slow and deep, all on its own. In, and out. Slow, and soft. With the rhythm of the rails. With the falling of the rain.

Side to side. Side to side.

There is nothing to do. Nothing at all. The night is long and gentle, and the train will carry you the whole way through. You can let go now. You can sink down, all the way down, into the warm and the dark and the soft.

The rain is quieter now. Softer. Almost gone. Just a whisper on the glass.

The rails are quieter now. Farther away. A faint, slow heartbeat, somewhere far below.

And the train rocks you, gently, gently, side to side.

So warm. So safe. So far away from everything.

Side to side.

Slower now.

Side to side.

The golden light grows dim. The window darkens. The countryside drifts by, silver and sleeping, and you are sleeping too, almost, nearly, drifting down into the warm.

There is nowhere to go. The train knows the way.

Just rest now. Just rest.

The rain. The rails. The gentle rocking, slow, and soft, and far.

Side to side.

Side.

To side.

Sleep now. The train has you. It will carry you all the way through the night.

Rest.
