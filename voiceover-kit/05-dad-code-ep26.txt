What You Are Actually Leaving Behind. Welcome to Episode twenty six.

I want to ask you something, and I want you to sit with it before answering.

When your kids are grown, and they are sitting around a dinner table with their own families, talking about you the way you probably talk about your dad sometimes — what do you want them to say?

Not what you want them to say about your career. Not your income or your title or the postcode you managed to move into. What do you want them to say about you?

I have thought about this a lot. And what I keep coming back to is that the things I most want to pass on are not things. They are not possessions or property or a financial position. They are ways of being. Orientations toward life. The values that made me feel, even on a hard day, that I had something solid to stand on.

Legacy is a word that gets used a lot in business and almost never in parenting, which is strange because the real legacy most of us will leave is not a company or a brand. It is how our kids turned out. How they treat people. What they reach for when things go wrong. Whether they are kind under pressure. Whether they know how to work hard and also stop working. Whether they can say sorry and mean it.

All of that was taught. Maybe not in formal lessons. But it was modelled, absorbed, internalized. It came from watching you.

That is the thing about raising kids. You are always broadcasting, even when you think you are off duty. They are studying you constantly. Not your words — your behaviour. Not your advice — your choices. The way you talk about people who are not in the room. The way you handle a setback. Whether you show up when it would be easier not to.

My dad taught me the value of showing up by showing up. He came to things. He was there. He did not always know what to say, but he was physically present at the events that mattered, and I understood that to mean that I mattered. He never said it in those words. He did not need to.

I want to pass on a few specific things. I want my kids to know that hard work is worth something but it is not everything. I want them to know that asking for help is a sign of intelligence, not weakness. I want them to know how to sit with someone who is suffering and not try to fix it, just stay. I want them to know that they are enough, exactly as they are, without needing to be impressive.

And I want them to remember that their dad laughed easily and meant what he said and did not take himself too seriously, even when things were genuinely difficult.

The way you build that kind of legacy is not in one big speech. It is in ten thousand small moments. The patience you show when you are tired. The interest you take when you would rather be somewhere else. The consistency of your presence over years, not weeks.

Here is the question I would leave you with. Not just today but to keep. When was the last time you said, out loud, to your kids, what you value and why? Not as a lecture. Just as a conversation. Just as an honest share.

Because the values you never name out loud are the ones they might have to guess at. And guessing leaves too much room for getting it wrong.

Tell them what matters. Show them who you are trying to be. Give them something they can carry.

That is the whole job, really.
