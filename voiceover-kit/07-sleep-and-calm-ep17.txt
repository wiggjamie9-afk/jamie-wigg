Welcome to Sleep and Calm. Take a slow breath in, and let it go.

Tonight we are exploring anxiety at bedtime — that particular kind of restlessness that arrives just as you lie down. When the day is over and the distractions are gone and your mind, suddenly very loud, begins to work through every unresolved thing.

This is more common than you might think. You are not alone in this.

When we are busy, anxiety often runs quietly in the background — masked by tasks, by noise, by movement. But the moment we stop, the moment we are still, it rises to the surface. The worry that was waiting. The thought that was circling. The question that has no answer yet.

The mistake many people make is to fight it. To tell themselves they should not be thinking this, they should be sleeping, they are wasting time. But fighting anxiety in bed only feeds it. Resistance creates tension, and tension is the opposite of sleep.

So instead, try something different tonight. Try allowing.

When a worried thought arrives, rather than pushing it away, simply notice it. Name it, gently. I am thinking about work. I am worried about tomorrow. I am replaying that conversation. Just naming what is happening creates a small but significant distance between you and the thought. You are no longer inside it. You are watching it, the way you might watch a cloud pass across a dark sky.

Clouds move. Thoughts move too.

There is a practice I want to share with you — something simple you can try right now. It is called grounding, and it uses your senses to anchor you in the present moment, away from the anxious future or the troubled past.

Start with five things you can notice right now. The weight of the blanket on your body. The temperature of the pillow against your cheek. A distant sound — rain, traffic, silence itself. The feeling of your breath moving in and out. The darkness behind your closed eyes.

These things are real. These things are now. And right now, in this moment, you are safe.

Keep coming back to the senses whenever the mind begins to drift toward worry. Not forcing anything. Just gently returning. Like guiding a child back to bed who has wandered into the hallway.

With patience. With gentleness. Without frustration.

Anxiety cannot maintain its grip when the body is grounded. When you are fully here, in your own warmth and quiet, there is simply no foothold for it.

Let the thoughts come. Let them pass. Return to your breath.

You are here. You are safe. Tonight, that is enough.

Sleep well.
