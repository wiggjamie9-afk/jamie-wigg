Welcome to Calm Mind. Find a comfortable place to settle.

Let your eyes close if that feels right. Let your hands rest open in your lap, or by your sides. And just breathe.

Today we are going to do something a little different. We are going to meditate together — a simple, guided practice that you can return to anytime you need to find your way back to stillness.

Begin by taking three intentional breaths. Breathe in through your nose, slowly and fully. Hold for just a moment at the top. Then breathe out through your mouth, completely and unhurried.

Again. In through the nose. A gentle pause. Out through the mouth.

And one more time. In. And out.

Now let your breathing return to its natural rhythm. You do not need to control it anymore. Simply observe it.

Notice the breath entering your body. Feel the slight coolness at the tip of your nose, or the back of your throat. Notice how the chest or belly rises with each inhale. Feel the gentle release of each exhale.

If your mind wanders — and it will, because that is what minds do — that is not a problem. When you notice you have drifted into thought, simply and gently return your attention to the breath. No frustration. No judgment. Just return.

This returning is the practice. Each time you come back, you are training the mind. You are building the muscle of awareness.

Now let us expand our attention slightly. Notice the sounds around you. Near sounds. Far sounds. The hum of air, the texture of silence. You do not need to identify or analyse them. Simply let them be part of the background. You are the still witness, and the sounds arise and pass without touching you.

Now bring your awareness to your body. Starting from the top of your head — feel the scalp, the forehead, the space behind your eyes. Let any tension there soften.

Move down to the jaw. This is a place so many of us hold stress without realising it. See if you can let it relax. Teeth slightly apart. Tongue soft in the mouth.

Down to the neck and shoulders. Let them fall away from your ears. You do not need to hold them up.

Down through the chest, the belly. With each exhale, feel yourself sinking slightly deeper into ease.

Down through the lower back, the hips, the legs. All the way to the soles of the feet, which are resting on the earth.

You are held. You are supported. You do not have to grip or brace or prepare.

Rest here for a moment. Just breathing. Just being.

If thoughts arise, let them pass like clouds across an open sky. They come, and they go. The sky does not chase them or push them away. It simply remains — vast, spacious, undisturbed.

You are that sky.

Whatever you have been carrying into this moment — the tasks, the worries, the unresolved questions — they will still be there when you return. But for right now, you can set them down. Even briefly. Even imperfectly.

Take one more deep breath in. Feel the aliveness in your body, the quiet in your mind. And as you exhale, let that quietness settle into you like warmth.

When you are ready, gently bring your awareness back to the room. Wiggle your fingers. Open your eyes, softly.

Carry a little of this stillness with you today. It belongs to you. It always has.
