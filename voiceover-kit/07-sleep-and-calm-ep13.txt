# PODCAST #07: SLEEP & CALM — Episode 13
**Voice to use:** Sarah or Charlotte (Soft, slow, soothing)
**Episode 1 voice used:** [Check your EP1 recording]

Hello, I'm Mark Coggins, and this is Sleep and Calm. Let the day settle behind you now. You've done enough. There is nothing left to carry. Just be warm, and still, and quiet.

Take a slow breath in, and let it fall away. And again. Let this next breath be a little slower than the last.

Tonight we are going to a walled garden, in the soft blue light of dusk, as the day quietly closes. You don't have to picture every detail. Just let the gentle evening settle around you.

You are sitting on a low wooden bench, in a small walled garden, at the very end of the day. The light is fading slowly, turning soft and blue and grey. The air is warm and still, and smells faintly of roses and cut grass.

All around you, the garden is settling for the night. The flowers have begun to close. The leaves hang heavy and quiet. A few late blooms glow pale in the gathering dusk, soft white and softest pink.

A gentle warmth rises from the stone path, holding the last of the day's sun. Somewhere nearby, water moves in a small fountain, a slow and quiet trickle, soft as a whisper. You listen to it without trying. In and out, slow and even.

A single bird sings its last few notes, low and unhurried, then falls quiet. The garden grows softer. The walls keep the world outside, far away. In here it is calm, and warm, and entirely still.

The first star appears above the wall, faint in the deep blue. The flowers nod gently as the air moves through them. Everything here is slowing down for the night, settling, softening, letting go.

There is nowhere you need to go. Nothing waiting beyond the wall. The garden asks nothing of you. It simply holds you in the warm blue dusk, and that is exactly enough.

Your shoulders grow heavy and loosen. Your jaw softens. Your hands rest, open and still. Your breathing slows to match the slow trickle of the water.

The dusk deepens, gentle and kind. The fountain murmurs on, soft and low. The closing flowers breathe their quiet scent into the still air, and the whole garden seems to fold itself around you, so you can rest.

You don't need to watch the light fade. It will go softly, all on its own, into a warm and quiet dark, whether you follow it or not.

There is nothing to do. Nowhere to be. Just rest now, all the way down, into the warm and the still and the soft blue dusk.

Rest now. Sleep well. I'll see you again, when you're ready.
