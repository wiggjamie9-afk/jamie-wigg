Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making flatbread pizza, and I mean a real, crispy, bubbling, cheese-pulling pizza, not in an hour with dough you had to make this morning, but in fifteen minutes using a flatbread as your base. Crunchy on the bottom, molten on top, and completely yours to load however you like. No special equipment, just your oven and a baking tray. Let's go.

Here's your kit. You'll want two flatbreads, naan, pita, or a tortilla all work brilliantly as the base. For the sauce: about four tablespoons of passata or pizza sauce, or honestly just a squeeze of tomato paste loosened with a splash of water. About one hundred and fifty grams of grated mozzarella or any cheese that melts. Then your toppings, whatever you love: pepperoni, ham, mushrooms, peppers, red onion, a handful of cherry tomatoes. A pinch of dried oregano, a drizzle of olive oil, and some fresh basil to finish. That's it. Let's cook.

Step one, get the oven screaming hot. Turn your oven up as high as it goes, usually around two hundred and forty degrees celsius, and slide a baking tray inside to heat up empty. A blazing oven and a preheated tray are the whole secret to a crispy base instead of a soggy, floppy one. While it heats, prep your toppings: slice your mushrooms, peppers, or onion nice and thin so they cook through in time.

Step two, sauce the base. Lay your two flatbreads out on a sheet of baking paper, this makes them easy to slide onto the hot tray later. Spoon about two tablespoons of sauce onto each one and spread it thin with the back of the spoon, leaving a little border around the edge for the crust. Go easy here, too much sauce is the number one cause of a soggy pizza, a thin layer is all you need.

Step three, cheese first, then toppings. Scatter your grated cheese over the sauce, then add your toppings on top of the cheese. This little trick helps anchor everything down and stops the toppings sliding off. Don't overload it, a few well-spaced toppings cook better and crisp up, a mountain of stuff just steams and goes soggy. Finish with a pinch of dried oregano and a light drizzle of olive oil.

Step four, into the hot oven. Carefully slide the flatbreads, paper and all, straight onto your preheated tray. Bake for about six to eight minutes. You're watching for the cheese to be fully melted, bubbling, and just starting to get those gorgeous golden brown spots, and the edges of the flatbread to turn deep and crispy. Every oven is different, so trust your eyes over the timer here.

Step five, finish and slice. Pull them out the moment the cheese is bubbling and the edges are crisp. Let them sit for just one minute, this lets the cheese set slightly so it doesn't slide off when you cut. Scatter over a few torn fresh basil leaves and a final tiny drizzle of olive oil. Slice into wedges and serve hot, with that satisfying crunch when you pick up a piece.

Here's my one tip that makes a flatbread taste like a real pizzeria: preheat that tray. A cold tray means the bottom of your base sits and steams while the top cooks, and you end up with a limp, bendy pizza. A ripping hot tray hits the base the second it lands, crisping the bottom instantly, so you get that proper crunch you can hear. It costs you nothing but a few minutes of preheating, and it's the difference every single time.

And that's dinner. Crispy, bubbling, cheese-pulling pizza in fifteen minutes, made exactly how you like it, with none of the dough drama. Make a couple, let everyone top their own, and eat them straight off the board while they're still too hot.

The no-soggy rules and the topping order are in the show notes, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
