Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making garlic shrimp pasta, the dish that looks and tastes like you booked a table somewhere fancy, but actually comes together in the time it takes the pasta to boil. Buttery, garlicky, a little bit of chili heat, a squeeze of lemon. Fifteen minutes, two pans, and you are eating like a restaurant. Let's go.

Here's your kit. You'll want about two hundred grams of spaghetti or linguine, whatever long pasta you love. About two hundred and fifty grams of shrimp, peeled, and frozen is completely fine, just thaw them under cool running water for two minutes. Four cloves of garlic, one lemon, a knob of butter, a glug of olive oil, a pinch of chili flakes, and a handful of fresh parsley if you have it. Salt and pepper. That's it. Let's cook.

Step one, get the pasta going. Fill a wide pan with water, add a really good pinch of salt, about one teaspoon, and get it boiling hard. Tip in your two hundred grams of pasta and set a timer for whatever the packet says, usually about ten minutes. This is your clock for the whole dish. While it cooks, we make the sauce.

Step two, prep while it boils. Thinly slice your four cloves of garlic. Pat your shrimp really dry with a paper towel, because dry shrimp catch color and wet shrimp just steam. Cut your lemon in half. Roughly chop your parsley. Line it all up. By now your pasta is maybe halfway done, so let's start the sauce.

Step three, the garlic butter. Set a second wide pan over medium heat. Add a glug of olive oil and your knob of butter, about two tablespoons. When the butter is foaming, add your sliced garlic and a pinch of chili flakes. Stir it gently for about one minute, just until the garlic turns pale gold and smells incredible. Do not let it brown, burnt garlic turns bitter, so keep that heat medium and keep it moving.

Step four, the shrimp. Turn the heat up to medium-high and add your dry shrimp in one layer. Let them sit for one minute, then flip. They cook fast, about two minutes total, and you'll know they're done the second they turn pink and curl into a loose C shape. A tight little O means overcooked, so pull them off the heat as soon as they're pink. Squeeze in the juice of half your lemon and a good pinch of salt and pepper.

Step five, marry the pasta and sauce. Just before you drain, scoop out a mug of that starchy pasta water, this is the secret to a glossy sauce that clings. Drain the pasta, then tip it straight into the shrimp pan. Add a splash of the pasta water, about four tablespoons, and toss everything together over low heat for thirty seconds. The starchy water and the butter come together into a silky sauce that coats every strand. Scatter over your parsley and finish with another squeeze of lemon.

Here's my one tip that makes it taste professional: never drain your pasta down to bone dry, and never skip the pasta water. That cloudy, starchy water is liquid gold, it's what turns a pile of buttery noodles into a proper glossy sauce that hugs the pasta instead of sliding off into a puddle. Save a mug every single time.

And that's dinner. Garlicky, lemony shrimp pasta in fifteen minutes, glistening in the pan and smelling like the best little bistro on the corner. Twirl it high, eat it the second it's hot, and pour yourself something nice while you're at it.

The garlic-don't-burn rule and the pasta-water trick are in the show notes, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
