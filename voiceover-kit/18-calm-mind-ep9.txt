Here's something most of us were taught, without anyone ever saying it out loud. We learned that some feelings are good and some feelings are bad. That happiness is welcome, and sadness should be hidden. That calm is acceptable, and anger is dangerous. And so, when a difficult feeling rises up, our very first instinct is to get rid of it. Push it down. Distract from it. Pour something over it. Anything but actually feel it.

And I understand that instinct completely. Difficult feelings are uncomfortable. Of course we want them gone. But here's the gentle, surprising truth I want to share with you today. The feelings we push away don't actually leave. They just go underground. They sit there, beneath the surface, waiting, and they tend to leak out sideways, in our bodies, in our moods, in the way we snap at someone we love over something small. A feeling that isn't felt doesn't disappear. It just goes quiet, and stays.

There's an old image I find helpful. A difficult emotion is a little like a wave in the ocean. When a wave rises, you cannot stop it by standing firm and pushing against it. It will simply knock you down. But if you let the wave lift you, if you let it move through, it rises, and it crests, and then, on its own, it falls and softens and passes. Feelings work much the same way. They are not permanent states. They are waves. They rise, they peak, and if we let them, they pass.

So today, instead of pushing the wave away, I want to gently teach you how to let it move through. And the first step is the hardest one to believe. You let the feeling be there.

When a difficult emotion rises, instead of bracing against it, try turning toward it, just a little. You might even say to yourself, softly, this is sadness, and it's okay that it's here. Or, I'm feeling anger right now, and that's allowed. Notice we're not acting on the feeling. We're not letting anger run our behavior. We're simply letting it be felt, which is a very different thing. You're allowed to feel anything. You don't have to do anything about it. The feeling and the action are not the same.

Then, the second step. Bring it into your body, because feelings live in the body, not just the mind. When the emotion rises, get curious, gently. Where do I feel this? Is it a tightness in my chest? A heaviness in my throat? A churning in my stomach? You don't have to make it go away. Just notice it, the way you might rest a warm hand on a frightened child's back. You're not fixing. You're keeping it company. And here's the quiet miracle. When a feeling is allowed to be felt, with a little kindness, it usually begins to soften. Not always quickly. But it moves, because that's what feelings, when welcomed, finally do.

Feelings are far more bearable when we stop fighting them. It's the fighting that exhausts us. It's the holding the wave back that takes all our strength. When we finally let the wave move through, it almost always turns out to be more survivable than we feared. You are bigger than any single feeling. You are the ocean, not just the wave.
