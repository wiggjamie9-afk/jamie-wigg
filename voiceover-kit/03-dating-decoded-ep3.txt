That strange, modern, undefined limbo that sits between meeting someone and actually being together. So today, that's exactly what we're doing. Why this stage even exists, why it makes so many of us so anxious, how to tell whether it's actually going somewhere, how to have the dreaded define-the-relationship conversation without sending someone running, and how to know when it's time to walk away. Take a breath. Let's decode it together.

First, let's name what the talking stage actually is, because it's a genuinely new thing. A generation or two ago, the path was clearer. You met someone, you went on a date, and at some point fairly quickly, somebody asked somebody to be exclusive, or to go steady. The categories were simpler. Today? We have this long, blurry runway before anything official. You're talking. You're seeing where it goes. You're getting to know each other with no labels and no defined commitment. It can last a few weeks. It can, unfortunately, last months.

So why does this stage exist at all? A few reasons. We have endless options now, with dating apps putting what feels like infinite choice in our pockets, and that makes a lot of people hesitant to close any doors. There's also a real cultural fear of vulnerability. Saying I like you and I want this to be something is a risk, and the talking stage lets people enjoy the connection while keeping one foot out the door, protected from rejection. And honestly, sometimes it's just uncertainty. People genuinely don't know yet, and the talking stage is where they figure it out.

Now, here's why it can feel so deeply anxiety-inducing, and I want you to hear this part clearly: your anxiety is not a character flaw. It's a completely logical response to genuine ambiguity. Our brains crave certainty. We are wired to want to know where we stand with the people who matter to us. So when you're putting real emotional energy into someone, but there's no agreed-upon definition of what you are, your mind fills that empty space with worry. Do they like me as much as I like them? Are they talking to other people? If I bring it up, will I scare them off? That's not you being needy. That's you being a human being who wants clarity. The ambiguity is the problem, not your reaction to it.

Okay. So how do you tell whether a talking stage is actually going somewhere, versus quietly going nowhere? I want to give you something practical here, because the answer is almost always in behavior, not words. Anyone can say sweet things over text. What you're watching for is consistency and effort. Is this person reaching out reliably, or do they go quiet for days and resurface only when it's convenient? Are they actually making plans to see you in person, or is it endless texting that never becomes a real date? Do they ask about your life, remember the things you tell them, follow up on how your big meeting went? And here's a big one: are they slowly integrating you into their actual world, mentioning their friends, making plans for next week, next month? When someone genuinely wants something with you, they move toward you. They make it easier, not harder, to be close to them. If you constantly feel like you're the only one rowing the boat, that itself is your answer.

Now let's talk about the conversation everyone dreads. Defining the relationship. The D T R talk. So many people avoid this because they're terrified that asking what are we will instantly scare the other person away. So let me reframe that for you. The right person, the person who's actually right for you, will not be scared off by a calm, kind, honest conversation about where things are going. If a simple question about the relationship sends someone sprinting for the exit, they've just handed you extremely valuable information, very early, and saved you months of confusion.

Here's how to actually do it. First, pick a low-pressure moment. Not in the middle of a fight, not over a frantic text thread, not at two in the morning. A calm, relaxed time when you're both present. Second, lead with how you feel, not with an accusation or a demand. Something like, I've really enjoyed getting to know you, and I'm starting to feel like I'd want something more serious. I'd love to know where your head's at. Notice what that does. You're being vulnerable and honest, you're not cornering them, and you're genuinely inviting their truth, not just fishing for the answer you want. Third, and this is the hard part: actually listen to the answer, and believe it. If they share that they want the same thing, wonderful. If they're hesitant, or they tell you they're not looking for anything serious, that is real, usable information. Don't argue with it. Don't try to convince them. Just let it tell you what you need to know.

Which brings us to the hardest part of all. Knowing when to walk away. Because not every talking stage is meant to become a relationship, and staying too long in ambiguity has a real cost. It quietly drains your confidence and ties up your heart in something undefined. So here's my honest guidance. If you've clearly communicated what you want, and the other person keeps you in limbo with no movement and no real answer, that delay is your answer. If the effort is wildly one-sided, week after week, that's your answer. And if the whole situation is consistently making you feel anxious, small, and unsure of yourself rather than happy and secure, please listen to that. You are allowed to want clarity. You are allowed to want commitment. And you are absolutely allowed to walk away from anyone who isn't able to meet you there. Choosing yourself is not giving up. It's standing up.

So let's hold onto the big truths from today. The talking stage is a real, modern, genuinely confusing phase, and your anxiety inside it makes complete sense. Watch behavior over words. Have the honest conversation without fear, because the right person will meet it with care. And trust yourself enough to walk away when someone won't give you the clarity you deserve.

Here's what I want you to remember most: you do not have to shrink yourself or play it cool to keep someone interested. The connection that's truly meant for you will not require you to live in painful uncertainty to hold onto it.
