Welcome to Wanderlust. I'm William. Today, breathe in, because the air where we're going is thick with spice and woodsmoke and a thousand years of stories. Morocco. North Africa, where the continent reaches up toward Europe across a narrow strait, and where a single city can hold the whole sensory weight of a country. We are going to lose ourselves, on purpose, in the labyrinth of Marrakech, and then escape into the silence of the desert beyond it.

Let me put you there. You step through an ancient gate in a red clay wall, and the modern world simply falls away behind you. You are in the medina, the old walled heart of the city, and it is a maze. Narrow lanes twist and fork and double back. A man leads a donkey stacked impossibly high with mint. The walls glow rose and ochre in the late light. Then you turn a corner and the lane spills open into a great square, and the square is alive. Snake charmers, storytellers, drummers, stalls of orange juice and dates and sizzling skewers, lanterns being lit one by one as dusk falls and the call to prayer rises over the rooftops. It is overwhelming, and it is one of the most thrilling places on earth.

Now, Morocco asks more of a traveler than a gentle European city does, and I want to prepare you honestly, because the preparation is what lets you relax and love it.

First, you will get lost in the medina. Everyone does. The lanes are not on any map your phone fully understands. Treat it as part of the adventure, not a problem to solve. Note a landmark near your accommodation, a particular gate or shop, and when you are truly turned around, a small tip to a shopkeeper or a child will buy you a guide back to a main street. Getting lost here is not danger. It is simply the price of the magic.

Second, stay in a riad. These are traditional houses built around a quiet inner courtyard, often with a fountain and a fig tree and a rooftop terrace for breakfast. Step inside off the chaotic lane and the noise vanishes, replaced by cool tile and trickling water. A riad is the secret to loving Morocco. The street energizes you, and the riad restores you.

Third, the haggling. In the souks, the markets, prices are a conversation, not a fact. This unsettles a lot of visitors, so let me make it simple. Decide what a thing is worth to you before you start. Offer perhaps half of the first price, smile, stay friendly, and be willing to walk away. Walking away is your greatest power, and it is completely polite. The whole exchange is meant to be warm, even theatrical. Enjoy it.

Fourth, dress with respect. Morocco is a Muslim country, and a little modesty goes a long way, especially away from the tourist core. Shoulders and knees covered, for men and women both, will earn you warmer smiles and smoother passage everywhere you go.

Fifth, and this is the one I beg you not to skip. Leave the city for the desert. Take the long road over the Atlas Mountains, past Berber villages clinging to the slopes, until the land flattens and reddens and finally becomes dunes. Sleep one night in a camp out on the sand. Watch the sun go down and the stars come up in numbers you have never seen in your life, the whole Milky Way spilled across a sky with no other light to compete. Sit by a fire, listen to drums, and feel the desert cool around you. There is nothing else like it.

And here is what Morocco gives you. It overwhelms your senses completely, all of them at once, and in doing so it pulls you fully into the present moment. You cannot be anywhere else. You cannot be checking anything, planning anything, worrying about anything back home. The country simply demands all of you, and in return it gives you a richness of experience that follows you home and colors your dreams for years.

So your homework this week. Make a pot of mint tea, the way Morocco does, strong and sweet, and drink it slowly while you picture that square at dusk, the lanterns lighting one by one. That cup is your first step through the gate.

Travel gently, travel openly, and remember, the most beautiful place you'll ever discover might just be how fully alive you become when every sense is awake at once. I'll be here next time. Until then, keep dreaming with your eyes open.
