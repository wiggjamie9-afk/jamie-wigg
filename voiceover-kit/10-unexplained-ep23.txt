On the second of October, nineteen seventy-one, a young man named Lars Mittank arrived with friends at the Golden Sands resort on the Bulgarian Black Sea coast. It was a holiday. There were beach bars, heat, and the kind of easy carelessness that comes with summer travel. At some point during the trip, Lars got into a fight. The details are vague, but he sustained a ruptured eardrum and was told by a doctor not to fly until it healed. His friends left without him. He stayed behind to wait.

What happened next is documented in fragments, and those fragments do not fit together.

The night before he was due to fly home, Lars called his mother. He was frightened. He said someone was following him. He said not to trust a doctor he had seen. He said he needed money, though he had a bank card and was not out of funds. He then asked her not to say his name because someone might be listening. His mother described a sudden shift in his personality, an urgency and a terror that had not been there before.

The next morning, Lars checked out of his accommodation and went to the airport to fly home. He was seen on the airport's closed-circuit cameras. The footage showed him sitting in the waiting area, appearing agitated. Then he stood up suddenly, left his luggage where it sat, and ran. He sprinted out of the airport and disappeared into the surrounding countryside. The cameras caught the last images of Lars Mittank at full sprint across the parking lot.

He was twenty eight years old. He was physically healthy. He had no documented history of psychiatric illness. His phone and bank card were never used again. His luggage remained at the airport. No body was ever found.

In the years that followed, his family searched. Investigators looked into the fight at the resort and the doctor he had seen. The doctor, when interviewed, said the visit was unremarkable. There were no obvious criminal explanations. There was no obvious mental health history. Some investigators theorized that the head trauma from the fight, or the pressure changes from a previous flight, could have triggered a sudden psychiatric episode. Some pointed to the possibility of drug use at the resort, though nothing was confirmed.

The case gained wide attention in two thousand fourteen when the airport footage was shared online. People who watched it described the same thing: a young man sitting quietly who then bolts as though terrified of something no one else in the frame can see.

A Romanian shepherd reported seeing someone matching Lars's description in the mountains near the Bulgarian border in the weeks after his disappearance. The sighting was never confirmed.

Lars Mittank has never been found. His case sits in a particular kind of limbo. It is not a drowning, not a crime scene, not a concluded story. It is a man at an airport, a suitcase left behind, a phone call in the night, and then seventy meters of surveillance footage ending in a sprint toward the tree line.

Whatever he was running from, he saw it clearly. We have never learned what it was.
