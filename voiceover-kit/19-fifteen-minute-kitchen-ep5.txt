Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making a grain bowl, and I know, I know, "grain bowl" sounds like something a wellness influencer would chirp at you. But stay with me, because this is genuinely one of the best fast dinners there is. Warm, filling, colorful, and endlessly different every time. Fifteen minutes. Let's build a bowl.

Here's what you'll grab, nice and flexible. A quick-cooking grain: a pouch of microwave rice, or some couscous, or quinoa. About one cup of grain, dry, feeds two. A protein: a drained can of chickpeas, some leftover chicken, or two eggs. A couple handfuls of veg, fresh or frozen. And then a sauce, which is the part that makes the whole thing sing. Olive oil, lemon, salt, and something creamy like yogurt or tahini. That's our kit. Let's go.

Step one, start the grain. If you're using couscous, this is the dream: put one cup of couscous in a bowl, pour over one and a quarter cups of boiling water, add a pinch of salt and a glug of olive oil, cover with a plate, and walk away. It cooks itself in five minutes while you do everything else. If you're using a microwave rice pouch, even easier, that's ninety seconds at the end. Quinoa takes a touch longer, about twelve minutes simmering, so start it first if that's your pick.

Step two, crisp the protein. Get a frying pan hot with a little oil. Tip in your drained can of chickpeas, spread them out, add a pinch of salt and half a teaspoon of cumin or paprika, and let them fry for about four minutes, shaking now and then, until they're golden and a little crisp. If you're using leftover chicken, just warm it through in the same pan. If you're going with eggs, fry two until the edges are lacy and the yolk is still soft.

Step three, the quick veg. While that crisps, prep your veg. Anything works. Slice a handful of cherry tomatoes, dice half a cucumber, grab a handful of baby spinach, or throw a cup of frozen peas straight into a mug with a splash of water and microwave them for two minutes. Raw, warm, crunchy, soft, it all belongs in the bowl.

Step four, the sauce, and this is the magic. In a small bowl, mix three tablespoons of yogurt or tahini, the juice of half a lemon, a tablespoon of olive oil, a pinch of salt, and a tiny splash of water to loosen it. Whisk it with a fork until it's drizzly. Taste it. It should be bright, a little tangy, a little rich. That sauce is what ties a pile of random ingredients into one dinner that feels designed.

Step five, build it. Fluff your couscous with a fork. Grain goes down first, as the base. Then arrange everything on top in little zones, the crisp chickpeas here, the veg there, the greens tucked in. Then spoon that sauce all over, scatter on anything fresh, a few herbs, a crack of pepper, a final squeeze of lemon. Eat it warm.

Here's my one tip: build it in zones, not a mush. When each thing has its own little spot in the bowl, every forkful tastes slightly different, and that's what keeps you interested right to the last bite. We eat with our eyes first, so make it pretty, it genuinely tastes better.

And there it is. A warm, gorgeous grain bowl in fifteen minutes, and it's never the same twice. Swap the grain, swap the protein, swap the sauce, and you've got a brand new dinner every night of the week.

The base, protein, veg, and sauce formula is written out in the show notes, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
