Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making a coconut curry in a hurry, a creamy, fragrant, gently spiced curry that tastes like it simmered all afternoon but is actually on your plate in fifteen minutes flat. Warming, comforting, and endlessly flexible, this is the dinner you'll come back to again and again on a tired Tuesday. One pan, a tin of coconut milk, and a spoonful of curry paste do most of the work. Let's go.

Here's your kit. You'll want one tin of coconut milk, that's about four hundred millilitres, the full-fat kind for richness. Two heaped tablespoons of curry paste, red, green, or yellow, whatever you fancy. About four hundred grams of protein, diced chicken thigh, prawns, or a drained tin of chickpeas to keep it meat-free. A couple of big handfuls of quick veg, frozen peas, spinach, or sliced peppers all work. One small onion, two cloves of garlic, a neutral oil, and to finish, a squeeze of lime and some fresh coriander. Rice or naan to serve. That's it. Let's cook.

Step one, prep and get the pan hot. Set a wide deep pan over medium-high heat. While it warms, finely chop your one small onion and mince your two cloves of garlic. Dice your chicken into small bite-size pieces so it cooks fast, or just have your chickpeas drained and ready. If you want rice, get a microwave pouch standing by, or you'll have plenty of time to boil some.

Step two, fry the paste. Add a splash of oil to the hot pan, then your onion, and cook for two minutes until it softens. Add the garlic for thirty seconds, then add your two heaped tablespoons of curry paste. Fry the paste in the oil for about one minute, stirring constantly, until it darkens slightly and smells amazing. This is the most important step, frying the paste instead of just dumping it into liquid unlocks all those deep, toasty, fragrant flavors.

Step three, the protein. Tip in your diced chicken or prawns and stir to coat every piece in that fragrant paste. Let it cook for about three minutes, stirring now and then, until the chicken is sealed and turning golden on the outside. If you're using chickpeas instead, just add them now and stir them through the paste for a minute to warm.

Step four, pour in the coconut. Add your whole tin of coconut milk, that's four hundred millilitres, and stir it through, scraping up all the tasty paste stuck to the bottom of the pan. Bring it up to a gentle bubble, then let it simmer for about four minutes. This both cooks the chicken through and lets the sauce thicken slightly into something silky and rich. Add your veg now, harder veg like peppers go in early, soft stuff like spinach or frozen peas just needs the last minute or two.

Step five, finish and balance. Take it off the heat and squeeze in the juice of half a lime, then taste. This is the moment that makes it: a pinch of salt deepens it, and that hit of lime brightens the whole thing and cuts through the richness. If it's a little flat, it almost always wants more salt or more lime. Scatter over a big handful of fresh coriander and serve over rice or with warm naan for scooping.

Here's my one tip that makes a hurry-curry taste slow-cooked: always fry your curry paste in the oil before any liquid goes in. People stir paste straight into coconut milk and wonder why it tastes flat and raw. Give it that one minute sizzling in hot oil first, and the spices bloom and toast, building a deep, rounded flavor that genuinely tastes like it's been on the stove for hours.

And that's dinner. A bowl of creamy, golden, fragrant curry with rice soaking up all that sauce, ready before you'd even have finished reading a takeaway menu. Cozy, generous, and completely yours to tweak hotter, milder, or whatever's in the fridge.

The fry-the-paste rule and the lime-and-salt balance are in the show notes, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
