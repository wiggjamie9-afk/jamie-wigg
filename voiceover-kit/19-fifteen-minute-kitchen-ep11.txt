Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making chicken lettuce wraps, the dish that's light and fresh but somehow still feels like a proper feast. Savory, sticky, gingery chicken spooned into cool, crunchy lettuce cups. No oven, barely any washing up, and it tastes like your favorite restaurant starter scaled up into dinner. Fifteen minutes, one pan. Let's go.

Here's your kit. You'll want about four hundred grams of chicken mince, or turkey mince if you prefer, or you can finely chop two chicken breasts. One small onion, three cloves of garlic, a thumb of fresh ginger, and two spring onions if you have them. For the sauce: soy sauce, a teaspoon of honey, and a splash of vinegar or lime. A neutral oil. And the star, one head of crisp lettuce, baby gem or iceberg are perfect, the cups need to hold their shape. Optional crunch on top: a handful of cashews or peanuts. That's it. Let's cook.

Step one, prep everything first, because this moves fast. Finely chop your one small onion. Mince your three cloves of garlic and your thumb of ginger. Slice your two spring onions. Then carefully peel the leaves off your lettuce, one at a time, and stack them on a plate, these are your cups, so try to keep them whole. Line everything up by the stove.

Step two, brown the chicken. Get a wide pan really hot over medium-high heat, add a splash of oil, then tip in your four hundred grams of chicken mince. Spread it out flat and leave it alone for two full minutes so it catches some golden color on the bottom, that color is flavor. Then break it up with your spoon and stir until it's cooked through, about three more minutes, with no pink left.

Step three, the aromatics. Push the chicken to one side of the pan and add your onion, garlic, and ginger to the bare patch with a tiny bit more oil. Cook for about one minute until fragrant and the onion softens, then stir it all through the chicken. Your kitchen will smell amazing right about now.

Step four, the sticky sauce. Pour in about three tablespoons of soy sauce, one teaspoon of honey, and a splash of vinegar or a squeeze of lime straight into the hot pan. Let it bubble and reduce for one minute, tossing the chicken through it, until everything is glossy and sticky and coated. Taste a little spoonful, this is your moment to add a touch more soy if it needs salt, or more lime if it needs brightness. Stir in most of your spring onions, saving a few for the top.

Step five, build the wraps. Bring the pan straight to the table with your stack of lettuce cups. Spoon a generous heap of the sticky chicken into each cool, crunchy leaf. Scatter over your reserved spring onions and a handful of crushed cashews or peanuts for crunch. Eat them with your hands, folded like a little taco, and don't worry about the drips.

Here's my one tip that makes these sing: get that pan properly hot before the chicken goes in, and let the mince sit undisturbed for those first two minutes. Most people stir mince constantly and end up with pale, grey, steamed meat. Leave it alone, let it brown, and you get deep, savory, golden flavor that makes the whole dish taste like a restaurant instead of a sad weeknight scramble.

And that's dinner. Hot, sticky, gingery chicken tucked into cold, crisp lettuce, that contrast of warm and cool, soft and crunchy, is the whole magic of it. Light enough that you feel great after, satisfying enough that nobody leaves the table hungry.

The sauce ratio and the build order are in the show notes, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
