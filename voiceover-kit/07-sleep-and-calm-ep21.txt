Welcome to Sleep and Calm. I am glad you are ending your day here.

Tonight we are talking about the evening routine — the gentle architecture of a night that leads reliably to rest. Not a rigid schedule or a list of things you must do, but a rhythm. A set of quiet signals you give yourself that say: the day is ending, and I am ready to let it go.

Every good night of sleep is built on a good evening. And every good evening begins with a transition — a deliberate shift from the activity and output of the day to something softer and more inward.

Think about how most evenings unfold without intention. We are busy until we are not, and then we fall into screens and noise until suddenly it is late and we are still wide awake. We have not given our nervous systems any signal that something different is coming. And so the body keeps running, and sleep feels like something we have to catch rather than something that comes to us.

What if the last hour or two of your evening became yours — truly yours — to use with care?

The transition might start with something as simple as changing into comfortable clothes. This small physical act signals to your brain that the day's work is done. You are no longer in the world. You are coming home to yourself.

Then perhaps something warm. A cup of chamomile or a simple herbal tea, held in both hands. Warmth is deeply calming to the nervous system. It lowers the body's alertness and invites the muscles to soften.

Dim the lights. As we spoke about in an earlier episode, light is a powerful signal. Let your home grow quieter and darker in the hour before bed. This alone can shift your body toward sleep before you have even lain down.

Choose something unhurried for this time. A book — physical, paper, not a screen. A light stretch on the floor. A brief journal entry: three things that happened today, one thing you are grateful for. Writing grounds the mind and creates a satisfying sense of closure on the day.

Avoid problem-solving in this hour. Not because the problems do not matter, but because the evening cannot solve them — and trying only extends the day into the night. If a thought arrives that needs attention, write it down. Give it a container. Tell yourself: I will return to this tomorrow.

And then, when the hour has passed and the lights are low and your body is warm and your mind is quieter, move toward your bed.

Lie down slowly. Feel the weight of your body being received. Take three long breaths — in through the nose, out through the mouth.

You have taken care of yourself tonight. You have made space for rest.

And rest will come.

Sleep well.
