And road three is local businesses. Look around your own town. The cafe with the sad, outdated website. The plumber with no social media presence. The boutique whose email newsletter hasn't gone out in a year. These owners are experts at their craft and often completely overwhelmed by the digital stuff. A friendly, specific offer to solve one concrete problem for them can turn into your first steady client. Walk in, or send a short email. Be specific about the one thing you can fix.

Step three. Pricing your time, because I know this is the part that makes everybody nervous. Here's the mindset shift. When you freelance, you are not charging what you got paid hourly at your job. As you build a track record and collect happy clients, that number climbs, and it climbs faster than you'd think. But here's the pro move I really want you to hear: wherever you can, price the project, not the hour.
