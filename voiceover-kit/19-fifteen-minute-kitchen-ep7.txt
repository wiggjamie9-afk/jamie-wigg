Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making an omelette for dinner. Not a sad, rubbery, folded-over breakfast omelette, but a proper, fluffy, loaded omelette dinner that feels like a treat and costs almost nothing. Eggs are the fastest dinner on earth, and tonight we're going to do them right. Fifteen minutes, one pan, let's go.

Here's the kit, beautifully cheap and flexible. For two people, you'll want six eggs, three each. A small handful of whatever filling you fancy: grated cheese always, plus maybe some ham, mushrooms, spinach, leftover veg, half an onion. A knob of butter. Salt and pepper. And some bread or a quick salad on the side to make it a real meal. That's it. Honestly, that's it.

Step one, prep your fillings first. This matters, because once the eggs hit the pan it all happens fast. So before you crack a single egg, get your fillings ready. Slice your mushrooms, chop your ham, grate your cheese, wash your spinach. Anything that needs actual cooking, like raw mushrooms or onion, you'll cook first. Anything already cooked or soft just gets scattered in later.

Step two, cook the raw fillings. Set a medium non-stick pan over medium heat with a little butter. If you've got mushrooms or onion, fry them now for about three minutes until soft and golden, then tip them out onto a plate for a moment. We want them cooked but not crowding the eggs later. If all your fillings are ready-to-go, like cheese and ham, you can skip straight ahead.

Step three, beat the eggs. Crack your six eggs into a bowl. Add a pinch of salt, a crack of pepper, and a splash of water, about one tablespoon. The water is the secret to fluffiness, it turns to steam in the pan and puffs the eggs up. Beat them well with a fork for a good twenty seconds, until they're completely smooth and a little frothy. No streaks of white.

Step four, the cook, and this is the whole game. Get your pan back to medium with a fresh knob of butter, let it foam but not brown. Pour in the eggs. Now wait a few seconds, then take a spatula and gently push the set edges into the middle, tilting the pan so the runny egg flows out to fill the gap. Keep doing that for about a minute. When the top is still just a little glossy and soft, stop. That's the moment.

Step five, fill and fold. Scatter your fillings over one half of the omelette: your cooked mushrooms, your ham, a good handful of cheese, a few spinach leaves that'll wilt in the heat. Then, gently, fold the empty half over the top like closing a book. Let it sit for thirty seconds so the cheese melts and the middle finishes cooking from its own heat. Then slide it out of the pan onto a plate. For two omelettes, just do it twice, the second one only takes two minutes.

Here's my one tip that changes everything: take it out of the pan while it still looks slightly underdone in the middle. Eggs keep cooking on the plate from their own heat, so a perfect omelette always looks a touch too soft right before you serve it. Cook it till it looks done and it'll be dry by the time you sit down. Pull it early, trust the carryover.

And that's a proper dinner. A golden, fluffy, loaded omelette in fifteen minutes, with a slice of bread or a handful of salad on the side. It feels fancy. It costs about a dollar. That's my kind of magic.

The egg ratio and the fold method are in the show notes, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
