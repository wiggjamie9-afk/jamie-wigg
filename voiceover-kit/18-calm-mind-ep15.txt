Today, I want to talk about something that quietly runs underneath so much of our anxiety. The exhausting, relentless pull toward perfect. Perfectionism. And I want to gently invite you to begin setting it down, because I think it may be costing you far more than you realize.

Let me start by saying what perfectionism actually is, because it disguises itself so well. From the outside, it can look like high standards, like ambition, like caring deeply. And we tend to wear it almost like a badge. But underneath, real perfectionism isn't about loving excellence. It's about fearing failure. It's the deep, often hidden belief that you are only acceptable, only safe, only worthy, if you get it exactly right, and that any flaw, any mistake, any falling short, will expose you as not good enough. And so you drive yourself, and you brace, and nothing you do ever quite feels like it counts, because there's always something that could have been better.

Here's the cruel trick at the heart of it. Perfect doesn't exist. It's a horizon. No matter how far you walk toward it, it keeps receding the very same distance away. So the perfectionist is running a race with no finish line, pouring everything they have into chasing something that, by its very nature, can never be reached. No wonder it's so exhausting. No wonder it so often ends in either burnout, from trying too hard, or paralysis, from being too afraid to even begin, because if you might not do it perfectly, some part of you would rather not do it at all.

So I want to offer you a different idea to lean on, a gentler and far more livable one. The aim is not perfect. The aim is good enough. And I know, I know how that can sound at first, almost like permission to be lazy, to stop caring. But it's the opposite. Good enough is not the enemy of excellence. Good enough is what lets the work actually get finished, get shared, get out into the world, instead of being polished forever in the dark, or never started at all. The meal that nourishes is the one that gets cooked, not the flawless one you were too daunted to attempt. The kind message that reaches your friend is the imperfect one you actually sent. Done and good enough will always, always do more good in the world than perfect and unfinished.

There's an old idea I find freeing, from the craft traditions, that a handmade thing should hold one small, deliberate imperfection, because only the imperfect is truly alive, and only the imperfect is truly human. The perfect is cold. It's the small flaws, the uneven edges, the marks of a real hand, that make a thing warm, and real, and ours. You are not meant to be flawless. You are meant to be alive. And alive is gloriously, beautifully imperfect.

So how do we begin to loosen perfectionism's grip? Let me offer a few gentle things. The first is simply to notice it. To catch the moment when good enough is actually finished, but the perfectionist voice insists, no, more, fix it, it's not ready. You can learn to recognize that voice, and to gently question it. Is this genuinely unfinished, or am I just afraid to let it be seen? The second is to practice, on purpose, doing some small things imperfectly. Sending the message without rereading it five times. Leaving the small task at good enough, and walking away. Letting one thing be a little messy, just to prove to your nervous system that the sky does not, in fact, fall. And the third, the deepest, is to gently separate what you do from what you are worth. You are not your output. Your value as a person was never up for assessment. You don't have to earn the right to exist by being flawless. You're allowed to be enough, simply as you are, before you've achieved anything at all.

So here is a small practice to carry with you. I call it good enough, on purpose. Choose one small thing today, and deliberately let it be merely good enough. Don't perfect it. Don't polish it past the point of done. Finish it, let it be imperfect, and then, gently, move on. And notice, with curiosity, that you are still okay. Good enough, on purpose. Each time you do it, you loosen the grip of a voice that has likely been driving you, hard and tiredly, for a very long time.

Please be patient and kind with yourself here, because this is genuinely difficult. If you've spent years believing that your worth depends on getting everything right, letting something be imperfect can feel almost unsafe, like you're risking something. That's all right. You're unlearning something old and deep, and that takes time and tenderness. And if perfectionism has tightened into something heavier, a harsh inner cruelty, or an anxiety that keeps you from living, please reach out to a professional who can help you gently unwind it. These small practices walk beside good support. They were never meant to replace it.

So as we close today, carry just one thing with you. You do not have to be perfect to be worthy, to be loved, or to be at peace. Perfect was always a horizon you could never reach. But good enough, warm, human, imperfect, beautifully real good enough, that is right here, fully within your reach, and it has been all along.
