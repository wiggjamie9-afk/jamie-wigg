Welcome to fifteen minute kitchen. Today is for the parents, the partners, the people who are cooking for someone who claims they do not like anything. We are making a family dinner that actually gets eaten — not tolerated, actually eaten — by adults and kids and picky teenagers and everyone at the table. The dish is a honey garlic chicken with roasted broccoli, served over rice. Crowd-pleasing ingredients, fast, and it has enough going on that the flavour is genuinely good.

Here is your grab list. Five hundred grams of chicken breast or thigh, cut into strips or chunks. One head of broccoli, cut into florets. Four cloves of garlic, minced. Three tablespoons of honey. Two tablespoons of soy sauce. One tablespoon of butter. A squeeze of lemon or a splash of apple cider vinegar. Olive oil. Salt and pepper. Cooked rice to serve.

The broccoli goes in the oven first because it takes the longest. Preheat your oven to two hundred and twenty degrees. Spread the broccoli florets on a baking tray, drizzle generously with olive oil, season with salt and pepper, and toss to coat. Slide them into the oven. They will take ten to twelve minutes, and the tips should be slightly charred and crispy. If there is one vegetable that everyone eats when it is roasted like this, it is broccoli. The char makes it sweet, not bitter.

While the broccoli roasts, season your chicken strips with a little salt and pepper. Into a wide pan over medium-high heat, a splash of olive oil. Add the chicken in a single layer — if your pan is not big enough to fit it all without crowding, do it in two batches. Cook for three to four minutes per side until golden and cooked through. Set the chicken aside on a plate.

Now the sauce in the same pan. Reduce the heat to medium and add the butter. Once it melts, add the garlic and cook for thirty seconds, stirring so it does not burn. Then pour in the honey and soy sauce. Let it bubble for about a minute, stirring, until it thickens into a sticky glaze. Add your lemon squeeze or splash of vinegar to balance the sweetness. Tip the chicken back into the pan and toss until every piece is coated in that glossy sauce.

By now your broccoli should be done. Pull it from the oven and bring everything to the table: a bowl of rice, the glazed chicken in the pan, and the roasted broccoli. Let people build their own plates.

Here is why this one gets eaten by everyone. The honey garlic sauce has just enough sweetness that kids respond to it immediately, but the garlic and the soy sauce make it actually interesting for adults. The broccoli is roasted, which removes any bitterness that puts people off steamed broccoli. And rice is the most universally accepted starch on the planet.

The tip for this one: do not skip the vinegar or lemon in the sauce. Sweet without acid tastes flat and cloying. Just that small splash lifts the whole sauce and makes it taste balanced rather than like something from a candy shop.
