Hello, this is Glinda, and you're listening to Sports Unfiltered. Today we're chasing the most loaded word in all of sport. A word people throw around like confetti but almost never stop to define. Dynasty. We call teams dynasties all the time. But what actually makes one? Where's the line between a great team and a true dynasty? Let's get into it.

Here's the first thing nobody wants to admit. Winning once does not make a dynasty. Winning twice doesn't either. A single championship can be a perfect storm. The right players peaking at the right moment, a kind bracket, a rival's key man going down with an injury. Even back to back titles can be a hot window that slams shut the second the core gets old or gets paid and walks out the door. A dynasty is something rarer. A dynasty is winning when everyone knows you're going to try to win, and beating them anyway.

Because that's the part people miss. The hardest thing in sport is not getting to the top. It's staying there. When you're the underdog, nobody's planning their entire season around stopping you. But when you're the dynasty, you are the game that every single opponent circles on their calendar. You get their best shot, every night, for years. Every rival rebuilds specifically to take your crown. The whole sport is engineered against you. And you have to keep winning into that headwind.

So what do the real ones have in common? Three things, and none of them are luck.

The first is a spine. A dynasty is built around a small group of people who simply do not leave. A franchise player, a coach, a culture that outlasts any single roster. Faces come and go on the edges, but the spine holds. That continuity is what lets a team reload instead of rebuild.

The second is hunger that survives success. This is the killer. Most teams, the moment they win, get comfortable. They get full. The fire that got them to the top quietly goes out, because winning is the most dangerous drug in sport. It makes you believe you've arrived. The dynasty is the team that wins, and the next morning is somehow angrier and more paranoid than the team that lost. They treat every title like it's about to be stolen.

The third is the ability to evolve. The game changes. Rules change, tactics change, a new generation of athletes shows up faster and stronger. The team that won five years ago using one style cannot win today using that same style, and the dynasty knows it. They tear up their own winning formula before it gets exposed. They change while they're still on top, which feels insane to everyone watching, and is exactly why they stay on top.

Now here's my take, and this is where I'll get some heat. I think we are far too generous with the word dynasty these days. A team makes three finals and wins one and we're ready to carve them into the mountain. No. A dynasty has to be tested by time and by rivals and come out the other side still standing. If your reign can be explained by one great player or one weak era, that's not a dynasty. That's a moment. A beautiful moment, but a moment.

And here's the strange, almost sad truth at the heart of all this. Every dynasty ends. Every single one. There is no team that wins forever. The bodies age, the hunger fades, the money pulls the group apart, and a younger, angrier team is always coming. So the real measure of a dynasty isn't that it lasted forever. It's that for a stretch of years, it bent an entire sport around its will and made everyone else play for second.

That's why we remember them. Not because they were unbeatable, but because they were beatable, and nobody could do it for the longest time. That's it from me. This has been Sports Unfiltered. Now go argue.
