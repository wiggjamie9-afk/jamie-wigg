This is History Uncovered.

Tonight I want to tell you a story you already know. And then I want to take it apart and ask you whether it ever happened at all.

For ten years, the Greeks had laid siege to the city of Troy. Ten years. Think about that. A generation of soldiers grew grey beards on a foreign beach. They came for a woman — Helen, the most beautiful in the world, carried off to Troy by a prince named Paris. They came in a thousand black ships. And for ten years, those high stone walls would not break.

Achilles fought there. Hector defended the gates. Kings and heroes died in the dust outside a city that simply would not fall.

And then — the story goes — a man named Odysseus had an idea so audacious it should never have worked.

The Greeks built a horse. An enormous wooden horse, hollow inside, big as a building. And one morning, the people of Troy woke up, looked out over their walls, and found two things. The horse — standing alone on the empty beach. And the Greek army — gone. The ships vanished over the horizon. The thousand campfires cold. After ten years, the enemy had simply left. And left behind this strange, beautiful gift.

The Trojans argued. Of course they did. A priest named Laocoön begged them not to trust it. I fear the Greeks, he said, even when they bring gifts. And then — in the legend — sea serpents rose from the water and dragged him and his sons under, and the Trojans took that as a sign from the gods that Laocoön was wrong. So they tore down part of their own wall to fit the horse through the gate. They wheeled it into the heart of the city. And they threw a party. Ten years of war, over. They drank. They danced. They fell asleep.

And in the dead of night, a hatch opened in the belly of the horse. And out climbed Odysseus and his best men, who had been hiding inside it the entire time. They opened the gates from within. The Greek fleet — which had only sailed just over the horizon and waited for the signal fire — came roaring back. And Troy, which had stood for ten years, was sacked and burned in a single night.

That's the story. It's three thousand years old. And here is the problem.

It might be fiction.
