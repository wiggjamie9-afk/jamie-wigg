You're listening to Wanderlust. Today, no single destination — instead, the question I get asked more than any other: How can you afford to travel that much? And the honest answer is, I can't. Not at full price. Nobody can. I just learned the tricks.

A few years ago I flew round-trip from London to Tokyo for the price of a nice dinner. Two hundred and forty pounds. The person sitting next to me, in the identical seat, paid over a thousand. Same plane, same lukewarm chicken, same arrival. The only difference between us was knowledge.

That's the secret nobody tells you about cheap travel. It is almost never about being rich. It's about being flexible and being informed. The cheapest travelers I know aren't the ones with the most money — they're the ones with the most patience and the fewest fixed plans.

I met a woman in a hostel in Lisbon who was sixty-three, retired on a small pension, and she'd been traveling continuously for four years on less than she used to spend living at home. Four years. She wasn't sleeping rough. She was house-sitting cats in Portugal, dog-sitting in New Zealand, staying for free in beautiful homes in exchange for feeding someone's pets. She'd cracked the code.

Number One: Flights — the biggest cost. Be flexible on dates and use a flexible-search tool where you search a whole month at once and see a calendar of prices. Tuesday and Wednesday departures are usually cheapest. Avoid Friday and Sunday. Set up price alerts on the routes you want — let the deal come to you while you sleep.

Number Two: The error fare. A few times a year an airline accidentally publishes a fare that's wildly wrong. There are free newsletters and apps whose entire job is to catch these. You won't pick the destination; the deal picks you. But if you can be flexible, this is the closest thing to a cheat code.

Number Three: Fly into the back door. Don't search flights to Italy. Search the cheapest flight to anywhere in Europe, then take a budget regional airline or a train the rest of the way. Getting to a major hub like London, Dublin, or Istanbul cheaply, then hopping onward, often beats a direct flight by hundreds.

Number Four: Where you sleep. Hostels aren't what you think anymore — most have private rooms now, often cheaper than a budget hotel. But the real money-saver is house-sitting and home exchange. You stay free, sometimes for weeks, in exchange for watching a home or a pet. Membership sites exist for this. The annual fee pays for itself in one stay.

Number Five: Cook one meal a day. You don't have to give up restaurants. But eating out three times a day is what destroys a travel budget. Hit a local market, make breakfast and a packed lunch, then splurge on one proper dinner out where the locals eat. You'll eat better and cheaper.

Number Six: Travel the country, not just the capital. Capital cities are where prices are highest. Spend two days in the famous city, then take a cheap regional bus an hour out, and your money doubles in value.

Number Seven: Shoulder season. Travel the few weeks just before or after peak. Same weather, often, but half-empty and a fraction of the price.

Number Eight: A money tip people forget. Use a debit or credit card with no foreign transaction fees, and always choose to pay in the local currency when the card machine asks. That pay in dollars prompt is a hidden five to ten percent fee called dynamic currency conversion. Say no every time.

Number Nine: Hand-luggage only. Budget airlines make most of their money on checked bags and seat selection. Learn to pack a week into a carry-on — it saves you a fortune in fees and a lifetime of waiting at baggage carousels.

Here's the mindset that ties all of this together. Cheap travel isn't about deprivation. It's about spending where it matters and saving where it doesn't. Nobody remembers the airline seat. Everybody remembers the meal, the view, the night they got lost and it turned into the best story.

So your homework this week: pick one route you've always wanted to fly, and set a price alert on it today. Just one. Then forget about it and let the deal find you.
