Welcome to fifteen minute kitchen. Tonight we are making spiced black bean tacos, and I am telling you now — this is one of those meals that sounds too easy to be real but absolutely delivers. Fifteen minutes, one pan, eight ingredients, and dinner is on the table.

Here is your grab list. One can of black beans, any size. A small onion. Two cloves of garlic. One teaspoon of cumin. One teaspoon of smoked paprika. Salt. Small corn or flour tortillas, six to eight of them. And whatever you like on top: shredded cheese, sour cream, avocado, a squeeze of lime, salsa, hot sauce — all optional, all welcome. Pull everything out now and we will get moving.

Step one: open and drain your can of black beans and set them aside. Then dice your onion small. Smash and roughly chop your garlic. The smaller the onion, the faster it softens, so go small if you have time.

Step two: a medium pan over medium heat, a good splash of oil, and your onion goes in. Stir it around and let it cook about four minutes. You are looking for it to soften and turn a little translucent at the edges. It will smell sweet and gentle. That is exactly what you want. Pause me here while you let that happen.

Step three: once the onion has softened, add your garlic, stir it for about thirty seconds until it smells great, then add your cumin and smoked paprika. Stir them into the oil for another thirty seconds — you are blooming the spices, which means warming them in fat to release all their flavour. Your kitchen is about to smell absolutely incredible.

Step four: your black beans go in now. Pour them straight from the strainer into the pan. Add a splash of water — about three tablespoons — and a big pinch of salt. Stir everything together and turn the heat down to medium-low. Let it simmer for about four minutes, stirring occasionally. You want the beans to absorb the spices and the liquid to reduce to a slightly saucy consistency, not soupy, not dry. If it gets too thick add a tiny splash more water.

While the beans are doing their thing, warm your tortillas. The fastest way is directly on the gas burner for about fifteen seconds per side until they get some char marks. If you have electric, wrap them in a damp paper towel and microwave for thirty seconds. Warm tortillas make an enormous difference.

Step five is assembly, which is the best part. Two tortillas per person. Big spoonful of the spiced beans. Then whatever toppings you like. A little crumble of cheese, a spoon of sour cream, some avocado, a serious squeeze of lime. The lime is non-negotiable — the acid wakes up the whole thing.

The tip today is about the spice bloom and it is genuinely important. When you add dried spices to oil and toast them for thirty seconds before the liquid goes in, they release compounds that would not come out in water. That thirty second step is the difference between tacos that taste homemade in a great way and tacos that taste like they came from a sad can. Never skip the bloom.

Quick, satisfying, and genuinely one of the cheapest dinners you can make. Full recipe in the show notes, plus three topping combinations that work brilliantly together.
