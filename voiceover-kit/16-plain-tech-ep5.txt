Hi, this is Kristen from Plain Tech. So glad you're here again.

Today we're tackling a phrase that gets thrown around constantly, and that I think confuses absolutely everybody. The cloud. Your photos are in the cloud. Save it to the cloud. It's all backed up to the cloud. And you nod along, but secretly you're picturing something floating in the sky, and you have no idea what's really going on.

So let me give you the plain truth, and it's wonderfully simple. The cloud is just somebody else's computer. That's it. When something is in the cloud, it means it's stored on a big, powerful computer in a building somewhere far away, instead of only on the phone or laptop in your hand.

Let me make it real. Imagine you take a photo on your phone. Without the cloud, that photo only lives in one place, on your phone. If you lose the phone, or it breaks, or it falls in the bath, that photo is gone forever. But with the cloud, the moment you take that photo, a copy quietly travels over the internet and gets saved on one of those big computers far away. So now it lives in two places. On your phone, and safely tucked away somewhere else.

Those big computers live in enormous buildings called data centres. Picture a giant warehouse, humming away, filled with thousands and thousands of computers, kept cool, kept running, looked after around the clock. Companies like the ones that make your phone own these buildings, and they rent out a little slice of space to you, often for free, sometimes for a small monthly fee.

So why should you, a normal busy person, care about any of this? Two big reasons, and they're both lovely.

The first is safety. Your precious things, your photos, your messages, your documents, are no longer trapped on one fragile device. If your phone is lost or stolen tomorrow, you buy a new one, sign in, and like magic, all your photos come flooding back. Because they were never only on the old phone. They were also safe in the cloud.

The second is reaching your things from anywhere. Because your photos live on that far-away computer, you can look at them from your phone, then from your tablet, then from a friend's computer. Sign in, and there they are. They follow you around, instead of being stuck on one gadget.

Now, two honest words of caution, because I never want to leave anything out. First, the cloud needs the internet. To send things up to it, or pull them back down, you need to be connected, usually on Wi-Fi to avoid using up your phone data. And second, just like anything online, you protect your cloud with a strong password, and ideally that second code sent to your phone we've talked about before. Because that account is the key to all your memories.

So, to sum up. The cloud is not magic and it's not in the sky. It's just a big computer in a far-away building, holding a safe copy of your important things, so they survive even if your phone doesn't, and so you can reach them from anywhere.

Your homework, if you want it. Find the photos app on your phone and just check. Is it backing up to the cloud? Usually there's a little setting that says back up, or sync. If it's on, lovely, your memories are safe. This has been Plain Tech. Take care of yourself, and I'll see you next time.
