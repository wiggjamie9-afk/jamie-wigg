Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making a quesadilla, but a real dinner quesadilla, crisp and golden on the outside, molten and loaded on the inside. It's the kind of thing that feels like a guilty takeaway treat but takes you fifteen minutes and one pan in your own kitchen. Grab a tortilla and let's go.

Here's the kit, gloriously simple. You'll want large tortillas, one per person plus a little extra. About two cups of grated cheese, something that melts well, cheddar or mozzarella. And a filling, which is where you get to play: leftover chicken, a drained half can of black beans, some sliced peppers, a handful of corn, half an onion, whatever needs using up. A little oil or butter for the pan. And to serve, sour cream, salsa, or just a squeeze of lime. That's everything.

Step one, prep the filling. Get everything sliced and ready before the pan goes on, because a quesadilla cooks fast. Slice half an onion thin, cut up any peppers, shred your leftover chicken, drain your beans. If you're using raw veg like onion or pepper, give them a quick fry in a hot pan with a little oil for about three minutes until they soften, then tip them onto a plate. Cooked fillings melt into the cheese beautifully, raw onion stays sharp and crunchy, so a quick fry is worth it.

Step two, build it flat. Wipe the pan, set it back over medium heat with the tiniest bit of oil or butter. Lay one large tortilla flat in the pan. Now here's the technique: scatter cheese over the whole thing first, right to the edges, then your fillings over one half only, then a little more cheese on top of the fillings to act as glue. The cheese on both sides is what holds the whole parcel together when you flip it.

Step three, fold and crisp. Once the bottom of the tortilla is going golden and the cheese is starting to melt, about two minutes, fold the empty half over the filled half with your spatula, pressing down gently so it sticks. You've now got a half-moon. Let it cook for another minute until the bottom is properly crisp and golden brown.

Step four, the flip. This is the only slightly nervy bit, and it's easy once you know the trick. Slide your spatula right under the folded quesadilla, hold the top edge steady with your other hand or a second spatula, and flip it confidently in one move. Confidence is the whole secret, a slow timid flip is what makes it fall apart. Cook the second side for another minute or two until it's golden and the cheese is fully molten inside.

Step five, rest and cut. Slide it onto a board and, this is important, let it sit for one minute before you cut it. If you cut straight away, all the molten cheese slides out and you're left with an empty shell. Let it settle for sixty seconds, then cut it into wedges like a pizza. Serve with sour cream, salsa, or a squeeze of lime for dunking.

Here's my one tip that makes or breaks it: medium heat, not high. People crank the heat to rush it and the tortilla burns black before the cheese inside has even thought about melting. Low and patient gives you that perfect golden, crackly outside and a fully melted middle. Two extra minutes, completely different dinner.

And that's it. A crisp, molten, loaded quesadilla in fifteen minutes, cut into wedges and ready to dunk. Use up your odds and ends, fold them into cheese, and crisp them golden. It's leftovers wearing a disguise, and it's wonderful.

The build order and the flip trick are written out in the show notes, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
