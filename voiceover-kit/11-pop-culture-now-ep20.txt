A show that would have run for five years on network television because of passionate fan investment does not necessarily survive the subscriber-count logic of a streaming platform. For the deep dive today, on episode twenty, I want to talk about what pop culture actually does for us. Not the gossip and not the drama, but the real function. Because I think there is a story we sometimes tell ourselves about entertainment, that it is shallow, that caring about it is a kind of frivolity, that there are more important things to spend your attention on. And I want to gently push back on that.

The stories we tell each other in the form of films and television and music and all the rest are how we figure out who we are and what we value. They are where we practise empathy, living inside someone else's experience for two hours, feeling things that belong to someone else, and coming out the other side a little different. They are where we find community, because nothing bonds a group of strangers faster than a shared experience of something they love. And they are how we process the things that are too big or too painful or too confusing to face directly, by finding them translated into story, given shape, made bearable.

That is not shallow. That is one of the most human things we do. So thank you for being here. Thank you for caring about this stuff. And let's keep talking about it.
