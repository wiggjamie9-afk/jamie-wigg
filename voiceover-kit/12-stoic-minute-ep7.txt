Welcome to Stoic Minute. I'm Harry.

Marcus Aurelius gives us today's line, and it is one of the most quietly radical things he ever wrote: The best revenge is not to be like your enemy.

Notice what he doesn't say. He doesn't say, get even. He doesn't say, win the argument or expose them or make them pay. He says the real victory is refusing to become what hurt you. When someone is cruel, the strongest temptation in the world is to answer cruelty with cruelty, to let their behaviour set the terms for yours. And the moment you do, they have changed you. They have, in a sense, won.

The Stoics believed your character was the one thing no one could take by force. Insult you, they might. Cheat you, perhaps. But they cannot make you petty, or bitter, or unjust. Only you can do that, by choosing to descend to their level. So Marcus reminds himself, again and again, that another person's bad behaviour is not a licence for his own.

There's a deeper mercy in this too. Marcus would tell himself that people do wrong because they don't know better, because they're confused about what's truly good. You can hold someone accountable without hating them. You can refuse what they did without becoming who they are.

So here is today's practice. The next time someone treats you poorly, pause before you respond, and ask one question. Who do I want to be in this moment, regardless of who they are choosing to be? Let your answer come from your own values, not from their provocation.

Remembering that you will die is, strangely, one of the most life-giving things you can do. It returns you to the one day you're actually holding: this one.
