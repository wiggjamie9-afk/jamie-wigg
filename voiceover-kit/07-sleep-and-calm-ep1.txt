Welcome. There is nothing you need to do now. Nowhere you need to be. You can let the day go.

Take a slow breath in… and let it fall away. And again. Slower this time.

Tonight, we are going to take a train ride. A slow one. Through quiet countryside, in the soft gold of late afternoon. You don't have to picture anything perfectly. Just let the words drift past, the way the fields will drift past the window.

You are sitting by a window, in a warm, half-empty carriage. The seat is soft beneath you. The light is honey-coloured. There is a gentle, steady rhythm underneath everything — the quiet rocking of the train as it moves. Da-dum. Da-dum. Slow. Even.

Outside, the countryside slides by without hurry. A wide green field, the grass moving softly in the wind. A line of old trees, their tops catching the last warm light. A small stone wall, mossy and low, following the train for a while, then falling away behind.

The window is cool against your hand. The carriage is warm. Somewhere far down the train, a quiet voice murmurs, too soft to make out. It doesn't matter what it says. You let it go.

A river appears beside the track. Slow water, the colour of pale tea. It curls past reeds and round smooth stones, in no hurry at all. The train follows it for a while. You watch the light moving on the surface. Soft. Gold. Endless.

The rhythm of the train settles into your body. Da-dum. Da-dum. Your shoulders are heavier now. They sink a little. Your hands rest, open and still.

A small station passes. Empty platform. A single hanging basket of flowers, swaying gently. A clock with hands that don't seem to move. The train doesn't stop. It carries you on, slowly, through the warm afternoon.

Now there are hills in the distance. Soft and rounded. Blue-green where they meet the sky. Sheep scattered across them like small white stones. They lift their heads as the train goes by, then lower them again, unhurried.

The light is beginning to turn. Deeper gold now. Long shadows lying across the fields. The carriage glows. Warm. Quiet. Safe.

You let your eyes grow heavy. The window is soft against your cheek. The rocking carries you. Da-dum. Da-dum.

There is nothing to do here but be carried. The train knows the way. You can rest. You can let go.

A field of tall wheat now, pale and gold, moving like slow water in the wind. A farmhouse with a thread of smoke rising straight into the still air. A dog, lying in the last patch of sun, lifting one ear, then settling again.

Everything out there is slowing down for the evening. And so are you.

The rhythm goes on, soft and even. Da-dum. Da-dum. Slower. Softer.

You don't need to follow the story any more. Just the rhythm. Just the warmth. Just the slow gold light, fading gently to dark.

Rest now. The train will carry you the rest of the way.
