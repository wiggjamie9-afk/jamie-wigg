The Balance Myth and What Actually Works. Welcome to Episode twenty five.

I want to say something a bit unpopular right at the start of this episode. Work-life balance, at least the way most people describe it, is not real. Or at least it is not a static thing you achieve and then maintain. It is more like balance on a bike than balance on a chair. You are always adjusting. The moment you stop adjusting you fall over.

Most dads I know tried to achieve balance and ended up feeling like they were failing at it constantly, which made them feel worse, which made them worse at everything. The goal of balance became the source of imbalance. Which is a fairly miserable outcome for something that was supposed to help.

So let me offer a different frame. Instead of balance, think about seasons.

There are seasons of your work life where things are genuinely intense. A project, a deadline, a launch, a crisis. During those seasons, being fully present with your kids every single evening is probably not possible and pretending otherwise just piles guilt on top of stress. What you can do in those seasons is be honest. Tell your kids, this week is a big one at work, I am going to be tired and a bit distracted, but on Saturday we are doing whatever you want, no phone, the whole day. And then do that.

The honesty matters as much as the plan. Kids who understand context behave differently than kids who just see a distant, stressed parent and do not know why. When you name what is happening, they can make sense of it. They can even be proud of you for working hard. But only if they know you are not just checked out.

Then there are seasons where the work is quieter. Use those. Do not fill them with more work out of habit or guilt. Fill them with the stuff you promised. The stuff you said you would do when things slowed down. Things slow down sometimes. Are you ready to actually use the space when they do?

The other thing I want to talk about is the guilt cycle, because most working dads I know are in one. You feel guilty at work for thinking about family. You feel guilty at home for thinking about work. You end up fully present nowhere and vaguely miserable everywhere. I lived in that cycle for years before I understood that it was not a productivity problem. It was a permission problem.

You need to give yourself permission to be fully in whichever place you are. When you are at work, the kids are fine. They are living their lives. You being mentally with them while you are at work does not help them and it costs you focus. When you are at home, the email is not going anywhere. It will still be there. You mentally staying at the office does not solve anything and it costs your family your attention.

Full presence, one place at a time. That is the actual skill. Not balance. Presence.

And finally, two practical things that made a real difference for me.

First: protect the rituals, not just the big moments. The dinnertime question. The bedtime check-in. The Sunday morning walk. These small recurring things are the connective tissue of your relationship with your kids. The big holidays and birthday memories are wonderful, but the daily rituals are what make kids feel consistently safe and seen.

Second: get your kids involved in your work, at least a little. Not the stress, but the story. Where you go. What you are trying to build. What a hard day felt like and what you did about it. Your kids can handle knowing that you have a real life and real challenges. And when they see you navigate difficulty with grace, that sticks with them forever.

Balance is not a destination. It is a practice. You will miss the mark regularly and that is fine. The practice is the point.
