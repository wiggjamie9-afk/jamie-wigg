Welcome to Stoic Minute. I am Harry.

Epictetus was born a slave. He walked with a limp for the rest of his life, the result of a cruel act by a former master. And from those conditions he became one of the most influential teachers of human freedom in the ancient world. The line that holds everything together in his philosophy is this: No man is free who is not master of himself.

Epictetus never owned much. He could not have. But he was insistent, with a fierceness that comes through even across two thousand years of translation, that the one thing no external force could reach was his will. His capacity to choose his own response. His character. That, he said, was always his.

We tend to think of freedom as something that comes from the outside. A society grants it. A contract guarantees it. Money buys a version of it. If circumstances are right, we feel free. If circumstances are wrong, we feel trapped. Epictetus spent his whole life dismantling this assumption. He had experienced its falseness from the inside. You can constrain a person's body completely and still not touch the most essential thing about them.

The inner citadel is what the Stoics called it. The self at the centre that remains yours regardless of what the world does. It can be abandoned through laziness or vice, through the slow erosion of bad habits, through choosing comfort over honesty year after year. But it cannot be taken. It can only be surrendered.

The practice this points toward is less dramatic than it sounds. Inner mastery is not about grand tests of resolve. It is mostly about small daily choices: whether to speak or hold your tongue, whether to react or pause, whether to give in to the easy path or take the slightly harder, more honest one. Each small choice is either a building of the inner citadel or a small crack in the wall.

Here is today's practice. Choose one moment today to exercise deliberate self-command over something trivial. An irritation you let pass without voicing. A distraction you set aside to finish the thing that matters. A reaction you choose rather than allow to happen automatically. The large freedoms are built from exactly these small ones.

No one outside you can make you free. But no one outside you can take that freedom either, if you choose to keep it.
