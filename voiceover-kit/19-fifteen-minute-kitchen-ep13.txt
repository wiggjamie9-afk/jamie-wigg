Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making crispy pan-fried gnocchi, and if you've only ever boiled those little potato dumplings into soft pillows, get ready, because today we're frying them straight from the packet until they're golden and crunchy outside and fluffy inside. Tossed with garlic, tomatoes, and basil, it's cozy and it's fast and it feels like a proper hug in a bowl. Fifteen minutes, one pan. Let's go.

Here's your kit. You'll want one packet of shelf-stable gnocchi, that's usually about five hundred grams, the kind from the dry-goods aisle, not frozen. A handful of cherry tomatoes, about two big handfuls. Three cloves of garlic, a knob of butter, a glug of olive oil, and a pinch of chili flakes. A handful of fresh basil or spinach, and some grated parmesan to finish. Salt and pepper. That's it. Let's cook.

Step one, here's the trick, no boiling. Forget the instructions on the packet that tell you to boil it. Set a wide frying pan over medium-high heat with a glug of olive oil and a knob of butter, about one tablespoon. While that heats, halve your handful of cherry tomatoes and thinly slice your three cloves of garlic.

Step two, crisp the gnocchi. Tip your gnocchi straight from the packet into the hot pan in one single layer. Now here's the important part: leave them alone. Let them sit for about three minutes without touching them so the bottoms turn deep golden and crispy. Then toss or stir, and let them go another two to three minutes until they're crisp and golden all over. They'll puff up a little and sound hollow and toasty. That crunchy crust is the entire reason we're doing it this way.

Step three, the aromatics. Push the gnocchi to one side and add your sliced garlic and a pinch of chili flakes to the bare patch of the pan with a tiny bit more oil if it looks dry. Stir for about thirty seconds, just until the garlic is fragrant and pale gold, never browned, or it turns bitter.

Step four, the tomatoes. Add your halved cherry tomatoes and a good pinch of salt. Toss everything together and let it cook for about two minutes. The tomatoes will start to slump and burst, releasing their juices, and those juices mix with the garlic butter to make a quick, glossy little sauce that coats every crispy dumpling. Press a few tomatoes with your spoon to help them along.

Step five, finish it. Take the pan off the heat and stir through a big handful of fresh basil or spinach, letting the residual heat wilt it down. Crack over plenty of black pepper. Spoon it into bowls and shower the top with grated parmesan so it melts into the warmth. Taste and add a final pinch of salt if it needs it.

Here's my one tip that changes everything: do not crowd the pan, and do not stir too soon. The whole magic of fried gnocchi is that golden, crispy crust, and you only get it if each dumpling is touching the hot pan and left undisturbed for those first few minutes. Pile them in too deep or fidget with them, and they steam soft and pale instead. If your pan is small, fry them in two batches, that extra minute is completely worth it.

And that's dinner. Crispy, golden gnocchi tumbled with burst tomatoes, garlic, and melting parmesan, the kind of bowl that disappears embarrassingly fast. Nobody will believe it took you fifteen minutes and didn't touch a pot of boiling water.

The no-boil method and the don't-crowd rule are in the show notes, print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
