Everyone online wants to talk about red flags. The warning signs, the dealbreakers, the moment you should run. And those matter, we'll get to them another day. But today I want to flip the whole thing over, because nobody ever taught us the other side. Nobody sat us down and said: here is what a genuinely good person looks like up close. So that's our episode. Green flags. The quiet, unglamorous, deeply reassuring signs that you might have found someone worth keeping.

Hi, and welcome back to Dating Decoded. I'm Julia, and I'm so glad you're here. Last time we untangled the talking stage, and I promised you we'd talk about the good signs next. So let's do it. Why green flags are easy to miss, what they actually look like in real life, and why the most boring qualities are often the most important ones of all.

Here's the first thing to understand. Green flags rarely sparkle. Red flags are dramatic. They grab you. But green flags are calm. They feel almost like nothing, which is exactly why we overlook them. We've been trained by stories and screens to associate love with intensity, with the chase, with the will they won't they. So when someone is simply steady, simply kind, simply there, our nervous system sometimes reads it as a lack of spark. I want you to gently question that. Sometimes what feels like no spark is actually the absence of anxiety. And that, my friend, is the whole point.

So let's name some real ones. The first green flag is consistency. They are the same person on Tuesday as they were on Saturday. Their warmth doesn't run hot and cold. You're not decoding their mood every time your phone lights up. When someone is consistent, your shoulders drop. You stop bracing. That feeling of not having to brace is one of the truest signs you're somewhere safe.

The second is that they're genuinely curious about you. Not just waiting for their turn to talk, but actually asking, remembering, following up. They recall the name of your difficult coworker. They ask how the thing you were nervous about went. Curiosity is love in its everyday clothes. It says, your inner world matters to me, and I want to keep learning it.

The third green flag is how they handle a small disagreement. You learn almost nothing about a person when everything is going well. You learn everything about them in the first little conflict. Do they get curious or defensive? Do they try to understand you, or just to win? A good person can hear that they've upset you without falling apart or turning it back on you. They can say, I see that hurt you, I'm sorry, let's figure it out. Repair is a skill, and watching someone repair gently is one of the greenest flags there is.

The fourth is respect for your no. Watch how someone responds the first time you set a small boundary. You can't make it tonight. You'd rather not talk about a certain topic yet. You need to head home. A good person hears your no and simply adjusts, without sulking, without guilt-tripping, without making you pay for it later. Someone who respects your small noes is showing you they'll respect the big ones too.

The fifth, and I love this one, is that they have a full life of their own. Friends they show up for. Things they care about. A world that existed before you and continues alongside you. This matters for two reasons. It means they're not looking for you to be their entire universe, which is a heavy thing to be. And it means that when they choose to spend time with you, it's a genuine choice, not just a way to fill an emptiness.

Now let me add the quietest green flag of all, the one almost nobody mentions. Notice how you feel about yourself when you're around them. Not just how you feel about them, but who you become in their presence. Do you feel more like yourself, or less? More relaxed, or more on edge? The right person tends to make you feel safe enough to be unimpressive. You can be tired, or wrong, or unsure, and the connection doesn't wobble. If you find yourself performing, shrinking, or carefully managing how you come across, pay attention to that. The best green flag isn't something they do. It's how much of yourself you get to keep.

So here's what I want you to take from today. Stop waiting only for things to go wrong, and start noticing what's quietly going right. Steadiness is not boring. Kindness is not a lack of passion. And the absence of anxiety is not the absence of love. Sometimes it's the very beginning of it.
