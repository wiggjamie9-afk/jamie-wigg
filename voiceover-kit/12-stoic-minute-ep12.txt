Welcome to Stoic Minute. I'm Harry.

Epictetus gives us a hard and useful truth today: No man is free who is not master of himself.

We tend to picture freedom as the absence of external chains. No boss, no obligations, no one telling us what to do. But Epictetus, who was born a slave and knew real chains, turned that idea inside out. He had seen men with every outward liberty who were utterly enslaved, ruled by their tempers, their appetites, their fear of what others thought. And he had known captive men whose minds no master could touch.

The freedom the Stoics cared about was internal. It was the gap between an impulse and an action, and whether you governed that gap or it governed you. The man who cannot resist the second drink, who cannot hold his tongue, who cannot sit with a craving without obeying it, is not free, however open his front door may be. He is being led around by parts of himself he has never learned to command.

This is not about harsh self-denial. The Stoics were not puritans. It is about ownership. To be master of yourself is simply to be the one deciding, rather than the appetite or the mood doing the deciding for you. And that mastery is built the way any strength is built. Through small, repeated reps of choosing the harder, truer thing.

So here is today's practice. Notice one impulse today and deliberately do not obey it. The urge to check the phone, to snap back, to reach for the easy comfort. Let it rise, and let it pass without acting. Feel, in that small refusal, what Epictetus meant by freedom.

Remembering that you will die is, strangely, one of the most life-giving things you can do. It returns you to the one day you're actually holding: this one.
