# PODCAST #07: SLEEP & CALM — Episode 12
**Voice to use:** Sarah or Charlotte (Soft, slow, soothing)
**Episode 1 voice used:** [Check your EP1 recording]

---

Hello, I'm Mark Coggins, and this is Sleep and Calm. Let the day settle behind you now. You've done enough. There is nothing left to carry. Just be warm, and still, and quiet.

Take a slow breath in, and let it fall away. And again. Let this next breath be a little slower than the last.

Tonight we are going to a quiet old library, late at night, when everything has grown still. You don't have to picture every detail. Just let the calm of the place settle over you.

You are in a deep armchair, in the corner of a grand old library. It is very late, and you are the only one here. The lamps are low and golden. The whole room is soft with warm, sleepy light.

All around you, the shelves rise high into the shadows, full of old books. Their spines are dark green and faded red and worn gold. The air smells of paper and warm wood. It is the kind of quiet you can sink into.

The chair is deep and soft. A blanket lies across your lap, heavy and kind. Your feet rest on a low stool. You are held on every side by warmth and quiet.

There is no sound but the faint settling of the old building. A soft creak, far off. The gentle tick of a clock you cannot see. A page, somewhere, turning slowly in the still air. Small, soft sounds, drifting in the quiet.

The lamplight pools gold on the table beside you. It touches the edges of the books, the curve of the chair, the soft folds of the blanket. Everything beyond the light fades into a calm and gentle dark.

There is nothing here you need to read. Nothing you need to find. The library asks nothing of you tonight. It simply holds you, warm and still, and that is exactly enough.

Your shoulders grow heavy and sink into the chair. Your jaw softens. Your hands rest, open and still. Your breathing slows in the deep quiet of the room.

The old clock ticks, soft and slow. The lamps glow low and steady. The high dark shelves keep their silent watch, and the whole still library seems to hold its breath, so you can rest.

You don't need to stay awake for any of it. The quiet will keep, soft and gentle, all night long, whether you follow it or not.

There is nothing to do. Nowhere to be. Just rest now, all the way down, into the warm and the quiet and the soft gold light.

Rest now. Sleep well. I'll see you again, when you're ready.
