Welcome to Stoic Minute. I'm Harry.

Today we begin with a line from Marcus Aurelius, written to himself in the cold hours of a military campaign: When you arise in the morning, think of what a precious privilege it is to be alive, to breathe, to think, to enjoy, to love.

It sounds gentle, almost sentimental, until you remember who wrote it. A man carrying the weight of an empire, surrounded by war and plague, reminding himself that simply waking up is a gift he did nothing to deserve.

We rarely feel this. The alarm goes off and the mind leaps straight to the list. What's owed, what's late, what went wrong. The first emotion of the day is often dread, before anything has even happened. Marcus offers a different opening move. Before the list, one breath of gratitude. Not for your circumstances, which may be hard, but for the bare fact of being here at all to face them.

This isn't about pretending everything is fine. The Stoics were not in the business of pretending. It's about noticing what is already true and usually ignored. You are breathing. You can think. You can love the people in your house. None of that was guaranteed to you, and one day none of it will be yours.

So here is the practice for today. Before your feet touch the floor, name three ordinary things that are quietly, easily good. Warmth. A working body. A person you'd miss. Let the day begin from there, rather than from the list.

Remembering that you will die is, strangely, one of the most life-giving things you can do. It returns you to the one day you're actually holding: this one.
