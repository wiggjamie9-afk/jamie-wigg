Good morning. This is The Stoic Minute. A short, steady moment before the day picks up speed. Today, one of the oldest and most useful ideas the Stoics left us. Epictetus opens his Handbook — the Enchiridion — with it. He writes: "Some things are within our power, and some things are not. Within our power are our own opinions, desires, and actions. Not within our power are our bodies, our reputations, what other people do." That's the whole hinge of Stoic practice. The dichotomy of control. Some things answer to you. Most things don't. Notice what Epictetus puts in your column: your judgments, your choices, your effort. And notice what he leaves out: the weather, the traffic, the email you're dreading, the opinion someone three desks over has of you. None of that is yours to command. You can influence some of it. You can't control it. Here's the ordinary version. You send a careful message — to a colleague, a friend, someone you care about. Then you wait. And the reply doesn't come. An hour. A morning. A day. You can feel the mind start to spin. Did I say the wrong thing? Are they angry? Should I send another? The Stoic move is quiet and almost boring: the message was yours. You wrote it with care — that part was in your power, and you did it well. The reply is not yours. It was never going to be. Their silence belongs to them, to their day, to a hundred things you can't see. So you set it down. Not coldly. Just honestly. You did your part. The rest isn't yours to carry. Reflection for today. Sometime this morning you'll feel that familiar tightening — the urge to control an outcome that was never in your hands. When you feel it, try asking one plain question: Is this mine, or not mine? If it's yours — your effort, your attention, your response — give it everything. If it's not yours, loosen your grip. You'll find you were holding the wrong end of the rope. The freedom Epictetus is offering isn't the freedom to control the world. It's the freedom from needing to. That's The Stoic Minute. Spend well the part of the day that is yours. I'll see you tomorrow.
