Welcome to Sleep and Calm. Take a slow breath and settle in.

Tonight we are talking about something that touches all of us at certain points in the year — the way our sleep shifts with the seasons.

As the days grow longer in spring and summer, many people find it harder to fall asleep at their usual time. The light stays in the sky long after the day has technically ended, and the body responds to that light by holding off on the release of melatonin — the hormone that signals to your brain that night is here. What this means, in practical terms, is that in summer your body may not feel sleepy until quite late, even if your alarm is set for the same time it always was.

In autumn and winter, the opposite happens. The days shorten, darkness comes earlier, and melatonin rises sooner. Many people feel drowsy by early evening. They sleep more deeply, and often more. This is not laziness or depression. It is biology responding to the world around you, the same way it has for thousands of years.

Understanding this rhythm can bring a kind of relief. You are not broken when your sleep changes with the year. You are seasonal, just like everything else in nature.

There are some things you can do to work with your season rather than against it.

In summer, when light lingers, treat your bedroom as a place of deliberate darkness. Heavy curtains or a sleep mask can make a real difference. Avoid bright screens in the hour before bed, and consider dimming your home lights as the evening progresses. These small acts help your body understand that night is arriving even when the sky disagrees.

In winter, when darkness comes early and the urge to retreat is strong, give yourself permission to sleep a little more. Your need for rest may genuinely be greater in these months. At the same time, seek out morning light. Even ten minutes of daylight early in the day helps anchor your circadian rhythm and keeps mood and energy more balanced through the dark season.

And in the weeks when the clocks change — in either direction — be patient with yourself. Your body takes time to adjust. Shift your bedtime gradually, by fifteen minutes or so each day, rather than trying to leap all at once. Give your sleep system a few days to recalibrate before you judge how you are feeling.

You are a creature of the seasons. Your sleep is too.

Let yourself follow the light, and rest when the dark arrives.

Sleep well.
