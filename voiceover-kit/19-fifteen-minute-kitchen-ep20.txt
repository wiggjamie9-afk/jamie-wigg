Welcome to fifteen minute kitchen. Today we are cooking on a budget, and I want to challenge something first. Cheap food does not have to mean boring food. Some of the most satisfying, delicious meals in the world are built from the least expensive ingredients — beans, eggs, lentils, tinned tomatoes, pasta, rice, cabbage. The trick is knowing how to make humble ingredients taste great, and that is entirely about technique, not price tag.

Today I am walking you through a lentil and tomato soup that serves four people for probably two dollars a serve, takes about fifteen minutes of actual hands-on work, and tastes like something that has been slowly simmering all day. It has not. You can cook this tonight.

Grab list: one cup of red lentils, one can of crushed tomatoes, one onion, four cloves of garlic, one teaspoon of cumin, one teaspoon of turmeric, half a teaspoon of chili flakes if you like heat, salt, olive oil, and about three cups of vegetable or chicken stock, or water with a stock cube. That is everything. Red lentils are key here — they dissolve as they cook and thicken the soup naturally, no blending required.

Step one: dice your onion small. Finely chop your garlic. Into a pot over medium heat with a generous glug of olive oil — about two tablespoons. Add the onion and stir it around. Cook it for about four minutes until it softens and starts to look a little glossy. You want softness, not colour. Pause me and let your onion get happy.

Step two: garlic goes in now for thirty seconds, then your cumin, turmeric, and chili flakes. Stir them into the oil and let them sizzle for thirty more seconds. The smell at this point should be deep and warm and a little smoky. That is the bloom working.

Step three: pour in your crushed tomatoes and stir to combine with the spiced onion base. Let it bubble for about one minute so the tomato cooks into the oil slightly and loses its raw edge.

Step four: add your red lentils and your stock or water. Stir everything together. Bring it to a boil, then turn it down to a steady simmer. Lid on, but slightly cracked. Cook for about ten to twelve minutes, stirring every couple of minutes. You are watching for the lentils to break down and dissolve into the broth — the soup will go from watery and grainy to thick and velvety. When it looks like a creamy thick soup, taste it for salt and add more if it needs it.

That is it. Serve it with a drizzle of olive oil on top, some crusty bread or a flatbread for dipping, and a squeeze of lemon if you have one. The lemon is not optional in my kitchen — it completely lifts the whole bowl.

The budget tip for this week: buy dried lentils and dried beans in bulk. A bag of red lentils costs almost nothing, lasts months in a sealed jar, and can become soup, a curry, a side dish, or a dip. Dried legumes are the single best-value ingredient you can keep in your pantry. Stock up once and cook cheaply for weeks.
