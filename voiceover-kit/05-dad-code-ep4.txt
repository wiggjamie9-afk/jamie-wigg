# PODCAST #05: DAD CODE — Episode 4
**Voice to use:** Brian or Bill (Warm, casual male)
**Episode 1 voice used:** Michael

Hey everyone, Michael Scott here, and welcome back to Dad Code. Last week we went somewhere heavy, all that stuff about discipline and repair. So today, on purpose, we're going lighter. We're talking about screens. Specifically, the war over screen time, and why I think most of us are fighting it from completely the wrong trench.

Let me set the scene. It's a Sunday morning. I'm tired. The kids are quiet. And the reason they're quiet is that they have been welded to a tablet for what I can only describe as a geological age. And there's this little voice in the back of my head going, you're failing, this is rotting their brains, real dads are outside building a treehouse right now. You know that voice. That voice is loud.

So here's the first thing I had to get straight. The guilt is not the same as the problem. I was carrying around this enormous cloud of shame about screens, and the shame was actually stopping me from thinking clearly about what was really going on. Because when you're drowning in guilt, you make panicky rules. You snatch the tablet. You announce a total ban at dinner that lasts about ninety minutes before you cave. None of it works, and everyone feels worse.

So let's get clear. Not all screen time is the same thing. An hour of a kid building something in a game, talking to their cousin on a video call, watching a documentary about deep sea fish they then talk about for three days, that is not the same as two hours of an algorithm firing tiny dopamine hits at a glazed-over child who can't hear you anymore. Same number of minutes. Completely different food. We count the minutes because minutes are easy to count. But minutes are the wrong unit.

So in our house we stopped asking how long and started asking what kind. Is this thing they're doing creating something, connecting them to a person, or teaching them something? Or is it just feeding them? Both can exist. But you should know which one is happening. That one question took more weight off my shoulders than any timer ever did.

Now, the actionable part, because I know that's why you're here. Three things that genuinely helped us.

One. We made screens a place you go, not a thing you carry. The tablet lives in the front room, in the open, never in a bedroom, never at the table. When it's a location, you naturally leave it. When it's in your pocket, it never ends. That one move did more than any rule about minutes.

Two. We name the transition before it happens. The hardest part of screens is the coming off. The meltdown when it ends. So now, instead of yanking it away, I give a heads up. Two more minutes, then we're done, and here's what we're doing next. There's always a next. You're not taking something away into a void. You're moving them toward the next thing. Build the bridge before you ask them to cross it.

Three, and this is the one I dodge the most. I looked at my own screen first. It is genuinely insane to ask a four-year-old to put down a tablet while you are scrolling your phone at the dinner table like it's an oxygen supply. They are watching us. They learn what a phone means by watching what it means to us. So before I police their screens, I had to get honest about mine. And that one stung.

Here's what I want to leave you with. Your kid is not going to be ruined by a tablet on a tired Sunday. Relax about that. But the deeper thing, the thing worth protecting, is teaching them that they are in charge of the device, and the device is not in charge of them. That's a skill they will need for the rest of their lives, in a world that is going to get a thousand times more tempting than a cartoon.

So don't fight the minutes. Win the relationship. Teach them how to walk away from a screen, by walking away from yours.

Next week we're talking about money. About how to raise a kid who isn't weird about it, when half of us never got taught a single useful thing about it ourselves. That should be a good one.

This has been Dad Code. Now go and put your own phone down for ten minutes. Lead from the front.
