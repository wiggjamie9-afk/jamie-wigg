Welcome to fifteen minute kitchen. Today we are talking about zero-waste cooking, which sounds like a sustainability lecture but I promise it is not. It is actually just about being a smarter, more creative cook who spends less money on groceries and throws away almost nothing. The concept is simple: every part of the ingredients you buy has value, and once you know how to use those parts, the idea of throwing away food starts to feel genuinely strange.

We are going to make dinner tonight from three things that most people throw in the bin — the stalks of herbs, the outer leaves of cabbage, and the rind from a block of parmesan. And we are making a rustic Italian-style broth that becomes the base for a simple vegetable and bean soup.

Here is your grab list. One can of cannellini beans, drained and rinsed. Two stalks of celery, including any leaves attached. One carrot. One onion. As many herb stalks as you have — parsley stalks, thyme sprigs, bay leaves. A parmesan rind if you have one in the fridge — if not, just a tablespoon of grated parmesan to finish. Three cloves of garlic. Olive oil. Salt. A litre of water or any light stock you have. Bread for serving.

The magic item in this soup is the parmesan rind. A lot of people keep the rind after finishing a block of parmesan — it keeps for months in the fridge or indefinitely in the freezer. When you simmer it in liquid, it releases a deep, savoury, umami richness that is genuinely remarkable from something that was headed for the bin. If you have never tried this, tonight is the night.

Start by dicing the onion, carrot, and celery roughly — no need to be precise, they are going to cook down. Into a pot over medium heat, a generous splash of olive oil. Add the onion, carrot, and celery with a pinch of salt. Cook for about four minutes until they start to soften and become fragrant. Add the garlic, sliced or roughly chopped, and cook for another minute.

Now pour in your litre of water or stock. Add the herb stalks, tied together if possible or just dropped in loose. Add the parmesan rind. Bring it to a boil, then reduce to a steady simmer. Let it go for eight minutes — and in that eight minutes, something really happens. The rind softens, the herbs infuse, the vegetables give up their sweetness.

Fish out the herb stalks and the rind — the rind will be soft and swollen by now. Add the drained beans and stir them through. Taste the broth and season with salt until it sings. Cook for another two minutes for the beans to warm through.

Ladle into bowls, finish with a drizzle of your best olive oil and some grated parmesan, and eat with good crusty bread.

The broader lesson here is what chefs call mise en place thinking applied to waste. Herb stalks have the same flavour as the leaves, sometimes more concentrated. Parmesan rinds are flavour bombs. Outer cabbage leaves, celery leaves, carrot tops — all of these can go into stocks, soups, and braises rather than the compost. Start keeping a bag in the freezer for scraps and you will always have the start of a great stock.
