Welcome to Sleep and Calm. Close your eyes when you are ready.

Tonight we are talking about stress — not the kind that arrives in a single dramatic moment, but the kind that accumulates slowly across a day. The kind that lives in the shoulders, the jaw, the chest. The kind that does not announce itself but simply settles in and waits.

By the time you reach your bed, you may be carrying the weight of dozens of small stresses. An email that stung a little. A task left undone. A difficult interaction. A worry about money, about health, about someone you love. None of it catastrophic. All of it heavy.

The body holds stress in its muscles long after the mind has moved on. This is why so many people feel wired at the end of the day even when they are exhausted. The nervous system is still running warm. The muscles are still braced.

So tonight, let us release it — physically, methodically, gently.

Start with your hands. Curl them into soft fists and then slowly open them, spreading your fingers wide. Feel the release. Now let them rest, loose and open on the bed beside you.

Move your attention to your forearms and upper arms. Imagine any tension there melting downward, draining out through your fingertips and disappearing.

Bring your attention to your shoulders. So many of us carry the whole day in our shoulders. Take a breath in, and as you exhale, let them drop just a little further than they already are. Not forced — just invited.

Your neck. Your jaw. Unclench the jaw now. Allow a small space to open between your upper and lower teeth. Let your tongue rest gently at the bottom of your mouth.

Your forehead. Smooth it. If you have been frowning today — and you probably have, whether you knew it or not — allow that to release. Feel the space between your eyebrows widen.

Your chest. As you breathe in, imagine the ribs expanding in all directions — not just forward but sideways, and gently into the mattress behind you. Let each breath create a little more space.

Your belly. Your hips. Your lower back. Let the mattress receive your full weight. You do not need to hold yourself up. You are supported. You are held.

Your legs. Your calves. The long muscles along the shins. The ankles. The feet.

All of it soft now. All of it heavy. All of it released.

Stress leaves the body through breath and stillness and permission. And you have given yourself all three tonight.

Whatever the day asked of you, you gave it. Now you can give yourself this: complete and total rest.

Sleep well.
