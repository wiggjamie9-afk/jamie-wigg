First, let's name what a breakup actually is, because we tend to underestimate it. It is a real loss, and your body treats it like one. You're not just losing a person. You're losing a future you'd imagined, a daily rhythm, a name that used to mean home, a version of yourself that existed inside that relationship. That's a lot. So please stop asking why you're not over it yet. There's no schedule. The grief is proportional to the love, and you don't get to fast-forward through it just because it's inconvenient.

So here's my first piece of guidance, and it's the hardest one. Let yourself actually feel it. Our instinct is to escape the pain as fast as possible, to numb it, to bury it in distraction or someone new. But pain that's avoided doesn't leave. It just waits, and it compounds, and it shows up later, often in the next relationship. The only way past it is through it. So let yourself cry. Let yourself miss them. Feel the wave when it comes, and trust that no wave lasts forever. Every single one crests and falls.

Second, give yourself distance, and be honest about contact. I know the pull to keep checking their profile, to keep the conversation going, to stay just close enough that it doesn't feel final. But you cannot heal a wound you keep reopening. Watching someone's every move online is just choosing to bleed slowly. For a while, you may need real space. Mute them, unfollow if you have to, put the phone in another room. This isn't bitterness. It's basic first aid. You can't begin to miss the closeness less while you're still feeding on it daily.

Third, resist the urge to rewrite the whole story, in either direction. When we're hurting, we do one of two things. We turn them into a villain so we can hate our way out, or we turn them into the one that got away and ache for them endlessly. The truth is almost always in the middle. It was a real connection with real good in it, and it also wasn't right, or wasn't lasting. Both things are allowed to be true. You don't have to hate them to be glad it's over, and you don't have to pretend it was perfect to honor that you loved them.

Fourth, and this is where the healing really lives, come back to yourself. Relationships have a way of slowly merging two lives, and a breakup, for all its pain, hands you back something precious. Your own time, your own space, your own self. So start small. Reconnect with the friends you may have drifted from. Return to the thing you loved that fell away. Rebuild a life that's fully yours, one ordinary day at a time. Not to prove anything to them, but to remember that you were a whole person before them and you are a whole person now.

And let me say something about the new-person temptation, because it's strong. A rebound can feel like a cure, a way to feel wanted again, to drown out the silence. But if you reach for someone new mainly to avoid feeling the loss, you're not actually moving on. You're just outsourcing the work, and usually you bring the unhealed parts straight into the new thing. Heal first, as much as you can. The next person deserves a version of you that's chosen them, not one that's running from someone else.

Here's the truth I most want you to hold onto. The intensity you feel right now is not a sign that you'll feel this way forever. It's a sign of how deeply you can love, and that capacity is not damaged. It's intact, and it's waiting. One day, and you won't notice the exact moment it happens, you'll realize a whole afternoon went by without the ache. Then a day. Then it becomes a memory you can hold without flinching. You will get there. Not by forcing it, but by living your way forward.

So be patient with yourself. Feel it, create distance, tell the honest story, and come home to your own life. You are not behind. You are healing, and healing has its own time.
