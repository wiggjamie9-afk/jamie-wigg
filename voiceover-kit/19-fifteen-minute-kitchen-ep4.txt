Hey, I'm Kel, and this is Fifteen Minute Kitchen. Today we are making tacos. Quick, smoky, weeknight tacos that come together faster than the delivery driver could even find your street. Fifteen minutes, start to plate, I promise. Grab a pan and let's go.

Here's your shopping list, and keep it loose. You'll want about four hundred grams of ground beef or ground turkey, or a drained can of black beans if you're going meat-free. You'll want one small onion, two cloves of garlic, and a handful of spice. Eight small tortillas, corn or flour, whatever you love. And then the toppings, which are the fun part: grated cheese, shredded lettuce or cabbage, a tomato, maybe some sour cream, and a lime. That's it. Let's cook.

Step one, get the pan hot. Set a wide frying pan over medium-high heat and give it a full minute to heat up. While that's warming, finely chop your one small onion and mince your two cloves of garlic. We're working as we go today, because fifteen minutes is fifteen minutes.

Step two, brown the meat. Add a splash of oil, then tip in your four hundred grams of meat. Break it up with a spoon and spread it out flat. Now leave it alone for a good two minutes, let it catch some color on the bottom, that's where the flavor lives. Then stir, add your onion, and cook it all together for another three minutes until the meat is browned through and the onion has softened.

Step three, the spice. Push the meat to one side, drop your garlic into the bare patch of pan for ten seconds until it smells amazing, then stir it in. Now season. About one teaspoon of cumin, one teaspoon of smoked paprika, half a teaspoon of chili powder, and a good pinch of salt. If you have a packet of taco seasoning, just use that instead, no shame at all. Pour in a small splash of water, about four tablespoons, and let it bubble for one minute so it turns saucy and clings to the meat instead of staying dry and crumbly.

Step four, warm the tortillas. While the meat finishes, warm your eight tortillas. Easiest way: lay them straight onto a dry hot pan or an open gas flame for about ten seconds a side until they puff and get a few toasty spots. Stack them on a plate under a clean tea towel to stay soft and warm. Warm tortillas are the difference between a sad taco and a great one, so don't skip this.

Step five, build and finish. Chop your tomato, shred your lettuce, grate your cheese if it isn't already. Now build: a spoon of that smoky meat into each tortilla, then cheese while it's hot so it melts a little, then the cool, crunchy stuff on top. Finish every single one with a squeeze of fresh lime. That bright little hit of lime at the end is the secret that makes them taste like a proper taco truck.

Here's my one tip that fixes everything: season the meat, then taste it before you build. A pinch more salt or one more squeeze of lime can take it from fine to incredible, and you'll never know unless you taste. You are the cook. Trust your own mouth.

And that's dinner. Smoky tacos, fifteen minutes, almost nothing to wash up. Pile them high, eat them with your hands, and don't be precious about the drips. That's how you know they're good.

I've popped the spice mix and the build order in the show notes so you can print it and stick it on the fridge. Until next time, keep it simple, keep it fun, and I'll see you in the kitchen.
