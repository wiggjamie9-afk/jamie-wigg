Welcome back to Side Hustle School. Yesterday we built your personal brand. Today we talk about one of the highest-leverage income models available to someone with genuine expertise and a day job — consulting.

Consulting is not coaching. Coaching is about helping someone develop their own capability. Consulting is about being hired to solve a specific problem with your expertise, and in many cases, doing the actual work of solving it. Companies hire consultants because they need a result faster than they can develop the internal knowledge to produce it. That urgency, and that knowledge gap, is what you get paid for.

Here is why consulting is one of the most powerful side hustle models for people who are already working professionals. Everything you have learned in your day job — the systems, the processes, the industry knowledge, the things that took you years to understand — all of that is worth money to a smaller business or a startup that has not built that expertise yet. You are not starting from scratch. You are monetising what you already know.

Let me walk you through how a consulting side hustle actually gets started.

Step one: define the specific problem you solve. Not a vague expertise. A specific problem. Not I help companies with marketing. I help early-stage SaaS companies build their first content strategy before they hire a full-time marketing person. Not I know a lot about supply chain. I help small manufacturers reduce lead times on imported components by mapping and renegotiating their supplier relationships. The more specific the problem, the easier it is for the right client to recognize you as the solution.

Step two: identify who has this problem right now. Think about the types of companies or individuals who are actively struggling with exactly the problem you solve. Early-stage startups. Businesses going through a particular transition. Companies entering a new market. Founders doing their first hire. The client is always someone with a problem that is urgent enough to pay for outside help.

Step three: start with your existing network. The fastest path to your first consulting client is through someone who already trusts you. Former colleagues, industry contacts, people you have worked alongside — these are your first conversations. You are not selling. You are asking. Something like: I am taking on a small number of consulting engagements to help companies with this specific problem. Do you know anyone who might be dealing with that right now? One conversation leads to a referral leads to a client.

Step four: price by the project, not by the hour. This is the single biggest mindset shift that separates consultants who earn well from those who underprice themselves. When you charge by the hour, you are capping your income and encouraging clients to monitor your time. When you charge by the project or by the outcome, you are pricing the value of the result, not the cost of your hours. A one-month engagement that solves a problem worth fifty thousand dollars to the client is worth five to ten thousand dollars to you, regardless of how many hours it takes.

Step five: structure the engagement clearly. Before any work begins, you need a short written scope of work. What you will deliver. What the client is responsible for. How long it will take. What it costs. Payment schedule. This protects both parties and signals that you are a professional, not a favor-doer. A simple one-page document is all you need to start.

Here is what the income looks like when you get this right. A single consulting engagement at three thousand to eight thousand dollars is realistic for someone with genuine mid-level expertise. Two or three of those a year as a side project, while keeping your day job, is meaningful additional income. As your reputation builds through that personal brand we talked about yesterday, you will find that inbound inquiries start to arrive — and you can be selective about the projects you take.

Your assignment this week: write a one-paragraph description of the specific problem you could consult on. Then list five people in your network who might know someone dealing with that problem. Send one message this week — not a pitch, a question. Ask if they know anyone who might benefit from what you do.

These are projections, not promises. Consulting income can be significant but it is not automatic. It is built on trust, reputation, and the courage to articulate your expertise as something worth paying for.
