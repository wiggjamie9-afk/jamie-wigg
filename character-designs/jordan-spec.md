# JORDAN Character Design Specification

## Overview
**JORDAN** is a middle-school student character representing anxiety and autism spectrum experience in an educational/narrative context. The design should convey thoughtfulness, hidden capability, and authentic neurodivergent representation.

---

## Core Character Profile

### Physical Description
- **Age:** Middle school (11–14 years)
- **Ethnicity & Skin Tone:** Medium skin tone with warm undertones
- **Gender Expression:** Open interpretation (character design works for any gender)
- **Build:** Age-appropriate, neither stereotypically athletic nor fragile

### Facial Features
- **Eyes:** Large, expressive, slightly worried or deeply thoughtful. Eyes convey intelligence and internal intensity. Slightly wider-set, suggesting an observant personality
- **Eyebrows:** Naturally shaped, capable of furrowing to show worry/concentration
- **Nose:** Simple, warm, age-appropriate proportions
- **Mouth:** Versatile for multiple expressions—worry, focus, slight smile, neutral
- **Hair:** Distinctly styled with personality. Options:
  - Slightly wavy or textured (natural texture)
  - Asymmetrical cut suggesting a thoughtful style choice
  - Shoulder-length or medium, with volume
  - Color: Dark (brown or black) with subtle depth
  - Styling conveys calm care, not perfectly neat (authentic to neurodivergent aesthetics)

---

## Color Palette

### Primary Colors (suggest introspection, calm, stability)
- **Dominant Blue:** Cool, calming blue (`#4A90A4` or similar)—suggests meditation, thoughtfulness
- **Alternative Purple:** Soft, introspective purple (`#7B5BA6` or similar)—suggests creativity and depth
- Choose one for main clothing/color identity

### Secondary Colors (grounding earth tones)
- **Warm Earth Accent:** Rust, warm brown, or sage green (`#B8956A`, `#8B7355`, `#A8B5A8`)
- **Usage:** Trim, accessory, or complementary piece

### Neutral/Contrast
- **Black:** Clean outlines and definition
- **White:** Background, highlights

---

## Clothing & Style

### Primary Outfit (center figure)
- **Hoodie or Comfortable Jacket** in primary color (blue or purple)
- **Simple shirt or long-sleeve underneath**
- **Casual pants** (jeans, soft joggers, or neutral colors)
- **Optional accessory:** Small detail suggesting personality—a pin, watch, or wristband in secondary color

### Design Philosophy
- Approachable, age-appropriate, non-threatening
- Suggests comfort over fashion perfection
- Conveys both vulnerability and quiet capability
- Avoids stereotypical "anxious kid" clichés (no exaggerated shaking, etc.)

---

## Emotional Design Language

### Posture & Body Language
- **Default stance:** Slightly closed posture, hands at front (comfortable, not defensive)
- **When anxious:** Shoulders slightly raised, arms closer to body, but not hunched
- **When focused:** Upright, engaged, eyes forward
- **When confident:** Grounded, shoulders back, steady gaze

### Key Design Principle
JORDAN should NOT look broken or helpless. Instead, the design should convey:
- **Thoughtfulness:** A mind that's active, observing, processing
- **Quiet strength:** Capable of deep focus and creative work
- **Authentic anxiety:** Present but not overwhelming; integrated into personality
- **Neurodivergent pride:** Different ≠ deficit

---

## Expression Variations (4 Key Poses)

### 1. Anxious / Worried
- **Brow:** Furrowed, slight crease between eyebrows
- **Eyes:** Wider, worried or concerned gaze
- **Mouth:** Slightly tightened, pressed lips, or small frown
- **Posture:** Shoulders slightly raised, hand possibly near neck or mouth
- **Context:** Moment of social stress, sensory overwhelm, or performance anxiety

### 2. Focused / Concentrating
- **Brow:** Knitted slightly, in deep thought
- **Eyes:** Narrowed slightly, intense, looking forward or at object of focus
- **Mouth:** Slight lip bite, neutral, or small determined line
- **Posture:** Forward-leaning slightly, engaged, hands may gesture or work
- **Context:** Problem-solving, creative work, or hyperfocus on interest

### 3. Uncertain / Hesitant
- **Brow:** Raised or softly worried
- **Eyes:** Looking away, uncertain, or seeking reassurance
- **Mouth:** Nervous smile, bitten lip, or small worried expression
- **Posture:** Slightly withdrawn, arms crossed or hands together
- **Context:** Social uncertainty, decision-making, seeking validation

### 4. Confident / Capable
- **Brow:** Calm, relaxed
- **Eyes:** Direct, assured, warm
- **Mouth:** Genuine small smile or neutral calm expression
- **Posture:** Upright, grounded, shoulders relaxed back
- **Context:** Standing by work, social moment with trusted peers, after success

---

## Action Poses (4 Full-Body Variations)

### 1. Thinking / Introspective
- **Hand position:** Hands clasped or touching chin, in front of body
- **Head angle:** Slightly tilted down or to side, introspective
- **Body angle:** Slightly turned, not fully frontal
- **Context:** Moment of reflection, processing thoughts, planning

### 2. Observing / Watching
- **Hand position:** Relaxed at sides or holding something small
- **Head angle:** Turned to side, looking attentively
- **Body angle:** Still but engaged, not tense
- **Eye direction:** Focused outward, attentive
- **Context:** Paying attention in class, watching an interaction, taking in information

### 3. Creating / Working
- **Posture:** Seated at desk or standing at workspace
- **Hand position:** Engaged with task—writing, typing, drawing, building
- **Head angle:** Bent slightly forward in focus
- **Facial expression:** Concentrated, in flow state
- **Context:** Hyperfocus on interest, schoolwork, creative project

### 4. Standing Proud / Grounded
- **Posture:** Fully upright, relaxed shoulders
- **Hand position:** Hands at sides, relaxed and open
- **Head angle:** Straight, looking forward with calm confidence
- **Facial expression:** Assured, proud, small smile optional
- **Context:** After accomplishment, presenting work, standing by values

---

## Head Variations (3 Views)

### 1. Front View (Calm/Neutral)
- Symmetrical face, centered
- Both eyes visible and equal
- Hair frames face symmetrically or with slight asymmetry
- Calm, neutral expression suggesting introspection

### 2. 3/4 Profile (Thoughtful Angle)
- Head slightly turned
- Shows hair volume and styling clearly
- One eye visible, one partially hidden
- Expression: Thoughtful, observing to the side
- Conveys thoughtfulness without sadness

### 3. Pure Profile (Distinctive Hair)
- Head turned 90 degrees
- Full profile silhouette visible
- Hair styling, texture, and distinctive features prominent
- Neutral calm expression
- Shows distinctive personal style

---

## Design Rules

### DO
- Use clean, bold black outlines for clarity
- Ensure eyes are expressive and a focal point
- Include subtle details suggesting neurodivergent identity (stim object, fidget, comfort item optional)
- Make the character look capable and thoughtful, not stereotypically "anxious"
- Use cool colors (blue/purple) as primary, warm earth tones as secondary
- Show vulnerability AND strength in different poses

### DON'T
- Avoid exaggerated "nervous" clichés (shaking, sweating, etc.)
- Don't use overly bright neon colors (contrary to calm blue/purple palette)
- Don't make the character look weak or pitiful
- Don't stereotype autism or anxiety as always sad/withdrawn
- Avoid generic "worried kid" cartoon designs
- Don't include medical/clinical imagery

## Usage Context

This character design is intended for:
- Educational content about anxiety, autism spectrum, neurodiversity
- Narrative/story content featuring neurodivergent protagonists
- School or therapeutic contexts
- Digital apps or web-based learning tools
- Inclusive representation in children's media

**Key Message:** Anxiety and autism are part of JORDAN's personality and identity, not deficits to overcome. The design should celebrate that authenticity while showing growth, capability, and pride.

---

## Design Notes for Illustrator

1. **Eyes are the focal point.** Spend time making them expressive and distinctive.
2. **Hair should feel real and intentional.** Show texture and personality.
3. **Expressions should be readable at small sizes.** Test clarity.
4. **Posture communicates more than facial expression.** Use body language to show emotion.
5. **Cool blues/purples are calming.** Balance with warm earth tone accents for approachability.
6. **Middle-school age matters.** Not a young child, not a teen. Age ~12–13.
7. **Neurodivergent representation should not be "special" or "sad."** It's just JORDAN being JORDAN.
