## TEMPLATE 1: "The Lie We Tell Ourselves Every Single Day"
**Duration:** 18 seconds | **Target:** 14-18 year olds | **Hook:** Emotional recognition

### VISUAL & AUDIO DIRECTION:
- **0-3s:** Kal (calm, confident expression) looking directly at camera
- **Pacing:** Slow setup (breathe room), fast payoff (rapid cuts), slow resolution
- **Color Mood:** Cool blues for "intention," chaotic neons for "reality," warm for resolution

---

### SCRIPT:

**[0-3s - THE HOOK]**

**KAL:** *(speaking clearly, direct to camera, confident)*  
"I'm going to organize my room tomorrow."

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "The Lie We Tell Ourselves Every Single Day 🤥"  
*(appears 0.2s after Kal speaks, white sans-serif, 24pt, center-bottom)*

**VISUAL:** Kal smiles. This is the setup. Viewers think: "Wait... is this a lie? How?"

---

**[3-8s - THE SETUP (Slow Pacing = Breathing Room)]**

**KAL:** *(slower, more thoughtful)*  
"Like, ACTUALLY organize it. Put things in bins. Organize by color. Maybe even—"

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "The Plan That Makes Perfect Sense"  
*(appears under Kal, yellow/gold color)*

**VISUAL:** 
- Cut to Kal's room: tidy, organized, light coming through window
- Simple animation: Kal's hands tidying (smooth, peaceful movements)
- This is what success looks like

**AUDIO NOTE:** Gentle, calm background sound. Maybe soft organizing sounds (things being placed gently).

---

**[8-14s - THE CONTRADICTION (Fast Pacing = Chaos Energy)]**

**ECHO:** *(voice suddenly overlaps, fast, excited)*  
"BUT THEN—"

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "What Actually Happens" ⚡  
*(flashes on screen with a whoosh sound effect)*

**VISUAL:** Hard cut to chaos:
- Kal's room, but ECHO appears (chaotic version, maybe slightly transparent or glitching)
- Echo bouncing around, touching everything
- Cut 1: Echo finds an old sketchbook → hyperfocuses
- Cut 2: "Wait, I need to reorganize my art supplies first"
- Cut 3: Finds old photos → gets emotional, distracted
- Cut 4: "I should text my friend about this"
- Cut 5: Realizes 3 hours passed, room now has MORE stuff scattered

**TEXT OVERLAY (rapid, appears with each action):**
- "Found old sketchbook"
- "Now THAT needs organizing"
- "Wait, who is this person in the photo?"
- "Checked phone: 4 hours gone"
- "Room: WORSE than before" ❌

**AUDIO NOTE:** Frantic, scattered sound design. Quick audio cuts (phone ding, shuffle sounds, time passing). Feels ADHD-fast.

---

**[14-18s - THE RESOLUTION (Slow Again = Realization)**

**KAL:** *(sighs, looks at camera with understanding, not judgment)*  
"So tomorrow never comes. Because my brain doesn't work like that."

**ECHO:** *(smaller, calmer, honest)*  
"And that's actually... okay?"

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "Your Brain Isn't Broken. It's Just Different. 💙"  
*(large, white, bold, appears 0.5s after Kal finishes, stays on screen to end)*

**VISUAL:**
- Kal and Echo standing together in the messy room
- Echo's chaotic energy isn't shameful—it's just *them*
- Kal puts hand on Echo's shoulder (acceptance, not defeat)
- Fade to Kal looking at camera with knowing smile

**AUDIO NOTE:** Return to calm. Maybe gentle, accepting music that mirrors the emotional shift.

---

### WHY THIS WORKS:

✅ **First 3 seconds hook:** "I'm going to organize my room" is relatable + text reveals it's a lie = stops scroll  
✅ **Education embedded:** Teaches that ADHD brains work differently (not broken, not lazy—different)  
✅ **Dramatized:** Shows the *real* experience (hyperfocus, context switching, time blindness) visually  
✅ **Real-life instant:** Every viewer with ADHD recognizes this exact sequence  
✅ **Meaningful:** Ends on self-acceptance, not shame (this is the education: you're not broken)  
✅ **Keeps watching:** Each cut reveals a new relatable derailment; viewer wants to see "how bad it gets"

## TEMPLATE 2: "Why My Brain Said 'Yes' to Everything (And Now I'm Dying)"
**Duration:** 20 seconds | **Target:** 14-18 year olds with people-pleasing ADHD | **Hook:** Overwhelm recognition

### VISUAL & AUDIO DIRECTION:
- **0-3s:** Kal in a calm, collected mood
- **Pacing:** Very slow at start (gives false sense of control), then increasingly fragmented
- **Color:** Starts cool, progressively gets warmer/chaotic as deadlines pile on

---

### SCRIPT:

**[0-3s - THE HOOK]**

**KAL:** *(nodding, agreeing)*  
"Yeah, I can help. Yeah, I'll do that. Yeah, no problem."

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "The Most Dangerous Words in My Vocabulary" 🚨  
*(red/orange tint, urgent sans-serif)*

**VISUAL:** Kal saying "yes" three times to three different people (quick montage of requests). Kal looks confident.

---

**[3-8s - THE CONFIDENCE (False Control)]**

**KAL:** *(to camera, genuinely believing this)*  
"I've got this. I can manage. My ADHD brain loves working under pressure anyway, right?"

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "The Lie I Tell Myself" 😅  
*(yellow, slightly sheepish)*

**VISUAL:**
- Kal in a clean workspace, calendar visible
- Four tasks listed (school project, help friend, chore at home, job deadline)
- Kal's expression: "This is fine, I can do this"

---

**[8-16s - THE REALITY (Fragmentation & Panic)]**

**ECHO:** *(voice getting louder, more frantic)*  
"Wait wait wait wait—"

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "Actually It's Been 7 Days" ⏰  
*(flashes, time-jump effect)*

**VISUAL (rapid cuts, 1 per 1-2 seconds):**
- Cut 1: Kal at desk, surrounded by 4 half-started projects
- Cut 2: Phone buzzing constantly (texts from friends: "Where's the help?" / "Did you finish?")
- Cut 3: Calendar showing all 4 deadlines... TODAY
- Cut 4: Kal's face: frozen panic (eyes wide, jaw clenched)
- Cut 5: Echo bouncing around frantically, pulling hair
- Cut 6: Kal typing fast, then stopping, then typing again (can't focus)
- Cut 7: Clock showing 2:47 AM (night before everything is due)

**TEXT OVERLAY (appears with each cut):**
- "Project 1: Not started"
- "Project 2: 20% done"
- "Project 3: Forgot about it"
- "Project 4: ????"
- "Me: PANICKING"

**AUDIO NOTE:** Building anxiety. Layered sounds: pen scratching, keyboard typing, phone dinging, heartbeat, breathing getting faster.

---

**[16-20s - THE LESSON (Acceptance + Practical)]**

**KAL:** *(to camera, honest and a bit broken)*  
"My ADHD brain says 'yes' because I genuinely want to help. But then my brain can't manage it all."

**ECHO:** *(calmer, learned)*  
"So now I say: 'Let me check my capacity first.'"

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "It's Not Flakiness. It's Honest Self-Knowledge. 💡"  
*(white, empowering, appears at the end)*

**VISUAL:**
- Kal taking a breath, looking grounded
- Echo standing beside Kal (no longer frantic)
- Both looking at camera with understanding

---

### WHY THIS WORKS:

✅ **First 10 seconds:** Builds relatability (people-pleasing ADHD is real) + escalates to chaos  
✅ **Education:** Teaches that saying "yes" to everything isn't generosity—it's overcommitment that backfires  
✅ **Dramatized:** Shows the real emotional arc (confidence → panic → acceptance)  
✅ **Real-life instant:** Every ADHD person knows this exact cycle  
✅ **Meaningful:** Reframes flakiness as self-awareness (education: boundaries are self-care)  
✅ **Keeps watching:** Escalating chaos hooks you, resolution teaches something useful

## TEMPLATE 3: "Time Doesn't Exist in My Brain (And That's Destroying My Life)"
**Duration:** 19 seconds | **Target:** 14-18 year olds struggling with time blindness | **Hook:** Frustration recognition

### VISUAL & AUDIO DIRECTION:
- **0-3s:** Kal looking at watch/phone, confused
- **Pacing:** Normal → glitchy → fragmented → slow realization
- **Color:** Gray/muted at start, flashes of neon when time "breaks"

---

### SCRIPT:

**[0-3s - THE HOOK]**

**KAL:** *(looking at phone, shocked)*  
"Wait. It's already 8 PM?"

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "Time Blindness Is Not An Excuse. It's My Reality. ⏰"  
*(appears with a clock-breaking sound effect)*

**VISUAL:** Kal's watch showing 8 PM. Kal looks distressed. Clock ticks loudly in background.

---

**[3-8s - THE EXPLANATION]**

**ECHO:** *(explaining matter-of-factly)*  
"In my brain, time is... weird. It's either infinite or it doesn't exist."

**KAL:** *(adding)*  
"An hour feels like 10 minutes. 10 minutes feels like an hour."

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "My Internal Clock Is Broken" 🔴  
*(red, bold)*

**VISUAL:**
- Show a clock face melting (surreal, dreamlike)
- Show Kal's perspective: doing something enjoyable (drawing, reading) and suddenly 3 hours have passed
- Show Kal's perspective: waiting for something boring and it feels like an eternity

---

**[8-15s - THE REAL-LIFE IMPACT (Fast, Relatable)]**

**ECHO:** *(voice overlapping, increasingly stressed)*  
"So I'm late. To everything. Always."

**TEXT OVERLAY (rapid):**
- "5 minute drive: I think 'one more thing'"
- "15 minutes pass: I'm still doing the thing"
- "Now I'm 10 minutes late"
- "Again"
- "For the 47th time this month"

**VISUAL (cuts every 1-1.5 seconds):**
- Cut 1: Kal says "I'll be ready in 5 minutes"
- Cut 2: Kal deep in a project/activity
- Cut 3: Clock showing 20 minutes passed (Kal hasn't noticed)
- Cut 4: Mom/friend texting "Where are you??"
- Cut 5: Kal realizing the time, panicking
- Cut 6: Kal rushing, looking frazzled
- Cut 7: Arriving late, apologizing, frustrated at *themselves*

**AUDIO NOTE:** Anxious, scattered. Clock sounds (ticking, alarm, notification pings). Builds pressure.

---

**[15-19s - THE REFRAME (Understanding)**

**KAL:** *(to camera, vulnerable)*  
"People think I'm lazy or disrespectful. But the truth is: my brain literally doesn't perceive time the way yours does."

**ECHO:** *(hopeful)*  
"So I'm learning tools. Alarms. External structure. Because my internal clock is... honest, I'm not sure it exists."

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "Time Blindness = Real. Executive Dysfunction = Real. Not An Excuse—A Different Brain. 💙"  
*(white, large, validates the struggle)*

**VISUAL:**
- Kal setting multiple phone alarms (practical solution)
- Kal looking at a visual timer (another tool)
- Kal being honest: "I'm not lazy. My brain is wired differently."
- Both Kal and Echo facing camera, grounded

---

### WHY THIS WORKS:

✅ **First 10 seconds:** Time blindness is extremely relatable and deeply frustrating for ADHD kids  
✅ **Education:** Teaches that time blindness is neurological, not moral failure  
✅ **Dramatized:** Shows the *cascade* of consequences (one moment of not noticing time → late → apologizing → shame)  
✅ **Real-life instant:** Every kid with ADHD knows this exact feeling (and the guilt)  
✅ **Meaningful:** Reframes the problem (not a character flaw, but a brain difference that needs tools)  
✅ **Keeps watching:** Escalating chaos of being late, plus practical hope at the end

## TEMPLATE 4: "Why I Started 5 Projects and Finished 0"
**Duration:** 18 seconds | **Target:** 14-18 year olds with hyperfocus/context-switching | **Hook:** Self-awareness shock

### VISUAL & AUDIO DIRECTION:
- **0-3s:** Kal confident, excited
- **Pacing:** Fast, montage-like, showing the chaos of hyperfocus shifts
- **Color:** Bright, shifting colors as each project starts and is abandoned

---

### SCRIPT:

**[0-3s - THE HOOK]**

**KAL:** *(enthusiastically)*  
"Okay, today I'm finally going to finish my art project."

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "Narrator: 'She Did Not Finish Her Art Project.' 🎨"  
*(white, ironic David Attenborough narrator vibe)*

**VISUAL:** Kal at desk with art supplies, determined expression.

---

**[3-9s - THE HYPERFOCUS CHAOS (Rapid Montage)]**

**ECHO:** *(voice-over, rapid, excited)*  
"But WAIT—"

**VISUAL (cut every 0.75-1 second, showing rapid task-switching):**
- Cut 1: Kal starts painting
- Cut 2: "Hmm, I need better paint brushes"
- Cut 3: Kal googles paint brushes (gets distracted researching)
- Cut 4: "Actually, I should reorganize my art supplies first"
- Cut 5: Kal pulling out ALL art supplies from the closet
- Cut 6: "Oh! I forgot I had THIS sketchbook!"
- Cut 7: Kal opening old sketchbook, hyperfocusing on old art
- Cut 8: "Maybe I should start a different art project using mixed media?"
- Cut 9: Kal pulling out scissors, paper, glue, magazines

**TEXT OVERLAY (appears with each scene):**
- "Step 1: Start project"
- "Step 2: Hyperfocus on researching the WRONG thing"
- "Step 3: Find something else interesting"
- "Step 4: Organize instead of create"
- "Step 5: Get distracted by memory"
- "Step 6: Start NEW project"

**AUDIO NOTE:** Bouncy, chaotic. Quick transitions. Sound design reflects ADD energy (dings, whooshes, door opens, papers rustling).

---

**[9-15s - THE AFTERMATH]**

**BOTH:** *(voice-over, exhausted)*  
"And now..."

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "8 Hours Later" ⏰  
*(flashes on screen)*

**VISUAL:**
- Pull back to reveal Kal's desk: 5 half-started projects, supplies everywhere
- Original art project: STILL BLANK
- Kal looking at the chaos, overwhelmed
- Echo looking equally confused

**TEXT OVERLAY:**
- "Original Project: 0% done"
- "New Projects Started: 5"
- "Projects Finished: 0"
- "My Sense of Accomplishment: ???"

---

**[15-18s - THE EDUCATION]**

**KAL:** *(to camera, frustrated but understanding)*  
"This is hyperfocus. But it's not focus on what I NEED to do. It's focus on whatever my brain finds interesting RIGHT NOW."

**ECHO:** *(honest)*  
"I'm not lazy. I'm not indecisive. My brain just... gets pulled in seventeen directions at once."

**TEXT OVERLAY:** "ADHD Hyperfocus = Intense BUT Chaotic. We're Not Scattered—We're Multi-Track. 🎵"  
*(white, validating)*

**VISUAL:**
- Kal and Echo surrounded by the half-finished projects
- Instead of shame, there's understanding
- They're looking at the chaos like "Yeah, this is us"

---

### WHY THIS WORKS:

✅ **First 10 seconds:** Every ADHD person knows this exact feeling (starting strong, derailing immediately)  
✅ **Education:** Teaches that hyperfocus is real, but it's uncontrollable—not a character flaw  
✅ **Dramatized:** Shows the CASCADING CHAOS (one distraction leads to another, to another)  
✅ **Real-life instant:** The 8-hour time jump is shocking and relatable  
✅ **Meaningful:** Reframes as neurological difference, not laziness or lack of willpower  
✅ **Keeps watching:** The speed of the montage is engaging; you want to see how many projects they abandon

## PRODUCTION NOTES FOR ALL 5 TEMPLATES:

### Character Design:
- **Kal:** Calm, thoughtful version (represents intention, planning, the "should be" self)
- **Echo:** Chaotic, honest version (represents the ADHD brain, impulsivity, the "actually is" self)
- Both appear together in resolution (integration, acceptance)

### Pacing Formula:
1. **Hook (0-3s):** Text + visual that makes viewer stop scrolling
2. **Setup (3-8s):** Slow, breathing room, establish the intention
3. **Chaos (8-15s):** Fast cuts, 1 per 1-2 seconds, shows the real experience
4. **Resolution (15-20s):** Slow return, education + emotional validation

### Text Strategy:
- All caps for urgency/punchlines
- Emoji for tone (🤥 shame, 💙 validation, ⚡ energy, ❌ chaos)
- Appears 0.2s after speech, never covers face
- White sans-serif, 20-26pt, readable at 480p

### Audio Strategy:
- Use trending ADHD audio from TikTok (don't create original music)
- Examples: @connordewolfe ADHD sounds, time blindness audio, hyperfocus audio
- Sync cuts to beat drops for engagement

### Target Engagement:
- Each video teaches something specific (not a joke, but a lesson with humor)
- Each video validates an ADHD experience (not mocking, understanding)
- Each video ends with emotional resonance (viewers share because they feel seen)
