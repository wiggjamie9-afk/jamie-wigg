## Mythology

Most people meet their nervous system for the first time the day they try this breath.

The vagus is the longest cranial nerve in the body. It wanders from the base of the skull down through the throat, the heart, the diaphragm, the gut. Yogis felt it before anatomy named it — the line of stillness that runs through the centre of the body. When you breathe out longer than you breathe in, you walk that line.

This is the protocol the Codex hands you first. Most users feel it inside ninety seconds.

## Mechanism

The Vagus Release is a fixed-ratio extended-exhale breath. The four-count inhale and the eight-count exhale put the user at three breaths per minute with an exhale-to-inhale ratio of 2:1.

A long exhale activates the parasympathetic branch of the autonomic nervous system via vagal afferents responding to thoracic and intra-abdominal pressure changes. Subjective effects commonly reported: a settling sensation in the chest and gut, a slight cooling around the eyes, slowed heart rate, and a softening of held tension in the jaw and shoulders.

It is a practice, not a treatment. Stop if you experience dizziness; lengthen the inhale if the long exhale feels strained.

## Script

```
[00:00] — opening

Find a position you can hold for five minutes. Spine long. Shoulders soft.
Unhinge your jaw — let your teeth come apart inside your mouth.

We're going to do the simplest breath in the Codex. Four counts in.
Eight counts out. Twice as long going out as coming in. That's the
whole thing.

[00:25] — settle

Take one normal breath first. Notice where you are.

[00:35] — cycle 1, guided

In. Two. Three. Four.
Out. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight.

Again.

In. Two. Three. Four.
Out. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight.

[01:00] — guided fade

The pacer is going to keep counting. I'm going to fade out.
You stay with the count. If your attention drifts, come back.

[01:30] — silent stretch with pacer

(narration silent, pacer + frequency layer only)

[04:30] — close

Two more breaths at your own pace.

Notice the difference between the room you walked into this protocol
and the room you're in now.

[04:55] — done

When you open your eyes, leave them open softly.
```

## Practice notes

If the eight-count exhale feels short of air, the inhale is too small. Most people don't take in enough on the inhale to support a long exhale — the count is a target, not a strain. Let the inhale fill the belly first, then the chest.

If you feel lightheaded, this is too much for now. Shorten to three in / six out and stay there for a few sessions.

This is the protocol to lead with on a hard day. Not the dramatic one. The reliable one.
