# Sunny and the Gentle Breeze — Episode 1
**Series:** Sunny the Quokka — West Australian Bedtime Stories  
**Runtime:** ~12 minutes | **Narrator:** Serena  
**Pace:** 120 words/min with pauses | **Total words:** ~1,450  
**Educational theme:** How wind carries seeds through the WA bush  
**Audience:** Kids 2–8 + parents

---

## SCENE 1 — GOLDEN HOUR (0:00–1:30)

*[Visual: Wide watercolour landscape — WA bush at sunset. Banksias glow amber. A fat quokka named Sunny sits on a warm sandstone rock, watching the sky turn pink.]*

*[Music: Soft acoustic guitar fades in. Ambient: distant kookaburra.]*

In the golden corner of Western Australia, where the banksias grow tall and the sky turns the colour of honey every evening... there lived a small, round, very happy quokka named Sunny.

Sunny had the softest brown fur, the roundest little nose, and a smile so wide that even the moon looked down and smiled right back.

Every evening, just as the sun dipped behind the tall gum trees, Sunny would climb to the top of the favourite sandstone rock — the one that was still warm from the day — and watch the world go quiet.

*[Pause — 3 seconds.]*

And tonight... something was different.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE BREEZE ARRIVES (1:30–3:00)

*[Visual: Leaves on the banksia begin to tremble. A gentle golden shimmer moves through the grass. Sunny's ears perk up.]*

*[Music: A single soft piano note. Wind chimes in the distance.]*

It started as a whisper.

A soft, warm breath moving through the long grass — rustling the silver leaves of the gum trees, tickling the round heads of the banksia flowers, making the whole bush lean and sway... just a little.

Sunny looked up.

"Hello?" said Sunny softly.

The breeze swirled around Sunny's ears in reply.

*[Pause — 2 seconds.]*

Now, you might wonder — can a breeze really say hello?

Well. In the WA bush, on a warm evening, if you sit very still and listen very carefully... you might just hear it too.

---

## SCENE 3 — FOLLOWING THE BREEZE (3:00–4:30)

*[Visual: Sunny hops down from the rock and follows a trail of swirling leaves through the bush. Kangaroo paw flowers sway. A blue fairy wren darts past.]*

*[Music: Gentle melody joins the piano.]*

Sunny hopped down from the rock and followed the breeze along the winding path through the bush.

The path was one Sunny knew well — past the twisted old paperbark tree, past the patch of pink everlastings, past the hollow log where old Grandpa Skink liked to sleep in the afternoon sun.

The breeze danced ahead, picking up leaves and spinning them in slow, lazy circles.

Sunny watched carefully.

*[Pause — 2 seconds.]*

"Where are you going?" Sunny asked.

The breeze didn't answer — at least, not in words. Instead, it swept under a tall banksia tree and lifted something into the air.

Something small. Something light. Something... fluffy.

---

## SCENE 4 — THE BANKSIA'S SECRET (4:30–6:30)

*[Visual: A banksia cone releases its seeds — tiny winged seeds floating upward in the golden light. Sunny watches in wonder, reaching up a paw.]*

*[Music: A soft, wondering melody. Slower. Quieter.]*

A seed.

A tiny banksia seed — no bigger than Sunny's smallest claw — lifted by the breeze and carried up, up, up into the glowing pink sky.

Sunny watched it float.

*[Pause — 3 seconds.]*

Here is something wonderful about banksias — and you can remember this forever.

Banksia trees keep their seeds locked tight inside their cones — sometimes for years and years. They wait for just the right moment. And when the time is right, the cone opens, and the breeze does the rest.

It carries the seeds away — sometimes far, far away — to a patch of soil where a brand new banksia will grow.

The breeze, you see, is one of the bush's most important helpers.

Sunny thought about this for a long, quiet moment.

All this time, the wind had been working. Carrying. Helping. Planting the future... one tiny seed at a time.

*[Pause — 4 seconds.]*

"Thank you," Sunny said quietly, to no one and everyone.

---

## SCENE 5 — MEETING MIKA THE GECKO (6:30–8:00)

*[Visual: Sunny spots a tiny gecko pressed against a smooth rock, sheltering from the breeze. The gecko has big golden eyes. Night is beginning to fall — the sky is deepening to blue-purple.]*

*[Music: Warm, gentle. A little slower now.]*

Sunny kept walking, following the breeze along the path as the sky began to deepen from pink to purple.

And there — pressed flat against a smooth grey rock — was Mika.

Mika was a gecko. Small and pale as moonlight, with enormous golden eyes that saw everything.

"Hiding from the wind?" Sunny asked.

Mika blinked those great golden eyes. "Just resting," said Mika. "The wind carried me a little too far today."

Sunny sat beside Mika on the warm rock.

*[Pause — 2 seconds.]*

"The wind does that sometimes," said Sunny. "Takes you somewhere new."

"Mmm," said Mika. "But I always find my way back."

They sat together quietly, watching the last of the pink leave the sky.

Sometimes, Sunny thought, that's all you need to do. Sit still. Wait for the world to settle. And find your way back home.

---

## SCENE 6 — THE BUSH GOES QUIET (8:00–9:30)

*[Visual: One by one, animals settle for the night. A possum tucks into a hollow. The blue wren disappears into a bush. Stars begin to appear. The breeze slows to almost nothing.]*

*[Music: Very gentle now. Almost a lullaby. The melody repeats, slower each time.]*

All around the bush, it was time to sleep.

The blue fairy wren tucked herself into the wattle bush, pulling her wings around her like a soft blanket.

The old possum in the paperbark tree found his hollow and curled himself into a warm ball.

Even the banksia flowers seemed to close a little — resting after a long day of growing in the sun.

*[Pause — 4 seconds.]*

The breeze slowed.

Slower... and slower... until it was barely a whisper. Just the gentlest movement of air — the kind that barely stirs a single leaf.

Sunny breathed it in.

It smelled of warm earth. Of eucalyptus. Of the day just passed, and the night just beginning.

---

## SCENE 7 — THE MOON RISES (9:30–11:00)

*[Visual: The great WA moon rises above the gum trees — enormous and golden-white. Stars appear one by one. Sunny lies back in the soft grass and looks up.]*

*[Music: Solo piano. Very slow. Very soft. Three-note repeating motif.]*

And then — there it was.

The moon.

Rising slowly above the tallest gum tree, round and full and impossibly beautiful — flooding the bush in silver light so soft it felt like a dream.

Sunny lay back in the warm grass and looked up.

The stars appeared one by one. First one. Then three. Then too many to count — scattered across the sky like seeds the breeze had carried there long, long ago.

*[Pause — 5 seconds.]*

Maybe, Sunny thought, the night sky was a banksia cone too. Holding all those stars safe — all that light — until just the right moment.

Until now.

*[Pause — 3 seconds.]*

Somewhere nearby, a cricket began to sing.

---

## SCENE 8 — COMING HOME (11:00–12:00)

*[Visual: Sunny curls up in the soft grass among the banksia flowers and daisies. Eyes slowly closing. The moon watches over. Everything still.]*

*[Music: The melody slows to almost nothing. Just the very softest notes. Then silence, except for gentle ambient cricket sounds.]*

Sunny didn't remember walking home.

But somehow, there was the sandstone rock. There was the warm patch of grass, soft as anything. There were the banksia flowers nodding their golden heads.

Sunny curled into a small, round ball — the way quokkas do when the whole world feels exactly right — and let out one long, quiet breath.

*[Pause — 4 seconds.]*

The breeze came one last time. Just barely there. Just enough to brush Sunny's ears and carry away the last thought of the day.

*[Pause — 3 seconds.]*

And Sunny... was asleep.

*[Music fades to silence. Only the very softest cricket. Then nothing.]*

*[Final visual: Wide shot — the moon over the WA bush. Sunny a small, still shape in the silver grass. Stars turning slowly overhead.]*
