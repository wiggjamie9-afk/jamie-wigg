# Sensitivity Checklist: SURGE Pilot Script Review
## ADHD Representation & Authenticity Framework

**Series:** SURGE — ADHD Superhero Animation  
**Task:** T1 — Create sensitivity checklist with ≥3 ADHD consultant roles identified  
**Date:** 2026-06-04  
**Review Stage:** Applied to pilot script BEFORE animation greenlight

---

## Overview

This checklist guides the sensitivity review team in assessing whether the SURGE pilot authentically depicts ADHD and validates both the struggle and the strengths, without patronizing, stereotyping, or exploiting the subject matter.

**Core Principle:** "Nothing About Us Without Us" — ADHD consultants shape the narrative; they don't just rubber-stamp it.

---

## Section A: Representation Red Flags (AVOID)

### A.1 — Inspiration Porn & False Empowerment
**Red flag:** Ziggy's ADHD "struggle" becomes voyeuristic entertainment for non-ADHD viewers; the difficulty is presented as inspiring rather than real.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Script does NOT frame Ziggy's classroom failure as "heartwarming" or "inspirational" (e.g., "Look how hard he's trying despite his disability!")
- [ ] When Ziggy succeeds, it's not treated as a surprise or a miracle ("against all odds")
- [ ] Peer characters do NOT have "teach the disabled kid" subplots (avoid peer learning valuable lessons from Ziggy's struggle)
- [ ] The superhero fantasy IS empowering, but does NOT suggest that embracing superpowers = classroom problems magically solve themselves

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Does the pilot exploit Ziggy's difficulty for emotional manipulation of the audience?
2. Would an actual ADHD kid watching this feel seen, or feel like they're being watched?

---

### A.2 — "Cure" Narratives & Deficit Framing
**Red flag:** ADHD is portrayed as something that can be "fixed," "overcome," or "managed away" through willpower, positive thinking, or external intervention.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Script does NOT suggest that "trying harder" or "focusing more" is the solution
- [ ] If medication is mentioned, it's ONE tool, not *the* fix (and SURGE is not a "med propaganda" vehicle)
- [ ] Superhero transformation does NOT erase ADHD; Ziggy is SURGE *because of* ADHD traits, not *despite* them
- [ ] "Snap back" moment (returning to classroom) does NOT frame ADHD as gone; it shows Ziggy negotiating a real constraint, not defeated
- [ ] No suggestion that "normal brain" = better/goal; different ≠ broken

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Does the script imply ADHD is something to be "cured" or overcome?
2. Does the superhero metaphor strengthen the "different = broken" message, or break it?

---

### A.3 — ADHD as Comic Relief / Patronizing Tone
**Red flag:** ADHD behaviors are played for laughs; the struggle is treated as silly, not serious; tone becomes patronizing.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Ziggy's fidgeting, interrupting, or distraction are NOT treated as funny quirks (they're real regulation struggles)
- [ ] Teacher's exasperation, peer eye-rolls, or family frustration are NOT cartoonish; they reflect real miscommunication, not moral judgment
- [ ] Voice direction for Ziggy does NOT sound "slow," "babyish," or "less intelligent" (ADHD ≠ low IQ)
- [ ] Dialogue from Ziggy reflects authentic 10-year-old speech patterns, not a "ADHD stereotype" voice
- [ ] Narrator/framing does NOT wink at the audience about Ziggy's "silly" disorder

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. If you mute the sound and watch the visual, does the animation mock Ziggy or respect him?
2. Would Ziggy's tone of voice make an ADHD kid feel seen or feel patronized?

---

### A.4 — Stereotyping: The "Hyper Kid" Reductionism
**Red flag:** ADHD is reduced to "hyperactivity" or "can't sit still"; inattention, emotional dysregulation, working memory, and time blindness are ignored.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Pilot does NOT assume all ADHD = bouncing off walls (Ziggy has inattention + hyperactivity mix; both are shown)
- [ ] Emotional dysregulation (shame moment, overwhelm, anger) is shown as ADHD-related, not just "bad behavior"
- [ ] Time blindness is depicted if relevant (Ziggy loses track of time, misjudges duration of assignments)
- [ ] Working memory limits are shown (Ziggy can't hold multi-step instructions; forgets what the teacher just said)
- [ ] Hyperfocus is shown positively (during sports, games, interests—not just negative disorganization)

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Does the script show the *full* ADHD profile, or just the "hyper" stereotype?
2. If Ziggy has inattention as primary trait, does the script reflect that?

---

### A.5 — Villainizing the Disorder or the Kid
**Red flag:** ADHD becomes the "bad guy"; Ziggy is treated as a problem to be solved or controlled.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Script does NOT frame ADHD as Ziggy's fault or moral failing ("you're choosing to fail")
- [ ] Teacher/parent frustration is shown with empathy, not punishment mentality (they're stressed, not evil)
- [ ] Supporting characters do NOT bully Ziggy under the guise of "helping him" (tough love that's just cruel)
- [ ] Classroom conflict is not reduced to "Ziggy is the problem"; systemic issues (noise, time pressure, rigid structure) are acknowledged
- [ ] Superhero transformation does NOT suggest Ziggy has to become "perfect" or "controlled" to be acceptable

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Does the script blame Ziggy for his neurobiology?
2. Are supporting characters allowed to struggle with ADHD frustration without being villains?

---

### A.6 — Erasure of Complexity: "Diversity as Tokenism"
**Red flag:** Cast is all white, all same family structure, all same socioeconomic status; representation is surface-level.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Protagonist Ziggy has a clear racial/ethnic identity (not ambiguous "default")
- [ ] Peer characters include racial diversity, gender diversity, body-type diversity
- [ ] Teacher, family members reflect realistic school staff/community
- [ ] Family structure is NOT nuclear default; includes single-parent, multigenerational, or other realistic models
- [ ] If characters of color are present, they have agency and distinct personalities (not background stereotypes)
- [ ] Socioeconomic realities are reflected (e.g., not all families have resources for private tutoring, therapy)

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Would a kid from a non-white, non-traditional family background see themselves in this pilot?
2. Are diverse characters present or just tokenized?

---

## Section B: Authenticity Checklist (BUILD TOWARD)

### B.1 — Classroom Struggle: Specific, Not Generic
**Authenticity marker:** Classroom scenes feel earned and grounded; not a cartoon villain teacher or unrealistic peer dynamics.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Classroom setting is specific: Does it feel like a real 3rd–6th grade classroom (noise level, lighting, student count, types of tasks)?
- [ ] Peer interaction is realistic: Ziggy's struggle with peers is not cruelty-for-cruelty's sake; it's based on real peer-processing differences (Ziggy interrupts, forgets social rules, loses focus during partner work)
- [ ] Teacher's reaction is earned: The teacher cares but is stretched thin; the response to Ziggy's behavior reflects real time/training constraints, not mustache-twirling villainy
- [ ] Reading scene (if included) captures authentic struggle: not "can't read" but "loses place," "decodes slowly," "can't hold meaning of sentence," "gives up"
- [ ] Task-initiation is shown: Ziggy wants to start homework but can't; gap between intention and execution (not laziness)

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Does this classroom feel like one you recognize from actual schools?
2. Are the peer conflicts rooted in neurodivergent-neurotypical miscommunication, not just peer cruelty?
3. Would a teacher recognize themselves in this portrayal (not as a villain, but as someone juggling constraints)?

---

### B.2 — Emotional Authenticity: Shame & Resilience Coexist
**Authenticity marker:** Ziggy's emotional experience is complex; shame, frustration, and insight coexist without resolution.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Shame moment is specific, not vague (e.g., what exactly triggered it? Was it social, academic, or both?)
- [ ] Shame is NOT resolved by a pep talk or sudden confidence; it's a real emotional state that lingers
- [ ] Ziggy's frustration with own brain is shown (internal conflict, not just external blame)
- [ ] Resilience is NOT presented as "bouncing back"; it's shown as "persisting despite."
- [ ] "Snap back" from superhero fantasy is not a defeat; Ziggy has *practiced* superhero thinking and brings some of that back to the real classroom

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Does the shame moment feel like a real emotional low, not a plot device?
2. Is Ziggy allowed to feel bad AND still be the protagonist we root for?

---

### B.3 — Character Voice: Does Ziggy Sound Like a Real 10-Year-Old with ADHD?
**Authenticity marker:** Dialogue, speech patterns, and emotional expression match authentic kids' voices (not stereotypes, not adult-in-child-voice, not "slow").

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Ziggy's dialogue is rapid/scattered when hyper, slower/focused when hyperfocused (not consistent slowness)
- [ ] Speech patterns: Does Ziggy interrupt, tangent, lose the thread mid-sentence? (YES, if realistic) Does he code-switch based on context? (YES)
- [ ] Vocabulary & maturity: Age-appropriate (10-year-old), not baby talk; shows intelligence without being "precocious"
- [ ] Emotional expression: Frustration, shame, joy are shown without heavy narration; kids feel things intensely
- [ ] When Ziggy is hyperfocused on an interest (sports, game, art), does his speech change (more detailed, organized, confident)?

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Does Ziggy sound like a real kid, or a character *playing* ADHD?
2. Would an ADHD kid recognize Ziggy's speech patterns as truthful?
3. If you listen only to voice (no visuals), does Ziggy's intelligence come through?

---

### B.4 — Supporting Cast: Agency & Personal Stakes
**Authenticity marker:** Teacher, peer, family, and fantasy mentor are full characters, not plot devices. Each has a perspective, a constraint, a desire—and conflict with Ziggy is real, not villainous.

**Checklist per character:**
- **Teacher:** What is the teacher's constraint? (Class size, standardized testing, lack of ADHD training) What do they want from Ziggy? (Compliance? Engagement? Both?) Does the teacher care, even if they're frustrated?
- **Peer:** Why does the peer react to Ziggy the way they do? (Feels excluded? Annoyed? Worried?) Does the peer have their own arc, or are they just "the popular kid"?
- **Family member:** What's the family's relationship to Ziggy's ADHD? (Accepting? Frustrated? Confused?) Does the family show love even when frustrated?
- **Fantasy mentor:** Is the mentor Ziggy's idealized self, or a separate character? Does the mentor push growth, or just validate?

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Does each supporting character have a reason for their behavior (not just "obstacles")?
2. Could you write a paragraph from each character's perspective? If not, they lack agency.

---

### B.5 — Superhero Transformation: Strength-Based, Not Magical Thinking
**Authenticity marker:** Transformation fantasy is grounded in real ADHD strengths; it validates the kid without erasing the difficulty.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] SURGE (superhero form) represents real ADHD strengths: hyperfocus becomes laser focus, kinetic energy becomes speed/power, rapid ideation becomes strategic insight, emotional intensity becomes empathy/drive
- [ ] Transformation happens because Ziggy *recognizes* and *uses* his ADHD abilities, not because an external force "fixes" him
- [ ] Fantasy action showcases these strengths: SURGE solves problems using unconventional/rapid thinking, persists through obstacles, harnesses kinetic energy
- [ ] Visual language (motion, color, sound) reflects ADHD internal experience during flow (tight focus, fast motion, clear purpose)
- [ ] Transformation is NOT a "magical cure" fantasy; it's a metaphor for what Ziggy *already has* (just expressed differently)

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Does the superhero form celebrate real ADHD strengths, or is it just generic "cool powers"?
2. Would an ADHD kid feel empowered by this metaphor, or feel it's unrealistic/mocking?

---

### B.6 — Diversity & Representation (Detailed)
**Authenticity marker:** Cast reflects realistic school diversity; characters of color/marginalized identities have agency.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Ziggy's racial/ethnic background is intentional, not accidental; informs character without defining them
- [ ] If Ziggy is a character of color, does the script address (or at least not erase) intersectional factors (e.g., ADHD + race bias in school discipline)?
- [ ] Peer characters include: at least one character of color with distinct personality, at least one character with visible disability/difference (not tokenized), at least one character with non-nuclear family
- [ ] Teacher & family members reflect realistic diversity (race, age, body type, gender presentation)
- [ ] If any character is a stereotype (e.g., "strict teacher," "cool older sibling"), are they still human?

**Questions for reviewer:**
1. Would a non-white kid, a kid with a disability, or a kid from a single-parent home see themselves authentically represented?
2. Are diverse characters allowed to be flawed, funny, or complex—not just "representation"?

## Section D: Recommended Sensitivity Panel (Consultant Roles)

**Core Panel: ≥3 Consultants (Lead + 2 Supporting)**

### Role 1: ADHD Researcher + Media Literacy Specialist (LEAD CONSULTANT)
**Qualifications:**
- PhD or equivalent in ADHD psychology, neuroscience, or clinical ADHD
- Published work on ADHD representation, neurodiversity in media, or child development + media
- Familiarity with animation/film production (not required, but valuable)
- Neutral/independent voice (not affiliated with a single organization or medication company)

**Responsibilities:**
- Serve as lead approval authority for script (per R9 in requirements.md)
- Provide clinical grounding for character authenticity (voice, emotional arc, ADHD-specific accuracy)
- Flag cure narratives, inspiration porn, or deficit framing
- Recommend revisions with clinical rationale
- Sign off on final script before animation greenlight

**Sample profiles to recruit:**
- University-based ADHD researcher with media/accessibility background
- Clinical psychologist specializing in ADHD in children + neurodiversity advocacy
- Director/producer with ADHD expertise + media portfolio

---

### Role 2: ADHD Adult Creator (Animator / Filmmaker / Writer)
**Qualifications:**
- Self-identified ADHD adult with lived experience (childhood + adult perspective)
- Professional experience in animation, film, TV writing, or related creative field
- Demonstrated commitment to authentic neurodiversity representation (portfolio, interviews, advocacy work)
- Understanding of how childhood ADHD *felt* + how media portrayal affects self-perception

**Responsibilities:**
- Review script for authentic character voice & emotional truthfulness
- Identify moments that feel voyeuristic or exploitative vs. validating
- Provide feedback on superhero metaphor & transformation fantasy (does it feel empowering or patronizing?)
- Offer creative suggestions for visual/narrative approaches
- Ensure peer conflict, shame, and resilience feel earned

**Sample profiles to recruit:**
- ADHD animator/storyboard artist (e.g., from Cartoon Network, Disney, indie studios)
- ADHD screenwriter or story consultant
- ADHD media critic or essayist with creative portfolio
- Independent ADHD filmmaker with youth-focused work

---

### Role 3: ADHD Parent/Educator Advocate
**Qualifications:**
- Parent of a child with ADHD OR K–8 educator with ADHD specialization
- Active in ADHD advocacy (CHADD, Understood.org, local school committees, community organizations)
- Understanding of classroom realities, peer dynamics, family impact
- Passion for authentic representation that validates *and* honors struggle

**Responsibilities:**
- Review classroom scenes & peer dynamics for authenticity (would a real teacher recognize this? A real kid?)
- Assess family representations (if family scenes present) for emotional truthfulness
- Flag moments that might shame or invalidate families watching (medication scenes, "trying harder," etc.)
- Provide feedback on whether the series would be recommended to parents/educators
- Ensure classroom authenticity goes beyond stereotype (not a "villain teacher," realistic constraints shown)

**Sample profiles to recruit:**
- Parent advocacy leader (e.g., local CHADD chapter, school board advocate)
- Teacher with ADHD specialization or personal ADHD diagnosis
- School counselor or special education coordinator with ADHD focus
- Educational consultant with neurodiversity background

---

### Consultant Recruitment Process

**Timeline:** Identify & recruit consultants during T1; schedule kickoff meetings week 2.

**Engagement:**
1. **Individual interviews** (1–2 hours each): Discuss SURGE concept, key creative choices, red flags to watch for
2. **Async script review** (1–2 weeks): Each consultant reviews pilot script using sensitivity checklist
3. **Feedback synthesis** (1 week): SURGE team compiles reviews, identifies patterns
4. **Revision sprint** (2 weeks): Writer revises script based on feedback
5. **Final sign-off** (1 week): Lead consultant (ADHD researcher) approves final script; conditional approval resolved

**Compensation:** Honorarium recommended ($500–$2000+ depending on scope and consultant level). All consultants credited in pilot end cards and series wiki/behind-the-scenes.

## Appendix: Sensitivity Training for Animation Team

**Before Production Begins:**
- All animators receive a 1-hour briefing from lead ADHD consultant on:
  - ADHD neuroscience & authentic experience (not a medical lecture, but a grounding)
  - Red flags to watch during animation (tone, pacing, visual metaphors that mock vs. validate)
  - Character movement language: how to show internal regulation struggles visually (jitter, focus, overwhelm)
  - Feedback loop: animators can flag script/storyboard moments that feel inauthentic during production

**Ongoing Sensitivity Review:**
- After rough cuts, lead consultant reviews animation against sensitivity checklist
- Iterative feedback on visual language, pacing, tone
- Final sign-off before compositing & delivery
