# SURGE — ADHD Superhero Animation Pilot

## The Pitch

**SURGE** is the first neurodiversity-affirmative animated series for kids 8–12, centered on Ziggy Chen, a 10-year-old with ADHD who discovers that his struggles—sensory overload, rapid mind-jumping, emotional intensity—are actually the fuel of a real superpower.

In a single Monday, classroom shame spirals into a daydream where Surge is born: a superhero whose hyperfocus, pattern-recognition speed, and emotional intuition are undeniable strengths. When Ziggy snaps back to reality, he's changed. Not fixed. But reframed. His brain isn't broken—it works differently. And that difference is powerful.

Unlike superhero media that frames disability as something to overcome, SURGE rejects the cure narrative. Ziggy doesn't medicate away his ADHD. He learns to work *with* it. By episode's end, his "weird" way of thinking solves a real problem. Mrs. Henderson, his skeptical teacher, sees him clearly for the first time. His Mom recognizes herself in him.

**What makes SURGE different:**
- **No inspiration porn.** Ziggy is not overcoming his disability through grit. He's accepting his neurology and building a life that honors it.
- **Authentic representation.** East Asian protagonist, diverse supporting cast, family complexity (Mom's undiagnosed ADHD mirrored in Ziggy's), real classroom struggle.
- **Strength-based, not deficit-focused.** ADHD traits (rapid thinking, emotional clarity, hyperfocus) are shown as genuinely valuable in the real world, not just fantasy.
- **Adult growth alongside child.** Mrs. Henderson and Mom Sarah learn alongside Ziggy, modeling neurodiversity-affirming parenting and teaching.

## Audience

### Primary: Kids 8–12 with ADHD + general audience

- **ADHD kids:** See themselves validated. Shame is normalized and navigable, not permanent. Their specific struggles (sensory overload, reading difficulty, time dilation) are named with respect.
- **Siblings/classmates (non-ADHD):** Enjoy a great hero's journey without needing ADHD expertise. Learn empathy for neurodivergent peers.
- **Parents/educators:** Co-view to understand their kids/students better. Modeling of affirming approaches.

### Secondary: ADHD parents, neurodiversity advocates, educators

- Intergenerational validation: Parents recognize their own undiagnosed childhood in Ziggy's struggle.
- Professional development: Teachers see how to shift from "sit still" to "let me meet you where you are."

## Differentiators

### Market Opportunity
- **Underserved niche:** 5–7% of kids have ADHD diagnosis (40M+ globally); nearly zero kid-facing animated content that's affirming (not clinical, not inspiration porn).
- **First-mover advantage:** No mainstream superhero series centered on neurodivergent protagonist.
- **International scalability:** ADHD affects kids worldwide; Australia (home market), US, Europe, Asia-Pacific all underserved.

### Creative Differentiation
1. **Motion language as ADHD voice:** Jittery anxiety, floaty hyperfocus, sharp distraction—all visible in character movement, not just dialogue. Accessible to kids with attention challenges.
2. **Fantasy-to-reality grounding:** Surge isn't a separate magical world; it's the externalized version of how Ziggy's brain actually works. Fantasy proves something true about reality.
3. **Series-long thematic depth:** 5-season arc explores different ADHD strengths (Discovery → Mastery → Identity → Peer Dynamics → Legacy), grounded in one protagonist's growth.
4. **Sensitivity-first production:** ADHD adult consultant embedded in every production phase; no red flags allowed (no cure narrative, no willpower framing, no inspiration porn).

## Representation & Sensitivity

### ADHD Affirmation Commitments
- ✓ **No cure narrative:** Ziggy's ADHD doesn't disappear; he learns to work with it.
- ✓ **Strengths are earned, not tokenized:** Hyperfocus, pattern-recognition, emotional intuition are shown in action—solving real problems.
- ✓ **Family complexity:** Mom's undiagnosed ADHD, intergenerational patterns, real struggle alongside love.
- ✓ **Adult learning, not savior:** Mrs. Henderson grows from deficit to affirming model, but it's her work, not Ziggy's job to educate.

### Diversity & Accessibility
- **East Asian protagonist** (Ziggy Chen) + diverse supporting cast (multiple ethnicities, family structures, body types, abilities)
- **Mandatory closed captions** (ADHD kids often have auditory processing challenges; not optional)
- **Accessible design:** WCAG AAA compliance, no seizure triggers, sensory-conscious color grading

### Sensitivity Review (Locked Gate)
- **ADHD consultant embedded in production:** Every script, every final frame reviewed for authenticity + no red flags
- **Red flags = auto-reject:** Cure narrative, inspiration porn, willpower framing, shame internalization—if present, content is held
- **Community feedback loops:** Monthly Patreon live calls; annual surveys; educator focus groups
- **10%+ revenue donated** to ADHD advocacy orgs (Understood.org, ADDA, community centers)
