## Frame 1: Classroom Establishing (Sensory Pressure Begins)

**Timecode:** 3:00–3:15 (Pilot)  
**Source:** Opening Sequence, Shot 1

**Visual Description:**  
Wide shot of Mrs. Henderson's 5th-grade classroom. Two rows of eight desks in precise lines. Fluorescent ceiling panels shimmer with 60Hz flicker effect (60dB hum audible). Ceiling fan rotating in corner. Wall schedule: ATTENDANCE / MATH / READING / RECESS in large letters. Clock reads 9:05. Window shows bright playground beyond (out of focus, tempting). Color grading: cool blue-gray cast, desaturated, institutional. The space is *present but heavy*—designed for different brains.

**Motion Archetype:** Static (establishing; grounded)

**Why It Matters:**  
This frame sets the fundamental tension of the series: **The classroom is not hostile; it's incompatible.** It's not that Ziggy is broken—it's that the environment is built for neurotypical brains. This visual becomes the metaphorical stage for all five seasons.

## Frame 2: Ziggy's First Anxious Moment (Dysregulation Begins)
**Timecode:** 3:23–3:33  
**Source:** Opening Sequence, Shot 3  
**Visual description:** Close-up on Ziggy's face + hands at desk. Eyes darting left-right-down (micro-movements, pupils scanning). Pencil spinning in fingers at 200ms intervals. Jaw tightens slightly. Shoulders show imperceptible 1px jitter at 80ms frequency.

**Emotional beat:** Anxiety onset. Audience recognizes Ziggy's discomfort before he speaks. His body is broadcasting: "Something's hard right now."

**Why this frame matters:** Establishes that ADHD is a nervous system thing, not a behavior problem. Fidgeting is regulation, not distraction. Sets visual language: jitter = dysregulation, smooth = hyperfocus.

**Motion archetype:** JITTERY (escalating)

## Frame 3: Sensory Montage (Cumulative Pressure)
**Timecode:** 3:33–3:53  
**Source:** Opening Sequence, Shot 4 (four quick cuts)  
**Visual description:** Four isolated sensory close-ups layered:
- Fluorescent light filling frame with shimmer effect (80dB hum)
- Ceiling fan blade rotating with click sound (synchronized, bearing wobble visible)
- Another child's hand tapping eraser on desk (tap-tap-tap rhythm, anxious energy)
- Classroom ambient settling (desaturation increases, color saturation drops 20%)

**Emotional beat:** Mounting pressure. No single stimulus is "bad," but together they create overwhelming DENSITY. Audience understands: this is what Ziggy's brain is processing constantly.

**Why this frame matters:** Visualizes ADHD sensory overload without being dramatic or villainizing. Each detail is real (actual classroom sounds), not exaggerated. Shows how a neurotypical kid hears silence + structure; Ziggy hears chaos + threat.

**Motion archetype:** Static sensory montage (emphasis through layering, not animation)

## Frame 4: Failed Hyperfocus Attempt (Effort Visible)
**Timecode:** 3:53–4:08  
**Source:** Opening Sequence, Shot 5  
**Visual description:** Close-up on Ziggy's hands opening his library reader book. Hands show subtle tremor (jitter visible). Book opens with SHARP instant moment (no easing). Eyes lock downward onto page, pupils dilate slightly (visible effort to focus). Interior monologue appears: *"Okay. First sentence. Just the first sentence. Focus."*

**Emotional beat:** Ziggy's *intention* is clear. He's trying. Audience sees effort + self-awareness of struggle. No pity—just honest effort meeting real resistance.

**Why this frame matters:** Demonstrates that ADHD kids aren't lazy; they're actively fighting their neurology. Makes the shame moment later *hit harder* because effort was visible. Reframes "trying harder" as insufficient (the problem is neurological, not motivational).

**Motion archetype:** JITTERY → attempted HYPERFOCUS

## Frame 5: Time Dilation Moment (Clock on Wall)
**Timecode:** 4:36–4:41  
**Source:** Opening Sequence, Shot 8  
**Visual description:** Close-up on analog classroom clock. Reads 9:07 AM. Second hand ticking visibly (mechanical, sharp 60ms intervals). Minute hand imperceptibly slow (real-time passage, no acceleration). Clock glows slightly; rest of frame dims. Interior monologue: *"The clock reads 9:07. Nineteen minutes left."*

**Emotional beat:** Time as invisible pressure. To Ziggy, 19 minutes feels eternal. Audience grasps the psychological weight of duration—not just duration itself.

**Why this frame matters:** Visualizes time dilation (a core ADHD experience) without dialogue. The clock IS the anxiety. Shows how neurobiology creates subjective suffering (objective time ≠ felt time).

**Motion archetype:** Static (mechanical), but psychologically powerful

## Frame 6: Isolation vs. Peers (The Visible Difference)
**Timecode:** 4:41–4:56  
**Source:** Opening Sequence, Shot 9  
**Visual description:** Wide classroom shot from overhead (30° angle). Most kids reading peacefully, completely still. Ziggy visible in third row: noticeably DIFFERENT. Constant micro-jitter (1px vertical oscillation, barely visible but CAN be seen). Pencil spinning in frame. Chair rocks slightly (leg bounce = 1px horizontal sway). Color slightly desaturated around Ziggy (15% saturation loss, subtle vignette)—visual isolation effect.

**Emotional beat:** ADHD in public. Ziggy's dysregulation is *visible* against the calm classroom. He's not invisible—his body is broadcasting something different. Shame begins here.

**Why this frame matters:** Shows that ADHD struggle is public, not private. Kids *notice* they're different. This validates the social cost of ADHD, not just academic struggle. Sets up why shame is central to the arc.

**Motion archetype:** JITTERY (Ziggy) vs. STATIC (peers)

## Frame 7: The Shame Moment (Freeze + Dissociation)
**Timecode:** 6:26–6:36  
**Source:** Opening Sequence, Shot 15  
**Visual description:** Close-up on Ziggy's face. Eyes WIDE (pupils dilated, whites visible). Face goes BLANK (dissociation beginning—not sad, not scared, just GONE). All jitter stops abruptly (sharp transition, no easing). Silence perceptual (not external, but Ziggy's perception shifts). Interior monologue (80% opacity, larger text, racing thoughts):
- *"Everyone's staring. I can feel it."*
- *"I can't do this. I can't. I can't."*
- *"What's wrong with me?"*

**Emotional beat:** Emotional peak of the struggle. Nervous system collapse. Audience feels Ziggy's complete shutdown. This is the moment that makes the transformation necessary—not magical, but psychologically earned.

**Why this frame matters:** Shame is visible and real. No melodrama—just a quiet moment of complete breakdown. Validates that ADHD shame is serious and deserves healing. The moment before the daydream is the moment most ADHD kids recognize in their own lives.

**Motion archetype:** SHARP freeze (instant stop of all motion)

## Frame 8: Surge Transformation (Power Revealed)
**Timecode:** 9:15–10:15  
**Source:** Transformation Sequence, Shot 4 (1-minute sequence)  
**Visual description:** Ziggy stands in center of frame. Gradual transformation: head begins to glow (Electric Blue outline, 1% → 30% opacity); body outlines become fully Electric Blue at 40% opacity; clothing dissolves into energy form (blue hoodie → Energy Blue silhouette, orange accents → Neon Yellow bursts); particles drift around him (Floaty motion, weightless). By 1-minute mark: Full Surge form—Electric Blue silhouette + Neon Yellow aura, hair stands on end with electricity effect.

**Emotional beat:** BEAUTY, not violence. This is revelation, not acquisition. Ziggy isn't becoming something new; he's *revealing* something that was always there. Audience feels wonder + safety (warm palette, no aggression).

**Why this frame matters:** Transformation proves something true about Ziggy's real brain (speed, pattern-recognition, emotional intensity). Surge is *metaphor made visible*. Shows that ADHD traits, when framed positively, become genuinely powerful. No external magic—internal recognition.

**Motion archetype:** Floaty (graceful, meditative, no urgency)

## Frame 9: Power Montage — Hyperfocus Clarity
**Timecode:** 10:45–11:05  
**Source:** Transformation Sequence, Shot 6A  
**Visual description:** Surge's eyes lock on distant shape (city, person, problem). Pupils dilate, focus-locked. Hyperfocus motion: smooth tracking (ease-in-out), body becomes statue-still (no fidgeting, pure focus). Everything else blurs (depth of field isolates focus target). Interior monologue: *"I can see it. I can see the whole picture."*

**Emotional beat:** CLARITY. For the first time in the sequence, Surge is calm and in control. Hyperfocus isn't a curse here—it's a tool. Audience experiences what rapid pattern-recognition *feels like* from inside.

**Why this frame matters:** Reframes hyperfocus as a strength. Shows that the exact same neurological trait that makes reading impossible can make problem-solving instant. Strength and struggle are two sides of the same coin.

**Motion archetype:** Hyperfocus (smooth, controlled, locked)

## Frame 10: Power Montage — Speed + Kinetic Energy

**Why this frame matters:** Shows that ADHD hyperactivity isn't "bad behavior"—it's *energy*. When not pathologized, that energy becomes speed, athleticism, kinetic grace. Validates that Ziggy's constant movement is fuel, not a problem.

**Motion archetype:** Sharp start → Hyperfocus smooth glide + Floaty particle wake

## Frame 11: Triumphant Moment (Self-Acceptance Peak)
**Timecode:** 11:45–12:15  
**Source:** Transformation Sequence, Shot 7  
**Visual description:** Surge stands in confident pose (arms slightly out, stable stance, but not aggressive). Electric Blue + Neon Yellow glow around body. Particles settle into steady rhythmic orbit (2-second cycle, organized pattern—mathematical beauty). Interior monologue (calm, resolved): *"This is me. All of me. Not broken. Not wrong. Mine."*

**Emotional beat:** ACCEPTANCE + POWER. This is the emotional climax. Audience experiences Ziggy's realization that his ADHD is not enemy but *identity*. The superpower is real because it's *him*.

**Why this frame matters:** Locks the series' core message visually. Ziggy's ADHD isn't fixed or cured—it's integrated. His neurology becomes an identity, not a disorder. This frame is the visual anchor for everything the show stands for.

**Motion archetype:** Hyperfocus calm (still, centered, complete control)

## Frame 12: Snap-Back + Reality (Jarring Return)
**Timecode:** 12:20–13:05  
**Source:** Transformation Sequence, Shots 8–9 (reverse crossfade)  
**Visual description:** Classroom fluorescent light flashes across screen (sharp, jarring intrusion). Warm Electric Blue/Orange Surge world dissolves back to cool desaturated fluorescent classroom. Pixelation effect reverses. Color shift warm → cool. Classroom sharpens into focus. Bell RING (loud, contrasting with ethereal music). Hum + click return to full volume.

**Emotional beat:** JARRING. No graceful exit. The real world crashes back in—unwanted, harsh by comparison. Fantasy is over; struggle resumes. The classroom looks extra bright + harsh (visual shock after warm fantasy).

**Why this frame matters:** Maintains realism. Ziggy isn't "fixed" by the fantasy. He's still in the hard classroom. But he's changed *internally*. The struggle is still real; the response to it is different. Shows that acceptance doesn't magic away the problem—it changes the relationship to it.

**Motion archetype:** Floaty → Jittery (weightlessness snaps back to gravity + dysregulation)

## Frame 13: Reading Aloud (Presence in the Struggle)
**Timecode:** 13:38–14:08  
**Source:** Transformation Sequence, Shot 13  
**Visual description:** Medium shot, Ziggy reading aloud in classroom. Voice quiet but *present*. Reads slowly, with intention. Other kids listen. Mrs. Henderson listens with genuine attention. Ziggy's body still shows jitter (1:1 micro-movements, not constant tremor), but head is steady on page. Not bouncing around. Interior monologue (matter-of-fact, not celebratory): *"I'm not broken. The classroom is just built for a different brain."*

**Emotional beat:** REAL-WORLD COURAGE. Ziggy is still dysregulated, still struggling, but he's *present* instead of hiding or dissociating. He knows what he is now, and that helps him *be* in the difficult space.

**Why this frame matters:** Proves the series concept. Ziggy's real-world "Surge moment" isn't superhero fantasy—it's just him being authentically himself in a hard environment. Shows that the transformation is internal acceptance, not external fix.

**Motion archetype:** Jittery (controlled within dysregulation)

## Why These 8–10 Frames Work for Investor Pitch

1. **Sensory authenticity (Frames 1–3):** Shows that ADHD isn't invisible or exaggerated—it's a real classroom experience, validated by specificity.
2. **Effort + agency (Frames 4–5):** Ziggy isn't passive or helpless. His effort is visible; his struggle is real.
3. **Social cost (Frame 6):** Demonstrates the isolation + visibility of ADHD—explains why shame is the central emotional driver.
4. **Emotional climax (Frame 7):** The shame moment is the turning point. It's why the transformation is necessary, not just whimsical.
5. **Fantasy-to-reality connection (Frames 8–9):** Surge isn't random magic—it externalizes real ADHD strengths (hyperfocus, speed, emotional intensity).
6. **Integration (Frames 11–13):** Shows that acceptance isn't about "fixing" ADHD; it's about understanding it. Real-world "Surge moment" proves the metaphor is grounded.

These frames form a visual journey: **Problem (frames 1–7) → Fantasy Solution (frames 8–10) → Real-World Application (frames 11–13).** They tell the entire story of the pilot visually.
