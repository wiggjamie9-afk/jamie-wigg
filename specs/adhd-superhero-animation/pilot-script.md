## ACT ONE: SUNDAY FREEDOM → MONDAY DREAD
### (0:00–3:00)

### COLD OPEN: SOCCER FIELD — SUNDAY AFTERNOON

*Golden sunlight cuts across a scuffed-up soccer field behind a elementary school. The grass is tall in patches; the goal is two school bags fifty feet apart. This is real, not perfect.*

*ZIGGY CHEN, 10 years old, stands at midfield. Dark hair, always messy. One sock up, one sock down. Eyes that move constantly, scanning the field. His body is loose, confident—this is home.*

*JAKE (O.S.), his best friend, shouts from the goal.*

JAKE (O.S.)
Ziggy! That's unreal, mate!

*A voice with boyish warmth and Midwest accent—Jake's natural enthusiasm.*

*We follow the scuffed soccer ball. It rockets through the air. Time slows. CRACK—a boot connects. Perfect contact. The ball curves, bends, and—*

*SWISH. Top corner. The bags tumble.*

*ZIGGY grins—pure, unselfconscious joy. Already sprinting back for another touch.*

ZIGGY
Again! Again!

*His voice is fast, bright, slightly nasal—Midwest energy. Full of electricity.*

*JAKE runs toward him, laughing. They collide in an awkward, delighted hug. Around them, the golden light stretches long. The soundtrack is warm, open—no tension, just movement.*

*[0:45]*

### CUT TO: INT. CHEN FAMILY KITCHEN — SUNDAY EVENING (5 HOURS LATER)

*The light through the windows is shifting to amber. Dinner dishes are piled in the sink. The kitchen is chaos without malice—a sketchpad, design pencils, an open laptop, a kid's art project all sharing counter space.*

*SARAH CHEN, Ziggy's mom, early 40s, sits at the table with a half-finished cup of coffee. She's focused on her laptop screen, hyperfocused in that specific way where 30 minutes became 3 hours. She doesn't notice the time.*

*ZIGGY enters, freshly sweaty from soccer, still riding the high.*

ZIGGY
Mom! Did you see the video Jake's dad took?

SARAH
(not looking up)
Hmm?

*Ziggy waits. Sarah is still typing, completely absorbed.*

ZIGGY
The goal. The—the one I told you about?

*SARAH blinks back to the present. A flash of guilt.*

SARAH
Oh, honey, I'm sorry. I—I was in the zone. Yeah, that's—that sounds awesome.

*She squeezes his shoulder, genuinely warm, but already her eyes are drifting back to the screen. Ziggy notices. He doesn't say anything, but something in his shoulders drops.*

ZIGGY
It's okay.

*He opens the fridge. Takes out a juice box. Sits down to undo his soccer cleats.*

SARAH
(back to present mode, suddenly)
Ziggy, wait—it's almost 7. You have school tomorrow.

ZIGGY
I know.

SARAH
Have you showered?

ZIGGY
Not yet.

SARAH
Okay, shower, dinner—I made pasta—then homework, then bed. We need to be awake by 7 to get you to school on time, and I—

*She stops herself. Softens.*

SARAH (CONT'D)
Let's just be on time tomorrow, yeah? I got those emails from Mrs. Henderson last week about you being late, and I don't—

*She trails off, not wanting to be the frustrated parent again, but the frustration is there.*

ZIGGY
(small voice)
Okay.

SARAH
You can tell me about the goal during dinner. Really tell me.

*She smiles, meaning it. But her laptop is still open behind her. Both of them know.*

*[2:15]*

### INT. CHEN FAMILY KITCHEN — SUNDAY NIGHT (10 P.M.)

*The kitchen is different now. Homework spread across the table. MAYA, Ziggy's 8-year-old sister, sits perfectly upright, her handwriting tiny and precise. A study model.*

*ZIGGY is slumped in his chair, pencil spinning in his fingers without him noticing. A math worksheet is in front of him. One problem solved. Nine to go.*

SARAH
Ziggy, eyes on the worksheet, please.

ZIGGY
(automatic response)
Yep.

*But his eyes are on the kitchen fan, which clicks unevenly as it rotates. Click. Click. Click-click. His fingers keep time with the pencil.*

*Sarah notices. She doesn't say anything this time, but her jaw tightens.*

MAYA
(not looking up from her perfect worksheet)
He's doing it again.

ZIGGY
I'm focusing.

MAYA
You're staring at the fan.

ZIGGY
Because I'm focusing on the math while staring at the fan. Multi-tasking.

SARAH
(tired)
Ziggy.

ZIGGY
(sincere)
I actually solve it better like this. Like, the fan helps me think.

*Sarah sighs—not cruel, but the sigh of someone who's had this conversation before.*

SARAH
Okay. Let's see how many you can finish before 10:30. Then bed, no exceptions.

*ZIGGY nods. SARAH leaves to clean the kitchen. For a moment, it's quiet. Then ZIGGY's pencil keeps spinning. He looks at the second problem. The numbers rearrange in his head. He writes it down.*

*[3:15]*

### INT. CHEN FAMILY KITCHEN — MONDAY MORNING (7:15 A.M.)

SARAH

SARAH (CONT'D)

Other shoe. Now.

ZIGGY
Where's my...

SARAH
(to Ziggy, covering the mic)
By the door. The left one is by the door where you left it yesterday.

SARAH (CONT'D)
(into mic)
Sorry, my kid—he's getting ready for school. Yes, I know it's last minute. We'll have it ready.

*CUT TO: ZIGGY scrambling into the car. Backpack thrown in. Hair uncombed. One sock still down. The car pulls out.*

*[3:45]*

*SARAH drives, one hand on the wheel, the other holding her phone to her ear—a Bluetooth conversation.*

SARAH
I know, I know, but he lost his permission slip again, and the field trip is this week, and I just need—

*She stops, hearing herself complain.*

SARAH (CONT'D)
(softer)

SARAH (CONT'D)

Ziggy, do you have your permission slip?

ZIGGY
(knowing the answer is no)
Um...

SARAH
Ziggy.

ZIGGY

SARAH

ZIGGY

SARAH

I know you are. That's not—

Have a good day. Love you.

## ACT TWO: CLASSROOM REALITY & SENSORY OVERLOAD

### INT. SCHOOL HALLWAY — JUST BEFORE CLASS (8:57 A.M.)

*The hallway is loud. Kids are moving toward their classrooms. Lockers slam. A girl pushes past. A boy shouts something down the hall that echoes.*

*ZIGGY stands at his locker, spinning the combination. 23-6-14. His fingers know it, but he has to think each number. He forgets the last number. Starts over.*

*JAKE appears beside him.*

JAKE
Ziggy! Did you do the math?

ZIGGY
(relieved to see him)
Only two of the nine.

JAKE
I did all of it. Well, my mom made me, but I did. Want to copy?

ZIGGY
Can't. Mrs. Henderson knows our handwriting.

JAKE
(shrugs)
Worth asking. You coming to soccer practice after school?

ZIGGY
Yeah, if I'm not—

*But the bell rings—a long, piercing electronic sound that cuts through everything.*

*JAKE jumps.*

JAKE
See you at lunch!

*He's gone, moving with the crowd. ZIGGY's locker spins one more time, and he pulls out his folder. It's empty of last night's math homework.*

*His stomach drops. He didn't finish. Didn't even bring what he did.*

*[5:15]*

### INT. CLASSROOM — 9:05 A.M.

*Mrs. Henderson's 5th-grade classroom. Two rows of eight desks, a teacher's desk at the front, a window looking out onto the school grounds. Fluorescent lights hum—a specific, relentless hum. A ceiling fan rotates, clicking unevenly with each rotation.*

*The wall has a schedule in big letters: ATTENDANCE / MATH / READING / RECESS / WRITING / LUNCH. The clock reads 9:05.*

*MRS. HENDERSON, mid-50s, stands at the front sorting papers. She has the look of someone who's been teaching for 19 years—patient underneath, tired on top. She's not unkind. She's just full.*

*The kids file in. About 25 desks are filled. Everyone has a reader—a thick workbook.*

*ZIGGY takes his seat in the third row. His hands are already moving—fingers spinning, tapping the desk, fidgeting with his pencil.*

MRS. HENDERSON
Alright everyone, take out your readers. We're doing twenty minutes of silent reading before we begin the day.

*The classroom settles. Kids reach into bags, pull out books, open them. For most of them, it's automatic. Routine.*

*For ZIGGY, it's different.*

*He opens his reader. Page 47. A story about a boy and a dog. The opening sentence is there, but the words immediately feel blurry. Not because he can't see them—he can read just fine—but because the fluorescent lights are washing them out. The fan clicks. Someone two rows back is tapping a pencil on their desk—tap-tap-tap, tap. The hum of the lights is a constant pressure.*

*He tries to focus. "The boy walked..." Wait, what boy? He reads the sentence again. Slower. The words still rearrange themselves.*

[INT. MONOLOGUE]

*Ziggy's internal voice—frustrated, fast, aware:*

ZIGGY (V.O.)
(interior monologue, fast, overlapping with thought)
Come on. Just read it. It's just a sentence. A boy walked down a road. That's simple. I can do this. That's like... five words. I've read five words before.

*[5:50]*

*He reads it again. The letters hold steady this time. The boy walked down a road. But now there's the next sentence, and the first one is already disappearing. He looks back up. The boy walked... he doesn't remember what boy. There's a girl in it? No, it says boy. The window behind him—the playground is there, bright and green.*

MRS. HENDERSON
(not unkind)
Ziggy, eyes on your book, please.

*The words land on him like a weight.*

ZIGGY (V.O.)
(interior, quieter now)
See? You're doing it again. She's noticed. Everyone's probably noticed.

*He brings his eyes back. Page 47. He starts the sentence from the beginning. A boy walked... he reads faster, hoping that speed will stick the words in place. But now he's not comprehending, just decoding. The fan clicks again. His pencil starts spinning in his fingers.*

[6:15]

*Mrs. Henderson is standing, looking out the window. Twenty-five kids reading. Twenty-five people working independently. This is what the system is built for. Twenty-five kids who can sit. Twenty-five kids whose brains don't rewire when there's a hum and a click and a window.*

*For ZIGGY, nineteen minutes remain. Nineteen minutes feels like three hours.*

*[6:30]*

---

### INT. CLASSROOM — 9:24 A.M. (END OF SILENT READING)

*ZIGGY has read the same three sentences seven times. He hasn't moved forward. His pencil is now in three pieces—he's been picking at it.*

*The bell chimes—a softer bell than the transition bell, but still jarring.*

MRS. HENDERSON
Alright, readers down. Let's transition to reading group.

*The kids move to a small circle of chairs near the window. ZIGGY joins them. There are five kids in this group: ZIGGY, JAKE, ELENA, MARCUS, and DAVID.*

MRS. HENDERSON (CONT'D)
We're continuing where we left off in "The River." Who can remember what happened last time?

*Hands go up. MARCUS raises his hand.*

MRS. HENDERSON (CONT'D)
Marcus.

MARCUS
The boy crossed the river and found a cave.

MRS. HENDERSON
Good. So today, we're starting on page 53. Who wants to read the first page?

*An uncomfortable silence. Reading aloud is the most exposed moment in the classroom.*

MRS. HENDERSON (CONT'D)
Ziggy, why don't you start?

*ZIGGY feels the floor disappear. Reading aloud is the worst—everyone is listening, watching, waiting. If he stumbles, it's public. If he slows down, it's visible.*

ZIGGY
(soft)
Okay.

*He opens to page 53. His hands are shaking slightly—not visibly, but enough that he notices.*

[INT. MONOLOGUE]

ZIGGY (V.O.)
(interior, panicked and fast)
Don't mess up. Just read it. You can read. You read in soccer stats, you read the goal times. You can read. Just—don't be weird. Don't—

*He takes a breath. Starts reading.*

ZIGGY
(reading)
"The cave was dark and—"

*He stumbles on the next word. Cavernous? Cavern? His eyes skip ahead, then come back.*

ZIGGY (CONT'D)
(reading)
"—cavern-ous. With rocks that..."

*He loses the sentence halfway through. His eyes jump down. There's a five-second pause. A long pause. Everyone is waiting.*

JAKE
(whispering, trying to help)
"Gleamed."

*Mrs. Henderson gives Jake a look—she's trying to teach, and Jake is being kind, but he's also not letting ZIGGY figure it out himself. But it breaks the spell. The shame moment is suspended.*

MRS. HENDERSON
(to ZIGGY)
"Gleamed," yes. Keep going.

*ZIGGY reads. The words come faster now, but he's not really understanding the story anymore. He's just trying to get to the end of the paragraph. When he finishes, he sits back, relieved and depleted.*

MARCUS
(not being cruel, just honest)
He reads weird.

*It lands. Not a huge hurt, not a massive shame—just a small truth that everyone was already thinking, now said out loud.*

MRS. HENDERSON
(gentle)
Marcus, that's not kind. Ziggy reads at his own pace, and that's okay.

*But the damage is done. ZIGGY knows what they're all thinking. The room knows it too.*

[7:20]

### INT. CLASSROOM — 9:35 A.M. (LATER, STILL READING GROUP)

*The reading group has moved on. ELENA is reading now, smoothly, confidently. MARCUS is following along. JAKE is doodling in the corner of his book (not listening, but not being called on). *

*ZIGGY is staring at the words on the page, but his brain is somewhere else. Shame is a specific thing—it's not fear or sadness. It's the feeling that everyone knows something true about you that you don't want them to know, and there's nothing to do about it.*

ZIGGY (V.O.)
(interior, smaller now)
Why am I like this? I'm not stupid. I know that. But I read weird. Marcus knows it. They all know it. And I can't fix it right now because I'm sitting right here, and if I try to read better, I'll be trying, and they'll see me trying, and that's worse.

*The reading group continues. ZIGGY says nothing else.*

---

### INT. CLASSROOM — 9:52 A.M. (AFTER READING GROUP)

*The kids are back at their desks. Mrs. Henderson is writing something on the board. The morning is grinding on. Lunch is not until 12:00. It's 9:52.*

*ZIGGY pulls out his math worksheet from last night. He wrote two answers. The rest are blank.*

*Mrs. Henderson collects homework as the kids work independently on math problems. She moves down the row.*

MRS. HENDERSON
Homework?

*Most kids hand over completed worksheets. When she gets to ZIGGY, his hand is already small.*

ZIGGY
(small voice)
I didn't finish.

MRS. HENDERSON
(not angry, but weary)
Again?

ZIGGY
My mom was working and I couldn't... I tried, but...

*MRS. HENDERSON sighs gently, then sits down beside Ziggy's desk (not standing over him).*

MRS. HENDERSON (CONT'D)
I know you tried. This is the third time this week. Let's figure out what you need to make it work at home. Okay?

*She's not angry—just tired, but also curious. She moves to the next student.*

ZIGGY (V.O.)
(interior, whisper-quiet)
I tried. I did try. But then the fan was clicking and the math was hard and I just... I couldn't.

*[8:20]*

### INT. CLASSROOM — 10:15 A.M. (SILENT MATH WORK)

*The classroom is quiet now. 25 kids working independently on math problems. The fluorescent lights are still humming. The fan is still clicking. The sensory world of the classroom has not changed, but ZIGGY's ability to regulate it is depleting.*

*His pencil is moving, but slowly. He's done three problems correctly. Seven remain. And his leg is bouncing—not visibly to others, but enough that he can feel it. He tries to stop. His body takes that as a challenge and bounces harder.*

ZIGGY (V.O.)
(interior, frustrated with himself)
Stop moving. Just sit still like everyone else. What is wrong with you? Maya can sit still. Jake can sit still. Mrs. Henderson is literally standing right there and you can't even—

*He forces his leg to stop. For five seconds, it works. Then it starts again, this time faster, as if it's making up for lost time.*

*Mrs. Henderson glances up. Sees the bouncing leg. Doesn't say anything, but sees it.*

[8:40]

## ACT THREE: SUPERHERO FANTASY & TRANSFORMATION
### (8:00–11:00)

### INT. CLASSROOM — 10:47 A.M. (FIFTEEN MINUTES BEFORE RECESS)

*ZIGGY has finished five of the ten math problems. Mrs. Henderson is circulating. When she reaches his desk, she looks at the work.*

MRS. HENDERSON
These five are right, but you skipped the last ones. Can you finish them?

ZIGGY
I'm trying.

MRS. HENDERSON
You have fifteen minutes before recess. Let's see what you can do.

*She moves on. ZIGGY looks at problem six. It's a word problem. There's text, and numbers, and he has to understand both. The text says: "Sarah has 28 apples. She gives 7 to Jake and 5 to Ziggy."*

*Wait. The problem is literally about him.*

*He reads it again. It's supposed to be funny, probably—the teacher using their names. But it lands differently. It says he's getting 5 apples, which is less than Jake (7), and he's reading it out loud in his head and his leg is bouncing and the fan is clicking and the light is too bright and he hasn't slept well because of SUNDAY DREAD and the morning was CHAOS and he couldn't remember his locker combination and he forgot his homework and he read WEIRD and now he can't even do SIMPLE MATH because the world is too—*

*Something breaks. Not visible on his face, but internally. A kind of shutdown that feels like drowning while sitting in a chair.*

ZIGGY (V.O.)
(interior, very quiet, very fast, overlapping with breathing)
You can't do this. You can't do it. Everyone can do it and you can't. What's wrong with you what's wrong what's—

*His pencil stops moving. His hands are shaking.*

[9:00]

*Mrs. Henderson notices. From across the room, she sees ZIGGY's face. It's not angry, not sad—it's blank. The look of a kid whose nervous system has just tapped out.*

MRS. HENDERSON
(gentle)
Hey. You okay?

ZIGGY
(automatic response)
Yeah.

MRS. HENDERSON
You don't look okay.

ZIGGY
(really quiet)
I can't do this.

(soft, kind)
The math?

MRS. HENDERSON (CONT'D)
That's okay. You got five right. That's something. Let's put this away and we'll come back to it later, yeah?

MRS. HENDERSON (CONT'D)
You can go get a drink of water if you need to. Calm down.

JAKE

*SMASH CUT TO:*

### INT. CLASSROOM — **ZIGGY'S SURGE FANTASY** (11:00–11:45)

**[The visual language shifts. The fluorescent lights dissolve. The real classroom fades. We're in a new space—SURGE-world.]**

*Color explodes. The room is no longer confined—it's open, endless, filled with kinetic energy. Electric Blue (from the style guide) and Neon Yellow and Warm Orange create a landscape that's both dreamlike and hyper-real.*

*The shapes are sharper here. The perspective is wider. And in the middle of it all—*

*SURGE ZIGGY.*

*He's still Ziggy, but transformed. His form is outlined in electric blue. His energy is visible—light radiates from him in waves. When he moves, he leaves trails, like long-exposure photography.*

*Around him, the classroom dissolves into a landscape of pure motion.*

---

### SURGE-WORLD SEQUENCE: THE PROBLEM

*In this space, the fluorescent lights are enormous, geometric shapes—beautiful and terrible, looming, casting long shadows. They're not humming; they're singing a note that's too high.*

*ZIGGY hears it. But here—in Surge-world—he can DO something about it.*

*He moves. Fast. Faster than running. It's not flight, exactly—it's acceleration. The world slows around him because he's moving so fast. He reads the space. Where are the sounds coming from? The lights. The fan. The pressure in the room.*

*He touches the lights. They shift color.*

*He reaches for the hum.*

*It stops.*

*The world is instantly quieter. Clearer.*

**[0:00 of fantasy — Removal of sensory overload]**

---

### SURGE-WORLD SEQUENCE: THE STRENGTH EMERGES

*Now the landscape changes. It's no longer about removing pain. It's about revealing what was always there.*

*Patterns emerge from the chaos—geometric, glowing, vast. Spirals and connections. The world is actually beautiful when Ziggy can see it without the sensory static.*

*A problem appears—not a math problem, a real problem. There's a kid in the distance who's falling, drowning in the same sensory chaos that Ziggy just escaped.*

*Without thinking, SURGE-ZIGGY reads the situation. Sees the problem in the pattern. Sees three solutions simultaneously—four branches of action, each with outcomes.*

*His mind is lightning. His body is lightning.*

*He moves. Not with effort, but with clarity. Each step is perfect. Each choice is right.*

*He saves the kid. Not heroically—just by seeing clearly and moving fast.*

**[1:30 of fantasy — Hyperfocus & rapid problem-solving as superpower]**

---

### SURGE-WORLD SEQUENCE: THE EMOTIONAL POWER

*The kid looks at him with tears. With fear.*

*SURGE-ZIGGY closes his eyes. For a moment, everything is still.*

*And then—he *feels* the kid's fear. Not as abstract sadness, but as a real, present thing. He knows what the kid needs.*

*He speaks:*

SURGE-ZIGGY
(voice clear, confident, almost musical)
It's okay. You're okay.

*The words land because they're true. And because Surge-Ziggy knows—truly knows—that they're true.*

*The kid's fear settles.*

*Around them, the landscape glows warmer—Warm Orange from the palette, filling the space. This is emotional power. This is empathy as a real force.*

**[2:45 of fantasy — Emotional intensity & intuition as superpower]**

### SURGE-WORLD SEQUENCE: SAGE APPEARS

*From the light, a figure emerges.*

*SAGE is older, calm, moving with slow deliberation. Opposite energy from Surge-Ziggy, but completely present. He's wearing something that might be from another time—it's unclear. His face is warm, old-soul, East Asian features. He moves like he's walked this landscape a thousand times.*

SAGE
(slow, warm, assured)
Hello, Ziggy.

SURGE-ZIGGY
(surprised but not scared)
Who are you?

SAGE
Someone who knows you. Or someone you know. The line is blurry here.

*He walks to SURGE-ZIGGY. Around them, the landscape has become still. The only motion is Sage's—slow, deliberate, perfect.*

SAGE (CONT'D)
You know what you just did?

SURGE-ZIGGY
(uncertain)
Saved someone?

SAGE
More than that. You saw clearly. You moved fast. You felt deeply. You helped.

SAGE (CONT'D)
That's not a superpower. That's just what you can do when you're not drowning in noise. When you can hear yourself think.

SURGE-ZIGGY
(realization dawning)

## Series Hook

The pilot ends with the question: Is Surge real? Is it metaphor? Is it just Ziggy's mind finally talking to him in a language he understands?

Season 1 will explore this through five episodes:
- **Ep. 2 ("The Hyperfocus")**: Ziggy hyperfocuses on a school project and discovers he can access Surge consciously. But hyperfocus also means losing time and ignoring other responsibilities. The series arc begins: strengths and struggles are inseparable.
- **Ep. 3 ("Speed Reading")**: Ziggy's rapid-fire thinking (seen as "ADHD energy" in the real world) becomes precision in Surge-world. But in the real world, it means he misses important details. A balance emerges.
- **Ep. 4 ("The Feeling")**: Emotional intensity is both Ziggy's superpower (empathy, intuition) and his vulnerability (dysregulation, shame spirals).
- **Ep. 5 ("The Pattern")**: Ziggy realizes he can see patterns others miss—connections, solutions, possibilities. But pattern-spotting also means anxiety about all the "what-ifs."
- **Ep. 6 (Season 1 finale)**: Mrs. Henderson, Sarah, and Jake begin to see Surge—not the fantasy, but the real manifestations of Ziggy's brain working at full capacity. A systemic shift begins.

The series never "fixes" Ziggy's ADHD. Instead, each season deepens the integration: **How do we live as we are, not as the world wants us to be?**
