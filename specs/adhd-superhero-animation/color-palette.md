# Color Palette: SURGE

**Design philosophy:** Bright, kinetic energy balanced with grounded classroom authenticity. Colors shift between cool (classroom constraint, emotional heaviness) and warm (fantasy freedom, Surge power, connection). This palette reflects Ziggy's duality: the exhausting weight of struggling to focus in a world not built for ADHD brains, and the explosive potential when that same mind finds hyperfocus or acceptance.

---

## Primary Colors (Ziggy's Identity)

### Electric Blue #0052CC
**Ziggy's core: restless, alert, electric energy**

- **Usage**: Ziggy's hoodie accents, clothing details (zipper, cuffs), interior monologue text boxes, Surge's primary form, "zap" effects during hyperfocus, alert/attention visual metaphors
- **Why**: High energy, youthful vibrancy without feeling chaotic; signals electricity (metaphor for ADHD neurology) and mental restlessness; reads true to 10-year-olds as "cool" and modern
- **RGB**: (0, 82, 204) | **HSL**: 217°, 100%, 40% | **CMYK**: 100%, 60%, 0%, 20%
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AAA compliant when paired with #FFFFFF (contrast ratio 8.6:1) and #F5F5F5 (contrast ratio 7.2:1)

### Warm Orange #FF8C3A
**Ziggy's opposite: grounding, warmth, belonging, joy**

- **Usage**: Jake's shirt, connection moments (hugs, high-fives, friendship), soccer ball, moment-of-joy bursts, Surge's secondary energy (when hyperfocus turns collaborative), socks on Ziggy's feet, Mrs. Henderson's warm gestures
- **Why**: Counterbalance to electric blue; represents stability, friendship, and belonging (opposite of shame/isolation); warm without being aggressive; reads as joyful to children
- **RGB**: (255, 140, 58) | **HSL**: 25°, 100%, 61% | **CMYK**: 0%, 45%, 77%, 0%
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AAA compliant when paired with #FFFFFF (contrast ratio 5.8:1) and #333333 (contrast ratio 6.5:1)

## Secondary Colors (Supporting Cast + World)

### Sage Green #2D8A3D
**Mrs. Henderson, nature, Grandpa wisdom, patient structure**

- **Usage**: Mrs. Henderson's cardigan, classroom plants, Sage/Grandpa's presence (imagined moments), growth metaphors, grounding moments when Ziggy feels safe, institutional calm
- **Why**: Educational, calming without being boring; represents patient authority (Mrs. H.) and generational wisdom (Sage); life/growth themes; neutral enough to fade into background but present in emotional moments
- **RGB**: (45, 138, 61) | **HSL**: 137°, 50%, 36% | **CMYK**: 67%, 0%, 56%, 46%
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AA compliant with white text (contrast ratio 5.4:1); pair with light neutral backgrounds

### Soft Lavender #D8B5E6
**Mom Sarah, introspection, emotional vulnerability, home**

- **Usage**: Mom's clothing, home interiors (bedroom, kitchen), bedtime scenes, vulnerability moments, emotional processing spaces, background washes during family scenes
- **Why**: Soft, introspective without melancholy; signals emotional safety and family bonds; reads as tender and age-appropriate; contrasts with classroom blues and orange
- **RGB**: (216, 181, 230) | **HSL**: 278°, 58%, 79% | **CMYK**: 6%, 21%, 0%, 10%
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AA when text is dark (#333333, contrast ratio 5.1:1); works as background wash with dark overlays

## Accent Colors (Surge Moments + Emotional States)

### Neon Yellow #FFFF00
**Surge's hyperfocus state, speed, clarity, moment of breakthrough**

- **Usage**: Speed lines (when Ziggy zooms), hyperfocus visual aura (background shifts to yellow when everything becomes clear), Surge's attack effects, attention markers, emphasis flashes on important dialogue, the moment Mrs. H. says "That's clever, Ziggy"
- **Why**: Unnatural (signals internal state/fantasy), high visibility, pure energy; represents the explosive clarity of hyperfocus; too intense for continuous use, so placement signals "this moment matters"
- **RGB**: (255, 255, 0) | **HSL**: 60°, 100%, 50% | **CMYK**: 0%, 0%, 100%, 0%
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AAA when text is dark (#333333, contrast ratio 19.56:1); use sparingly to avoid visual fatigue; pair with dark text only

### Deep Burgundy #5D1E3B
**Shame, overwhelm, emotional weight, struggle, heaviness**

- **Usage**: Classroom overwhelm sequences (background tint), shame spirals (Ziggy's thought bubbles during difficult moments), emotional anchors during reading struggle, weight of struggling to belong, moments when the world feels "too much"
- **Why**: Dark without being cold or villainous; signals emotional intensity and seriousness; not black (which feels evil), but heavy—it's the feeling, not a punishment; realistic portrayal of ADHD dysphoria without romanticizing it
- **RGB**: (93, 30, 59) | **HSL**: 330°, 51%, 24% | **CMYK**: 0%, 68%, 37%, 64%
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AA when paired with light text (#F5F5F5, contrast ratio 6.8:1); use as background overlay, not primary text color

## Neutral / Foundation

### Light Gray #F5F5F5
**Breathing room, classroom reality, grounding**

- **Usage**: Classroom walls, neutral backgrounds, whitespace in visual compositions, paper texture, moments of calm or stillness, default background for dialogue/text
- **Why**: Grounds the show in real school spaces; off-white (not cold white) for a softer, more lived-in feel; provides contrast for bold colors without feeling sterile
- **RGB**: (245, 245, 245) | **HSL**: 0°, 0%, 96% | **CMYK**: 0%, 0%, 0%, 4%
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AAA with dark text (#333333, contrast ratio 18.5:1)

### Charcoal #333333
**Text, outlines, grounding details**

- **Usage**: Character outlines, dialogue text, labels, structure lines, shadows
- **Why**: Not pure black (softer, more organic); reads as sophisticated and readable without harshness
- **RGB**: (51, 51, 51) | **HSL**: 0°, 0%, 20% | **CMYK**: 0%, 0%, 0%, 80%

## Palette Mood Sequence (Narrative Arc)

This palette tracks Ziggy's emotional journey through the pilot:

1. **Sunday Freedom** → Warm Orange (joy, soccer, friends, ease)
2. **Monday Dread** → Electric Blue + Light Gray (anticipation, constraint, classroom edges)
3. **Classroom Chaos** → Electric Blue + Jittery layering (overstimulation, sensory overload)
4. **Reading Struggle** → Deep Burgundy wash, Electric Blue anxiety (shame, heaviness, weight)
5. **Surge Transformation** → Neon Yellow explosion → Sage Green grounding → floaty light states (breakthrough, fantasy, clarity)
6. **Snap Back** → Light Gray + Charcoal (reality, but changed)
7. **Resolution Hint** → Warm Orange + Electric Blue balance (new possibility)

## Color Relationships & Contrast Matrix

| Primary | vs. Secondary | vs. Accent | Contrast Level |
|---------|---------------|-----------|-----------------|
| Electric Blue (#0052CC) | Warm Orange | Neon Yellow | **Very High** (simultaneous contrast) |
| Electric Blue (#0052CC) | Sage Green | Deep Burgundy | **High** (cool family tones) |
| Warm Orange (#FF8C3A) | Soft Lavender | Neon Yellow | **Very High** (warm-cool split) |
| Sage Green (#2D8A3D) | Light Gray | Deep Burgundy | **Medium-High** (analogous + dark accent) |

**Application rule**: Never place Neon Yellow and Deep Burgundy directly adjacent without a neutral buffer. They signal opposite emotional states and visual proximity creates cognitive dissonance.

## Mood Board References

This palette draws visual language from:

- **Craig of the Creek** (Cartoon Network): Warm neighborhood palette, character clothing colors pop against muted earth-tone backgrounds. Accessibility through character silhouettes + color.
- **Infinity Train** (HBO Max): Surreal color shifts between mundane reality (cool grays) and impossible fantasy (hot, saturated hues). Effective use of color to signal internal/external states.
- **Over the Garden Wall** (Cartoon Network): Dark, muted forest palette with bright accent moments (Greg's yellow coat). Color as emotional anchor; restraint makes bursts powerful.

**Key learning**: Bold colors gain power through contrast with restraint. Constant saturation = visual noise. Use primaries for character/movement, neutrals for breathing room.

## Implementation Notes

### For Animation Teams

- **Character flat fills**: Use pure palette colors; no gradients or textures in character outlines (simplifies outsourcing consistency).
- **Background washes**: Layer opacity (0.3–0.7) of secondary/accent colors over neutral base to signal mood without losing readability.
- **Lighting shifts**: Use color overlays to signal emotional/internal states rather than traditional lighting. Example: during Ziggy's shame moment, overlay Deep Burgundy at 40% opacity over the scene.
- **Particle effects**: Electric Blue zaps, Warm Orange glows (connection), Neon Yellow streaks (hyperfocus speed).

### For Outsourced Studios

All hex codes are locked. Do not:
- Adjust saturation or brightness for "style preference"
- Create gradients or add textures to character colors
- Substitute similar colors for workflow convenience

Enforce color-locking in production files (layer-naming, design tokens, swatches) to ensure consistency across teams and episodes.

## Accessibility Checklist

- [ ] All text passes WCAG AA contrast (minimum 4.5:1) over intended backgrounds
- [ ] Color is never the only visual cue (pair with pattern, shape, or motion)
- [ ] Neon Yellow used sparingly to avoid eyestrain / fatigue for viewers with light sensitivity
- [ ] Color-blind safe palette verified with **Colblindor** or **Color Brewer 2.0** tools
- [ ] Deep Burgundy + Dark Blue combinations tested with deuteranopia/protanopia simulators (common red-green colorblindness)
