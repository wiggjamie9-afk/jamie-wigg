Executive summary

Gold's role as a core strategic asset is more critical than ever, justifying a strategic overweight despite its surge past US$4,000/oz. The thesis rests on a confluence of factors: persistent geopolitical risk, secular US-dollar weakness, and expectations of a Fed easing cycle provide immediate tailwinds. More fundamentally, a structural shift in central-bank behaviour toward sustained, price-insensitive net buying — to diversify reserves away from the dollar — provides a robust price floor. This de-dollarization is a reaction to the perceived "weaponization" of the dollar, pivoting to gold as a neutral, zero-credit-risk asset.

As of late 2025, gold has delivered a +47% YTD return — its best since 1979 — and has posted positive returns in five of the last seven US recessions. Critically, the traditional negative bond-equity correlation has turned positive in the high-inflation regime, increasing gold's value as a diversifier. The market looks tactically overbought (RSI > 90 in some analyses) but positioning is not yet at a contrarian extreme: September 2025 saw record ETF inflows of $17.3B, yet holdings remain below 2020 peaks.

Central-bank de-dollarization engine

Central banks have been net buyers for 15 consecutive years, accelerating since 2022. Net purchases: 2022 1,082 t (record), 2023 1,037 t, 2024 1,045 t; 2025 244 t in Q1 with continued buying. The USD's share of global reserves fell from 71.5% (2000) to 58.4% (Q2 2024); gold's share reached ~18%. 73% of the 2025 CB survey expect the USD share to fall further.

Geopolitical risk premium

A 100-unit rise in the Caldara-Iacoviello GPR index ≈ +2.5% impact on gold return (WGC GRAM), concentrated in the index's top quintile and driven by the "Threats" sub-index ("buy the rumor" dynamic). Event study — Israel-Hamas war (7 Oct 2023): abnormal return +3.09% at t+4; cumulative +8.03% over 15 days. Safe-haven bids persist: GFC 2008 +26.5% in 12 weeks; Russia-Ukraine 2022 +11% in 11 weeks.

Current diagnostic — rich valuation, intact momentum

Mid-Oct 2025 spot ~$4,225–$4,250/oz (+57.8% YoY) despite a firm 10-yr TIPS real yield of 1.71% — a major deviation from the usual inverse correlation, implying the market prices a future collapse in real rates or is driven by CB buying + geopolitical premia. Technicals: aggregate "Strong Buy" but MACD turning negative (short-term corrective risk). Positioning supportive but not extreme — Sept 2025 ETF inflows $17.3B (146 t), holdings 3,857 t still 2% below the Nov-2020 peak; COMEX managed-money net longs below prior ATH levels.

  • Entry — trend-following breakout on GLD at a new 20-day high (backtest 2004+: avg +0.86% over next 20 days, 53% win). Optional 200-day SMA filter.
  • Stop-loss — 2× ATR below entry (volatility-adaptive, Turtle-style).
  • Profit ladder — sell 1/3 at 1:2 R:R (or +10%), 1/3 at +20%, trail the last 1/3 with a 3× ATR stop.
  • Zero-cost collar (example, GLD $303.60) — buy Sep $300 put ($8.70), sell Sep $317 call ($8.60); net debit $0.10. Caps loss ~1.2%, gain ~4.5%.

Other signals

Microstructure — COMEX GC ADV > 250k contracts (~25M oz), ~30× GLD, near 24-hr access; March-2020 showed basis risk as EFP spreads blew out. Seasonality — autumn (Sep–Oct) averages +4–7% (since 2010: +4.1%), driven by Indian festive/wedding demand (Q4 ~40% above mean), Chinese pre-CNY stocking and H2 CB buying. Currency — gold hedges local depreciation: JPY investors +41.7% (2024), +44.0% YTD (Apr-2025); EUR up in 80.8% of years since 2000. Miners — Barrick: 2025 guidance 3.15–3.50M oz at AISC $1,460–1,560; a $100/oz move ≈ ±$320M EBITDA. A 70% bullion / 30% royalty blend captures leverage while muting operational blow-up risk.

Final recommendation

Strategic overweight: 10–15% gold

  1. Phase into a 10–15% allocation, reallocating from both equity and fixed-income sleeves.
  2. Prefer tax-efficient vehicles: futures for tactical exposure, PHYS (with QEF election) for long-term holdings; avoid GLD/IAU in taxable accounts (28% collectibles rate).
  3. Execute "buy the dips" with strict rebalancing (annual or 5% threshold).
  4. Reassess on: 10-yr real yield sustained > 2.0%, DXY > 105, or a reversal in central-bank net buying. Manage tactical positions with ATR-based stops.
