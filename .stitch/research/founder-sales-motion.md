# Founder sales motion — what are you actually selling on?

> **Question this answers:** Founder-led sales works at this stage but it doesn't transfer to a hire (or to marketing copy) unless you can articulate what's *actually* closing deals. Most founders think they're selling on product; most are selling on relationship + a single demo moment.
>
> **Time:** 90 minutes solo, then a 30-minute review with one teammate.

## Step 1 — Pull the last 10 closed-won deals

For each:

| Field | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Customer name | — |
| First touch date | — |
| Close date | — |
| Days from first touch to close | — |
| First touch source | (Inbound / referral / outbound / event) |
| First call type | (Discovery / demo / casual chat) |
| Number of touches before close | — |
| Discount given? | (% if any) |
| Annual / monthly | — |
| Plan tier | — |
| The one moment they said yes (your honest read) | — |

Don't sanitise. "Discount" includes "I threw in a free month."

## Step 2 — Pull the last 10 closed-lost deals

Same fields. Add: "Why they said no (their words, not yours)."

## Step 3 — Self-diagnostic questions

> Write 1-2 sentences for each. Don't think too hard — gut answer first.

1. When you closed the last 10 deals, what made them say yes?
2. What's the demo moment that always lands? (Specific feature, screen, or story.)
3. What objection do you handle most? Word-for-word, what do you say?
4. Who do you walk away from? (And: do you actually walk, or do you discount?)
5. How long is your typical first call? What's it about?
6. If you doubled the number of demos tomorrow, what would break?
7. What's your closing line? (The thing you say right before they sign.)
8. If you had to write one sentence on the website that would make your best 10 customers nod, what would it be?

## Step 4 — Read the data

Match what you wrote to one of these patterns:

### Pattern A — Selling on **price**
- Discounts on >40% of deals
- Lost deals cite price as the reason
- Closing line involves a number going down
- Risk: doesn't scale — discounts compound, AE will discount more, gross margin crumbles
- **Action:** Build a value-led pitch deck (3 slides). Anchor on outcome value, not feature count. Set a hard discount cap and stick to it.

### Pattern B — Selling on **relationship / founder credibility**
- Most deals are inbound / referral / "I followed you on X"
- First call is casual, not structured
- Many touches before close (5+)
- Closing line is personal ("I'd love to work with you")
- Risk: doesn't transfer — first AE you hire will close at 1/3 the rate
- **Action:** Codify the founder's first call into a playbook *before* hiring. Record three calls, transcribe, extract the moves. Don't hire an AE until the playbook exists in writing.

### Pattern C — Selling on a **specific pain point**
- Deals close fast (< 14 days)
- The same word/phrase keeps showing up in why-they-said-yes
- The "one demo moment" is the same feature for every deal
- Lost deals tend to be people without that pain
- This is the gold pattern — you have positioning hidden in your sales calls
- **Action:** That pain point becomes the website headline, the first slide, the demo opener. Stop showing other features in the first 5 minutes.

### Pattern D — No qualification
- Long sales cycles (60+ days) for small deals ($89)
- Walking away from few or no deals
- Lost deals have a wide range of reasons
- Risk: founder time is the bottleneck and it's spread thin
- **Action:** Write a one-page ICP. Anyone who doesn't match in two questions gets a polite "we're not the right fit yet."

> Most founders are some mix of B and C. The instructive question is: *which one would survive removing the other?* If you took yourself out of the call but kept the pain-point pitch, would deals still close? If yes → Pattern C is real. If no → Pattern B is real, codify before hiring.

## Step 5 — One-page output

```
Dominant pattern: [A / B / C / D / mixed]

The single sentence on the website should be:
  [headline]

The discount policy from now on is:
  [rule — e.g. "no discount in first 90 days; 1 month free for annual prepay only"]

Before hiring an AE, the playbook needs:
  [list of 3-5 things — recorded calls, ICP doc, value deck, etc.]

ICP in 2 questions:
  Q1: [question that filters out 80% of bad fits]
  Q2: [question that filters the rest]
```

## Anti-patterns specific to this stage

- **Hiring an AE because demos are stacked up.** Almost always wrong at $45K MRR. Either prices are too low, or you're not qualifying, or the pain point isn't sharp enough. Fix the bottleneck, not the throughput.
- **"Selling on the team" when you mean selling on yourself.** Founders confuse these. The team isn't on the call.
- **Building a deck before you understand what's working.** A polished deck without the right one-sentence headline is dressed-up hopium.
