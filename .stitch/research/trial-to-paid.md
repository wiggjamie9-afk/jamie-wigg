# Trial → paid conversion analysis

> Behavioral-economics framing for the B2B SaaS in `SITE.md`. Audience: remote team leaders, 10–50 person companies. Goal: convert free-trial users to paid plans.

## Diagnosis: where remote-team-leader trials usually die

For a 10–50 person team SaaS with 200 beta users 6 months in, trial→paid stalls at four predictable points:

1. **Activation gap** — the leader signs up, never gets the team in. Without team data flowing, the product looks empty. Most B2B SaaS that target managers convert <5% if a second user never logs in within 7 days; >25% if they do.
2. **Decision-deferral** — leaders are busy. The trial expires while the tab is open in another window. Hyperbolic discounting, not disinterest.
3. **Authority-handoff friction** — they need finance/IT signoff. If your only path is self-serve, you lose deals that *wanted* to convert.
4. **Value attribution** — they used it, but can't articulate the win to their team or boss. No story, no purchase order.

## Frameworks worth applying (in priority order)

- **Activation, not acquisition** (Reichheld). Define one "magic moment" — e.g. "leader sees the team's first weekly summary." Measure trials by whether they hit it, not by signup count. Optimize the first 10 minutes ruthlessly.
- **Loss aversion** (Kahneman/Tversky). At day 10 of a 14-day trial, prospects experience the *threatened removal* of something they're already using as ~2× the pull of the original gain. Trigger this deliberately: "Your team's data export will be archived in 4 days unless you upgrade."
- **Choice architecture** (Thaler/Sunstein). Default to the annual plan with monthly toggle, not vice versa. Anchor with three tiers where the middle one is the obvious choice (decoy effect — make the top tier 2.2–2.5× the middle, not 1.5×).
- **Goal-gradient effect** (Kivetz). Show progress toward a meaningful threshold ("8/10 weekly check-ins captured — unlock retrospectives at 10"). Closer to the goal, harder to abandon.

## Specific moves for your stage

- A "demo call" CTA on a 14-day trial is unusually effective at the 10–50 size — these leaders prefer talking to a human over self-serve at this ACV. Lean into it; don't try to remove the human step.
- 200 beta users is a *cohort goldmine*, not vanity. Find the 20 with highest weekly active team members → they're your case studies and your testimonial source. The other 180 inform churn, not conversion.
- Pricing should be **per-seat with a team minimum** (e.g., $X/seat, min 10 seats). It anchors at "team buy" not "individual try" — closer to the actual decision.

## What to measure, weekly

- Activation rate (% of trials where ≥3 team members log in within 7 days)
- Day-10 logged-in cohort → conversion rate
- Demo-booked → conversion rate
- Per-cohort revenue retention by acquisition channel
