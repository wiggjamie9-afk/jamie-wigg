# Streaming Royalty Math Nobody Shows You — Research Brief
Date: 2026-05-10
Pillar: 4 — The Industry Lie

## 3 most interesting angles
1. **The 1,000-stream cliff.** 87% of tracks on Spotify are below the 1,000-stream-per-12-months threshold — they earn exactly $0. The "$0.003 per stream" headline already excludes most of the catalogue.
2. **The median is $1,450, not the average.** Average independent musician income in 2024 was $11,523. Median streaming income alone was $1,450. The gap is the entire story — outliers drag the average up while half of artists earn less than a phone bill.
3. **Tidal pays 4–5x more than Spotify but nobody's there.** Tidal: $0.013–$0.015. Apple Music: $0.007–$0.01. Spotify: $0.003–$0.005 (worldwide average closer to $0.00238). Yet Spotify still drives 60–75% of total revenue for indie artists because volume.

## Top 3 stats / data points
- Spotify worldwide average payout: **$0.00238 per stream**. Most indie artists report ~$0.004. — [Soundcamps](https://soundcamps.com/spotify-royalties-calculator/), [DittoMusic](https://dittomusic.com/en/blog/how-much-does-spotify-pay-per-stream)
- **87% of tracks** on Spotify earn nothing because they're under the 1,000-stream-per-year threshold. **42% of users are premium**, but they generate **90% of revenue**. — [Chartlex 2026 streaming pay](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/money/how-much-does-spotify-pay-per-stream-2026)
- **Median musician streaming income 2024: $1,450.** Only **1 in 20** full-time musicians lists streaming as their top income source. — [Two Story Media](https://twostorymedia.com/musician-income-report/), [Chartlex how musicians make money](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/money/how-musicians-make-money-2026)

## Contrarian take most people miss

The whole "streaming pays $0.003 per stream" debate is a distraction. The real number is **$0**, because 87% of tracks never clear the threshold to qualify for any payout at all. The $0.003 conversation is what artists who already won talk about. The conversation for the other 87% is "your distributor took $20 to put a track on a platform that decided it didn't exist." The fix isn't fighting Spotify for higher rates — it's stacking owned monetisation (sync, fan investment, merch, NFTs) so streams become marketing, not income.

## What to avoid

- "Streaming is broken!" hot takes — overdone. Lead with the number that does the work.
- Comparing Spotify rates to vinyl royalties from 1992. Doesn't land with creators under 35.
- Vague "pivot to merch" advice without a concrete platform / margin.
- Treating Spotify as a villain. They're a venue with bad acoustics — useful, just don't expect rent.
- Citing Bandcamp as the answer. Bandcamp's user base is too small to scale; it's a supplemental, not a replacement.
