Defendant's Answer to Complaint for Unlawful Detainer

I, Rosa Hernandez, the Defendant in this action, respectfully answer the Complaint as follows:

1. General Denial. Pursuant to Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 431.30(d), Defendant generally denies each statement of the verified Complaint.

2. Affirmative Defenses. Defendant alleges the following affirmative defenses:

a. Breach of warranty of habitability. Pursuant to Cal. Civ. Code § 1941.1 and Green v. Superior Court (1974) 10 Cal.3d 616, the premises at 1842 Foothill Blvd, Oakland CA 94601 contained substantial defects affecting habitability, including [describe — e.g., persistent mold, broken heater, plumbing leak], which Plaintiff failed to repair after written notice on [date of notice].

b. Improper notice. The 3-day notice to pay or quit served on Defendant did not comply with Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1161(2) in that it [overstated rent owed / failed to credit prior payments / improperly served].

c. Retaliation. Pursuant to Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5, this action was filed in retaliation for Defendant's good-faith complaint regarding habitability on [date].

3. Request for Jury Trial. Defendant requests trial by jury pursuant to Cal. Const. art. I, § 16.

4. Prayer for Relief. Defendant respectfully requests that the Court (a) deny Plaintiff's requested relief, (b) award Defendant costs of suit, and (c) grant such other relief as is just.

I declare under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California that the foregoing is true and correct.

Dated: May 22, 2026

Rosa Hernandez, Defendant in Pro Per

Based on what we read in the complaint and California eviction law. Check the ones that apply — they go into your Answer.

✓
Habitability — the unit wasn't livable
Mold, no heat, no hot water, infestation, broken plumbing, unsafe wiring. Even if you didn't pay rent, an uninhabitable unit reduces what you owe. Civ. Code §1941.1 · Green v. Superior Court (1974)
✓
Retaliation — landlord is punishing you
If you complained to the landlord, code enforcement, or organized with other tenants in the last 180 days, retaliation is presumed. Civ. Code §1942.5
✓
Improper notice — the 3-day notice was wrong
Common errors: rent amount overstated, didn't credit a payment, served incorrectly, demanded other charges (late fees, utilities) inside a "pay rent" notice. Any one of these often kills the case. CCP §1161(2)
✓
Just cause — Oakland's rent ordinance applies
Oakland tenants in qualifying units are protected from no-fault evictions. Your landlord must have one of the listed "just causes." Oakland Mun. Code §8.22.300
✓
Acceptance of rent waiver
If the landlord accepted rent after serving the notice, they may have waived their right to evict on that ground. EDC Block v. Tanenhaus (1956)

Filing fees

Answer to unlawful detainer: $240 — or $0 if you qualify for a fee waiver (Form FW-001). Most tenants in eviction court qualify; the form takes about 10 minutes.

If you can't make it in person

Mail your Answer with a check (or fee waiver application) so it arrives before the deadline — not just postmarked. Use certified mail with return receipt.
