Five cries · five responses

It's not one cry. It's a vocabulary.

Pre-verbal babies aren't random. They have a small lexicon — five repeating patterns that a trained ear (and a small neural net) can tell apart. LULL handles the listening so you can handle the holding.

i.

Hungry

Rhythmic, low-pitched, building. Predictable interval since last feed. Often paired with rooting.

ii.

Tired

Drawn out, escalating slowly. Eyes heavy, fussiness building over ten to fifteen minutes.

iii.

Pain

Sudden onset, high pitched, short bursts. A different shape from the others — your gut already knows.

iv.

Overstimulated

Erratic, arching, inconsolable to soothing voices. The day was too much. The room needs to be less.

v.

Colic / discomfort

Long, intense, often evening. The honest answer is: nobody knows for sure. We help you ride it out.

Coaching philosophy

Gentle. Adapted. Yours.

Not Ferber-only

Cry-it-out works for some families and is unworkable for many. In most of the markets we serve, co-sleeping is the norm and grandmothers live close. We assume that and design around it — instead of pretending the Anglophone middle-class nuclear family is the universal default.

Decision support, not commands

LULL never tells you what to do. It tells you what the cry sounds like, and offers two or three calm things to try, in your voice and in your script. You are still the parent. We're the friend who has done this before, sitting beside you at three in the morning.

Safety · plainly

What LULL is not.

LULL is decision support, not medical advice. It is built to help a tired parent at 3 AM make a slightly better call than guessing — not to replace your pediatrician, your midwife, your mother, or your own instinct.

Call a doctor if your baby has a fever (38°C+ under 3 months), unusual lethargy, refuses to feed for hours, a cry that sounds different from anything you've heard before, or you simply don't feel right about how they are. Your instinct is data. We trust it more than ours.

Cry classification is research-grade, not clinical-grade. Confidence scores are honest — when LULL says "73%", it means it. We'd rather under-promise than ask you to trust a black box at the worst hour of the night.
