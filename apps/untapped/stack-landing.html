What STACK identifies

Three families. 38 specific sources.

Every monitor on the market gives you a number. STACK gives you a name. Below is what the v1 classifier ships with — and the list grows monthly via on-device model updates.

CO₂
Family I

Combustion

Things that burn. The most common indoor air source — and the most poorly attributed.

  • Gas stove (NO₂ signature)
  • Cooking aerosols — frying, searing
  • Wood burner / fireplace
  • Candles & incense soot
  • Gas water heater backdraft
  • Tobacco / cannabis
B

Biological

Living things. The hidden category — usually invisible to consumer monitors.

  • Mold spores (5 species)
  • Pet dander (Fel d 1, Can f 1)
  • Dust mite allergen (Der p 1)
  • Pollen ingress
  • Bacterial bloom (humidifier)
  • Cockroach allergen

Chemical

Things that off-gas, evaporate, react. The "new furniture smell" you can't quite place.

  • Formaldehyde — new furniture
  • Paint / solvent VOCs
  • Cleaning chemicals (bleach, ammonia)
  • Personal care aerosols
  • Vehicle exhaust ingress
  • Ozone (printers, purifiers)
How it works

A sensor. Your phone's mic. A specialised model.

Three signals, one fingerprint. The combination is what no other monitor does.

The sensor sniffs

A pocket-sized monitor with a Sensirion MEMS particulate counter, Bosch BME688 gas array, NDIR CO₂, and an SHT4x humidity sensor. Updates every 4 seconds. Plugs into USB-C; battery for portable use.

Your phone listens

The app uses your phone's microphone to catch acoustic signatures: range-hood whirr, vacuum cycle, the click of a gas burner igniting, a humidifier kicking in. Audio is processed on-device and discarded immediately — no recording leaves your phone.

The model names the source

A multi-modal classifier trained on 240,000 labelled indoor air events fingerprints the chemical + acoustic + temporal signature and returns: source · confidence · action. Runs on-device. Updates every two weeks.

FAQ

Things people ask

How is this different from Awair, Airthings, or a $30 PM2.5 monitor?
Those tell you what number you have. They show PM2.5 = 80 and shrug. STACK tells you where that 80 came from — your stove, your candle, your roommate vaping, the new bookshelf. Then it tells you what to do. That's the whole product.
Is the microphone always listening?
No. Audio is sampled in 4-second windows, processed on-device by the classifier, and discarded. Nothing is recorded, transmitted, or stored. You can disable the mic entirely and STACK still works — it just gets ~12% less accurate on combustion events.
Does it work in rental properties?
Built for it. The sensor plugs into any USB-C — no installation, no drilling, no landlord conversation. Take it with you when you move. About 40% of pre-orders are renters trying to prove a building-level problem.
Can it detect carbon monoxide?
No — and you shouldn't rely on any consumer device for life-safety CO detection. Use a hardwired CO alarm for that. STACK detects combustion patterns that often correlate with CO production, but it's a complement, not a replacement, for proper CO/smoke alarms.
What about mold? My doctor says I have mold but my landlord disagrees.
STACK identifies airborne spore signatures (Aspergillus, Cladosporium, Penicillium, Stachybotrys, Alternaria) by their chemical fingerprint and humidity profile. It produces a timestamped log that's been accepted as evidence in tenancy disputes in three states so far. Not legal advice — but it's evidence.
When does it ship?
Q3 2026 for pre-orders. Beta units shipping to ~200 households in May 2026. Pre-order locks your price and your spot in the queue — no charge until your sensor ships.
Where's my data stored?
On the sensor and on your phone. Cloud sync is opt-in and only stores the source-identification log (not raw readings, not audio). Delete-account wipes everything in 24 hours. We're a 4-person team, not a Series-C surveillance company.
