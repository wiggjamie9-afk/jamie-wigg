# Reference: Sandler Selling System Prompts

## Purpose
The Sandler Selling System (David Sandler) inverts traditional sales: the rep qualifies hard, disqualifies fast, and never gives away advice or strategy without a commitment in return. The goal is equal business stature — a peer-to-peer conversation, not a vendor pitching a prospect. These prompts help a rep set up-front contracts, work the Pain Funnel, qualify budget and decision process honestly, and avoid the trap of free consulting. Not templates — starting points.

## The framework
- **Up-front contracts (UFCs):** Agreements at the start of every call on the agenda, time, outcomes, and what happens at the end. No ambiguity about next steps.
- **Pain Funnel:** A structured probing sequence that moves from surface-level complaint → real business impact → personal emotional impact. Pain is the buying motive in Sandler.
- **Budget (BUDGET):** Qualified directly and early. A prospect who won't discuss budget is not qualified. Sandler challenges reps to raise this before investing time.
- **Decision (DEC):** Understand the decision-making process, the people involved, and the timeline — confirmed, not assumed.
- **Reverse selling:** Instead of pitching, ask the buyer to articulate why they'd want to move forward. The rep holds back, which creates pull rather than push.
- **Equal business stature:** The rep is a peer, not a supplicant. Sandler reps do not chase, beg for meetings, or over-accommodate.
- **No free consulting:** The rep does not share methodology, strategy, or a solution until the buyer has committed to a mutual evaluation process and the rep has confirmed qualification.
- **Dummy curve / negative reverse selling:** Use strategic self-deprecation or apparent confusion to surface the buyer's true objection rather than debating the stated one.

## Prompt 01 — Set an Up-Front Contract
**Role:** Account Executive / Sales Development Rep
**Trigger:** At the opening of every sales call, whether cold, warm, or deep in the funnel
**Structure:** State the time available → propose the agenda → state your outcome for the call → state the buyer's possible outcomes → ask for confirmation
**Example output:** "We've got 30 minutes — does that still work? My plan was to spend the first half understanding where things stand for you today, and the second half on whether it makes sense to go further. At the end, you'll either say 'this is interesting, let's continue' or 'not for us right now' — and either one is a fine outcome. Does that work for you, [Name]?"
**Why it works:** Up-front contracts eliminate the ambiguity that produces ghosting and vague follow-ups. When both parties know what the end of the call looks like, the rep can qualify decisively rather than fishing for enthusiasm.
**Word count:** 65–80 words
**Avg. score:** 82–89

## Prompt 02 — Open the Pain Funnel
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Immediately after the UFC; moving into discovery of the real problem
**Structure:** Ask a broad question about what brings them to the conversation → resist offering a solution → follow each answer with "tell me more about that"
**Example output:** "What made you take the call today — what's going on at [Company] that made this feel worth 30 minutes? [Pause.] Tell me more about that — how long has that been the case, and is this something that's been building or did something specific happen recently?"
**Why it works:** Sandler's Pain Funnel starts wide and drills down. The rep's job is to ask and listen, not interpret or fix. Buyers who feel heard go deeper; buyers who feel sold to go quiet.
**Word count:** 45–60 words
**Avg. score:** 83–89

## Prompt 03 — Drill to Emotional Pain
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** After the buyer has described a business problem; moving below the surface
**Structure:** Acknowledge the business impact → ask what it costs the buyer personally → make it safe to be honest
**Example output:** "That sounds like it creates real friction for the business — what does it do to you personally? Is it the kind of thing that keeps you up at night, causes difficult conversations, or is it more a constant low-grade frustration? I ask because solving the business problem but not the one that affects you directly isn't really solving it."
**Why it works:** Sandler holds that emotional pain is the true buying motive. Functional problems get evaluated rationally; emotional problems create urgency and internal advocacy. A rep who only finds business pain leaves the strongest buying signal untouched.
**Word count:** 55–70 words
**Avg. score:** 84–90

## Prompt 04 — Qualify Budget Early
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** After pain is confirmed; before investing more than one conversation
**Structure:** Raise budget without apology → frame it as mutual respect for time → make it easy to answer honestly
**Example output:** "Before we go further, I want to make sure we're working in realistic territory for both of us. Solutions that address [problem] at [Company]'s scale typically run in the [$X–$Y] range annually. Is that a conversation that's even possible for you right now — or is this more exploratory for budget planning purposes? Either answer is fine, I just want to be useful to you."
**Why it works:** Sandler treats budget qualification as respect, not aggression. Reps who avoid it waste cycles on unqualified deals. Raising it early and framing it as mutual efficiency is standard Sandler practice.
**Word count:** 65–80 words
**Avg. score:** 81–88

## Prompt 05 — Confirm the Decision Process
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** After budget is qualified; before proposing next steps
**Structure:** Ask who else is involved → ask how decisions like this have been made before → ask what would have to be true to move forward
**Example output:** "[Name], help me understand how decisions like this typically get made at [Company]. Is this something you can move on, or does it involve other people or a committee? And if we get to a point where you want to proceed, what does that process actually look like — from 'yes, we want this' to a signed agreement? I want to make sure I understand all the steps."
**Why it works:** Sandler's decision qualification prevents late-stage surprises. A buyer who will not explain the decision process is either not the real decision-maker or is not serious. Either finding saves significant time.
**Word count:** 65–80 words
**Avg. score:** 82–89

## Prompt 06 — Use Reverse Selling
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When the buyer seems interested but passive; to create pull rather than push
**Structure:** Withhold the pitch → ask the buyer what they think would need to be true for this to work for them → let the buyer sell themselves
**Example output:** "Based on what you've told me, I think there's probably something here — but I'm curious what you think. Does what you've heard so far feel like it could actually solve [problem] for you, or are there gaps that would make it hard to move forward? I'd rather hear your honest take than walk you through a presentation that doesn't fit."
**Why it works:** Reverse selling surfaces the buyer's real objections and motivations without the rep having to guess. When buyers articulate why something would work for them, they have effectively pre-sold themselves — which is far more durable than a rep's pitch.
**Word count:** 60–75 words
**Avg. score:** 84–90

## Prompt 07 — Defend Equal Business Stature
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When the buyer attempts to set an unequal dynamic — asking for deep advice before committing to an evaluation, or using the rep as free research
**Structure:** Acknowledge the request → hold position diplomatically → redirect to a mutual commitment that earns the detail
**Example output:** "I'm happy to go deep on [topic] — that's where the real value is. What I've learned is that the most useful way to do that is inside a proper evaluation where I understand your full picture, not a one-off conversation where I'm working with partial context. If that kind of structured process makes sense to you, I'll give you everything I have. Does that work?"
**Why it works:** Sandler's no-free-consulting principle protects the rep's time and prevents the buyer from using the sales process as free consulting. Equal business stature is not arrogance — it is the signal that the rep's expertise has value and conditions.
**Word count:** 70–85 words
**Avg. score:** 82–89

## Prompt 08 — Handle the "Think It Over" Stall
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** At the end of a call when the buyer says "we need to think about it" or "let's stay in touch"
**Structure:** Use negative reverse selling — make it safe to say no → invite honesty → surface the real objection
**Example output:** "Totally understand. But between us — and I'd rather know the honest answer now than chase you for six weeks — is this something that's genuinely worth exploring further, or is 'think it over' a polite way of saying it's not the right fit right now? No judgment either way. I'd rather close the loop cleanly than waste your time."
**Why it works:** Sandler reps do not chase. The "think it over" stall is almost always a buried objection or a soft no. Inviting the buyer to be direct respects their time, preserves the rep's dignity, and often surfaces the real issue that a genuine conversation can resolve.
**Word count:** 65–80 words
**Avg. score:** 83–90
