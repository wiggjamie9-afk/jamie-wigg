# Reference: MEDDPICC Prompts

## Purpose
MEDDPICC is a qualification framework for complex B2B sales that forces reps to verify the mechanics of every deal before investing time. These prompts help a rep surface missing information, coach champions, and pressure-test close dates against hard evidence. Not templates — starting points.

## The framework
- **M — Metrics:** Quantified business impact the buyer expects (time saved, revenue gained, cost reduced).
- **E — Economic Buyer:** The single person with budget authority and final sign-off power.
- **D — Decision Criteria:** The explicit criteria the buying committee uses to evaluate solutions.
- **D — Decision Process:** The sequence of steps, approvals, and gates between now and a signed order.
- **I — Identify Pain:** The specific, acute business problem driving urgency — and its cost if unsolved.
- **P — Paper Process:** Legal, procurement, security review, and any other contractual steps post-verbal yes.
- **C — Champion:** An internal advocate with power and motivation to sell for you when you're not in the room.
- **C — Competition:** Who else is being evaluated, their strengths, and how the buyer perceives each option.

## Prompt 01 — Quantify Metrics
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** After discovery; before preparing a business case or proposal
**Structure:** Restate the problem the buyer named → ask for a number → anchor to a time horizon → confirm who owns the metric
**Example output:** "You mentioned that reconciling reports manually takes your team around eight hours a week per analyst. If we could cut that to under an hour, what would that be worth to the business over the next 12 months — and who at [Company] would be tracking that as a success metric?"
**Why it works:** MEDDPICC scores deals on quantified outcomes, not anecdote. Pinning a dollar or hour figure creates a shared "cost of inaction" that justifies both the purchase price and the urgency.
**Word count:** 40–60 words per turn
**Avg. score:** 84–91

## Prompt 02 — Surface the Economic Buyer
**Role:** Account Executive / Sales Manager
**Trigger:** When the rep has only spoken with a champion or influencer, not a budget holder
**Structure:** Acknowledge the champion's role → ask who has final authority → seek a path to access
**Example output:** "I want to make sure we're putting the right information in front of the right people at [Company]. Beyond yourself, who ultimately signs off on an investment of this size — and would it make sense to include them in our next session so we can tailor the business case to what they care most about?"
**Why it works:** Deals stall when economic buyers are not engaged early. This prompt surfaces them without undermining the champion by framing access as helpfulness, not distrust.
**Word count:** 50–70 words per turn
**Avg. score:** 82–89

## Prompt 03 — Uncover Decision Criteria
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Early in the evaluation, before a vendor shortlist is formed
**Structure:** Ask what "ideal" looks like → probe for must-haves vs. nice-to-haves → ask who defined the criteria
**Example output:** "When [Company]'s team sits down to compare options, what does the scorecard look like? Are there non-negotiables — security certifications, integration requirements, SLAs — that would knock a vendor out immediately? And who built that list?"
**Why it works:** Knowing the criteria lets you shape how they're weighted. If you discover them late, a competitor who arrived earlier has already influenced the evaluation in their favour.
**Word count:** 40–55 words per turn
**Avg. score:** 85–90

## Prompt 04 — Map the Decision Process
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** After a verbal indication of interest; before agreeing on a timeline
**Structure:** Ask for the next step → ask what happens after → surface dependencies and veto points
**Example output:** "[Name], assuming the demo goes well, what does the path to a signed agreement look like from there? Is there a security review, a procurement committee, or a legal redline process that typically runs in parallel — and are there any steps that have tripped up deals like this in the past?"
**Why it works:** Most slipped deals are caused by unknown steps in the process. Mapping the paper trail before it matters lets the rep compress cycle time and plan realistic close dates.
**Word count:** 50–65 words per turn
**Avg. score:** 83–90

---

## Prompt 05 — Identify Pain (Depth Probe)
**Role:** Account Executive / Sales Development Rep
**Trigger:** During initial discovery when the buyer states a surface-level problem
**Structure:** Reflect the stated problem → ask what it costs → ask how long it has existed → ask why now
**Example output:** "You mentioned that customer onboarding takes three weeks longer than it should. How long has that been the case, and what has that delay cost you — in customer satisfaction scores, churn, or headcount? What's made this a priority to fix right now?"
**Why it works:** MEDDPICC treats vague pain as unqualified pain. Probing depth reveals whether the problem is truly acute or merely a preference — and separates sponsors from curious bystanders.
**Word count:** 40–55 words per turn
**Avg. score:** 86–92

## Prompt 06 — Navigate Paper Process
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** After a verbal commitment; before submitting a contract
**Structure:** Congratulate the decision → ask about legal and procurement steps → surface InfoSec and DPA requirements → agree on a realistic signed date
**Example output:** "Fantastic — glad we're aligned on moving forward. Before I send the agreement, help me understand what [Company]'s process looks like from here. Do contracts go through a procurement or legal review? Is there a security questionnaire or data processing addendum required? I want to make sure our target date of [date] is realistic."
**Why it works:** The paper process is where deals die silently. Surfacing it at verbal yes rather than contract send prevents weeks of unexpected delay from erasing a hard-won commit.
**Word count:** 55–70 words per turn
**Avg. score:** 81–88

---

## Prompt 07 — Coach the Champion
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Between buying committee meetings when the rep has no direct access to decision-makers
**Structure:** Share a specific insight the champion can use → provide a talk track they can own → ask what objections they're hearing internally
**Example output:** "Here's something that tends to land well with CFOs at companies your size: the first-year ROI model showing $[X] saved in [category]. Would it be useful to walk through how to present that to [Name at Company], and what pushback you might get? What's the current sentiment in the room?"
**Why it works:** A champion who can't sell internally is just a fan. MEDDPICC requires a champion with power and the ability to navigate internal politics. This prompt builds both.
**Word count:** 50–65 words per turn
**Avg. score:** 85–91

---

## Prompt 08 — Handle Competition
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When the buyer mentions another vendor or the rep suspects a competitive evaluation
**Structure:** Acknowledge the market openly → ask how the buyer is differentiating options → surface the criteria where you win → plant a question to stress-test the competitor
**Example output:** "We often see [Company] evaluating us alongside [Competitor]. That makes sense given the market. How are you thinking about the tradeoffs between the two approaches? One thing our customers tell us matters most is [specific differentiator] — has that come up in your conversations with them?"
**Why it works:** MEDDPICC demands honest competitive intelligence, not avoidance. Understanding how the buyer perceives the competition lets the rep reframe criteria rather than pitch features in a vacuum.
**Word count:** 45–65 words per turn
**Avg. score:** 83–89
