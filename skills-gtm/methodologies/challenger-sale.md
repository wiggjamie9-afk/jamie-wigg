# Reference: The Challenger Sale Prompts

## Purpose
The Challenger Sale (Matthew Dixon & Brent Adamson, CEB/Gartner research) reframes the rep's role: instead of building rapport and responding to stated needs, Challengers teach buyers something new about their business, tailor the message to the buyer's specific priorities, and take control of the sales conversation. These prompts help a rep deliver commercial insight, reframe the buyer's thinking, and create constructive tension that drives decisions. Not templates — starting points.

## The framework
- **Teach:** Open with a provocative, data-backed commercial insight that reframes how the buyer thinks about a problem or opportunity — one the buyer did not already know.
- **Tailor:** Adapt the teaching to what the specific economic buyer cares about: their KPIs, their industry pressures, their risk profile. One message does not fit all stakeholders.
- **Take Control:** Guide the buying process proactively. Be willing to push back on timelines, budget objections, and status-quo bias rather than accommodating them.
- **Commercial insight:** The core mechanism of the Challenger model. A genuine commercial insight leads to the rep's unique solution — it is not a feature pitch with a story in front of it.
- **Reframe:** Surface a problem the buyer does not know they have, or show that their current framing of a known problem is wrong in a way that costs them.
- **Constructive tension:** Challengers create discomfort with the status quo. They do not cave to pushback; they acknowledge it and redirect to the evidence.
- **Contrast with Relationship Builder:** Dixon and Adamson's data showed that Relationship Builders — the most common rep profile — perform worst in complex sales. Challengers perform best, especially in a difficult economy.

## Prompt 01 — Lead with a Commercial Insight
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Opening a presentation to a new economic buyer or executive sponsor
**Structure:** Open with a counterintuitive finding relevant to the buyer's industry → tie it to a business consequence they own → lead to your solution as the natural resolution
**Example output:** "Most [industry] leaders we talk to are focused on [expected initiative]. What they don't realise is that [counterintuitive finding from data or research] — and that's quietly costing companies your size around [quantified impact]. Let me show you what we're seeing, and what the leaders who've reversed that trend did differently."
**Why it works:** Buying intent is created when a rep teaches the buyer something they didn't know. If the buyer already believes what you're saying, you have no insight — you have confirmation. The insight must be genuinely new and tied to the rep's solution.
**Word count:** 50–70 words
**Avg. score:** 86–92

## Prompt 02 — Reframe the Problem
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When the buyer has already defined the problem — often incorrectly or incompletely
**Structure:** Acknowledge the stated problem → introduce evidence that the root cause is different → invite the buyer to reconsider
**Example output:** "You've described this as a [stated problem] — and I understand why it looks that way. But in the 30 companies we've worked with who presented the same symptoms, the root cause was almost always [actual root cause]. Here's why that matters: fixing [stated problem] without addressing [root cause] typically produces short-term gains that erode within [timeframe]."
**Why it works:** Reframing is the core move of the Challenger model. A buyer who accepts a new frame needs a new solution — and if the rep built the frame, they have shaped the criteria in their favour.
**Word count:** 55–75 words
**Avg. score:** 85–91

## Prompt 03 — Tailor to the Economic Buyer
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Preparing an executive-level presentation; tailoring after a discovery call with a lower-level champion
**Structure:** Identify the economic buyer's top two metrics → reframe the insight to those metrics → remove everything that is not relevant to their level
**Example output:** "For [Name], the CFO frame is really about risk and efficiency: what does [problem] cost in write-offs or rework, and what's the payback period on fixing it? I'll cut the product deep-dive from the deck and lead with the financial model — [Name] doesn't need to know how it works, they need to know what happens to margin if they do vs. don't act."
**Why it works:** Challengers tailor relentlessly. A message optimised for a VP of Operations will lose a CFO in the first 60 seconds. Tailoring is not softening — it is precision.
**Word count:** 55–70 words
**Avg. score:** 83–90

## Prompt 04 — Assert, Don't Capitulate
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When the buyer says "your price is too high" or "we're not sure the timing is right"
**Structure:** Acknowledge the objection → hold position with evidence → redirect to cost of inaction
**Example output:** "I hear you on the price — it's a real number. What I'd ask you to weigh against it is what [problem] is costing [Company] right now, which we calculated at [figure] per [period]. At that run rate, the payback period on our solution is [X months]. Does the timing objection change if we look at it through that lens?"
**Why it works:** Dixon and Adamson found that Challengers push back constructively rather than discounting to close resistance. Caving on price signals low confidence and trains buyers to apply pressure in every deal.
**Word count:** 55–70 words
**Avg. score:** 84–90

## Prompt 05 — Take Control of the Process
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When the buyer says "send me a proposal and we'll review it" with no committed next step
**Structure:** Decline to send a proposal cold → propose a structured next step instead → explain why this benefits the buyer
**Example output:** "I could send something over, but in my experience proposals reviewed without context get set aside or misread — and that wastes everyone's time. What I'd like to do instead is spend 30 minutes walking [Name] through the business case so I can tailor it to what matters most to them. Would [day] work for a call with you and [economic buyer]?"
**Why it works:** Challengers control the buying process. Sending unsolicited proposals is a Relationship Builder behaviour — it feels helpful but hands over control and reduces urgency. Proposing a structured session keeps momentum.
**Word count:** 60–75 words
**Avg. score:** 82–89

## Prompt 06 — Introduce Constructive Tension
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When the buyer is comfortable with the status quo and not acknowledging urgency
**Structure:** Name the status quo explicitly → quantify what it is costing → create discomfort with staying put without being adversarial
**Example output:** "The current approach works — I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But 'works' is doing a lot of heavy lifting. Based on what you've shared, [status quo process] is producing [specific inefficiency or cost] every [period]. At that rate, staying put is a decision, and it has a price tag. I want to make sure that's an explicit tradeoff you're making, not a default."
**Why it works:** Challengers are not afraid to make the buyer uncomfortable. Constructive tension disrupts complacency without being aggressive — it reframes inaction as a choice with consequences, not a neutral default.
**Word count:** 60–75 words
**Avg. score:** 85–91

## Prompt 07 — Deliver the Unique Value Wedge
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When positioning against the status quo or a competitor; the "why us" moment
**Structure:** Identify the one thing your solution does that the alternatives do not → connect it directly to the business outcome the buyer cares about → make it memorable and specific
**Example output:** "Every vendor in this space will tell you they [common claim]. What none of them can show you is [specific differentiator], because [brief reason it is unique]. For [Company], that matters specifically because [direct tie to buyer's stated priority]. That's the gap we close that the others can't."
**Why it works:** The Challenger model's unique value wedge is not a feature — it is a commercially relevant differentiation tied to a consequence. Reps who skip this and list features lose to incumbents every time.
**Word count:** 45–65 words
**Avg. score:** 83–90

---

## Prompt 08 — Coach a Champion to Teach Internally
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** After delivering a commercial insight; prepping a champion to carry the message to the economic buyer
**Structure:** Summarise the core insight in one sentence the champion can own → give them a talk track → anticipate the challenge they will receive and prep them for it
**Example output:** "The one thing I'd want [Economic Buyer] to take away from your conversation is this: [one-sentence version of the insight]. If they push back with 'we've already tried something similar,' the response is: 'That was [prior approach], which only addressed [symptom]. This addresses [root cause] — here's the evidence.' Would it help to run through that briefly before you go in?"
**Why it works:** Challengers coach champions to teach — not just to advocate. A champion who can replicate the commercial insight inside the organisation becomes a force multiplier; one who only says "I really liked the demo" does not.
**Word count:** 65–80 words
**Avg. score:** 84–91
