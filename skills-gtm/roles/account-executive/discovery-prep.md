# Reference: AE Discovery Prep Prompts

## Purpose
A curated collection of pre-call and in-call prompts for Account Executives preparing for discovery conversations. Calibrated for mid-market and enterprise sales where research depth directly affects first-call conversion. Each prompt is designed to surface insight, not just information — turning background reading into sharp hypotheses and pointed questions before you pick up the phone. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Pre-Call Company Snapshot
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** 48 hours before a first discovery call
**Structure:** Company name > industry > recent signal > specific question
**Example output:** "[Company] just announced a Series B and is hiring 40 SDRs in Q3. Draft five pain hypotheses a VP of Sales at a high-growth SaaS company typically faces when scaling outbound from 5 to 20 reps — focus on tooling fragmentation, onboarding lag, and pipeline quality."
**Why it works:** Anchoring hypotheses to a real hiring signal makes your pre-call guesses genuinely predictive rather than generic, so your questions in discovery feel prescient rather than scripted.
**Word count:** Keep output under 300 words; skim-readable before the call.
**Avg. score:** 84-92

---

## Prompt 02 — Stakeholder Power Map
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When two or more contacts are involved in the deal
**Structure:** Role list > org structure clue > request for influence mapping
**Example output:** "I'm meeting [Name], VP Ops, and [Name], CFO, at [Company]. The VP Ops introduced the CFO last week. Map likely decision authority, potential champion vs. economic buyer tension, and the question each person will privately want answered in this call."
**Why it works:** Most AEs over-index on the champion and neglect the CFO's silent veto; the prompt forces explicit thinking about each stakeholder's hidden agenda before you walk in.
**Word count:** One paragraph per stakeholder, max 400 words total.
**Avg. score:** 80-89

---

## Prompt 03 — Pain Hypothesis Builder
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** After reviewing a prospect's LinkedIn, website, and any inbound intent signals
**Structure:** Observed signals > industry context > three ranked pain hypotheses
**Example output:** "Based on [Company]'s job postings for three RevOps analysts and their recent Gartner CRM summit sponsorship, generate three ranked pain hypotheses — ranked by likelihood — that would make a new revenue operations platform urgent rather than nice-to-have."
**Why it works:** Ranking forces prioritisation; walking into discovery with a ranked list means you can test hypothesis one early and pivot cleanly if it doesn't land.
**Word count:** 50-80 words per hypothesis with a one-line confidence rationale.
**Avg. score:** 83-90

---

## Prompt 04 — Question Bank by Stage
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When building a discovery call agenda for a specific deal stage
**Structure:** Stage label > personas attending > outcome needed > generate 12 questions
**Example output:** "I'm running a Stage 2 discovery with a Director of IT and their Security Architect at [Company]. I need to surface integration complexity and compliance requirements. Generate 12 open questions that progress from situation to implication — avoid anything answerable with a yes/no."
**Why it works:** Specifying the SPIN progression (situation → implication) in the prompt produces questions that build momentum rather than a checklist that stalls the conversation.
**Word count:** 12 questions, one line each; group under situation / problem / implication headers.
**Avg. score:** 85-93

## Prompt 05 — Competitor Landscape Briefing
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** When the prospect is known to be evaluating one or more named competitors
**Structure:** Named competitors > shared prospect profile > generate differentiation talking points
**Example output:** "[Company] is also looking at [Competitor A] and [Competitor B]. Our strengths are native CRM integration and sub-24-hour onboarding. Write a one-page briefing on likely objections they will raise in favour of each competitor, and draft one reframe for each."
**Why it works:** Pre-loading reframes — not rebuttals — means you respond to competitive mentions with genuine curiosity rather than defensive positioning, which builds trust faster.
**Word count:** One objection + one reframe per competitor; max 80 words each.
**Avg. score:** 82-91

## Prompt 06 — Agenda Email Pre-Send
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** 24 hours before discovery; sending a confirmation and agenda to the prospect
**Structure:** Meeting goal > attendees > two or three agenda items > soft question to prime thinking
**Example output:** "Write a 120-word agenda email for tomorrow's 30-minute call with [Name] at [Company]. Goal: understand their current sales cycle bottlenecks. Include one pre-read question they can consider in advance — something that signals we've done our homework without being presumptuous."
**Why it works:** A pre-read question shifts the prospect from passive to active before the call, shortening the context-setting phase so you spend more time on real discovery.
**Word count:** 100-130 words; subject line under 8 words.
**Avg. score:** 81-88

---

## Prompt 07 — Trigger Event Interpretation
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** A prospect has just experienced a relevant external event (funding, leadership change, acquisition, product launch, earnings miss)
**Structure:** Event description > company context > three opportunity angles
**Example output:** "[Company] announced the departure of their CRO last Tuesday. They're a 200-person SaaS company in logistics. Give me three angles on how this creates urgency for a sales enablement conversation — and the opening line I should use in my outreach."
**Why it works:** Trigger events have a short half-life; interpreting them quickly and through the lens of pain — not product — is what separates relevant outreach from noise.
**Word count:** Three angles, 50 words each; opening line under 20 words.
**Avg. score:** 86-93

---

## Prompt 08 — Multi-Threading Intro Request
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** A deal is progressing with a single champion; you need to introduce additional stakeholders
**Structure:** Champion name/role > roles you want to add > deal stage > draft introduction request
**Example output:** "My champion [Name] is a VP of Marketing at [Company]. I need to get in front of the CFO before proposal stage. Draft a short message [Name] can forward internally that frames the next conversation as being about risk and ROI — not about our product."
**Why it works:** Framing the introduction around the CFO's currency (risk, ROI) rather than your product removes the champion's fear that they're creating extra work for a sceptical colleague.
**Word count:** Forwarding message under 80 words; feels written by the champion, not the AE.
**Avg. score:** 80-87

---

## Prompt 09 — ICP Fit Scorecard
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** A new inbound or outbound lead needs rapid qualification before spending discovery time
**Structure:** Prospect data points > ICP criteria > score and narrative summary
**Example output:** "Score this inbound lead against our ICP: 150-employee B2B SaaS, Series A, VP of Sales title, US-based, uses Salesforce. Our ICP is 100-500 employees, B2B, series A-C, revenue ops or sales leadership buyer, CRM-native integrations required. Output a score out of 10 with a two-line rationale and one disqualifying question to ask on the first call."
**Why it works:** Including a disqualifying question in the prompt forces honest qualification thinking rather than optimistic scoring — protecting pipeline integrity from the first touchpoint.
**Word count:** Score + two-line rationale + one question; under 100 words.
**Avg. score:** 82-90

---

## Prompt 10 — SPIN Flow Planner
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Preparing for a discovery call where a specific business pain has been hinted at in email exchange
**Structure:** Hinted pain > company context > draft SPIN progression for a 20-minute segment
**Example output:** "[Name] mentioned in email that their team 'struggles to keep demos consistent across reps.' They're a 30-person sales team at [Company]. Design a SPIN question flow for a 20-minute discovery segment: four situation questions, three problem questions, three implication questions, and two need-payoff questions — with transition phrases between sections."
**Why it works:** Writing out transition phrases prevents the interview from feeling like an interrogation; smooth bridges between question types signal genuine listening rather than a scripted checklist.
**Word count:** 12-14 questions with transitions; print-ready for call notes.
**Avg. score:** 87-94
