# Reference: AE ROI & Business Case Prompts

## Purpose
A set of prompts for Account Executives building cost-of-inaction arguments, quantified value cases, and written business cases for economic buyers. Calibrated for deals where price scrutiny, procurement involvement, or CFO sign-off require more than a capabilities pitch. These prompts bridge the gap between discovered pain and a number the buyer can take to their leadership team. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Cost-of-Inaction Quantifier
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** After discovery surfaces a measurable pain but the prospect hasn't connected it to a dollar figure
**Structure:** Pain description > available data points > generate cost-of-inaction calculation
**Example output:** "[Company] has 30 reps, average quota of $800K, and told me new reps take 4 months to ramp vs. the 2-month industry benchmark. Build a cost-of-inaction model that calculates lost pipeline per rep per month of excess ramp time, and express it as an annual cost to the business."
**Why it works:** Anchoring to a number the prospect gave you (their ramp data, quota size) makes the model feel discovered rather than imposed — the buyer is more likely to defend a number they originated.
**Word count:** Model in a short table or three-line calculation; narrative summary under 80 words.
**Avg. score:** 86-93

## Prompt 02 — Value Hypothesis Email
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** After a strong discovery call; sending a follow-up that plants the ROI seed before the formal proposal
**Structure:** Three discovered pains > paired capability > draft value hypothesis email to champion
**Example output:** "Draft a 150-word email to [Name], VP of Sales at [Company], summarising the three gaps we uncovered — inconsistent onboarding, no deal-risk visibility, manual forecasting — and pairing each with a rough value hypothesis. Avoid product names or pricing. This should feel like a shared diagnosis, not a sales pitch."
**Why it works:** A pre-proposal value email lets the champion road-test the ROI story internally before you're in the room, surfacing objections early when you still have time to address them.
**Word count:** 130-160 words; three pain-value pairs in brief bullet format.
**Avg. score:** 83-91

---

## Prompt 03 — Payback Period Framing
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Prospect raises price concern or asks "what's the payback period?"
**Structure:** Annual contract value > quantified value metric > generate payback period statement
**Example output:** "Our ACV is $72,000. We've calculated that [Company]'s ramp-time reduction saves them approximately $18,000 per rep per quarter across 30 reps. Generate a clear payback period statement and a one-sentence reframe that positions the investment as a Q3 decision with Q4 return — not a cost but a timing question."
**Why it works:** A payback period reframed as a timing question changes the buyer's internal conversation from "can we afford this?" to "when does this pay back?" — a fundamentally different and more solvable problem.
**Word count:** Calculation in two lines; reframe in one sentence under 25 words.
**Avg. score:** 84-92

---

## Prompt 04 — CFO-Ready Business Case Structure
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Champion has sign-off but needs CFO approval before contract
**Structure:** Company profile > pain summary > generate a one-page business case outline for a CFO
**Example output:** "[Company] is a 300-person SaaS company. The VP of Sales wants to improve forecast accuracy and reduce rep ramp time. The CFO has not been in any calls. Outline a one-page business case — executive summary, problem statement, quantified opportunity, solution overview, cost, payback, and risk of inaction — that [Name] could present without us in the room."
**Why it works:** Building a document the champion can present without you means they don't have to schedule another meeting to get CFO buy-in — removing a gatekeeping delay that typically costs two weeks.
**Word count:** Section headings + 2-3 bullet points each; full outline max 400 words.
**Avg. score:** 85-92

## Prompt 05 — Competing Priority Reframe
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Prospect says "we have bigger priorities right now" or budget is being allocated elsewhere
**Structure:** Named competing priority > shared outcome > reframe our solution as enabling their priority
**Example output:** "[Company] told me they're prioritising a Salesforce implementation over the next six months and don't have budget for anything else. Our product integrates with Salesforce and reduces admin overhead by 40%. Draft a reframe that positions our solution not as a competing budget line but as an accelerant to their Salesforce rollout success."
**Why it works:** Competing with another priority is unwinnable; positioning as an accelerant of that priority turns a zero-sum budget conversation into an additive one.
**Word count:** 60-80 words; avoid "actually, our product..." constructions.
**Avg. score:** 82-89

---

## Prompt 06 — Benchmark Comparison Build
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Prospect wants to know how their current performance compares to peers before committing to change
**Structure:** Metric name > prospect's current data > industry benchmark > gap narrative
**Example output:** "[Company] told me their average sales cycle is 94 days. Our customer base average is 62 days for comparable deal sizes. Build a benchmark comparison that frames the 32-day gap in revenue terms — how much more closed business per quarter would that represent for their team at their average ACV?"
**Why it works:** Benchmark data is compelling precisely because it isn't your number — it reflects what peers are achieving, which creates competitive pressure that price negotiations can't easily neutralise.
**Word count:** Gap calculation in two lines; revenue implication in one sentence.
**Avg. score:** 83-90

---

## Prompt 07 — Three-Scenario Model
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Prospect is risk-averse and wants to see downside protection before committing
**Structure:** Optimistic / realistic / conservative assumptions > generate three-scenario ROI summary
**Example output:** "[Company] is cautious about projected outcomes. Build a three-scenario ROI summary — conservative (20% of projected gains), realistic (55%), and optimistic (90%) — based on their 30-rep team, $800K average quota, and our benchmarked 30% ramp improvement. Show break-even in each scenario."
**Why it works:** The conservative scenario does the most selling work — if the buyer can see a break-even even in the downside case, the risk calculus shifts dramatically in your favour.
**Word count:** Three-row table with scenario label, assumption, ROI, and break-even month.
**Avg. score:** 87-94

## Prompt 08 — Price Justification Narrative
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Prospect has received the proposal and pushes back on the price without a specific counter
**Structure:** Contract value > value delivered > draft a price justification narrative (not a discount offer)
**Example output:** "Our proposal to [Company] is $84,000 annually. They pushed back saying it 'feels expensive.' We've quantified $310,000 in annualised value from reduced ramp time and improved forecast accuracy. Draft a 100-word response that reframes the price relative to the value quantified — without discounting, apologising, or asking what number they had in mind."
**Why it works:** Responding to vague price pushback with the value ratio — not a counter-number — forces the buyer to either engage with the ROI argument or name a specific number, both of which move the conversation forward.
**Word count:** 90-110 words; no discount language; end on a question.
**Avg. score:** 82-90

---

## Prompt 09 — Hidden Cost Excavator
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Prospect believes their current process or incumbent vendor "is working fine"
**Structure:** Current state description > cost categories to surface > generate hidden cost questions
**Example output:** "[Company] uses spreadsheets and Slack for pipeline management. They think the current process is free because there's no software cost. Generate eight questions I can ask in a business case conversation that surface the hidden costs: manager time, data errors, rep productivity, forecast inaccuracy, and attrition risk from tool frustration."
**Why it works:** Questions that surface hidden costs are more persuasive than telling the prospect those costs exist — when they calculate the number themselves, they own the problem.
**Word count:** Eight questions; one per line; open-ended; no leading language.
**Avg. score:** 84-91

---

## Prompt 10 — Executive Summary for Internal Sharing
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Champion needs a brief they can forward to a committee, board, or CFO before approval
**Structure:** Key metrics > solution summary > generate a 200-word executive summary
**Example output:** "Draft a 200-word executive summary that [Name] can paste into a Slack message to the CFO at [Company]. It should cover: what problem we're solving, the quantified cost of inaction ($310K/year), our proposed solution, the investment ($84K), and the payback period (3.2 months). Tone: confident and crisp, not salesy."
**Why it works:** A Slack-pasteable summary meets buyers where they actually communicate internally — an attached PDF or long email is far less likely to reach the economic buyer in a time-pressured organisation.
**Word count:** Exactly 200 words; no bullet points — narrative prose that reads like a recommendation, not a pitch.
**Avg. score:** 83-91
