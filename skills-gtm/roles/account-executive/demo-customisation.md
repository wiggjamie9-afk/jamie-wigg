# Reference: AE Demo Customisation Prompts

## Purpose
A collection of prompts for Account Executives who need to tailor product demonstrations to specific buyers, use cases, and discovered pain points rather than delivering a generic feature walkthrough. Calibrated for deals where the demo is a pivotal evaluation moment — typically mid-market through enterprise. Each prompt helps you build the narrative thread, tee-up language, and transition logic that moves a demo from impressive to convincing. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Use-Case Narrative Frame
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Before building a demo deck or screen flow for a specific account
**Structure:** Discovered pain > buyer role > product capability > narrative thread request
**Example output:** "My buyer at [Company] is a VP of Sales who told me their biggest pain is that new reps take four months to hit quota because onboarding is inconsistent. Our product accelerates rep onboarding through guided playbooks. Write a three-act demo narrative: the world before us, the moment of change, and the measurable world after."
**Why it works:** A three-act narrative gives the demo emotional structure, not just a feature sequence — buyers remember stories, not feature lists, which means the demo stays alive in internal buying conversations after you've left the room.
**Word count:** 150-200 words; one paragraph per act.
**Avg. score:** 85-92

---

## Prompt 02 — Tee-Up Script Builder
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Immediately before each major demo section
**Structure:** Section name > pain it solves > tee-up line request
**Example output:** "Before I show the pipeline analytics dashboard, I want to tee up why it matters to a Revenue Operations director who told me they spend three hours every Friday manually pulling forecast data. Write a two-sentence tee-up that references their specific pain, then transitions naturally into the demo click."
**Why it works:** Tee-ups anchor features to previously stated pain, converting a product walkthrough into a direct answer — which is why the buyer nods along instead of mentally checking out.
**Word count:** Two sentences; max 50 words.
**Avg. score:** 83-91

---

## Prompt 03 — Tell-Show-Tell Sequence
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Designing the structure of a specific feature demonstration
**Structure:** Feature name > discovered pain it addresses > generate tell-show-tell script
**Example output:** "Write a tell-show-tell script for demonstrating our automated deal-risk scoring feature. The buyer is a Sales Manager at [Company] who told me they find out deals are at risk too late to intervene. Tell: what problem this solves. Show: what to click and narrate. Tell: the outcome and the question to ask after."
**Why it works:** The closing question after each tell-show-tell creates a micro-confirmation loop that keeps the buyer engaged and surfaces unspoken objections before they calcify.
**Word count:** Tell: 30 words. Show: step-by-step narration, max 80 words. Tell: 30 words + one question.
**Avg. score:** 86-93

---

## Prompt 04 — Feature Question Handler
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Prospect asks about a feature that is on the roadmap, limited, or not yet built
**Structure:** Feature request > honest status > generate a bridge response
**Example output:** "[Name] asked in the demo whether our platform integrates natively with HubSpot. We have a Zapier workaround but not a native integration — it's on the Q3 roadmap. Write a response that is honest about the current state, demonstrates we understand why they're asking, and redirects to what we can deliver today."
**Why it works:** Buyers remember when an AE was straight with them on a gap; the redirect to present capability builds credibility precisely because you didn't oversell.
**Word count:** Under 60 words; no hedging phrases like "that's a great question."
**Avg. score:** 81-89

---

## Prompt 05 — Demo Persona Switcher
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** The same demo must land for two different roles in the room (e.g. technical buyer and business buyer)
**Structure:** Two roles > shared pain > split-focus demo plan
**Example output:** "I'm demoing to [Name], the IT Director, and [Name], the VP of Revenue at [Company]. IT cares about security, SSO, and API access. Revenue cares about adoption and forecast accuracy. Write a demo outline that opens with a shared pain, then forks for five minutes into role-specific sections, then reunites on the business outcome."
**Why it works:** A structured fork prevents you from losing one persona while speaking to the other — which is the most common reason multi-stakeholder demos stall at next steps.
**Word count:** Outline format; max 300 words with clear fork/reunite labels.
**Avg. score:** 84-91

## Prompt 06 — Demo-to-Next-Step Transition
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** As the demo concludes and you need to close the room on a specific next step
**Structure:** What was covered > buyer's stated outcome > proposed next step > transition language
**Example output:** "The demo covered pipeline analytics and deal risk. [Name] said their goal was to improve forecast accuracy by Q4. Draft the closing two minutes: a summary of what we showed and why it maps to their Q4 goal, followed by a specific next-step proposal — not 'let me know what you think' — something with a date and an owner."
**Why it works:** Demos often die in vague post-meeting follow-up; a scripted transition that names a date and an owner converts demo energy into committed momentum before anyone leaves the room.
**Word count:** 100-130 words; close with a direct next-step question.
**Avg. score:** 83-90

---

## Prompt 07 — Proof Point Insertion
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Moments in the demo where a claim needs evidence to land (metrics, case studies, testimonials)
**Structure:** Claim being made > similar customer profile > generate proof-point insertion
**Example output:** "I'm about to say that our platform reduces ramp time by 30%. My prospect is a mid-market SaaS company with 25 reps. Find me a natural way to insert a customer proof point during the demo — something brief, name-checkable, and positioned so it reinforces the visual I've just shown rather than interrupting it."
**Why it works:** Proof points land harder when they follow a visual rather than preceding it — the buyer has just seen the capability, so the social proof confirms rather than sells.
**Word count:** One to two sentences; include a company-type reference even if anonymised.
**Avg. score:** 82-89

---

## Prompt 08 — Objection-in-Demo Handler
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** A prospect raises a concern or sceptical question mid-demo (pricing, complexity, competitor comparison)
**Structure:** Objection verbatim > demo context > generate an in-demo response that keeps momentum
**Example output:** "Midway through the demo, [Name] said 'this looks complicated for our small team.' We're showing the admin configuration screen, not the end-user view. Write a response that acknowledges the concern, pivots to the rep-facing interface, and keeps us on track without making them feel dismissed."
**Why it works:** Pivoting to the relevant interface is a show-not-tell response to a complexity concern — far more convincing than explaining that it's actually simple.
**Word count:** Under 60 words; ends on a question or a natural resume cue.
**Avg. score:** 80-88

---

## Prompt 09 — Discovery Echo Opening
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Opening the demo for a prospect who completed a prior discovery call
**Structure:** Two or three discovered pain points > generate a call-back opening that references their own words
**Example output:** "On our discovery call, [Name] said: 'our reps spend more time updating Salesforce than selling' and 'we have no visibility into why deals slip.' Open today's demo by referencing those two points before touching the product — make it feel like everything I'm about to show was built specifically for those two problems."
**Why it works:** Echoing a buyer's own language back at the start of a demo triggers a recognition response that primes them to see the product through the lens of their own pain rather than as a generic tool.
**Word count:** 80-100 words; include at least one verbatim phrase from discovery.
**Avg. score:** 88-95

---

## Prompt 10 — Live Q&A Parking Script
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** A question arises during the demo that would derail the flow if answered immediately
**Structure:** Question type > reason to park > generate a parking phrase and follow-up commitment
**Example output:** "[Name] asked about our data migration process midway through a flow demo. Answering fully would take 10 minutes and derail the story. Write a parking phrase that validates the importance of the question, commits to a specific follow-up format (e.g. 'I'll send a one-pager'), and smoothly resumes the demo."
**Why it works:** A parking phrase that names the follow-up format is a micro-commitment — it signals to the buyer that the question isn't being deflected, just sequenced correctly.
**Word count:** Two sentences: park + resume; max 40 words.
**Avg. score:** 81-88
