# Reference: SDR/BDR Signal Targeting Prompts

## Purpose
Prompts for SDRs and BDRs who want to move faster than the competition on buying signals. Each prompt is wired to a specific external event — funding round, exec hire, tech-stack change, layoff, product launch, earnings call, G2 review, job posting, or M&A — and gives you a first-line that makes the signal impossible to ignore. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Funding Round Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target company announced a Series A, B, or C in the last 14 days
**Structure:** Congratulations (specific round) > typical spend pattern at this stage > question
**Example output:** "Congrats on the $18M Series B — saw it in TechCrunch this morning. Most sales orgs at this stage are suddenly asked to 3x pipeline without 3x headcount. Is that the conversation happening at [Company] right now?"
**Why it works:** Funding announcements are public and time-stamped, so the outreach feels urgent rather than random. The pain point (scaling without headcount) is the most predictable post-round pressure point.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 83-91

---

## Prompt 02 — New Executive Hire LinkedIn DM
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** New VP/C-suite joined the target company within 30 days
**Structure:** Welcome the hire > "first 90 days" framing > specific offer
**Example output:** "Welcome to the VP Revenue seat at [Company], [Name]. Most new VPs I talk to spend the first 90 days auditing what's actually driving pipeline vs. what looks like it is. Happy to share what we're seeing from similar-stage teams if useful."
**Why it works:** New executives are actively shaping opinions and building relationships. They're more receptive before they've locked in vendors or inherited allegiances.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 81-90

---

## Prompt 03 — Tech Stack Change (Job Posting Intent Signal)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Job posting mentions a specific tool the prospect is replacing or adopting
**Structure:** Stack observation > transition pain > value bridge
**Example output:** "Noticed [Company]'s open Sales Ops role requires experience with Salesforce AND HubSpot migration. Transitions between CRMs are where pipeline data usually gets corrupted. We've helped 3 teams in [industry] come through that cleanly — worth a quick call?"
**Why it works:** Job postings reveal internal decisions before press releases do. The mention of a specific tech stack signals intent, not just interest.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 79-88

---

## Prompt 04 — Layoff Signal Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target company announced layoffs in the last 30 days (especially affecting revenue/ops teams)
**Structure:** Acknowledge the environment (neutral) > expected pressure on survivors > offer
**Example output:** "Saw the news about the team restructure at [Company]. When sales headcount gets cut, the remaining reps are usually asked to carry more pipeline with less support. We've helped a few teams automate the manual parts of that. Worth seeing if it fits?"
**Why it works:** Layoffs create urgency to do more with less — directly relevant to efficiency tools, automation, or outsourced capacity. Neutral framing avoids appearing opportunistic.
**Word count:** Under 70 words
**Avg. score:** 75-84

---

## Prompt 05 — Product Launch Signal Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target company launched a new product, feature set, or market expansion in the last 21 days
**Structure:** Product launch observation > implied GTM challenge > tie-in
**Example output:** "Saw [Company] just launched [Product] into the enterprise segment — exciting move. Usually means the GTM motion needs to shift from volume to qualification, or sales cycles get longer than expected. Would it be worth 15 minutes to share what we've seen work?"
**Why it works:** New products create new sales motion challenges. Reaching out on launch day (or within 3 weeks) positions you as someone tracking their business, not mass-emailing a list.
**Word count:** Under 70 words
**Avg. score:** 78-87

---

## Prompt 06 — Earnings Call Signal (Public Company)
**Role:** SDR/BDR (or AE)
**Trigger:** Target company published earnings call transcript flagging a specific operational challenge
**Structure:** Quote from earnings call > shared pattern > invitation to explore
**Example output:** "Your CEO mentioned on the Q2 call that 'pipeline coverage is the biggest constraint going into H2.' That's the exact phrase we hear before teams ask us for help with signal-based prospecting. Would it be worth a conversation before the quarter ends?"
**Why it works:** Quoting the CEO's own words removes objection deflection at the contact level. The problem is already public — you're responding to it, not inventing it.
**Word count:** Under 70 words
**Avg. score:** 84-92

---

## Prompt 07 — G2 Review Signal Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target company left a negative or mixed G2 review for a competitor product
**Structure:** Review observation > pain validation > bridge to alternative
**Example output:** "Noticed [Company] left a 3-star G2 review for [Competitor] citing '[specific pain from review]'. That's a pretty common complaint — it's actually one of the main reasons teams switch to us. Would it be worth 20 minutes to see if we'd fit better?"
**Why it works:** G2 reviews are proof-of-pain in the prospect's own words. Referencing the exact complaint removes skepticism that you've actually done research.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 80-89

---

## Prompt 08 — M&A Signal Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target company announced an acquisition or was acquired in the last 60 days
**Structure:** M&A acknowledgement > typical integration pain > offer
**Example output:** "Congrats on the acquisition of [Company]. Post-M&A, the hardest thing is usually aligning two separate go-to-market motions — different tech stacks, different reporting, different comp plans. We've helped a few combined sales teams get to one view of pipeline faster. Worth a call?"
**Why it works:** M&A creates immediate operational pain at the VP and CRO level. It's a legitimate reason to reach out without it feeling like cold prospecting.
**Word count:** Under 70 words
**Avg. score:** 81-89

---

## Prompt 09 — Job Posting Volume Signal (Rapid Hiring)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target company has 5+ open roles in a single function posted within 30 days
**Structure:** Hiring velocity observation > scaling challenge > ask
**Example output:** "Saw [Company] is hiring 6 Account Executives right now. Rapid hiring usually means onboarding time is the variable that kills quota attainment in Q3 and Q4. We cut that ramp time in half for teams scaling at this speed — would it be worth a quick chat?"
**Why it works:** Volume of job postings is a proxy for growth pressure. The faster a team is hiring, the more acute the ramp and productivity problem becomes.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 78-87

---

## Prompt 10 — Competitive Win/Loss Signal (Social Listening)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect's company was mentioned losing a deal to a competitor in a case study, press release, or social post
**Structure:** Competitive context (neutral) > alternative positioning > low-stakes ask
**Example output:** "Noticed [Competitor] published a case study naming [Company] as a prospect they won business from. Happy to share what [Company]-like teams have found works better — no agenda, just a conversation. Would 15 minutes be worth it?"
**Why it works:** Losing a public-facing competitive deal creates internal pressure and re-evaluation moments. The neutral framing keeps the door open without piling on.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 76-85
