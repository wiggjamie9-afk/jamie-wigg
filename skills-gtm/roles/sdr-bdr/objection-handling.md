# Reference: SDR/BDR Objection Handling Prompts

## Purpose
Prompts for SDRs and BDRs hitting the standard wall of brush-offs — from "not interested" to "we built it in-house." Each prompt reframes the objection without arguing, validates the prospect's position, and creates a minimal opening to stay in the conversation. Calibrated for cold and warm outbound contexts at VP and Director level. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — "Not Interested"
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect replies with "not interested" or "no thanks" via email or LinkedIn
**Structure:** Validate > reframe the actual ask > minimum viable door-opener
**Example output:** "Totally fair — I didn't give you much of a reason to be. Let me ask one honest question: is it that the timing is off, or that the problem I mentioned doesn't actually exist for you? Happy to step back if it's the latter."
**Why it works:** Most "not interested" replies are reflexive, not considered. Asking which type of "no" it is invites a real response without pressuring the prospect.
**Word count:** Under 60 words
**Avg. score:** 77-86

---

## Prompt 02 — "No Budget"
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect says "we don't have budget right now" on a call or in email
**Structure:** Validate the constraint > shift to problem existence > plant a seed for next cycle
**Example output:** "Completely understand — budget cycles are real. Quick question before I let you go: is [the problem] actually on your radar as something to solve, or is it not a priority even if budget opened up? That tells me whether it's worth staying in touch."
**Why it works:** Separating "no budget" from "no problem" keeps the door open without chasing a dead deal. If the problem exists, you've earned a future conversation.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 78-87

---

## Prompt 03 — "Already Using [Competitor]"
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect says they're already using a named competitor and are happy with it
**Structure:** Acknowledge the incumbent > neutral curiosity question > narrow comparison
**Example output:** "Good to know — [Competitor] is solid for [core use case]. The teams we work with who made the switch typically hit one specific wall with them: [specific known gap]. Has that come up yet, or is it working well end-to-end?"
**Why it works:** Doesn't attack the competitor directly. Focuses on one specific, known gap that the prospect might already be feeling — but hasn't connected to the vendor yet.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 79-88

---

## Prompt 04 — "Just Send Me an Email"
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect ends a cold call with "just send me an email"
**Structure:** Agree immediately > reframe the email as a question not a pitch > set expectation
**Example output:** "Will do — I'll keep it short. Instead of a pitch deck, I'll send you one question about [specific pain area]. If it resonates, great. If not, no follow-up from me. Look out for it in the next 10 minutes."
**Why it works:** Agreeing quickly removes friction. Committing to a single question instead of a deck dramatically increases the chance of a reply. The "no follow-up" promise reduces risk.
**Word count:** Under 60 words
**Avg. score:** 78-87

---

## Prompt 05 — "Call Me Next Quarter"
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect asks to be contacted in a future quarter (Q3, after the holidays, new FY, etc.)
**Structure:** Agree > confirm the specific reason > lock in a real placeholder
**Example output:** "Absolutely — I'll reach out in October. Quick question so I reach out at the right time: is it that budget opens up then, or that a specific project will have wrapped up? That way I'm actually useful when I call back."
**Why it works:** Asking why pushes the prospect to commit to a real reason, not a soft deflection. If they give a real answer, you have a qualified future opportunity. If they can't, you know it's a brush-off.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 77-86

## Prompt 06 — "We Built It In-House"
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect says their team has already built or is building an internal solution
**Structure:** Validate the investment > surface the cost of ownership > invite an honest comparison
**Example output:** "That's impressive — building in-house means you know the problem deeply. The honest question teams usually hit 6-12 months in is whether the maintenance burden is worth it vs. the ongoing cost. Would it be worth a 20-minute comparison, even just to validate the build decision?"
**Why it works:** Attacking the in-house build creates defensiveness. Positioning it as a validation exercise removes the ego threat and gives the prospect a safe reason to engage.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 77-85

---

## Prompt 07 — "Too Busy Right Now"
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect says they're in a busy season, end of quarter crunch, or dealing with a major internal project
**Structure:** Acknowledge the crunch > tie your offer to reducing the crunch > minimal ask
**Example output:** "That makes sense — end of quarter is the worst time for a vendor call. What if I sent you one thing in writing this week and followed up in 3 weeks when the pressure's off? I'd rather wait and be useful than talk when you're heads-down."
**Why it works:** Showing you understand their reality and are willing to wait builds goodwill. A 3-week callback with a low-stakes written piece keeps the thread alive without burning it.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 76-85

---

## Prompt 08 — "We're Happy With Our Current Process"
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect says their current process is working fine and they're not looking to change
**Structure:** Validate satisfaction > probe for measurement > subtle gap question
**Example output:** "Good to hear — if it's working well, I won't push. One question: is there a metric you're tracking to know if it's working well, or is it more of a 'nothing's broken' situation? Just curious because that's where the teams I work with usually discover the gap."
**Why it works:** "Nothing's broken" is not the same as "it's working well." The question invites self-reflection without confrontation, and often surfaces a measurement gap the prospect hadn't named.
**Word count:** Under 70 words
**Avg. score:** 76-84

---

## Prompt 09 — "We Tried Something Like This Before and It Didn't Work"
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect says they've already evaluated or used a similar product and had a bad experience
**Structure:** Acknowledge the history > ask what specifically failed > differentiate or disqualify fast
**Example output:** "That's really useful context — thank you for saying so. Would you mind telling me what specifically didn't work? I want to be honest with you: if we'd have the same problem, I'll tell you now. If it's something we've fixed, it's worth 10 minutes."
**Why it works:** Asking what failed shows genuine interest rather than dismissal of their experience. It also lets you qualify out quickly if the failure is structural rather than specific to a prior vendor.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 79-88

---

## Prompt 10 — "We're Going With a Competitor"
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Late-stage prospect informs you they've selected another vendor
**Structure:** Congratulate the decision > ask for the real reason > leave the door open
**Example output:** "Congrats on landing on a decision — that's never easy. Would you be willing to tell me the one thing that pushed it their way? I'm not looking to reopen it, I just want to understand what we missed so we can get better. And happy to stay in touch if anything changes."
**Why it works:** Asking why without trying to re-open the deal removes threat. Many prospects will give you honest feedback in this moment — and some will recall the conversation when the new vendor underdelivers.
**Word count:** Under 70 words
**Avg. score:** 78-87
