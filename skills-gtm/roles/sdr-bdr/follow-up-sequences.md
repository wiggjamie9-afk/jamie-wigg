# Reference: SDR/BDR Follow-Up Sequences Prompts

## Purpose
Prompts for SDRs and BDRs managing multi-touch follow-up after no reply, a no-show, or a cold sequence that went quiet. Covers bump emails, breakup emails, value-add touches, channel switches, multi-threading to a second contact, and re-engagement after long silences. Each prompt is designed to add something — a new angle, a new piece of value, a new channel — rather than just asking "did you see my last email?" Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Simple Bump (Touch 2)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** No reply to first cold email after 3-4 business days
**Structure:** One-line re-surface > new angle or question > low-pressure CTA
**Example output:** "Bumping this up — wanted to add one thing I forgot to mention: [specific customer result or stat relevant to their situation]. Does that change whether it's worth a quick call?"
**Why it works:** A bump that adds new information performs significantly better than a bump that just re-asks the same question. One new data point is enough to re-earn attention.
**Word count:** Under 40 words
**Avg. score:** 74-83

---

## Prompt 02 — Value-Add Touch (Touch 3)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** No reply after 2 touches; want to add something before asking again
**Structure:** Lead with the asset > explain why it's relevant to them specifically > soft ask
**Example output:** "I put together a one-page breakdown of how [Peer Company in same industry] cut their sales cycle from 47 to 28 days. Thought it might be relevant given [Company]'s push into enterprise. Happy to send it over if useful — or not, no worries either way."
**Why it works:** Leading with a piece of value rather than a meeting request resets the dynamic. The recipient gets something before being asked for anything.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 78-87

---

## Prompt 03 — Channel Switch to LinkedIn (Touch 4)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** 3 emails sent with no reply; switching to LinkedIn DM
**Structure:** Acknowledge the channel switch > tie back to original email > new low-friction ask
**Example output:** "Hey [Name] — tried a couple of emails but they may have gotten buried. Figured I'd try here instead. One honest question: is [the problem I mentioned] actually on your radar, or did I get the targeting wrong? Happy either way."
**Why it works:** Naming the channel switch shows self-awareness. The question gives the prospect an easy way to redirect you — or confirm you're on the right track — without committing to a meeting.
**Word count:** Under 60 words
**Avg. score:** 76-85

## Prompt 04 — Cold Voicemail Follow-Up (Touch 4 or 5)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Switching to phone after email and LinkedIn touches produced no reply
**Structure:** Quick callback to original email > one new hook > simple ask
**Example output:** "Hey [Name], [Your Name] at [Company]. Sent a couple of emails — didn't want to keep emailing without trying to actually reach you. Saw that [Company] just [signal — e.g. closed a funding round / launched a new product]. Made me think the timing might actually be right now. If I've got it wrong, totally fine — [number]. Thanks."
**Why it works:** Voicemail after emails shows genuine persistence, not automation. Referencing a signal specific to this week keeps it feeling timely rather than templated.
**Word count:** Under 60 words spoken
**Avg. score:** 74-83

## Prompt 05 — Multi-Thread to Second Contact
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Primary contact has not responded after 4-5 touches; identified a second relevant contact in the org
**Structure:** Introduce yourself briefly > reference the org (not the silent contact) > earn their input independently
**Example output:** "Hi [Name 2] — I've been trying to reach [Name 1] about [specific problem area] without luck. Didn't want to assume you're the wrong person to talk to, so reaching out directly. Is [problem] something that lives on your team, or should I keep trying [Name 1]?"
**Why it works:** Doesn't call out the silent contact or create awkwardness. Positions the second contact as a routing resource, which is low-stakes, and often results in being redirected with an introduction.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 77-86

## Prompt 06 — No-Show Re-Engagement Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect accepted a meeting and no-showed with no warning
**Structure:** Low-key acknowledgement > easy reschedule > give them an out
**Example output:** "Hi [Name] — missed you at [time] today. Totally understand things come up. I'll keep it simple: here's a 15-minute slot for later this week if you still want to connect — [link]. If the timing isn't right anymore, just let me know and I'll close it out on my end."
**Why it works:** No-shaming the no-show. Making it easy to reschedule AND easy to cancel keeps the relationship intact. The "I'll close it out" line removes the fear of an awkward ongoing chase.
**Word count:** Under 70 words
**Avg. score:** 78-87

---

## Prompt 07 — Breakup Email (Final Touch)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** 6-8 touches with no engagement across multiple channels; ending the sequence
**Structure:** Acknowledge the silence > remove pressure completely > leave the door open
**Example output:** "Hey [Name] — I've reached out a few times without hearing back, so I'll assume the timing's off or it's not a fit right now. I'll stop reaching out. If anything changes and it's worth revisiting, my email's [address] — reach out anytime. Best of luck with everything at [Company]."
**Why it works:** Breakup emails consistently generate the highest reply rates in a sequence because they remove all pressure. Many prospects reply specifically because they no longer feel chased.
**Word count:** Under 70 words
**Avg. score:** 80-91

## Prompt 08 — Re-Engagement After Long Silence (90+ Days)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect went cold 3-6 months ago; new signal or trigger makes re-outreach relevant
**Structure:** Acknowledge the gap > name the new signal > soft restart
**Example output:** "Hi [Name] — we last spoke back in [Month]. Didn't want to re-appear without a reason, so: saw that [Company] just [new signal — new product / funding / hire]. Made me think the conversation we started might be worth picking back up. Worth 15 minutes?"
**Why it works:** Returning after 90+ days with a new signal rather than "just checking in" shows you've been paying attention. The gap itself can be an asset — things may have changed.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 77-86

---

## Prompt 09 — Post-Demo No-Reply Follow-Up
**Role:** SDR/BDR (or AE)
**Trigger:** Ran a product demo; prospect has not replied in 5-7 business days
**Structure:** Reference a specific moment from the demo > surface a next step > make it easy to say no
**Example output:** "Hey [Name] — wanted to follow up after last week. You mentioned during the demo that [specific thing they said — pain point, feature reaction, question]. I've been thinking about that, and I put together [one-pager / brief note / comparison] specifically around that. Happy to send it over — or if the direction has changed, totally understand."
**Why it works:** Referencing a specific moment from the demo proves you were listening, not running a script. Tying the follow-up to their exact words makes it personal and relevant.
**Word count:** Under 75 words
**Avg. score:** 79-88

---

## Prompt 10 — Trigger-Timed Re-Entry (New Quarter Start)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect said "not now" or "next quarter" and a new quarter has just started
**Structure:** Name the timing > reference the original conversation > ask if the situation changed
**Example output:** "Hey [Name] — we spoke back in [Month] and you mentioned timing would be better in Q[X]. That's now. Has anything changed on your end, or is this still a conversation worth having? Happy to pick up exactly where we left off."
**Why it works:** Holding prospects to their own stated timeline shows follow-through without aggression. It also lets the prospect opt out cleanly if things haven't changed — or re-engage if they have.
**Word count:** Under 60 words
**Avg. score:** 79-88
