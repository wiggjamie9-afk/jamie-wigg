# Reference: SDR/BDR Cold Outreach Prompts

## Purpose
400+ prompts for SDRs and BDRs running signal-based outbound. Copy-paste starters calibrated by persona, trigger, and seniority. Covers email, LinkedIn DM, voicemail, and video — all wired to a real signal or a specific pain pattern, never a generic spray. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Trigger-Based Cold Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Job change in last 30 days
**Structure:** Signal observation > pain bridge > ask
**Example output:** "Saw you moved to VP Sales at Acme last month. Most new VPs I speak to are rebuilding pipeline visibility in the first 90 days — usually because the CRM data they inherited is a mess. Worth a 15-minute call to share what's working for teams in your spot?"
**Why it works:** Uses a verified, recent signal in line one. Prospect knows you did real research.
**Word count:** Under 75 words total
**Avg. score:** 82-91

---

## Prompt 02 — Hiring Signal LinkedIn DM
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target role posted on LinkedIn
**Structure:** Hiring observation > implied pain > ask
**Example output:** "Noticed Acme is hiring 3 SDRs. Usually means outbound is being rebuilt from scratch. Worth 10 minutes to share what's working for us?"
**Why it works:** Hiring signals are public and verifiable. Prospect can't dispute the premise.
**Word count:** Under 50 words
**Avg. score:** 78-88

## Prompt 03 — Founder-Led Cold Email
**Role:** Founder/CEO
**Trigger:** None required
**Structure:** Outcome lead > peer framing > question
**Example output:** "Most VP Sales I speak to are losing deals at demo stage, not discovery. Curious if that's showing up for you at [Company]."
**Why it works:** Founder tone removes the sales dynamic. Peer-to-peer framing earns the reply.
**Word count:** Under 60 words
**Avg. score:** 80-90

## Prompt 04 — MEDDPICC Discovery Question Set
**Role:** Account Executive
**Trigger:** Discovery call scheduled
**Structure:** 5 pain questions + 1 budget qualifier
**Example output:** "What does success look like in 90 days if this works? Who else needs to say yes before you can move?"
**Why it works:** Surfaces metrics, decision criteria, and the economic buyer in one pass.
**Word count:** 6 questions
**Avg. score:** 84-92

## Prompt 05 — Cold Voicemail: Pattern Interrupt
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** No prior contact; 2nd attempt after cold email
**Structure:** Quick credential > counterintuitive claim > single CTA
**Example output:** "Hey [Name], [Your Name] at [Company]. Quick one — I'm not calling to pitch you. I actually have a question about how [Company] handles [specific process]. 30 seconds if you call me back at [number]. Totally okay if it's a no."
**Why it works:** Saying "I'm not calling to pitch" pattern-interrupts. The ask is low-stakes and curiosity-driven — much easier to return than a voicemail selling something.
**Word count:** Under 50 words spoken
**Avg. score:** 74-85

## Prompt 06 — Cold Video DM (LinkedIn / Loom)
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect posted thought leadership content on LinkedIn
**Structure:** Content callback > specific insight > invite
**Example output:** "Hey [Name] — watched your post on pipeline efficiency. You said something I've been thinking about: '[their exact quote]'. We've been working on exactly that with teams like [Peer Company]. Recorded 90 seconds on what we found. Happy to send it over if useful?"
**Why it works:** Video reply to a public post is highly differentiated. Quoting them verbatim proves you actually read it, not just liked it.
**Word count:** Under 80 words in the accompanying message
**Avg. score:** 81-90

---

## Prompt 07 — Mutual Connection Cold Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Shared LinkedIn connection who can be named
**Structure:** Warm bridge > context > soft ask
**Example output:** "I noticed we're both connected to [Mutual Name] — she mentioned [Company] is scaling the sales team this year. I work with a few orgs going through that same motion and wanted to reach out directly. Would it be worth 15 minutes this week?"
**Why it works:** Social proof without a formal introduction. Naming a mutual connection increases trust and response rate even without a direct referral.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 79-88

---

## Prompt 08 — Competitive Displacement Cold Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Prospect is a known user of a direct competitor (tech stack tool, G2 review, job posting mentions competitor)
**Structure:** Competitor acknowledgement > gap insight > invitation to compare
**Example output:** "Saw [Company] is running [Competitor] for [use case]. Most teams we speak to who made the switch told us [Competitor] works fine for X but breaks down when [specific scale/complexity trigger]. Would it be worth a quick call to see if that's showing up for you?"
**Why it works:** Doesn't trash the competitor — identifies a specific, known limitation. Neutral framing makes the prospect feel in control of the comparison.
**Word count:** Under 70 words
**Avg. score:** 78-87

---

## Prompt 09 — ICP Cold Email: Persona Pain Pattern
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** No specific signal; persona-fit account
**Structure:** "Most [role] I talk to" > one specific problem > question
**Example output:** "Most RevOps leads I talk to at Series B companies tell me their biggest headache isn't tooling — it's that their stack doesn't talk to each other, so they're building reports manually. Is that happening at [Company], or have you solved it?"
**Why it works:** The "most [role] I talk to" opener creates social proof without a specific reference. Asking whether they've solved it implies capability without arrogance.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 76-86

---

## Prompt 10 — End-of-Quarter Urgency Cold Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** End of Q (prospect's likely fiscal quarter end, inferred from their industry/funding round timing)
**Structure:** Quarter context > outcome framing > ask tied to timing
**Example output:** "With Q3 wrapping up in the next few weeks, most VP Sales I speak to are either locking in wins or doing post-mortems on missed targets. Either way, usually worth a quick conversation. Do you have 15 minutes before the quarter closes?"
**Why it works:** Time-boxing the ask to a natural deadline creates low-pressure urgency. It's relevant to their world, not invented by the seller.
**Word count:** Under 65 words
**Avg. score:** 77-86
