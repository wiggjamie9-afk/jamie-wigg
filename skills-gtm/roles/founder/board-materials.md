# Reference: Founder Board Materials Prompts

## Purpose
A collection of prompts for founders preparing board updates, metric commentary, and GTM narrative slides. Covers the board-update narrative arc, metric commentary framing, the ask/risk slide, one-thing summaries, GTM deck sections, and how to present an honest miss. Calibrated for founders presenting to seed through Series B boards — investors who want signal, not spin. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Board Update Narrative Arc
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** 2–3 weeks before each board meeting
**Structure:** One paragraph on where we are (the honest current state) → one paragraph on what happened (the 3 big things, not 10) → one paragraph on what we're doing next and why → the ask
**Example output:** "We ended Q2 at $1.1M ARR — $100K below plan but with a pipeline that's healthier than we expected at this stage. The miss came from two enterprise deals that slipped on procurement timing; both are still live and one signed in early July. The three things that mattered most this quarter: the [Product Feature] launch drove a 40% lift in trial-to-paid conversion, we hired a Head of Sales who started Monday, and we lost one customer for the first time (full post-mortem below). Q3 is about closing the two slipped deals and proving the new AE motion works before we accelerate spend."
**Why it works:** Board narrative that tries to cover everything covers nothing. Three signal events — one positive, one negative, one forward-looking — give investors the context to ask useful questions rather than fishing for information.
**Word count:** 200–300 words; written narrative, not bullets
**Avg. score:** 86-94

## Prompt 02 — Metric Commentary Framing
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** For every metric in the board pack that moved significantly — up or down
**Structure:** State the metric and its movement → explain what drove it (one specific cause, not a list) → state whether the driver is structural or one-time → state the implication for next quarter
**Example output:** "MRR grew 12% MoM vs. a 9% target. The outperformance came almost entirely from the [Enterprise Tier] launch in week 3 — the first three enterprise logos added $30K MRR. The underlying SMB motion held flat at 7% MoM growth, which is the number I'm actually watching. Don't read the 12% as a new baseline; enterprise deal timing is lumpy and Q3 will likely be below it. I'm targeting 9–11% for the next three months."
**Why it works:** Metric commentary that just restates the number ('MRR was $320K, up from $285K') is noise. Explaining the single driver — and flagging whether it's repeatable — is the entire job of commentary.
**Word count:** 80–120 words per metric; inline in the board pack
**Avg. score:** 85-93

## Prompt 03 — The Ask/Risk Slide
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Every board meeting; placed near the end of the board pack
**Structure:** The Ask — one to three things you need from the board (introductions, a decision, a heads-up on a future round). The Risk — one to two things that could materially change the trajectory if they go wrong.
**Example output:** "THE ASK: (1) Introductions to two enterprise heads of operations in FinTech — [Board Member A]'s network would be the right starting point. (2) A decision on the marketing budget reallocation proposal I'll walk through in the session. THE RISK: If the two slipped enterprise deals don't close in July, we'll need to revisit Q3 targets. I'll have a clearer read by 20 July and will flag before it becomes a board-level issue."
**Why it works:** Most boards want to be useful and most founders don't tell them how. A specific ask (an introduction to a named profile, not 'intros to enterprise') is one a board member can act on in 10 minutes. A pre-announced risk maintains trust if the situation deteriorates.
**Word count:** 80–120 words; 1 slide, two sections
**Avg. score:** 84-92

## Prompt 04 — The One-Thing Summary
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Opening of the board meeting; also useful as the email preview before the board pack
**Structure:** One sentence on the company's overall state → one sentence on the single most important thing that happened → one sentence on the single most important question for the session
**Example output:** "We're on track at the company level but the GTM motion is in transition — we're replacing outbound-only with a hybrid inbound/outbound model and this quarter is the first real test of whether it works. The most important data point from Q2 is that inbound trial volume doubled, but conversion is 8 points below our outbound benchmark. The question I want to leave this meeting with an answer to: should we delay the sales headcount plan until conversion improves, or press ahead?"
**Why it works:** Founders who bury the key question in slide 14 lose the board's attention before they get there. Surfacing the one question at the start primes every board member to engage with it as evidence comes up during the meeting.
**Word count:** 80–120 words; email or verbal opening
**Avg. score:** 86-94

## Prompt 05 — GTM Section of the Board Deck
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Every board meeting; GTM section covers pipeline, channel, sales motion, and next-quarter targets
**Structure:** Pipeline health (created vs. target) → source mix (where pipeline came from) → conversion benchmarks (by stage) → sales capacity and ramp → next-quarter targets and the key assumptions behind them
**Example output:** "GTM Slide Structure: (1) Pipeline Created Q2: $2.1M vs. $1.8M target. Source mix: 44% inbound, 38% outbound SDR, 18% partner. (2) Stage conversion: Lead → Opp 22% (up from 17%); Opp → Close 19% (down from 24% — see note on enterprise slip). (3) Sales capacity: 1 AE fully ramped, 1 AE at 50% ramp, 1 AE joining Q3. (4) Q3 target: $3.2M pipeline created; assumes AE ramp holds and inbound trend continues. Sensitivity: if inbound stalls, we're at $2.4M."
**Why it works:** GTM slides that show only 'ARR closed' hide the leading indicators that predict whether the next quarter will close. Showing pipeline creation, source mix, and conversion by stage gives the board a complete read on the machine.
**Word count:** N/A — structured slide content; 4 components
**Avg. score:** 85-92

## Prompt 06 — Honest Miss Explanation
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Any quarter where a key metric came in below plan
**Structure:** State the miss precisely (the number, the plan, the gap) → give the single primary cause → distinguish what you control from what you don't → state what you're doing about the controllable part → give the revised forward expectation
**Example output:** "We came in at $920K ARR vs. a $1.1M plan — a $180K miss. The primary cause: two enterprise deals that I expected to close in June slipped to July and August. One slipped because we lost the procurement contact mid-process; one slipped because the customer delayed their fiscal year budget approval. Both deals are still live. The controllable lesson: we need an executive-level relationship at every enterprise account by Stage 3, not Stage 5. We've implemented that as a stage-gate. Q3 plan is $1.25M; I'd be comfortable with $1.1M as a floor if the two slipped deals both close."
**Why it works:** Founders who explain a miss with a list of external factors ('market conditions', 'timing') erode board trust fast. A specific root cause — with one controllable lesson and a revised target — shows that you're learning from the miss, not rationalising it.
**Word count:** 150–200 words; written narrative in the board pack
**Avg. score:** 87-94

## Prompt 07 — Investor Update (Between Board Meetings)
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Monthly; the rhythm of communication between board meetings
**Structure:** Top line metric and trend → one thing that went well → one thing that went badly → what you need (specific and optional to respond to)
**Example output:** "July: $1.08M ARR (+8% MoM). Went well: enterprise deal [Name] closed at $72K — thank you to [Board Member] for the intro to their CMO. Went badly: our first SDR quit on 30 days' notice; we're back-filling and it'll cost us 4 weeks of outbound capacity. Need (optional): if any of you know a strong enterprise SDR who's open to an early-stage environment, please send them my way."
**Why it works:** Monthly investor updates that run four pages get skimmed. A four-section format (metric, good, bad, ask) can be read in 90 seconds and gets more engagement — which means more useful responses — than a comprehensive narrative.
**Word count:** 100–150 words; email
**Avg. score:** 84-92

## Prompt 08 — Risk Register Slide
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Quarterly; important to maintain through growth, not just in difficult quarters
**Structure:** List 3–5 risks → for each: probability (High/Medium/Low), impact (High/Medium/Low), current status, and mitigation in progress
**Example output:** "Risk: Enterprise sales cycle extends beyond 90 days and compresses Q3 cash. Probability: Medium. Impact: High. Status: Two enterprise deals currently at 110-day cycle; both expected to close in August. Mitigation: implemented exec-sponsor cadence to prevent procurement delays; AE-level relationship mapped to C-suite. Owner: [CEO]. Next review: board meeting August."
**Why it works:** Risk registers that only appear when something is already going wrong feel reactive. A standing risk register — maintained even in good quarters — signals maturity and gives the board context to help before a risk becomes a crisis.
**Word count:** N/A — table format; one row per risk
**Avg. score:** 83-91

## Prompt 09 — Team/Org Update Section
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Any board meeting where there's been a significant hire, departure, or org change
**Structure:** Name the change → the strategic rationale → the transition plan (if departure) → the time-to-productivity expectation (if hire) → the one risk this change introduces
**Example output:** "We promoted [Name] to Head of Sales effective 1 August. Rationale: she's been the highest-performing AE for two consecutive quarters and already mentoring the two junior AEs informally. We're promoting on demonstrated behaviour, not a vacancy. Time-to-productivity in the new role: I expect her first 60 days to be a drag on her personal quota as she builds the playbook. Risk: she's our only fully-ramped seller and any transition friction hits near-term pipeline. Mitigation: I'm covering her book personally for the first 30 days."
**Why it works:** Board members who learn about a significant org change through a board pack rather than proactively are investors who feel less trusted. The post-hoc rationale here is structured to pre-empt the four questions a board member would otherwise ask in the session.
**Word count:** 120–160 words; 1 paragraph or 1 slide
**Avg. score:** 84-92

## Prompt 10 — Pre-Board Meeting Briefing Email
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** 48–72 hours before the board meeting; sent to all board members
**Structure:** The one question you want to leave the meeting having answered → the one decision you need to make in the session → any pre-reading they should prioritise → start time, location/link, and agenda
**Example output:** "Board meeting is Thursday at 2pm PT (Zoom link below). The board pack went out yesterday — if you only have time to read one section before Thursday, read the GTM section (slides 8–12). The one decision I need from Thursday: whether to accelerate the sales hire plan or hold until we have three months of data from the new inbound motion. The one question I want us to leave with answered: what's our appetite for increasing burn by $40K/month in H2 if the inbound conversion holds?"
**Why it works:** Founders who send board materials but no framing leave board members showing up without context for what actually matters. A pre-meeting briefing that names the decision and the question turns a status-report meeting into a working session.
**Word count:** 100–130 words; email
**Avg. score:** 85-93
