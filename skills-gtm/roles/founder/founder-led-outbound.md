# Reference: Founder-Led Outbound Prompts

## Purpose
A collection of prompts for founders doing direct outreach — cold outreach to prospective customers, design-partner recruitment, warm-intro asks, and the first-10-customers push. Calibrated for early-stage founders (pre-Series A) who are selling personally and want to sound like a human being, not a sales team. The voice should be curious, direct, and non-salesy. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Cold Outreach to ICP Prospect
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** None required — proactive outbound to a specific person who fits your ICP
**Structure:** One line of specific research → the problem you think they have → why you're the person sending this (founder context) → a low-friction ask
**Example output:** "Hi [Name], I saw your post about [specific operational challenge] last week — it's exactly the problem that made me start [Company]. We're building [one sentence on what you do] for teams like yours. I'm not trying to pitch you anything; I'm trying to understand whether the problem I think you have matches the problem you actually have. Would you be up for a 20-minute call this week? I'll bring questions, not slides."
**Why it works:** The founder position is the most credible cold-outreach position in B2B — you built the thing and you want to understand the problem. Leaning into curiosity rather than a value prop disarms the reflex to delete.
**Word count:** 70–90 words; email or LinkedIn DM
**Avg. score:** 85-93

## Prompt 02 — Design-Partner Recruitment
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Pre-launch or early-build phase; seeking 3–5 committed design partners
**Structure:** What you're building → why you're asking this specific person → what design partnership involves → what they get out of it → low-commitment ask
**Example output:** "Hi [Name], I'm building [Product] for [specific role/team], and based on [specific reason — their role, company, content, or a referral], I think you're exactly the kind of person whose perspective would shape what we build. I'm looking for a small group of design partners — people who are willing to use an early version, break things, and tell me what's wrong. In exchange, you get early access, a direct line to me, and pricing that reflects the fact that you helped build it. Is this something you'd be open to exploring?"
**Why it works:** Design-partner asks that lead with what the founder gets (validation, feedback) fail. This version leads with the ask framed as the prospect's influence over the product — which is the honest value exchange.
**Word count:** 90–120 words; email
**Avg. score:** 84-93

## Prompt 03 — Warm-Intro Ask (to Investor)
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Preparing to fundraise; asking an existing investor or advisor for an introduction
**Structure:** Context on who you're asking for → why this specific investor is a fit → what you're asking (precisely) → a forwardable blurb → close with gratitude and permission to decline
**Example output:** "Hey [Name], I'm starting to talk to investors ahead of our seed round and [Investor Name] at [Firm] came up as someone who'd be a natural fit — they've backed [two relevant companies] and focus on [space]. Would you be comfortable making an intro? If yes, here's a blurb you can forward: '[Founder Name] is building [one-line description] at [Company]. They're at [traction milestone] and looking for [amount]. Worth 30 minutes.' No pressure at all if the fit isn't obvious to you."
**Why it works:** Warm-intro requests that make it easy to say no get more yeses. Including the forwardable blurb removes the cognitive load from the introducer — they don't have to write anything.
**Word count:** 80–100 words; email
**Avg. score:** 83-91

## Prompt 04 — Warm-Intro Ask (Investor to Customer)
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Investor has a portfolio company or connection who fits the ICP; you want an intro to the operator, not the investor
**Structure:** Name the specific person you want to meet → why they're relevant → what the intro is for (learning, not selling) → forwardable context
**Example output:** "Hey [Investor Name], I think [Portfolio Company Name]'s Head of Ops, [Name], is exactly the kind of person we're building for. Would you be comfortable dropping them a note? Here's something you could send: '[Founder Name] is building a tool for operations teams doing [specific workflow]. No pitch — they're trying to talk to 10 operators this month to understand the problem space better. 20 minutes would genuinely help them.'"
**Why it works:** Investor-to-customer intros are the highest-quality early conversations a founder can have — the trust transfer is significant. Framing it as a learning conversation (not a sales call) makes the intro easier to give and easier to receive.
**Word count:** 70–90 words; email or Slack
**Avg. score:** 86-93

## Prompt 05 — First 10 Customers Outreach — Personal Network
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Pre-launch or immediately post-launch; reaching out to people in your personal network who fit the ICP
**Structure:** Be direct about what you're doing and why you're asking them specifically → make the ask easy → offer reciprocal value
**Example output:** "Hey [Name], I'm going to be straightforward: I've started a company and I'm looking for my first 10 customers. You were the first person I thought of because [specific reason: their role, a conversation you've had, their company size]. I'd love to give you early access and figure out together whether this is useful for you. If it's not, I'll know faster with your help than without it. Can we get 30 minutes on the calendar?"
**Why it works:** Personal-network outreach that hedges and buries the ask is worse than a direct cold email. People who know you want to help — tell them specifically what you need.
**Word count:** 70–90 words; email or WhatsApp
**Avg. score:** 85-93

## Prompt 06 — Conference/Event Follow-Up
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Within 48 hours of meeting someone at an event; strong contextual memory exists
**Structure:** Name the specific thing you talked about → connect it to why your product is relevant → propose a concrete next step
**Example output:** "Hi [Name], great to meet you at [Event] yesterday — the conversation about [specific topic they mentioned] stuck with me. It's actually the core problem we're trying to solve at [Company]. I think a follow-up call would be valuable for both of us — I'd love to understand your workflow in more detail, and if what we're building is relevant, I'll show you what we have. Does 30 minutes this week work?"
**Why it works:** Post-event follow-ups that reference 'great to meet you' without specifics get ignored. Referencing the exact thing discussed proves you were paying attention and that this isn't a batch email.
**Word count:** 60–80 words; email or LinkedIn
**Avg. score:** 84-92

## Prompt 07 — Cold LinkedIn Outreach (Connection Request Note)
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** Targeted outreach to a specific person whose public content or role signals ICP fit
**Structure:** One specific trigger (post, article, job change) → a genuine question, not a pitch → no ask to buy, demo, or connect for 'synergy'
**Example output:** "Hi [Name], I read your piece on [specific topic] and it changed how I'm thinking about [specific aspect]. I'm building something in this space and I'd find your perspective genuinely useful. Would you be open to a 15-minute call?"
**Why it works:** LinkedIn connection notes have a 300-character limit, which forces brevity. The discipline of a single genuine question — rather than a pitch — converts at a higher rate than any value-proposition note.
**Word count:** 40–60 words maximum
**Avg. score:** 83-91

## Prompt 08 — Re-Engagement After No Response
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** No reply to a cold email after 7–10 days
**Structure:** Acknowledge they may have missed it → give them a one-line reminder of context → try a different angle or a simpler ask → make it easy to say no
**Example output:** "Hi [Name], I sent a note last week about [one-line context] and I know how fast inboxes move. I'll make this easy: if there's a better time or a better way to reach you, just tell me and I'll work around it. If it's not a fit, no need to reply — I appreciate you reading this far."
**Why it works:** The permission to say no ('no need to reply') paradoxically increases response rates. It signals confidence rather than desperation and shows respect for the prospect's time.
**Word count:** 40–60 words; email
**Avg. score:** 82-90

## Prompt 09 — Problem-Exploration Call Opener
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** First call with a prospective customer or design partner; goal is learning, not selling
**Structure:** Set expectations upfront → ask the broadest version of your problem question first → follow their answer; don't steer toward your solution
**Example output:** "Thanks for making time. I want to be upfront: I'm not going to pitch you anything today. I'm trying to understand how [role] teams like yours actually deal with [broad problem area] right now. The goal for me is to walk away understanding whether the problem I'm building for is the problem you actually have. Can I start with: walk me through the last time [specific workflow] caused you a headache?"
**Why it works:** Founders who open with a problem question — and genuinely follow the customer's answer — learn more in 30 minutes than they could learn from 10 pitching calls. The discipline of not steering toward your own solution is the whole point.
**Word count:** 70–90 words; call opening
**Avg. score:** 86-94

## Prompt 10 — Referral Request from a Happy Early Customer
**Role:** Founder
**Trigger:** A customer has given positive feedback unprompted; NPS 9 or 10; strong verbal endorsement
**Structure:** Thank them genuinely → name the specific feedback they gave → make the referral ask concrete (one person, not 'anyone') → offer to make it easy for them
**Example output:** "You mentioned on our last call that [specific thing] had made a real difference — that genuinely means a lot at this stage of building. Can I ask for a favour? Is there one person in your network who you think has the same problem? Not a list — just one person. If yes, I'll write the intro email and you just forward it. Totally fine if no one comes to mind."
**Why it works:** 'Do you know anyone?' is too open-ended to act on. 'One person' is a concrete, low-effort ask that most happy customers will attempt to answer. Offering to write the message removes the final friction point.
**Word count:** 70–90 words; email or follow-up to a call
**Avg. score:** 85-93
