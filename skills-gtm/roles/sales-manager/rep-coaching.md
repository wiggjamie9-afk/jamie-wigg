# Reference: Sales Manager Rep Coaching Prompts

## Purpose
A collection of prompts for Sales Managers conducting call reviews, skill-gap diagnostics, ride-along debriefs, structured 1:1s, and performance-improvement conversations. Calibrated for managers who want to coach rather than inspect — moving reps toward self-awareness and skill development rather than compliance with a process. Each prompt is designed to surface specific, coachable behaviours rather than general feedback. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Call Review Feedback Frame
**Role:** Sales Manager
**Trigger:** After listening to a recorded discovery or demo call with a rep
**Structure:** Call type > rep's stage > two or three specific moments observed > generate structured feedback
**Example output:** "I reviewed [Rep Name]'s discovery call with [Company]. Three observations: they talked for the first 6 minutes without asking a question, they accepted 'budget is tight' without probing further, and they closed strongly on next steps. Generate a feedback structure: one strength to reinforce, one specific moment to examine, and one behaviour to change — framed as a question, not a correction."
**Why it works:** Framing the behaviour to change as a question ("what do you think would have happened if...") activates the rep's own diagnosis rather than triggering defensiveness to a manager's judgment.
**Word count:** Three-part structure; max 50 words per section; coachable, specific language.
**Avg. score:** 85-93

## Prompt 02 — Skill-Gap Diagnosis by Stage
**Role:** Sales Manager
**Trigger:** A rep is consistently losing deals at a specific pipeline stage
**Structure:** Stage where deals stall or lose > sample deal count > generate skill-gap diagnostic questions
**Example output:** "[Rep Name] has lost 5 of their last 7 deals at the proposal stage. Generate a diagnostic framework to determine whether the root cause is: (1) discovery not surfacing the right pain, (2) proposals not addressing the right buyer, (3) pricing / value gap, or (4) competitive positioning. Include two questions I can ask the rep and two questions I should answer from CRM data."
**Why it works:** Stage-specific losses almost always trace back to a skill gap one or two stages earlier — a proposal stage failure is usually a discovery failure in disguise.
**Word count:** Four diagnostic categories; two rep questions + two CRM questions per category.
**Avg. score:** 84-92

## Prompt 03 — Ride-Along Debrief Structure
**Role:** Sales Manager
**Trigger:** You joined a rep on a call or in-person meeting and want to debrief immediately after
**Structure:** Call type > one thing done well > one gap observed > generate a 15-minute debrief structure
**Example output:** "I joined [Rep Name] on a demo call with [Company]. They handled a competitive question well but spent 20 minutes on features the prospect never mentioned in discovery. Generate a 15-minute debrief structure: (1) let them self-assess first, (2) reinforce the competitive handling, (3) examine the feature-dump with a Socratic question, (4) agree one behaviour to try on the next call."
**Why it works:** Letting the rep self-assess first is not a courtesy — it's diagnostic data; reps who identify the same gap the manager saw are ready to change; reps who don't see it need a different coaching approach.
**Word count:** Four-step structure; time allocation per step; opening Socratic question for step three.
**Avg. score:** 83-91

## Prompt 04 — 1:1 Agenda Builder
**Role:** Sales Manager
**Trigger:** Preparing for a weekly or bi-weekly 1:1 with a rep
**Structure:** Rep's performance trend > current deal focus > development goal > generate a 45-minute 1:1 agenda
**Example output:** "[Rep Name] has been ahead on activity but behind on conversion from discovery to proposal for six weeks. Their development goal for the quarter is improving multi-threading. Generate a 45-minute 1:1 agenda that starts with what's going well, moves to the conversion gap, drills into one specific deal to examine it together, and closes with one skill commitment they make — not one I assign."
**Why it works:** Closing the 1:1 with a commitment the rep names themselves (not one assigned by the manager) dramatically increases follow-through because ownership is internal rather than compliance-driven.
**Word count:** Agenda with time blocks; closing question that invites the rep's own commitment.
**Avg. score:** 82-89

## Prompt 05 — Self-Assessment Prompt for Reps
**Role:** Sales Manager
**Trigger:** Before the 1:1; sending a pre-call prompt for the rep to reflect on their own performance
**Structure:** Week or fortnight period > deal focus > generate a three-question self-assessment
**Example output:** "Generate a three-question self-assessment I can send [Rep Name] the evening before our 1:1. They've had a strong prospecting week but one deal just slipped for the second time. Questions should help them self-identify: what's working, what's not, and what one thing they'd do differently on the slipped deal — without me naming the slipped deal explicitly."
**Why it works:** A pre-call self-assessment surfaces the rep's own frame of reference before the manager introduces theirs, which makes the 1:1 a conversation rather than a debrief.
**Word count:** Three questions; each under 20 words; open-ended; no leading language.
**Avg. score:** 81-88

## Prompt 06 — Objection Handling Roleplay Setup
**Role:** Sales Manager
**Trigger:** A rep is consistently struggling with a recurring objection (price, incumbent vendor, timing)
**Structure:** Objection text verbatim > rep's typical response > generate a roleplay setup with coaching notes
**Example output:** "[Rep Name] always hears 'we're happy with our current vendor' and responds with 'we're better because...' which shuts down the conversation. Generate a roleplay setup where I play the prospect saying that exact line, three possible rep responses for them to try (ranging from bad to good), and coaching notes on why each lands or doesn't."
**Why it works:** Showing three response options rather than prescribing one correct answer gives the rep a comparative lens — they learn to evaluate responses rather than memorise a script they'll forget under pressure.
**Word count:** Three responses (bad / okay / good) + coaching note per response; max 40 words each.
**Avg. score:** 85-92

## Prompt 07 — Performance Improvement Conversation Framing
**Role:** Sales Manager
**Trigger:** A rep has been below quota for three or more consecutive months; a structured conversation is needed
**Structure:** Performance gap > rep's context > generate a fair-but-clear PIP conversation framework
**Example output:** "[Rep Name] has been at 58%, 61%, and 55% of quota over the past three months. They've cited territory challenges, but deal velocity data shows their average cycle time is 40% longer than the team average. Draft a conversation framework that: names the data clearly, distinguishes controllable from environmental factors, identifies one or two root causes to work on together, and sets a 30-day leading indicator target — not a trailing revenue target."
**Why it works:** A 30-day leading indicator target (call volume, discovery quality scores, multi-threading rate) gives the rep something they can control immediately; a trailing revenue target for a rep in a rut produces anxiety without direction.
**Word count:** Four-section framework; one paragraph each; specific metric in the target section.
**Avg. score:** 83-90

## Prompt 08 — Ramp Acceleration Plan for New Hires
**Role:** Sales Manager
**Trigger:** A new rep is in their first 60 days and you want to build a structured 90-day ramp plan
**Structure:** Rep's prior experience > product complexity > generate a 90-day milestone plan
**Example output:** "[Rep Name] joined two weeks ago from a background in media sales with no SaaS experience. Our product has a technical integration element. Generate a 90-day ramp plan with milestones at day 30, 60, and 90 — covering: product knowledge benchmarks, shadowing requirements, first solo calls, pipeline build targets, and the criteria I'll use to assess ramp progress at each checkpoint."
**Why it works:** Explicit checkpoints with defined criteria convert onboarding from a passive schedule into a performance contract — both manager and rep know what success looks like at each stage.
**Word count:** Three milestone blocks; bullet format; criteria listed not described; max 400 words.
**Avg. score:** 84-91

## Prompt 09 — Top-Rep Pattern Extraction
**Role:** Sales Manager
**Trigger:** Identifying what your top performer does differently in order to replicate it across the team
**Structure:** Top rep's metrics > team average metrics > generate an analysis framework
**Example output:** "My top rep closes at 28% from demo to proposal while the team average is 16%. They also have the fewest deals slip past close date. Generate a framework for identifying what behaviours might explain this gap — covering: discovery depth, multi-threading score, demo customisation, and follow-up cadence — and the CRM data points or call-review signals I should look at to test each hypothesis."
**Why it works:** Identifying a top rep's advantage as a testable hypothesis (rather than "they're just naturally good") makes it replicable and teachable — specificity is what converts individual talent into team process.
**Word count:** Four hypotheses with CRM/call signal to test each; max 60 words per hypothesis.
**Avg. score:** 83-90

## Prompt 10 — Promotion Readiness Assessment
**Role:** Sales Manager
**Trigger:** A rep is approaching the conversation about promotion to senior AE or team lead
**Structure:** Rep's performance history > skills demonstrated > gaps remaining > generate an assessment framework
**Example output:** "[Rep Name] has been at or above 110% quota for four quarters and is asking about promotion to Senior AE. They're strong on discovery and closing but haven't handled enterprise deals or multi-threaded complex accounts. Generate a promotion-readiness assessment covering: (1) quota consistency, (2) deal complexity, (3) peer contributions, (4) gap to senior role criteria, and (5) a 60-day development plan to close the gap."
**Why it works:** A structured readiness assessment transforms a sensitive career conversation into a transparent, evidence-based discussion — the rep knows exactly what evidence is needed, which removes ambiguity and protects the manager from perceived bias.
**Word count:** Five-section assessment; bullet format; gap section includes specific examples needed.
**Avg. score:** 85-92
