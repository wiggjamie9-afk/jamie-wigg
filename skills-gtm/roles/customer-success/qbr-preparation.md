# Reference: CSM QBR Preparation Prompts

## Purpose
A collection of prompts for Customer Success Managers preparing Quarterly Business Reviews. Covers value-realised recaps, usage-trend narratives, exec-alignment agenda design, expansion tee-ups, and risk surfacing. Calibrated for B2B SaaS QBRs with executive stakeholders at accounts between $50K–$500K ARR. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Value-Realised Recap Narrative
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** 1–2 weeks before each QBR
**Structure:** State the goals agreed at the last QBR → show outcomes achieved (with data) → translate outcomes into business language, not product language → connect to the customer's stated business priorities
**Example output:** "At our Q1 review, [Company] set three goals: reduce manual reporting time by 40%, onboard two additional teams, and hit 85% platform adoption across the sales org. This quarter: reporting time is down 52% (measured via your ops team's survey); the RevOps and Marketing teams are fully onboarded; adoption sits at 81% — 4 points short of target. Here's what drove the gap and what we're doing about it in Q3."
**Why it works:** Translating product usage data into business outcomes (time saved, teams enabled) gives executives a reason to care. Acknowledging the gap honestly — and having a plan — builds more trust than glossing over a miss.
**Word count:** 80–120 words spoken narrative; 1 slide
**Avg. score:** 86-94

## Prompt 02 — Usage Trend Narrative
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** QBR prep; also useful for mid-quarter check-in calls with champions
**Structure:** Pull usage data (WAU/MAU, feature adoption, active users vs licensed seats) → identify the trend → explain the trend in business context → connect to the value story or risk flag
**Example output:** "Active users have grown from 34 to 61 over the quarter — a 79% increase driven by the RevOps team onboarding in week 6. However, the [Advanced Reporting] module still sits at 12% adoption despite being central to your Q3 efficiency goal. We'll walk through three things that typically unlock adoption at this stage: a targeted training session, a workflow template, and a champion-led internal use case."
**Why it works:** Raw usage data without narrative context lands flat with executives. Explaining the 'why' behind the trend — and tying low adoption to a specific business goal at risk — creates urgency without alarmism.
**Word count:** 70–100 words spoken; 1–2 slides
**Avg. score:** 84-92

## Prompt 03 — Exec-Alignment Agenda Design
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** When the QBR attendee list includes VP or C-level stakeholders
**Structure:** Open with business context (their priorities, not yours) → value realised → gaps and plan → look ahead to next quarter → strategic discussion (1 topic only) → clear ask
**Example output:** "Agenda for [Company] Q3 QBR (45 min): 1. [5 min] Your Q3 priorities — confirm we're aligned. 2. [10 min] What we achieved together this quarter. 3. [5 min] Where we fell short and the plan. 4. [10 min] Q4 success plan. 5. [10 min] Strategic discussion: [Company]'s expansion into APAC — how [Product] can support. 6. [5 min] Our ask and next steps. Send agenda to [Exec Sponsor] 5 days in advance for edits."
**Why it works:** Executives will derail a QBR that feels like a product demo or a vendor status report. Starting with their priorities signals that this meeting is for them — which makes them want to attend.
**Word count:** Agenda = bulleted; 6 items max
**Avg. score:** 85-93

## Prompt 04 — Expansion Tee-Up Slide
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** QBR for accounts at or above 70% seat utilisation, or accounts with an upcoming renewal
**Structure:** Show current usage against licensed capacity → project the gap → frame expansion as a natural next step, not a sales pitch → propose a specific next conversation
**Example output:** "You're currently using 87 of 100 licensed seats, and with the Marketing team onboarding in Q4, you'll likely exceed capacity by December. Rather than have growth cause friction, let's set up a 30-minute call with [AE Name] to look at your options — including multi-year pricing that could reduce your per-seat cost as you scale. I'll send a calendar invite for next week."
**Why it works:** Framing expansion as a logistics conversation ('you'll run out of capacity') rather than a sales motion removes the pressure dynamic. The CSM retains the relationship; the AE gets a warm, contextualised introduction.
**Word count:** 60–80 words; 1 slide
**Avg. score:** 83-91

## Prompt 05 — Risk Surfacing Framework
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** QBR prep for any account with a health score below 70, a sponsor change, or declining usage
**Structure:** Name the risk explicitly → show the evidence → state the impact if unaddressed → present the mitigation plan → make a specific ask of the customer
**Example output:** "I want to be direct about something before we move on: active usage in the [Finance] team dropped 40% in the last six weeks. Based on what we've seen at similar companies, this usually signals one of three things — a process change, a key user departure, or a fit issue with a specific workflow. I'd like to understand which it is. Can we spend 10 minutes on this today?"
**Why it works:** Most CSMs bury risk in a footnote or raise it only on internal calls. Naming it directly in the QBR — with evidence and a specific question — forces a conversation that would otherwise surface only when it's too late.
**Word count:** 60–90 words spoken; optional 1-slide risk summary
**Avg. score:** 87-94

## Prompt 06 — Success Plan Review
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Every QBR; also useful at the 90-day check-in post-onboarding
**Structure:** Restate the goals from the success plan → show progress against each → update goals that have been achieved or superseded → agree revised goals for next quarter → get written sign-off from customer
**Example output:** "At kickoff we agreed three 12-month outcomes: (1) 90% adoption across the sales team — currently at 81%, on track. (2) Reduce time-to-report from 3 hours to 45 minutes — achieved at the 4-month mark. (3) Train two internal admins — one trained, one departure delayed this. Let's update goal two to a stretch target and replace goal three with a new focus on manager dashboard adoption. I'll update the success plan in our shared doc today."
**Why it works:** Success plans that aren't reviewed quarterly become shelfware. Updating them live in the QBR — and removing goals that are done — keeps them as a live working document rather than an onboarding artifact.
**Word count:** N/A — structured review; output is the updated success plan
**Avg. score:** 83-91

## Prompt 07 — Stakeholder Map Update
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Before every QBR; especially after any org change at the customer
**Structure:** List current known stakeholders → role, influence level, sentiment → identify gaps (who's missing?) → update CRM before the QBR → use to shape who should be in the room
**Example output:** "Current stakeholder map for [Company]: [Name A] (VP Operations, Economic Buyer, Positive), [Name B] (Sales Ops Manager, Champion, Positive), [Name C] (CFO, Budget Approver, Neutral — has not attended any QBR). Gap: CFO has never seen a value summary. Recommend inviting [Name C] to Q3 QBR with a CFO-specific agenda section covering ROI and renewal investment context."
**Why it works:** CSMs often manage deep relationships with champions while being invisible to economic buyers. Surfacing the stakeholder gap before the QBR — and building it into the agenda — is how CS protects the renewal without waiting for procurement to start.
**Word count:** N/A — map/table format; input to QBR agenda design
**Avg. score:** 82-90

## Prompt 08 — ROI Quantification Narrative
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Annual renewal QBR, or at any QBR where the economic buyer will be present
**Structure:** Identify 2–3 quantifiable outcomes → attach the customer's own data or estimates → translate into financial terms → compare to contract value → present as ROI ratio
**Example output:** "Based on the data your team shared: you've saved an estimated 6 hours per week in manual reporting across 4 team members. At a blended hourly rate of $65, that's roughly $81,000 in time value over the year. Your annual contract is $48,000. That's a 1.7× ROI on time savings alone, before we account for the two deals your team attributes to faster pipeline visibility last quarter."
**Why it works:** Procurement teams ask 'what are we getting for this?' Giving the economic buyer a specific number — even an estimate with stated assumptions — is more defensible than a slide full of feature benefits.
**Word count:** 80–100 words spoken; 1 slide with a simple ROI table
**Avg. score:** 86-93

## Prompt 09 — Competitive Displacement Risk Brief
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** QBR for any account where a competitor has been mentioned, or where a new IT or procurement leader has joined
**Structure:** Name what you've heard → confirm or deny with the champion → prepare talking points → loop in AE if the risk is material
**Example output:** "During our last check-in, [Name B] mentioned that [Competitor] had reached out to the broader team. I want to address that directly: here's a quick summary of where we're differentiated on the three things your team told us mattered most at kickoff — reporting depth, integration with [Stack], and support SLA. I'd also like to set up a call with your new IT Director to make sure we're in good shape before renewal."
**Why it works:** Competitive risks that go unacknowledged in QBRs fester. Naming it — calmly, with evidence — is the CS version of objection handling and signals confidence rather than defensiveness.
**Word count:** 70–90 words spoken; no slide needed (talking point only)
**Avg. score:** 84-92

## Prompt 10 — QBR Follow-Up Summary
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Within 24 hours of every QBR
**Structure:** Thank → restate 3 things agreed → list action items with owners and due dates → confirm next touchpoint → attach updated success plan
**Example output:** "Thanks for a great Q3 review, [Name]. Here's what we agreed: (1) You'll loop in [Name C] (CFO) before renewal — target: two weeks. (2) We'll run an [Advanced Reporting] training session for the Finance team — I'll send a calendar invite by Friday. (3) I'll share the updated success plan by EOD today. Our next QBR is 14 October. Let me know if anything changes in the meantime."
**Why it works:** QBRs without written follow-ups produce commitments that evaporate. A 24-hour summary anchors action items to named owners, creates an audit trail, and signals to the customer that you run a tight ship.
**Word count:** 80–110 words; email format
**Avg. score:** 85-92
