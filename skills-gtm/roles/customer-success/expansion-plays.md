# Reference: CSM Expansion Plays Prompts

## Purpose
A collection of prompts for Customer Success Managers identifying and executing expansion opportunities within existing accounts. Covers seat-expansion outreach, upsell and cross-sell triggers, multi-product adoption, advocacy-to-expansion motions, and renewal-with-uplift framing. Calibrated for CSMs who own expansion as part of their NRR target. Not templates — starting points.

---

## Prompt 01 — Seat Utilisation Expansion Outreach
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Account reaches 80%+ seat utilisation, or a new team is mentioned in conversation
**Structure:** Reference current utilisation data → frame the growth as their success, not a sales signal → propose a logistics conversation with a specific timeline
**Example output:** "Hi [Name], I was pulling your Q3 usage data ahead of our upcoming call and noticed you're at 89 active users across 100 seats — impressive growth since you started with 40 users six months ago. As you onboard the [Marketing] team in Q4, you'll likely hit capacity before December. I want to get ahead of that rather than have growth cause any friction. Would it make sense to loop in [AE Name] for a quick conversation this month about your options? Happy to set it up."
**Why it works:** Framing expansion as a response to the customer's own growth removes the vendor-pushing-product dynamic. The CSM positions themselves as a planning partner, and the AE gets a warm intro with full context.
**Word count:** 90–110 words; email
**Avg. score:** 85-93

## Prompt 02 — Cross-Sell Trigger: New Use Case Mention
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Customer mentions a problem in conversation that maps to an adjacent product or module
**Structure:** Acknowledge the problem they raised → connect it to the capability → propose a short discovery call, not a demo
**Example output:** "You mentioned in our last call that the team is spending a lot of time manually reconciling data between [Tool A] and [Tool B]. That's actually something our [Integration Module] was built for — a few of our customers in similar situations reduced that reconciliation work by about 70%. Before I set up anything formal, I'd love to understand the specifics of your workflow. Do you have 20 minutes this week for me to ask a few questions?"
**Why it works:** Jumping to a demo before understanding the specific workflow is a common CSM mistake. A brief discovery call validates fit and keeps the CSM in a trusted-advisor role rather than a sales role.
**Word count:** 70–90 words; email or Slack
**Avg. score:** 84-92

## Prompt 03 — Multi-Product Adoption Kickoff
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Customer has purchased a second product but adoption is below 20% at 60 days post-purchase
**Structure:** Acknowledge the slow start without blame → surface the specific barrier → propose a focused enablement sprint
**Example output:** "Hi [Name], at the 60-day mark for [Product B] I wanted to check in — adoption is sitting at 14%, which is lower than I'd like to see at this stage. In my experience this usually comes down to one of two things: competing priorities or a setup step that got skipped. Can you tell me which it is? I'd like to propose a 2-week enablement sprint with the team most likely to get value from it first, rather than trying to roll it out broadly."
**Why it works:** Low post-purchase adoption is a churn signal masquerading as an expansion win. Naming it at 60 days — rather than letting it drift — gives the CSM time to recover the investment before the next renewal conversation.
**Word count:** 70–90 words; email
**Avg. score:** 83-91

## Prompt 04 — Advocacy-to-Expansion Motion
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Customer has given a positive NPS score, agreed to a case study, or referred another customer
**Structure:** Thank for the advocacy → connect their success story to a natural next step → propose the expansion as the next chapter of their story
**Example output:** "Thank you again for the [G2 review] last week — it genuinely helps other teams discover us. I'd love to close the loop with something useful for you: based on what you've achieved with the core platform, I think the [Analytics Add-on] would let you tell an even more compelling internal story to your leadership team about ROI. Would you be open to a 20-minute walkthrough? No commitment — I just think the timing is right."
**Why it works:** Advocates are psychologically consistent — they've already said publicly that the product works. Framing expansion as the next chapter of a success story they've publicly told makes the conversation feel natural.
**Word count:** 70–90 words; email or post-call follow-up
**Avg. score:** 84-92

## Prompt 05 — Renewal-with-Uplift Framing
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** 90 days before renewal for any account where expansion is part of the renewal discussion
**Structure:** Recap the value delivered → tie the uplift to specific growth in their usage or footprint → frame the pricing change as a reflection of their increased investment in the platform
**Example output:** "As we head into renewal, I want to walk you through what this year has looked like: you've grown from 40 to 91 users, expanded into two new teams, and achieved [specific outcome]. The renewal proposal [AE Name] is sharing reflects that growth — you're now in our [Growth tier], which includes [additional features] that weren't in your original agreement. I'm available to walk through the business case together before you meet with procurement."
**Why it works:** CSMs who stay silent during pricing conversations leave the champion without ammunition for internal approval. Arming them with the value narrative before they face procurement is the most useful thing a CSM can do in a renewal cycle.
**Word count:** 80–100 words; email or QBR talking point
**Avg. score:** 86-94

## Prompt 06 — Executive Sponsor Expansion Introduction
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Expansion opportunity that requires executive sponsorship at the customer side, or approval above the champion's level
**Structure:** Brief the champion first → get their blessing → request the introduction → prepare a 5-minute exec briefing (not a demo)
**Example output:** "Before we bring [AE Name] in, I'd love to get your read on something: would [CFO Name] be open to a 15-minute conversation about what the team has achieved this year and where we think there's a bigger opportunity? I can prepare a one-page summary that makes it easy for you to introduce the context. The goal isn't to sell — it's to make sure the expansion has executive visibility before renewal."
**Why it works:** Bypassing the champion to get to the executive buyer is a relationship-damaging shortcut. Getting the champion's blessing — and giving them the credit for the introduction — keeps the alliance intact.
**Word count:** 60–80 words; spoken or Slack
**Avg. score:** 83-91

## Prompt 07 — Usage Data Expansion Signal Briefing (Internal)
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Monthly account review; when account health data shows a power-user cluster in a specific team
**Structure:** Identify the power users → name the team → estimate ARR potential if expanded to that team → propose a playbook step
**Example output:** "Internal note for [Company]: Usage data shows 6 power users (>15 sessions/week) all sit in the [Data Engineering] team, which isn't currently licensed. That team has approximately 12 members. If we expanded access to the full team at our standard per-seat rate, that's $14,400 ARR additional. Champion is [Name B] in RevOps — recommend asking him to introduce us to the Data Engineering team lead as a use-case conversation, not a sales conversation."
**Why it works:** Expansion plays fail when they're handed to AEs without context. An internal briefing that identifies the specific team, the champion link, and the ARR potential makes it easy for the AE to act and for the CSM to stay in the loop.
**Word count:** N/A — internal note format
**Avg. score:** 82-90

## Prompt 08 — Feature Adoption → Upsell Bridge
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Customer is actively using a feature that acts as a natural gateway to a paid tier or premium add-on
**Structure:** Name the feature they're using heavily → surface the limitation they're about to hit → introduce the upgrade as the natural progression
**Example output:** "I noticed your team has been running [Custom Reports] almost daily this month — great adoption. You're currently on the [Starter] plan, which caps custom reports at 10. You've created 8. When you hit the limit, it'll block the workflow your team has built. I'd like to get ahead of that: the [Pro] plan removes the cap and adds scheduling and export features your team will likely want. Want me to put together a quick comparison?"
**Why it works:** Upsells that arrive before the customer hits a wall feel like customer success. Upsells that arrive after the customer hits the limit feel like a gotcha. Timing is everything.
**Word count:** 70–90 words; email or in-product message
**Avg. score:** 85-92

## Prompt 09 — New Hire / Team Expansion Trigger Response
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Customer mentions hiring into a team that uses the product, or LinkedIn signals a wave of hiring
**Structure:** Acknowledge the growth → connect to seat capacity → make the logistics conversation easy
**Example output:** "Congratulations on the [Sales Ops] hires — I saw a few LinkedIn updates from [Company] last week. As you bring new people onto the team, I want to make sure onboarding them is seamless. You're at 91 of 100 seats right now, so we're a few hires away from needing to look at your licence count. I can put together a quick options summary before that becomes urgent — want me to do that this week?"
**Why it works:** Using a public signal (LinkedIn) rather than a cold usage-data prompt makes the outreach feel attentive rather than intrusive. Connecting it to a concrete logistics concern removes any awkwardness about discussing pricing.
**Word count:** 70–90 words; email or Slack
**Avg. score:** 84-91

## Prompt 10 — Department Head Introduction Play
**Role:** CSM
**Trigger:** Expansion opportunity into a new department; champion is willing to facilitate but needs support
**Structure:** Give the champion a draft internal introduction email → make it easy for them to forward → offer to prepare a department-specific value summary
**Example output:** "I've drafted a short note you could forward to [Department Head Name] if it's helpful — feel free to edit it: 'Hey [Name], I've been working with [CSM Name] at [Vendor] on [outcome]. Given what you mentioned about [relevant team challenge], I thought it was worth introducing you. They're not trying to sell you anything — I just think 20 minutes would be worth your time.' Let me know if you'd like me to put together a one-pager tailored to [Department]'s workflow."
**Why it works:** Champions who want to help but don't know what to say often do nothing. Giving them exact words they can forward — lightly edited in their own voice — dramatically increases the probability of the introduction happening.
**Word count:** Draft intro: 50–70 words; outer note: 30–40 words
**Avg. score:** 86-93
