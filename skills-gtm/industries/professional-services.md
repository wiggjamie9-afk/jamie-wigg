# Reference: Professional Services GTM Prompts

## Purpose
Selling into professional services (consulting, legal, accounting, architecture, engineering) means selling to the people who sell their own expertise for a living. These buyers are highly skeptical of vendor claims, protective of client confidentiality, and motivated by two levers: billable utilization and project margin. Partner-led buying is common — the decision-maker is often the Managing Partner or Practice Lead, not an IT or operations executive. Talent retention and delivery quality are existential concerns in a business where people are the product. Not templates — starting points.

## Buyer landscape
- **Typical buyers:** Managing Partner, Chief Operating Partner, Director of Operations, IT Director (at larger firms), Practice Group Leader, CFO
- **Common pains:** Utilization rates below target eating into partner income, project margin eroding from scope creep and unbilled overtime, talent attrition from burnout and poor tooling, client delivery inconsistency across offices or teams, knowledge silos making cross-sell harder
- **Watch-outs:** Professional services firms will not share client data or firm financial data with a vendor without strict NDA and data-handling assurances. Never ask to see utilization numbers or billing rates without establishing trust first. Partner-led buying means consensus is required — one partner champion is rarely enough.

## Prompt 01 — Utilization Cold Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target is a mid-market consulting or professional services firm where billable utilization is a known P&L driver
**Structure:**
```
Subject: [Firm]'s utilization rate — a rough number

[Name], for a [firm type] at [Firm]'s scale, a 2–3 point improvement in billable utilization
across your team typically adds [$ estimate] to partner distributions without adding headcount.
We helped [comparable firm] move from [X%] to [Y%] in [timeframe]. Worth 20 minutes?
```
**Example output:** "Subject: Protiviti's utilization rate — a rough number\n\nAlex, for a management consulting firm at Protiviti's scale, a 2–3 point improvement in billable utilization across senior consultants typically adds $3–6M to partner distributions without adding headcount. We helped Grant Thornton Advisory move from 68% to 74% utilization in two quarters. Worth 20 minutes?"
**Why it works:** Utilization is the single P&L metric every professional services partner tracks. Translating a percentage into a partner distribution dollar figure converts an abstract efficiency conversation into a compensation conversation — which is always heard.
**Word count:** 60–80 words
**Avg. score:** 82–90

## Prompt 02 — Project Margin / Scope Creep Discovery
**Role:** AE
**Trigger:** Prospect has mentioned project profitability or write-offs as a pain in prior conversation
**Structure:**
```
When you see margin erode on a project — is it usually visible before the project closes
or does it only show up in the post-engagement analysis? And when you catch it early,
what does the correction process actually look like for your project leads?
```
**Example output:** "When you see margin erode on a litigation support engagement — is it usually visible before the matter closes, or does it only show up in the write-off analysis at billing? And when your senior associates do catch it early, what does the correction process actually look like — do they have the authority to adjust scope, or does it escalate to a partner?"
**Why it works:** Professional services firms that lose margin lose it quietly and late. Questions that expose the gap between when problems occur and when they're visible earn trust with Practice Leads who are tired of discovering problems at invoice time.
**Word count:** 55–70 words
**Avg. score:** 83–91

## Prompt 03 — Knowledge Silo / Cross-Sell Angle
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target firm has multiple practice areas or offices and is known to struggle with cross-practice collaboration
**Structure:**
```
Subject: Cross-practice revenue at [Firm]

[Name], most multi-practice firms we talk to have the same problem: a tax team that doesn't
know the M&A team just closed a deal with a client who needs restructuring advice.
We helped [comparable firm] identify [X] cross-sell opportunities in the first 60 days
without any change to how partners manage their relationships. Worth a look?
```
**Example output:** "Subject: Cross-practice revenue at RSM\n\nCaroline, most multi-practice firms we talk to have the same problem: an advisory team that doesn't know the audit team just onboarded a PE-backed client who needs transaction services. We helped BDO identify 34 cross-sell opportunities in the first 60 days without asking partners to change how they manage client relationships. Worth a look?"
**Why it works:** Cross-sell at professional services firms is political — partners guard client relationships. Any solution framed as "changing how partners work" will be rejected immediately. "Finding opportunities without changing relationship management" removes that objection upfront.
**Word count:** 65–85 words
**Avg. score:** 81–89

## Prompt 04 — Talent Retention / Burnout Framing
**Role:** AE / SDR
**Trigger:** Target firm has high associate turnover or is known to be investing in employee experience
**Structure:**
```
Subject: Associate attrition at [Firm]

[Name], the cost of replacing a third-year consultant or senior associate is roughly
[X months of their fully loaded cost]. Most firms we talk to are losing [Y%] of
that cohort annually — usually to burnout from manual work that shouldn't touch
a senior person. What's [Firm]'s current attrition picture looking like?
```
**Example output:** "Subject: Associate attrition at Deloitte Digital\n\nPatrick, the fully loaded cost of replacing a senior consultant is roughly 1.5× their annual salary once you factor in recruiting, ramp time, and lost billable hours. Most firms we talk to are losing 18–22% of that cohort annually — often to burnout from manual data work and status reporting that shouldn't be touching a $150K resource. What's Deloitte Digital's current attrition picture in the analytics practice?"
**Why it works:** Talent retention at professional services firms is measured in recruiting cost and lost billable revenue — not just HR metrics. Framing attrition as an economic problem (not a culture problem) aligns with how Managing Partners and COOs think about the issue.
**Word count:** 65–85 words
**Avg. score:** 80–88

## Prompt 05 — Partner Buy-In Conversation
**Role:** AE
**Trigger:** Mid-level champion (Director of Operations, IT Director) is excited but has not yet brought partners into the evaluation
**Structure:**
```
[Name], I've seen a few of these evaluations stall when it gets to the partner group
because they haven't been part of the conversation yet. Would it make sense to get
one or two partners in the room now — even just for 30 minutes — so their concerns
are part of the solution design rather than a surprise at the end?
```
**Example output:** "Sandra, I've seen a few of these evaluations stall when it reaches the partner group at the management committee stage because they haven't been part of the conversation yet. Would it make sense to get one or two practice group leaders in the room now — even just for 30 minutes — so their concerns around client data handling and billing workflow are part of our solution design rather than a surprise at sign-off?"
**Why it works:** At professional services firms, partners who weren't involved in the evaluation often kill deals they view as "IT impositions." Getting even one partner voice in early transforms the vendor from an IT project into a partner-endorsed initiative.
**Word count:** 65–80 words
**Avg. score:** 83–91

## Prompt 06 — Client Delivery Consistency Angle
**Role:** AE
**Trigger:** Target firm is scaling quickly, expanding to new offices, or managing delivery across distributed teams
**Structure:**
```
[Name], as [Firm] scales across [X] offices — how consistent is the client delivery experience
actually getting? Specifically, when a partner in [City A] hands off a project to a team in
[City B], what's the mechanism that ensures the quality standard travels with it?
```
**Example output:** "Helen, as Alvarez & Marsal scales across 40 offices globally — how consistent is the client delivery experience actually getting? Specifically, when a Dallas partner hands off a restructuring engagement to a team in Amsterdam, what's the mechanism that ensures the methodology and quality standard travels with it — or is that largely dependent on individual partner judgment?"
**Why it works:** Delivery inconsistency is a reputation risk at professional services firms. The question surfaces a real operational gap without making an accusation, and positions the vendor as a delivery infrastructure partner rather than a productivity tool.
**Word count:** 55–75 words
**Avg. score:** 82–90

## Prompt 07 — Competitive Displacement (Legacy Time-Tracking / PSA)
**Role:** AE
**Trigger:** Prospect is currently using a legacy PSA tool (Maconomy, BigTime, Deltek) and has surfaced dissatisfaction
**Structure:**
```
[Name], a lot of firms that come to us from [Legacy Tool] say the tipping point was
[specific limitation — e.g., reporting latency, ERP sync failures, mobile experience].
Has that come up for your team, or is there a different gap that's driving the evaluation?
```
**Example output:** "Tom, a lot of firms that come to us from Deltek Vision say the tipping point was the reporting latency — pulling a real-time utilization view requires a report run that's always 24–48 hours behind actual time entries. Has that come up for your partners at Huron, or is there a different gap driving the evaluation?"
**Why it works:** PSA replacements are high-friction because the tool is embedded in billing workflows partners depend on. Naming the known friction point of the incumbent signals product knowledge and opens a specific problem-solution conversation instead of a generic demo.
**Word count:** 50–70 words
**Avg. score:** 81–89

## Prompt 08 — Renewal / Expansion Multi-Office Pitch
**Role:** AE / CSM
**Trigger:** Customer is live in one office or one practice and renewal is approaching — expansion opportunity exists
**Structure:**
```
[Name], [Office/Practice] has been at [utilization metric] for [timeframe] — that puts you
in the top [X%] of our customers your size. The teams that get the most out of expanding
to a second office typically do it before renewal, not after, because the data network
effect kicks in faster. Is there a practice lead in [Target Office] who'd be the right
conversation to have?
```
**Example output:** "Yuki, the Chicago M&A practice has been at 76% utilization for 18 months — that puts you in the top 20% of advisory firms your size on the platform. The teams that get the most out of expanding to a second office typically do it before renewal, not after, because the cross-office benchmarking data becomes useful faster. Is there a practice lead in the New York restructuring group who'd be the right conversation to have?"
**Why it works:** Expansion at professional services firms requires a named internal champion in the new office — it can't be imposed from above. Asking for an introduction rather than a meeting with "leadership" respects the partnership structure and moves faster.
**Word count:** 70–85 words
**Avg. score:** 83–91
