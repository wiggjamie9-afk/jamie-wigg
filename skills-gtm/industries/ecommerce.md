# Reference: Ecommerce GTM Prompts

## Purpose
Ecommerce buying is driven by marketing and growth leaders who think in conversion rates, CAC, AOV, and ROAS — not procurement timelines. Deals move fast compared to enterprise verticals, but they're also dropped fast if ROI isn't demonstrated quickly. Peak-season cycles (Q4 for most, back-to-school for others) create both urgency and frozen-budget windows — time your outreach accordingly. DTC brands operate very differently from marketplace sellers, who in turn operate differently from omnichannel retailers. Returns, logistics, and post-purchase experience are increasingly the differentiating layer. Not templates — starting points.

## Buyer landscape
- **Typical buyers:** CMO, VP of Growth, Director of eCommerce, VP of Marketing, Head of Retention (CRM), Head of Merchandising; CFO or COO at $50M+ brands
- **Common pains:** Rising CAC squeezing contribution margin, cart abandonment and low CVR, poor post-purchase experience driving returns and low LTV, inventory forecasting failures leading to stockouts or markdowns, marketplace fee erosion on Amazon/Walmart reducing DTC margin
- **Watch-outs:** Ecommerce buyers are inundated with vendors — every pitch claims "we increase conversion." Lead with a specific metric and a named comparable brand. Never pitch to an ecommerce leader during October or November — they're in peak-season lockdown. Post-BFCM (December–January) is the best window for new vendor evaluations.

## Prompt 01 — CAC / Contribution Margin Cold Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target brand is DTC-native and has recently raised capital or is known to be scaling paid acquisition
**Structure:**
```
Subject: [Brand]'s CAC — a conversion angle

[Name], DTC brands at [Brand]'s scale are typically paying $[X] CAC on Meta — and every
point of CVR improvement on the PDP is worth [$ estimate] in recovered contribution margin.
We helped [comparable brand] lift CVR [X%] without touching their paid budget.
Worth a 20-minute look?
```
**Example output:** "Subject: Jolie's CAC — a conversion angle\n\nRaj, DTC brands at Jolie's scale are typically paying $55–70 CAC on Meta right now — and every point of CVR improvement on the product page is worth roughly $180K per month in recovered contribution margin at your volume. We helped Caraway lift PDP CVR 18% in 60 days without touching their paid budget. Worth a 20-minute look?"
**Why it works:** Ecommerce growth leaders can do the CAC math in seconds. Translating a CVR lift into recovered margin (rather than revenue) speaks directly to the contribution-margin conversation every DTC CFO is having with their CMO.
**Word count:** 60–80 words
**Avg. score:** 83–91

## Prompt 02 — Abandoned Cart Recovery Email
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Target brand has a checkout flow and no visible abandoned cart tooling (identifiable via tech stack scan)
**Structure:**
```
Subject: [Brand]'s checkout — [X]% leaving before purchase

[Name], roughly [X%] of shoppers who reach [Brand]'s checkout won't complete. The recoverable
slice — people with intent who hit a friction point — is typically [Y%] of that.
We helped [comparable brand] recover [$ amount] in 90 days from that segment alone.
Quick look?
```
**Example output:** "Subject: Parade's checkout — 72% leaving before purchase\n\nKatie, roughly 72% of shoppers who reach Parade's checkout won't complete — industry average for apparel DTC. The recoverable slice — people with real intent who hit a payment friction point or got distracted — is typically 18–22% of that group. We helped Mate the Label recover $340K in 90 days from that segment alone, before touching a single acquisition dollar. Quick look?"
**Why it works:** Checkout abandonment is a known problem every ecommerce leader lives with. Segmenting it into "recoverable" vs. "unrecoverable" demonstrates analytical depth and makes the addressable opportunity feel concrete rather than theoretical.
**Word count:** 65–85 words
**Avg. score:** 81–89

## Prompt 03 — BFCM Peak-Season Readiness Outreach
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Outreach window is August–September (ahead of Q4 peak; never during October–November lockdown)
**Structure:**
```
Subject: [Brand]'s Q4 readiness — one question

[Name], Q4 is 8 weeks out. Most brands we talk to in [category] have the traffic plan locked
but discover their [checkout / retention / logistics] stack has a capacity or conversion problem
at 3× traffic — too late to fix. Is [Brand] running a peak-season tech audit before October,
or is that not part of the planning cycle?
```
**Example output:** "Subject: Ruggable's Q4 readiness — one question\n\nBrittany, Q4 is 8 weeks out. Most home goods DTC brands we talk to have the paid media plan locked but discover their checkout and post-purchase flow has a conversion problem at 3× BFCM traffic — and it's too late to fix in November. Is Ruggable running a peak-season tech audit before your October freeze, or is that not part of the planning cycle?"
**Why it works:** Ecommerce leaders are uniquely responsive to pre-peak urgency because the cost of a Q4 failure is catastrophic. Framing the vendor as a risk-mitigation resource (not a feature pitch) turns the timing into an advantage.
**Word count:** 65–85 words
**Avg. score:** 82–90

## Prompt 04 — Returns / Reverse Logistics Discovery
**Role:** AE
**Trigger:** Prospect is in apparel, footwear, or electronics — sectors with return rates above 20%
**Structure:**
```
Before I go into the platform — tell me where returns sit as a percentage of revenue right now,
and whether that's a COGS problem, a logistics cost problem, or a customer experience problem
for [Brand]. Those three usually need very different answers.
```
**Example output:** "Before I go into the platform — tell me where returns sit as a percentage of revenue for Allbirds right now, and whether your team thinks about that as a COGS problem (product fit or sizing issues), a logistics cost problem (return shipping eating contribution), or a customer experience problem (shoppers who return once and don't come back). Those three usually need very different answers and I don't want to show you the wrong one."
**Why it works:** Returns are emotionally loaded for ecommerce operators — they represent failed transactions, lost margin, and logistics cost in one event. Asking the buyer to classify their own problem (not jumping to a solution) demonstrates strategic thinking and earns discovery depth.
**Word count:** 60–80 words
**Avg. score:** 83–91

## Prompt 05 — LTV / Retention Angle (Post-Purchase)
**Role:** AE / SDR
**Trigger:** Target brand has a subscription or loyalty program, or has publicly discussed repeat purchase rate
**Structure:**
```
Subject: [Brand]'s LTV after first purchase

[Name], for a brand like [Brand], the margin difference between a one-time buyer and a
three-time buyer is typically [X multiplier]. What percentage of [Brand]'s first-time buyers
make a second purchase within 90 days, and is that number moving?
```
**Example output:** "Subject: Brooklinen's LTV after first purchase\n\nAlex, for a premium home brand like Brooklinen, the contribution margin difference between a one-time buyer and a three-time buyer is typically 4–6×. What percentage of Brooklinen's first-time buyers make a second purchase within 90 days — and is that number moving in the direction you want it to?"
**Why it works:** LTV conversations require the buyer to know their own metrics — and most ecommerce operators don't have clean 90-day repurchase numbers. The question either surfaces a data gap (which is a selling opportunity) or invites a benchmark conversation where the vendor can add value.
**Word count:** 55–75 words
**Avg. score:** 82–90

## Prompt 06 — Marketplace vs. DTC Margin Tension
**Role:** AE / SDR
**Trigger:** Target brand sells on Amazon as well as their own DTC site and is known to be managing channel conflict
**Structure:**
```
Subject: [Brand]'s channel mix — a margin question

[Name], brands selling on both Amazon and DTC typically see [X%] gross margin on DTC vs.
[Y%] on Amazon after referral fees and FBA costs. How is [Brand] thinking about shifting
mix toward DTC — or has the Amazon volume made that conversation hard to have internally?
```
**Example output:** "Subject: Stanley 1913's channel mix — a margin question\n\nMike, brands at Stanley's volume typically see 55–60% gross margin on DTC vs. 30–35% on Amazon after referral fees and FBA fulfillment costs. How is Stanley thinking about shifting mix toward the owned channel — or has the Amazon velocity made that conversation politically difficult to have internally?"
**Why it works:** Channel conflict between DTC and marketplace is one of the most sensitive strategic debates inside a consumer brand. Naming the margin differential gives a factual frame for the conversation, and acknowledging the internal politics of the question builds trust with the growth or ecommerce leader who's living it.
**Word count:** 65–85 words
**Avg. score:** 80–88

## Prompt 07 — Inventory Forecasting / Stockout Cost Opener
**Role:** AE
**Trigger:** Prospect operates in a demand-volatile category (seasonal, trend-driven, viral-product-dependent)
**Structure:**
```
[Name], when [Brand] had a stockout on a top-10 SKU last [season / period] — what was the
actual cost? Not just lost revenue on that SKU, but the CAC you spent to bring someone in
who couldn't convert, and the LTV you gave up when they went to a competitor.
```
**Example output:** "Sarah, when SKIMS had a stockout on a best-selling shapewear SKU last Q4 — what was the actual cost? Not just the lost revenue on that SKU, but the Meta and Google CAC you spent to bring someone to the PDP who couldn't convert, and the LTV you gave up when they bounced to a competitor and didn't come back. Those three numbers usually add up to more than the GMV miss."
**Why it works:** Stockout conversations in ecommerce typically focus on the inventory miss. Expanding the frame to include paid acquisition waste and lost LTV triples the impact number — and opens a much larger budget justification for the solution.
**Word count:** 65–80 words
**Avg. score:** 83–91

## Prompt 08 — Post-BFCM New-Vendor-Evaluation Window Outreach
**Role:** SDR/BDR
**Trigger:** Outreach timing is December 1 – January 15 (post-peak, open to new evaluation conversations)
**Structure:**
```
Subject: BFCM debrief — what broke?

[Name], peak season just finished. Most [category] brands we talk to right now have a list
of 2–3 things that didn't perform — [checkout, email, returns, logistics handoff, something else].
What's on [Brand]'s list, and is there appetite to fix any of it before Q1 planning locks?
```
**Example output:** "Subject: BFCM debrief — what broke?\n\nTaylor, peak season just finished. Most fashion DTC brands we talk to right now have a short list of things that didn't perform — checkout drop-off at 3× traffic, post-purchase email that didn't hit, a returns surge that overwhelmed the 3PL. What's on Reformation's list, and is there appetite to fix any of it before the Q1 planning cycle locks in March?"
**Why it works:** Post-BFCM is the highest-signal window in ecommerce — buyers have fresh pain, budget planning is open, and the emotional memory of what went wrong is still sharp. Asking "what broke" before pitching a solution is the fastest path to a real conversation.
**Word count:** 65–80 words
**Avg. score:** 84–92
